- **Location**: Allahdino, about 40 km east of Karachi in Sindh, on a terrace near the Malir river, close to the Indus delta and the Arabian Sea coast.
- **Period/Level**: A single-period mature Harappan village, c. 2500-2000 BCE, excavated by Walter Fairservis (1973-77). Only 1.4 ha in extent and, unusually, unfortified.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The classic small Harappan village - no citadel, no wall - which nonetheless carries the full urban artefact repertoire and startling wealth.
  - Mud-brick houses on stone foundations laid out west-south-west to east-north-east; a large multi-roomed building on a mud-brick platform in the north-east appears to have had special importance.
  - Three wells with very narrow mouths (60-90 cm), apparently to make ground water rise higher under hydraulic pressure; Fairservis read the well-and-drain complex as an irrigation system, though the evidence is inconclusive.
  - A small terracotta jar held a hoard of gold, silver, bronze, agate and carnelian ornaments - a massive belt or necklace of 36 long carnelian beads with bronze spacers, and a multi-strand silver necklace. Seals, copper objects, toy carts and triangular terracotta cakes also occurred.
  - Typical black-on-red Harappan pottery formed barely 1 per cent of the finds here, a caution against assuming uniformity across the culture zone.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Fairservis, Allahdino excavation reports.
