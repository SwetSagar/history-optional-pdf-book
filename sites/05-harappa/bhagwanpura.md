- **Location**: Bhagwanpura, Kurukshetra district, Haryana, on the right bank of the Sarasvati (modern Sarsuti).
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by J. P. Joshi (ASI, 1975-77). Period IA is late Harappan, c. 1700-1400 BCE; Period IB is the late Harappan-Painted Grey Ware overlap, c. 1400-1000 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site for the overlap of the late Harappan and PGW cultures - the single most-cited evidence on the Harappan-Vedic relationship.
  - Late Harappan pot forms occur interlocked with PGW in the same levels (the same overlap appears at Dadheri in Haryana and Katpalon and Nagar in Punjab), showing the two cultures coexisted rather than simply succeeded one another.
  - Flourishing specialised craft: two clay tablets and nineteen sherds carry graffiti that may represent a surviving script. Faience ornaments, semi-precious stone beads, terracotta cart frames, kilns and fire altars.
  - A thirteen-room house of the PGW phase and horse bones from the overlap layers, dated c. 1600-1000 BCE. Burnt bricks reported here are the only late Harappan instance in the region, though R. S. Sharma questions the dating of that layer.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Joshi in ASI, *IAR*.
