- **Location**: Sotka Koh, near Pasni on the Makran coast of Baluchistan, on the Shadi Kaur river a few kilometres inland from the Arabian Sea.
- **Period/Level**: A mature Harappan coastal station, c. 2500-1900 BCE. Discovered by George F. Dales during his Makran coast survey of 1960 and tested thereafter.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the two Harappan stations on the Makran coast, plotting the sea route westward to the Persian Gulf.
  - A rectangular structure on a hill terrace within a stone and mud-brick enclosure, and open pit ovens used for firing pottery on the spot.
  - Black pottery with reddish bands around the neck and geometric motifs, alongside classic Harappan red ware and potsherds of Indus type.
  - With Sutkagendor and Balakot it marks the coastal chain linking Sindh to Oman and the Gulf; the settlement appears to have been deserted as the coastline shifted seaward and its creek silted up.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4 (Map 4.1); Dales, Makran coast survey (1962).
