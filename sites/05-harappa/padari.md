- **Location**: Padri (Kerala-no-dhoro), near Talaja in Bhavnagar district, Gujarat, on a tidal creek close to the Gulf of Khambhat coast of Saurashtra.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Vasant Shinde (Deccan College) in the 1990s. Period I is an early Harappan 'Padri ware' phase, c. 3000-2600 BCE; Period II mature Harappan, c. 2600-2000 BCE; a late Harappan occupation follows.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Established that Gujarat had a well-developed early Harappan horizon of its own, indigenous and distinct from the Amri-Nal tradition of Sindh.
  - Padri ware: sturdy storage jars with painted buffalo-horn and allied motifs, a local ceramic tradition that precedes Harappan contact - the basis for treating Saurashtra as a separate early Harappan region.
  - Symbols akin to Harappan writing occur in the early Harappan levels here, as at Kalibangan, Dholavira and Harappa - evidence that the roots of the script lie in this phase.
  - Rectangular houses and workshops, huge copper fish-hooks, and salt-making installations - the earliest evidence of salt production on the Indian coast, and the likely reason for the site's position.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Shinde, Padri excavation reports.
