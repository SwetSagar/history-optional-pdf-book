- **Location**: Rojdi, Rajkot district, Gujarat, on the north bank of the Bhadar river in central Saurashtra.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by M. K. Dhavalikar, G. L. Possehl and M. H. Raval (1982-86). Rojdi A and B are mature (Sorath) Harappan, c. 2500-2000 BCE; Rojdi C is late Harappan, c. 2000-1700 BCE. Size 5-10 ha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Type site of the Sorath Harappan of Saurashtra, and the clearest proof that the Harappan 'decline' was regional rather than universal.
  - At about the time Mohenjodaro was being abandoned, the people of Rojdi were expanding and rebuilding their settlement, adding a new stone enclosure wall - Upinder Singh's standard illustration of the eastward and southward shift of population.
  - Hard red-to-buff ware with the hemispherical bowl as the commonest form, and short Harappan inscriptions on the rims of potsherds; black-and-red ware also occurs, as at Lothal, Surkotada and Desalpur.
  - The richest millet record of any Harappan site - ragi, bajra, jowar and other summer millets - marking the shift to a monsoon-based, drought-tolerant agriculture that outlasted the cities.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Possehl & Raval, *Harappan Civilization and Rojdi* (1989).
