- **Location**: Jhukar, Larkana district, Sindh, on the Indus plain a short distance from Mohenjodaro.
- **Period/Level**: Type site of the Jhukar culture, the late Harappan phase of Sindh, c. 1900-1500 BCE; excavated by N. G. Majumdar in the 1930s. Jhangar culture levels follow above.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Gives Sindh its post-urban identity and shows that the transition from the mature phase was gradual, not a rupture.
  - Buff ware with a red or cream slip painted in black; urn burials; circular stamp seals with geometric designs (a form also found at Daimabad).
  - The diagnostic signs of de-urbanisation: writing survives only on pottery, cubical weights become infrequent, seals change in form, and female figurines and the Indus script disappear.
  - Pottery indicates reciprocal contact with late Harappan Lothal and Rangpur in Gujarat. The same culture is represented at Chanhudaro and Amri, so 'Jhukar' names a regional phase, not a single site.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
