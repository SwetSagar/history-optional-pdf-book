- **Location**: Mohenjodaro ('the mound of the dead'), Larkana district, Sindh, on the right bank of the Indus about 5 km from the present channel; in protohistoric times the river ran closer.
- **Period/Level**: Found by R. D. Banerji (1922); excavated by John Marshall, E. J. H. Mackay, Mortimer Wheeler (1950) and G. F. Dales (1964). Mature Harappan, c. 2600-1900 BCE. About 200 ha, the largest Harappan city; Fairservis (1967) estimated some 41,250 people in the lower town alone.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The fullest expression of Harappan urbanism - the benchmark against which every other site is described.
  - Two mounds: a smaller but higher western mound, the citadel, raised on an artificial mud and mud-brick platform about 400 x 200 m and rising 12 m above the plain, girt by a 6 m thick retaining wall with projections and a tower; and the larger, lower eastern mound, the lower town.
  - On the citadel: the Great Bath, about 14.5 x 7 m, sealed with bitumen and flanked by rooms and a well; the so-called 'granary', from which no grain was actually reported; the 'college of priests'; and a pillared hall to the south. The lower town has the DK, HR and S areas, over 700 wells, shops and workmen's quarters, and the finest drainage system of any Bronze Age city.
  - The bronze 'dancing girl', the steatite bearded 'priest-king', the Pashupati (proto-Shiva) seal, and seated male figures in shawls. Hoards of jewellery buried in the last levels and skulls huddled at one spot have been read as signs of insecurity, though not of the 'Aryan invasion' Wheeler once proposed.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Marshall, *Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization* (1931).
