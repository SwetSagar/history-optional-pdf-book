- **Location**: Alamgirpur, Meerut district, western Uttar Pradesh, on the left bank of the Hindon, a tributary of the Yamuna; the easternmost Harappan site known.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Y. D. Sharma (ASI) in 1958-59. Period I is late Harappan, c. 1900-1400 BCE; after a break in occupation comes Period II (Painted Grey Ware), then NBPW and early historic levels.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Marks the eastern limit of the Harappan world, in the upper Ganga-Yamuna doab, and the point where the Harappan and PGW sequences meet.
  - Late Harappan pottery of well-levigated clay with a thin cream wash or bright red slip, painted in black with geometric and naturalistic designs; dishes, cups, vases, beakers, and cubical dice. A few burnt bricks occurred, unusual for the doab.
  - A broken copper blade, chert blades, querns and pestles, bone points; beads and bangles of steatite, faience, carnelian, agate and terracotta; terracotta cakes, carts and wheels.
  - A cloth impression on a trough is taken as evidence of cotton. The break between the late Harappan and PGW levels here contrasts with the overlap at Bhagwanpura, and is central to dating the doab sequence.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4-5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI, *IAR* 1958-59.
