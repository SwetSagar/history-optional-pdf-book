- **Location**: Manda, Jammu district, on the right bank of the Chenab at the foot of the Pir Panjal - the northernmost Harappan site known.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by J. P. Joshi and Madhu Bala (ASI, 1976-77). Period I pre-Harappan; Period II mature Harappan, c. 2500-1900 BCE; Period III late Harappan, followed by PGW and early historic levels.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Fixes the northern frontier of the Harappan world and identifies its timber-procurement zone.
  - Harappan red ware in the full range - jars, dish-on-stand, beakers, goblets and perforated vessels - with potsherds bearing Harappan graffiti and script signs.
  - Its position where the Chenab leaves the hills suggests a station for procuring deodar and other Himalayan timber for the brick-and-beam architecture of the plains cities.
  - Bone points, copper objects, chert blades and terracotta cakes; the sequence continues into PGW, tying the Harappan occupation of the Jammu foothills to the later iron age.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI, *IAR* 1976-77.
