- **Location**: Rakhigarhi (villages Rakhi Khas and Rakhi Shahpur), Hisar district, Haryana, on the dry Ghaggar-Drishadvati plain.
- **Period/Level**: Discovered by Suraj Bhan (1963); excavated by Amarendra Nath (ASI, 1997-2000) and Vasant Shinde (Deccan College, 2011-16). Early Harappan Period I; mature Harappan c. 2600-1900 BCE. Over 80 ha across five mounds - by some measurements the largest Harappan site of all.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest Harappan city in India, with all three phases represented, and the source of the first ancient genome from the civilisation.
  - Period I was already a planned settlement with mud-brick structures; it yielded uninscribed seals, pottery with graffiti, terracotta wheels, carts, rattles and bull figurines, chert blades, weights, a bone point and a muller - and a stacked set of hopscotches, suggesting a game like pithu goes back to early Harappan times.
  - The citadel mound is walled in mud brick and has platforms, a brick well, fire altars, streets and drains. A lapidary workshop held about 3,000 unfinished beads and roughly cut carnelian, chalcedony, agate and jasper, with polishers and a heating hearth; elsewhere bones, antlers and ivory with finished and unfinished points, combs, needles and engravers showed bone and ivory working.
  - A cemetery of mostly brick-lined pit burials, one with a wooden coffin. DNA from a female skeleton here produced the first Harappan genome (Shinde et al., 2019), which showed no Steppe ancestry - now central to the Aryan-origins debate.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI, *IAR*; Shinde et al. (2019).
