- **Location**: Nageshwar, near Dwarka in Devbhumi Dwarka (formerly Jamnagar) district, Gujarat, immediately on the Gulf of Kutch coast.
- **Period/Level**: A single-period mature Harappan settlement, c. 2500-2000 BCE, with no pre- or post-Harappan occupation. Surveyed and excavated by the M. S. University of Baroda (K. T. M. Hegde, K. K. Bhan and others, 1983-86). Size 1-5 ha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The purest example of a Harappan single-craft settlement, founded to work one raw material and abandoned with it.
  - Devoted exclusively to shell working and specialising in bangles, using the Turbinella pyrum of the adjacent gulf; unfinished bangles, sawn cores and shell waste dominate the assemblage.
  - Pottery of reddish-yellow sandy clay with floral motifs, perforated jars, and channel-spouted bowls with a red slip and black painted designs; fire altars have also been reported.
  - With Balakot, Chanhudaro, Kuntasi, Bagasra, Rangpur, Nagwada, Dholavira and Lothal it defines the Gujarat shell-working network that supplied ornaments to the inland cities - the clearest case for Harappan site specialisation.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Hegde, Bhan et al., Nageshwar reports.
