- **Location**: Desalpur (Gunthli), Nakhatrana taluka, Kutch district, Gujarat, on a seasonal stream in the inland Kutch uplands, on the route between Sindh and Saurashtra.
- **Period/Level**: A mature Harappan fortified settlement of c. 2500-1900 BCE with no early Harappan level (like Lothal, Chanhudaro, Mitathal, Alamgirpur and Ropar). Excavated by P. P. Pandya and K. V. Soundara Rajan (ASI, 1963-64).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A small fortified Harappan station holding the interior Kutch route, with unusually clear administrative evidence for its size.
  - A massive fortification wall of stone rubble and mud brick with a stone revetment, serving as flood protection as well as defence; streets and drains within.
  - Three seals bearing the Harappan script, together with stone weights and copper objects - a concentration of sealing and weighing equipment that implies a control or transit function.
  - Black-and-red ware occurs alongside classic Harappan red ware, part of the Gujarat group (with Lothal, Surkotada, Rojdi and Rangpur) in which BRW appears in a Harappan context.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4-5; ASI, *IAR* 1963-64.
