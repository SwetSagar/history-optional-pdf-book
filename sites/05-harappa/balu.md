- **Location**: Balu, Kaithal district, Haryana, on the Ghaggar-Hakra (Sarasvati) tract of the Indo-Gangetic divide, near Siswal and Banawali.
- **Period/Level**: A small fortified rural settlement with early Harappan levels of the Kalibangan I type (c. 2900-2600 BCE) and a mature Harappan phase (c. 2600-1900 BCE); excavated by the Haryana Department of Archaeology.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Yields the fullest archaeobotanical record of any early and mature Harappan site, and shows that fortification was not confined to towns.
  - Crop remains studied by K. S. Saraswat and A. K. Pokharia (2001-02): several varieties of barley and wheat, rice, horse gram, green gram, chickpea, field pea, grass pea, sesamum, melon, watermelon, date and grapes - plus the earliest evidence of garlic in the subcontinent.
  - A fortified village rather than a town: a reminder that the Harappan defensive wall was a settlement type, not a marker of urban rank.
  - Mud-brick houses and pottery of the Kalibangan I range in the early Harappan level, with a continuous transition into the mature phase - part of the Ghaggar cluster that documents indigenous origins.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Saraswat & Pokharia, *Pragdhara* 2001-02.
