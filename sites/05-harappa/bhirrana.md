- **Location**: Bhirrana, Fatehabad district, Haryana, on the left bank of the dry Ghaggar (Sarasvati).
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by L. S. Rao (ASI, 2003-06). Period IA Hakra ware, IB early Harappan, IIA early mature Harappan, IIB mature Harappan - one of the few sites carrying the whole sequence from Hakra ware to the mature phase. The excavators' dates reaching into the 6th-5th millennium BCE are debated.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A key witness to the indigenous, unbroken origins of the Harappan civilisation on the Ghaggar.
  - Period IA had subterranean dwelling pits cut into the natural soil with Hakra ware; Period IB brought mud-brick structures in the 1:2:3 ratio, including a six-room house with a central courtyard and chullahs, and copper arrowheads, rings and bangles.
  - The mature Harappan settlement was ringed by a massive mud-brick fortification. Three multi-roomed house complexes were exposed, one of ten rooms with verandah and courtyard, another of six rooms with a kitchen and four courtyards; a 4.8 m wide street ran along the wall, with three lanes off it. A circular tandoor and chullahs were found, with charred bones and a bovine skull beside one.
  - Beads of carnelian, jasper, steatite, shell and terracotta; terracotta gamesmen, rattle, bull figurine and bangles. A thick red-ware sherd bears an incised female figure whose pose recalls the Mohenjodaro bronze 'dancing girl'.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Rao et al. in ASI, *IAR* 2004-05.
