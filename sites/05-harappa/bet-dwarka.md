- **Location**: Bet Dwarka (Shankhodhar), an island off Okha in the Gulf of Kutch, Devbhumi Dwarka district, Gujarat.
- **Period/Level**: Late Harappan, with a thermoluminescence date of c. 1570 BCE. Explored on land and underwater by the Marine Archaeology Centre of the NIO (S. R. Rao, A. S. Gaur, Sundaresh) from the 1980s.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the very few late Harappan settlements large enough to be called urban, and the best evidence that Persian Gulf contact outlived the cities.
  - A partly submerged settlement originally about 4 x 0.5 km, with fortification remains - inner and outer walls, bastions and a large stone jetty - plus stone anchors of the type also found off Dwarka.
  - A rectangular conch-shell seal engraved with a three-headed animal of Persian Gulf type, and a jar inscribed in the Harappan script; lustrous red ware and black-and-red ware.
  - A coppersmith's stone mould and shell bangles indicate craft activity. With Kudwala in Cholistan and Daimabad in the Godavari valley, it shows that a few late Harappan places kept an urban character.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 1, 4-5; NIO Marine Archaeology Centre reports.
