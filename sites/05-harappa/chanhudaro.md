- **Location**: Chanhudaro, Sindh, about 130 km south of Mohenjodaro on the left bank of an old course of the Indus.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by N. G. Majumdar (1930-31) and E. J. H. Mackay (1935-36). Mature Harappan c. 2500-1900 BCE (5-10 ha), followed by the late Harappan Jhukar levels and then Jhangar.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Harappan industrial town - an unfortified craft settlement rather than a city, and the best single index of Harappan manufacturing.
  - No citadel and no fortification wall; streets with drains covered by burnt brick.
  - A major centre of craft production: a bead factory with furnaces, drills and beads at every stage, mostly of steatite; seal making; shell working (with Balakot, the principal centre); and the manufacture of cubical stone weights. Raw carnelian, agate, amethyst, jasper and crystal were stocked here.
  - Famous small finds include a bronze model of a bullock cart and a brick impressed with the footprints of a dog chasing a cat. The Jhukar levels above yielded circular stamp seals and buff ware, with writing surviving only on pottery.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Mackay, *Chanhu-daro Excavations 1935-36*.
