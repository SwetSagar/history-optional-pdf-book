- **Location**: Lewan (Lewan Dara), in the Bannu basin of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, on the western tributary drainage of the Indus below the Sheikh Budin hills.
- **Period/Level**: Early Harappan, going back to the early 3rd millennium BCE; excavated by the Bannu Archaeological Project (F. R. Allchin, Farid Khan and others, 1977-79).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The clearest factory site of the Harappan age - an industrial locality rather than a settlement, and proof of specialised production before urbanism.
  - Beside a small habitation area, an expanse of about 450 x 325 m was littered with stone tools at every stage of manufacture: chert microliths, querns of various types, stone balls, long triangular stone axes, ring stones and pointed hammerstones. Heavy grinding equipment dominates.
  - Part of the industrial area was given over to bead making, with beads and bead-making material; ceramic bangles and beads also occurred.
  - Little direct evidence of agriculture at Lewan itself, but nearby Tarakai Qila yielded wheat, barley, lentil and field pea with sickle-sheened blades, and bones of cattle, water buffalo, sheep and goat - the farming base that supported the industry.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Allchin, Khan & Allchin, *Lewan and the Bannu Basin* (1986).
