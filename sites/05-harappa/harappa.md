- **Location**: Harappa, Sahiwal district, Punjab (Pakistan), on an old bed of the Ravi; about 483 km from Mohenjodaro, the two linked by the Indus system.
- **Period/Level**: Noticed by Charles Masson (1826) and Cunningham; excavated by Daya Ram Sahni (1921 - the first Harappan excavation), M. S. Vats, Mortimer Wheeler (1946), and since 1986 by the Harappa Archaeological Research Project (Meadow and Kenoyer). Period I Ravi/Hakra phase; Period II early Harappan, over 25 ha; Period III mature Harappan, c. 2600-1900 BCE, over 150 ha; Cemetery H, late Harappan, c. 1900-1300 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site that names the civilisation, and the only city with a documented sequence from the Ravi phase to the late Harappan.
  - The citadel, shaped like a parallelogram, lies to the west on massive mud-brick platforms. To its north are the 'granary' of twelve rooms in two rows, circular brick working floors where wheat and barley husk survived in the crevices, and two-roomed barracks read as labourers' quarters.
  - Writing on pottery and seals, inscribed seals and standardised weights were already present in the early Harappan Period II, along with chert blades, terracotta female figurines and bangles, and beads of lapis lazuli, carnelian and steatite.
  - Cemetery R-37 of the mature phase and Cemetery H of the late phase, the latter with urn burials and distinctively painted jars. Unlike the single-craft towns, Harappa manufactured a wide range of goods; rice and millets appear in its late Harappan levels.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *The Wonder That Was India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Meadow & Kenoyer (2001).
