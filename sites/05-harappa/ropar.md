- **Location**: Ropar (Rupnagar), Punjab, on the left bank of the Sutlej where the river leaves the Siwaliks - the first Harappan site found east of the Indus system.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Y. D. Sharma (ASI, 1953-56), the first excavation undertaken in independent India. Period I mature to late Harappan, c. 2100-1700 BCE, with no early Harappan level; Period II PGW; Period III NBPW; then Kushana-Gupta and medieval levels - a six-period sequence.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Supplies the standard stratified sequence from the Harappan to the medieval period in the Sutlej valley.
  - Harappan levels with typical red ware, chert blades, steatite seals, beads, terracotta cakes and bangles; one burial had a dog laid at the feet of the human, a rite not recorded elsewhere in the culture. Horse remains are reported from the post-urban phase.
  - PGW follows directly on the Harappan level, which makes Ropar - with Bhagwanpura and Alamgirpur - one of the fixed points in dating the Harappan-Vedic transition.
  - Early historic levels yielded punch-marked coins, uninscribed cast copper coins and a seal with a Mauryan Brahmi legend, so the site also carries the NBPW chapter.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI, *IAR* 1953-56.
