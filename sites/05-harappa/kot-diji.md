- **Location**: Kot Diji, Khairpur district, Sindh, on the left bank of an old flood channel of the Indus, about 160 km north-east of Amri and near the Rohri hills chert sources.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by F. A. Khan (1955-57). Early Harappan Period I dated from c. 3300 BCE, separated by a burnt deposit from the mature Harappan level above. Size 1-5 ha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Type site of the 'Kot Dijian' horizon, the widest early Harappan cultural umbrella, from Cholistan to the Gomal and the Potwar.
  - Fortified with a massive wall of limestone rubble and mud brick, enclosing a citadel complex and a separate lower residential area - the citadel-and-lower-town plan already present before the mature phase.
  - The distinctive vessel is a short-necked ovoid pot painted with brownish bands and the 'horned deity', pipal leaves and fish-scale designs; the same deity recurs on jars at Rehman Dheri and incised on a terracotta cake at Kalibangan I, evidence of 'cultural convergence' in the religious sphere.
  - Objects of stone, shell and bone, terracotta figurines including a bull, bangles, beads and a bronze bangle fragment. The burnt deposit implies a major fire at the transition, as at Gumla; Amri and Nausharo also show burning.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Thapar, *Early India*.
