- **Location**: Surkotada, Rapar taluka, eastern Kutch district, Gujarat, on the northern fringe of the Little Rann, on the old overland route between Sindh and Saurashtra.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by J. P. Joshi (ASI, 1964 and 1970-72). Periods IA to IC, c. 2300-1700 BCE, running from the mature into the late Harappan phase with no break or desertion. Size 1-5 ha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A fortified regional centre or garrison town, and the source of the most-debated horse evidence in Indian archaeology.
  - The citadel exists from the earliest level and is not set apart: a single stone and mud-brick fortification with a rubble revetment encloses both the residential annexe and the citadel, each with its own ramped gateway - a plan shared with Lothal rather than with Mohenjodaro.
  - Horse bones from the upper levels were identified as true horse (Equus caballus) by Sandor Bokonyi and disputed by Richard Meadow; R. S. Sharma treats the identification as doubtful. Bones of mongoose (kept against snakes), elephant and wolf were also found.
  - A cemetery with pot burials and a rock-cut chamber; new pottery types appear in the later phase although the settlement plan continues unchanged. A heap of sling stones and a soldier depicted on a potsherd have been read as pointing to a standing force. Black-and-red ware and millets occur here.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4-5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Joshi, *Excavation at Surkotada* (1990).
