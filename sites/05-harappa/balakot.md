- **Location**: Balakot, Las Bela district, Baluchistan, on the Sonmiani Bay coast about 88 km north-west of Karachi, near the mouth of the Windar river.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by George F. Dales (1973-79). Period I is a pre/early Harappan horizon of the 4th millennium BCE with Nal-related pottery; Period II is mature Harappan, c. 2500-2000 BCE. Size 1-5 ha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-excavated Harappan coastal village and, with Chanhudaro, one of the two principal shell-working centres of the civilisation.
  - Period I: wheel-made painted pottery, polychrome and similar to Nal ware; microliths; humped-bull figurines; terracotta objects; beads of lapis lazuli and other stones; mud-brick houses.
  - Period II: conch-shell bangle manufacture on a large scale, alongside classic Harappan red ware - the site supplied shell ornaments into the Indus network.
  - Marine shell and fish remains show a coastal subsistence base; Balakot sits at the interface of the Kulli-Nal traditions of Baluchistan and the Harappan culture of the plains.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Dales, Balakot excavation reports.
