- **Location**: Kalibangan ('black bangles'), Hanumangarh district, northern Rajasthan, on the left bank of the dry Ghaggar (identified by many with the Vedic Sarasvati).
- **Period/Level**: Discovered by A. Ghosh (1951); excavated by B. B. Lal and B. K. Thapar (1960-69). Period I early Harappan, calibrated c. 2920-2550 BCE, about 4 ha; Period II mature Harappan, c. 2600-1900 BCE, 10-50 ha. The break between the two may have been caused by an earthquake.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The third great Harappan city dug in India and the clearest early-to-mature transition anywhere in the culture zone.
  - Period I: massive mud-brick fortification; houses built around courtyards with hearths, lime-plastered storage pits and saddle querns; bricks standardised at 3:2:1; over a hundred copper objects; distinctive red or pink pottery painted in black and sometimes white, with moustache-like scrolls, plants, fish and cattle. South of the site lay a ploughed field with north-south and east-west furrows - the earliest ploughed field known in the subcontinent.
  - Period II: both citadel and lower town fortified, the citadel divided in two. Its southern half held mud-brick platforms with a row of seven clay-plastered pits, read as fire altars for community ritual, with a well and bath pavements nearby; oblong fire altars with a central stele stood in lower-town houses. B. B. Lal suggested the northern half housed the officiants.
  - Street drains of the Mohenjodaro type are absent - house sewage went into troughs or jars sunk outside. Bangle making (terracotta, shell, alabaster, steatite, faience) was the leading craft. The burial ground, 200 m to the west-south-west, held extended burials and also circular pits with grave goods but no human remains.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Lal (1984).
