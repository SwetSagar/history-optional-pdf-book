- **Location**: Kunal, Fatehabad district, Haryana, on the dry Sarasvati-Ghaggar in the Indo-Gangetic divide, near Ratia and not far from Banawali.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by the Haryana Department of Archaeology (J. S. Khatri and M. Acharya, 1985-98). Period IA Hakra ware; Period IB early Harappan with Kalibangan I pottery; Period IC transitional between early and mature Harappan, c. 2600 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Documents both the architectural shift from pit dwellings to a planned brick village and the earliest concentration of elite wealth in the Harappan tradition.
  - The below-ground houses of the earlier phases give way in Period IC to ground-level houses of standardised mud brick in the 1:2:3 and 1:2:4 ratios; sturdy red beakers and jars of Harappan type appear for the first time.
  - Six steatite seals and one shell seal with geometric patterns - which, with the seals from Nausharo, are the earliest sealing evidence in the tradition and are linked to traders or emerging elites.
  - Hoards of jewellery inside houses: two silver tiaras, gold ornaments, and beads of lapis lazuli and agate. Upinder Singh reads the tiaras as marking a concentration of wealth with possible political implications - authority before urbanism.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; ASI, *IAR*.
