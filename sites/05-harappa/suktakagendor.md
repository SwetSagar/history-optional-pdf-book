- **Location**: Sutkagendor, Gwadar district, Baluchistan, on the Dasht river about 48 km inland from the Makran coast and close to the Iranian border - the westernmost Harappan site.
- **Period/Level**: Mature Harappan, c. 2500-1900 BCE. Noticed by Major Mockler (1875), surveyed by Aurel Stein (1927) and excavated by George F. Dales (1962).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Fixes the western limit of the civilisation and anchors its maritime route to the Persian Gulf.
  - A citadel raised on a stone and mud-brick platform with a massive fortification wall and bastions, and a lower town below - the standard Harappan plan reproduced on the frontier. R. S. Sharma groups it with Surkotada as a 'coastal city marked by a citadel'.
  - Stone vessels, stone and copper arrowheads, shell beads, pottery and terracotta figurines; material is sparse compared with the inland towns.
  - Originally sited on a navigable creek of the Dasht, it served as a port of call on the run between Lothal and Dholavira and the Persian Gulf, before the sea receded from it.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Dales (1962).
