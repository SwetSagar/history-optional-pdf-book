- **Location**: Pirak, in the Kachi plain of Baluchistan near Sibi, at the southern foot of the Bolan pass on the Nari river.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by J.-F. Jarrige and M. Santoni (1968-74). Period I begins c. 1800 BCE and Periods II-III run to about 600 BCE - the post-Harappan iron age of Baluchistan, not a Harappan settlement itself.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site that documents the beginning of double cropping and the earliest secure horse in South Asia.
  - Double cropping begins here: wheat and barley as winter crops, with rice (under irrigation), millet and sorghum as summer crops - the agricultural diversification that defines the late Harappan period as a whole.
  - Terracotta figurines of horses and of camels with riders. Richard Meadow, who reviewed the osteological evidence, holds the Pirak complex to show the earliest true horse in the subcontinent, about 1700 BCE, roughly contemporary with the disputed Surkotada specimen.
  - Mud-brick architecture with compartmented buildings and painted geometric pottery; iron appears in Period III. With the Gandhara Grave culture of Swat, Pirak anchors R. S. Sharma's account of the horse entering north India from the 18th century BCE.
  - *Sources*: R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Jarrige & Santoni, *Fouilles de Pirak* (1979).
