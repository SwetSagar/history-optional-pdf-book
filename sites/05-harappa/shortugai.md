- **Location**: Shortughai, in Darqad of Takhar province, north-eastern Afghanistan, on the upper Oxus (Amu Darya) near its confluence with the Kokcha, close to the lapis lazuli mines of Badakhshan.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Henri-Paul Francfort (1976-79). Mature Harappan, c. 2200-2000 BCE, roughly 1,100 km from the Indus heartland - an isolated Harappan colony.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only true Harappan trading outpost outside the subcontinent, and the strongest single evidence for organised long-distance procurement.
  - A square Harappan seal with a rhinoceros motif, classic Harappan pottery, terracotta cakes, carts and figurines - a complete Harappan material kit, not a scatter of traded objects, so actual Harappan settlers are implied.
  - Beads and raw lapis lazuli, carnelian, agate, gold and steatite. The colony was evidently planted to secure Badakhshan lapis and central Asian tin for the cities; millets were also grown here.
  - Irrigation canals were found at the site - the clearest Harappan canal evidence anywhere. It lay on the northern overland route to Turkmenistan and Mesopotamia, via Tepe Hissar and Shah Tepe.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Thapar, *Early India*; Francfort, *Fouilles de Shortughai* (1989).
