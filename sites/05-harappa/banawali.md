- **Location**: Banawali (ancient Vanavali), Fatehabad district, Haryana, close to the dry bed of the Rangoi river in the Sarasvati-Ghaggar system.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by R. S. Bisht (1973-84). Period I early Harappan (c. 2900-2600 BCE), Period II mature Harappan (c. 2600-1900 BCE), Period III late Harappan/Bara. The fortified area measures about 300 x 500 m.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A fortified town whose plan departs from the standard citadel-and-lower-town formula, and one of the richest sites for reading Harappan social difference.
  - The citadel is semi-elliptical, has its own mud-brick fortification and is surrounded by a moat; a ramp led from it into the lower town. Baked bricks were used only for wells, bathing pavements and drains; houses had raised chabutaras outside.
  - A multi-roomed house with a kitchen and a toilet, holding many seals and weights, is read as a merchant's; another, with beads of gold, lapis lazuli and carnelian, tiny weights and a gold-streaked touchstone, as a jeweller's. Seals occur only in the lower town, never in the citadel.
  - A terracotta model of a plough, a cubical chert weight-blade, and fire altars in several houses - in one case attached to an apsidal structure with an apparently ritual function. The early Harappan level had mud-brick houses with hearths and plastered storage pits in the courtyards.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Bisht in ASI, *IAR*.
