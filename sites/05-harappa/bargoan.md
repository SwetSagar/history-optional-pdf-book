- **Location**: Bargaon, Saharanpur district, western Uttar Pradesh, in the upper Ganga-Yamuna doab beside Hulas and Ambakheri.
- **Period/Level**: A late Harappan village of c. 1900-1300 BCE. One of only three excavated late Harappan sites in the doab (with Alamgirpur and Hulas), out of 563 identified between the Yamuna and the Sutlej.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type locality for the overlap of the late Harappan and Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) phases, and a marker of the copper-hoard zone.
  - Late Harappan pottery with a cream wash or bright red slip and black-painted designs occurs together with OCP. Bargaon's OCP belongs to the western zone that retains Harappan links (with Jodhpura, Siswal, Mitathal, Bara and Ambakheri), as against the eastern zone of Lal Qila, Atranjikhera and Saipai, which does not.
  - A fragmentary copper chisel and rings, chert blades, querns and pestles, bone points, terracotta cakes, carts and wheels; bangles and beads of terracotta, steatite, agate, carnelian and faience. Over 80 copper-hoard findspots are known in this tract.
  - Houses of wattle and daub, with wheat and barley continuing from the mature Harappan phase - the de-urbanised, rural face of the late Harappan.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4-5.
