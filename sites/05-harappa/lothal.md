- **Location**: Lothal, in the Bhal tract of Ahmedabad district, Gujarat, between the Sabarmati and its tributary the Bhogavo. The sea is now 16-19 km away, but boats from the Gulf of Khambhat could once sail up to the site.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by S. R. Rao (1955-62). Period A is mature Harappan, c. 2400-1900 BCE, in five phases; Period B is late Harappan with Lustrous Red Ware, c. 1900-1600 BCE. A modest 280 x 225 m, yet more tightly planned than Kalibangan, twice its size.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-planned small Harappan town and the civilisation's principal port on the Gujarat coast.
  - The dockyard on the eastern edge: a roughly trapezoidal basin walled in burnt brick (eastern and western walls 212 m and 215 m, northern and southern 37 m and 35 m), with a sluice gate and spill channel to hold a regular water level, and a mud-brick wharf along the western embankment. Leshnik's alternative reading of it as an irrigation reservoir is not generally accepted.
  - South of the acropolis stood a warehouse where sixty-five terracotta sealings were found, impressed with reed, matting, woven fibre and cord on one face and with Harappan seals on the other - direct evidence of packed and dispatched cargo.
  - The citadel (Rao's 'Acropolis') is trapezoidal and lies within the settlement rather than apart from it; the cemetery is to the north-west, outside the wall. Copper and bead workshops, a 'bazaar street' of shops, fire altars in houses, rice (with Rangpur, among the earliest at a Harappan site), and a Persian Gulf seal.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; S. R. Rao, *Lothal and the Indus Civilisation* (1973).
