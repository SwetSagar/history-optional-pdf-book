- **Location**: Ganweriwala, in the Cholistan desert of Bahawalpur district, Punjab (Pakistan), on the dry bed of the Hakra, roughly midway between Mohenjodaro and Harappa.
- **Period/Level**: Mature Harappan, c. 2600-1900 BCE. Discovered by M. R. Mughal in 1974 and surveyed intensively, but never excavated.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the five largest Harappan cities and the largest gap in the evidence - an unexcavated capital-scale site.
  - Over 81.5 ha, ranking with Mohenjodaro (over 200 ha), Harappa (over 150 ha), Rakhigarhi (over 80 ha) and Dholavira (about 100 ha); nearby Lurewala in Cholistan may have been as large as Mohenjodaro.
  - Surface finds include a terracotta tablet or seal showing a figure seated cross-legged in a 'yogic' posture, paralleled at Harappa, Mohenjodaro and Kalibangan; also pottery, kiln waste and weights.
  - Its position on the Hakra, in the tract where Kot Dijian and mature Harappan sites are densest, is the principal argument for a second settlement axis away from the Indus - and hence for the term 'Sindhu-Sarasvati' civilisation.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Thapar, *Early India*; Mughal, *Ancient Cholistan* (1997).
