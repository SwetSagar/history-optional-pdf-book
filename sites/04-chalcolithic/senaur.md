- **Location**: Senuar, on the banks of the Kudra river at the foot of the Kaimur range, Rohtas district, Bihar, not far from Sasaram.
- **Period/Level**: Four periods of occupation: Period I is Neolithic and Neolithic-Chalcolithic, Period II Chalcolithic, Period III belongs to the Northern Black Polished Ware culture and Period IV to the early centuries CE. The lower levels of Period IB are dated by radiocarbon to about 1770-1400 BCE, so Period IA probably begins in the latter half of the 3rd millennium BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The most important of the excavated Neolithic-Chalcolithic sites of the Bihar plains, and the clearest demonstration that double cropping was practised in the middle Ganga valley in the 3rd millennium BCE.
  - Carbonised grains show that two crops a year were grown. Rice was the main crop, with barley, dwarf wheat, sorghum millet, ragi millet, lentil, grass pea and field pea; bread wheat, chickpea and further pulses appear in Period IB.
  - Period IA was a 1.5 m deposit with wattle-and-daub houses and three main pottery types, red ware, burnished red ware and burnished grey ware, some rusticated and some cord-impressed; most was wheel-made though handmade pottery also occurs.
  - Artefacts include abundant microliths of chert, chalcedony, agate, quartz and quartzite, a few triangular polished celts, stone pestles, saddle querns, hammer stones, sling balls, bone points with use marks, and beads of semi-precious stones.
  - The animal bones have been carefully studied: domesticated cattle, buffalo, sheep, goat, pig, cat and dog, with wild nilgai, antelope and chital, many bearing charring and cut marks showing they were eaten. Senuar belongs with Chirand, Chechar-Kutubpur, Maner and Taradih in the group of excavated village sites that establish full agricultural settlement in the Gangetic plains of Bihar.
