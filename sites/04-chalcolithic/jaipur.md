- **Location**: The Jaipur district of north-eastern Rajasthan, part of the nuclear zone of the Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture; Jodhpura itself, on the banks of the Sahibi river, lies in this district.
- **Period/Level**: Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture. Jodhpura is the first site at which the culture was identified, and its calibrated dates range between 3309-2709 BCE and 2879-2348 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Jodhpura and the Jaipur district sites represent the eastern wing of the copper-working complex that supplied metal to the early and mature Harappans.
  - The typical pottery is wheel-made, orange to red in colour, with incised designs of various shapes; the forms include a dish-on-stand with a thick slip.
  - Over 80 sites of the culture have been identified, the largest concentration in Sikar district with further sites in the neighbouring Jaipur and Jhunjhunu districts; the distribution matches the copper ore resources of the Baleshwar and Khetri areas, where traces of ancient copper working survive.
  - Harappan pottery has been reported from the surface of Ganeshwar culture sites, and the profiles of these settlements show them to be part of an important process of metallurgical growth in Rajasthan whose roots go back to the 4th millennium BCE.
