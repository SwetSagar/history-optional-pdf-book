- **Location**: Prabhas Patan, better known as Somnath, in Gir Somnath district (formerly Junagadh district) on the Saurashtra coast of Gujarat, near the mouth of the Hiran river.
- **Period/Level**: The Chalcolithic phase is dated about 2000-1400 BCE and gives its name to Prabhas ware; the site continues through the historic period, yielding Northern Black Polished Ware, and remained a major pilgrimage centre into medieval times.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A post-Harappan Chalcolithic culture of coastal Saurashtra that shows how Harappan traditions were absorbed into regional cultures after the decline of the cities.
  - Houses were rectangular and built of soft stone, a departure from the mud and wattle-and-daub construction usual in Chalcolithic India.
  - Fine painted ware was recovered along with a range of jars and bowls; gold ornaments and a steatite seal were also found, the seal pointing to continuing Harappan connections.
  - The site's later history is inseparable from the Somnath temple, repeatedly built and rebuilt, whose sack by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1025-26 became one of the most contested episodes in Indian historiography.
