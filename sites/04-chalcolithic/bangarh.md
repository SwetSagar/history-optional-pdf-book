- **Location**: Bangarh, near Gangarampur in Dakshin Dinajpur district, West Bengal, in the Punarbhaba valley of the Bengal plains; identified with the ancient city of Kotivarsha.
- **Period/Level**: A long sequence whose lowest levels carry a pre-urban Chalcolithic occupation, followed by the Northern Black Polished Ware phase; the brick rampart can be assigned to the 2nd century BCE, and occupation continues into the early medieval period.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal excavated urban site of northern Bengal, and the main evidence that the Gangetic urban tradition extended east of Bihar.
  - Excavation revealed a settlement of some 1800 by 1000 feet enclosed by fortifications, with NBPW in the early historic levels.
  - It lay within the networks connecting the Bengal plains with Bihar, Tibet and the Brahmaputra valley of Assam, which explains its growth as a trading and administrative centre.
  - As Kotivarsha it served as an administrative headquarters (a vishaya centre) under the Guptas and later dynasties, and the site anchors the archaeological sequence of northern Bengal alongside Mahasthangarh in Bogra and Chandraketugarh in the 24 Parganas.
