- **Location**: Ahar, on the outskirts of Udaipur, Rajasthan, on the Ayad (Berach) river in the Banas system; the ancient name of the site was Tambavati, "the place that has copper". Excavated in 1953-54 and 1961-62.
- **Period/Level**: Type site of the Ahar or Banas culture, dated about 2100-1500 BCE. Period I is divided into phases Ia, Ib and Ic, with earliest calibrated dates of about 2500 BCE, 2100 BCE and 1900 BCE, and fifteen building phases identified.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site of one of the three great Chalcolithic cultures of Rajasthan, and the clearest Indian evidence of a Chalcolithic community practising copper smelting from the very outset.
  - The diagnostic pottery is a black-and-red ware with linear and dotted designs painted on in white; the site also produced the lustrous red ware of Rangpur type in Period Ic.
  - Copper objects are abundant: rings, bangles, antimony rods, a knife blade and four socketless axes, with copper sheet and slag showing that smelting was done locally. Unlike other Chalcolithic farming cultures, Ahar used virtually no microlithic tools and stone axes or blades are almost absent, because copper ore was locally available.
  - Ordinary houses were of mud on stone foundations, with walls strengthened by bamboo screens or quartz nodules and floors of black clay mixed with yellow silt; multiple-mouthed ovens were found, and one house was over 10 m long. Rice grains and bones of cow, buffalo, goat, sheep, deer, pig, fish, turtle and fowl were identified.
  - An iron ring and nail occurred in Period Ib and iron objects are common in Period Ic; whether these levels were intact or disturbed is debated, but this may be one of the earliest occurrences of iron in the subcontinent.
  - Etched carnelian beads, a lapis lazuli bead and Rangpur-type lustrous red ware point to contact with the Harappan sites of Gujarat; over 90 Ahar culture sites are known in the Banas and Berach systems, roughly between Udaipur and Jaipur.
