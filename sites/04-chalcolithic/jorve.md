- **Location**: Jorwe, on the left bank of the Pravara river, a tributary of the Godavari, in Ahmednagar district, Maharashtra.
- **Period/Level**: Type site of the Jorwe culture, dated about 1400-700 BCE, which covered the whole of modern Maharashtra except parts of Vidarbha and the Konkan coast.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Jorwe gives its name to the most widespread Chalcolithic culture of the Deccan, known from roughly 200 sites, which owed much to the Malwa culture while also sharing elements of the Neolithic culture of the south.
  - The diagnostic Jorwe ware is a wheel-made painted pottery with a matt surface, an orange-red slip and designs in black; flat rectangular copper axes have been found here and at Chandoli.
  - Although the culture was essentially rural, some of its settlements such as Daimabad and Inamgaon almost reached an urban stage, and a settlement hierarchy is visible, with sites of as much as twenty hectares alongside others of five hectares or less, implying that larger settlements dominated smaller ones.
  - Jorwe sites lie in semi-arid areas on brown-black soil carrying ber and babul vegetation but within riverine tracts; the culture outlasted the other Chalcolithic cultures of central and western India, which had disappeared by about 1200 BCE, continuing until about 700 BCE.
