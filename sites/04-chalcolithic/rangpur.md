- **Location**: Rangpur, on the Bhadar river in the Saurashtra peninsula of Gujarat; excavated by S. R. Rao.
- **Period/Level**: A Harappan and post-Harappan sequence. Period II is mature Harappan and Period III, the late or "Rangpur" phase, carries the lustrous red ware that marks the post-urban Chalcolithic of Gujarat.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Rangpur supplied the stratigraphic sequence on which the idea of a Late Harappan phase in Gujarat was built, showing the transition from the mature cities to smaller regional Chalcolithic communities.
  - Finds include black-and-red painted ware and high-necked jars, together with the diagnostic lustrous red ware, which also reached Ahar in Rajasthan in its Period Ic.
  - There is evidence of shell working, and of axes and beads of steatite and carnelian, alongside ordinary earthenware.
  - The absence of the full urban apparatus of the mature phase, combined with continuity of ceramic tradition, made Rangpur the type case in the debate over whether the end of the Harappan cities was a collapse or a transformation.
