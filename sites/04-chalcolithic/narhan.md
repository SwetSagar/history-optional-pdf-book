- **Location**: Narhan, Gorakhpur district, Uttar Pradesh, on the northern bank of the Sarayu (Ghaghara), about 30 km east of Imlidih; excavated by Purushottam Singh (1994).
- **Period/Level**: A cultural sequence stretching from the second half of the 2nd millennium BCE to the 7th century CE. Period I, which gives its name to the Narhan culture, is dated about 1300-700 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site of the Narhan culture, whose sequence is repeated at many places across the middle Ganga plains, and which documents the agricultural colonisation of that region before the coming of iron.
  - Remains of wattle-and-daub houses with post-holes and hearths were found. The pottery is marked by a white-painted black-and-red ware, along with white-painted black-slipped ware, red-slipped ware and plain red ware.
  - Copper objects include a ring and a fishhook; chemical analysis shows they were made of a low-tin bronze, and the metalworkers were familiar with alloying, cold working, annealing and casting, the copper ore apparently coming from the Rakha mines of Bihar.
  - An exceptionally wide range of plant remains was recovered - cultivated rice, hulled and six-row barley, three kinds of wheat, pea, green gram, chickpea and khesari, with mustard and flax as oilseeds and seeds of jackfruit, mango, mahua, tamarind and bamboo. An impression of a fishhook and thread on a mud clod showed the hook was of iron and the thread of ramie, a strong water-resistant fibre.
  - Two iron pieces, including a 13 cm bar, were found in the upper deposits of Period I, with iron objects increasing in the following period; at Khairadih nearby, Period I was likewise marked by black-and-red ware, with calibrated dates of 1395-848 BCE.
