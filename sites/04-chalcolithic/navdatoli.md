- **Location**: Navdatoli, on the southern bank of the Narmada opposite Maheshwar, in the Nimar region of western Madhya Pradesh; excavated by H. D. Sankalia.
- **Period/Level**: A Chalcolithic settlement of the Malwa culture, about 1700-1200 BCE, with the main occupation often given as 2000-1750 BCE; the site shows an overlap of Chalcolithic and Harappan-related material and does not show signs of planning.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Navdatoli has produced a greater variety of cereals than any other excavated site in India, which makes it the standard reference for Chalcolithic agriculture.
  - The plant remains include wheat, rice, bajra, lentil (masur), black gram, green gram, grass pea, ber and linseed, nearly the whole range of Chalcolithic crops at a single site.
  - The pottery has a coarse core and a thick buff fabric with an orange slip, the designs in black or dark brown depicting plants, animals and human figures, among them blackbuck, deer and peacock; Malwa ware is generally reckoned the richest of Chalcolithic ceramics.
  - Houses were circular or oblong wattle and daub, with chulhas and storage jars. Each household made its own tools, so the stone blade industry was widely distributed rather than concentrated in specialist hands.
  - Religious life is indicated by fire altars and by storage jars carrying a large female figure, which with the terracotta female figurines of other Chalcolithic sites is taken as evidence of a mother goddess cult.
