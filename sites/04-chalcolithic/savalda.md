- **Location**: Savalda, in the Tapi valley of northern Maharashtra; the culture's sites lie between the Tapi and Godavari rivers.
- **Period/Level**: Type site of the Savalda culture, the earliest farming culture of the western Deccan, going back to the 3rd millennium BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Savalda ware is the diagnostic pottery of the first agricultural communities of Maharashtra, and is remarkable for painted designs that depict tools and weapons.
  - The typical Savalda ware is a wheel-made chocolate-coloured pottery of medium to coarse fabric with a thick, crackled slip; shapes include the high-necked jar, dish, dish-on-stand, bowl, basin, ring stand, vase, beaker and knobbed lid, and the designs painted over the crackled slip include tools, weapons and geometric motifs.
  - At the related site of Kaothe, a 20 hectare site with a shallow 50 cm deposit suggesting short-duration, semi-nomadic occupation, houses were round or oval with sloping roofs.
  - Bone tools were recovered together with beads of shell, opal, carnelian and terracotta; bones of wild deer and of domesticated cattle, buffalo, sheep or goat and dog were identified, and plant remains included a millet and two pulses, gram and moong.
  - A Savalda phase also occurs at Daimabad, where the evidence indicates a settled rather than semi-nomadic community, showing that the culture was not uniformly mobile.
