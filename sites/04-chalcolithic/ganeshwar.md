- **Location**: Ganeshwar, near Nim-ka-Thana in Sikar district, north-eastern Rajasthan, on the Kantli-Sahibi drainage, close to the copper ores of the Khetri belt.
- **Period/Level**: Type site, with Jodhpura, of the Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture. Three cultural phases: Period I from about 3800 BCE, Period II from about 2800 BCE and Period III from about 2000 BCE, so the deposits largely predate the mature Harappan phase.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A small site of only 1.2 to 1.6 hectares that yielded hundreds of copper objects, marking it as a specialised copper-working centre supplying communities far beyond itself, probably including the Harappans.
  - Period I reflects a hunting-gathering community using microliths of chert and quartz, with charred bones almost all of wild animals. Period II saw the beginning of metallurgy, with a few copper objects - five arrowheads, three fishhooks, a spearhead and an awl - and people living in circular huts with floors paved with pebbles and rock fragments.
  - Period III is dominated by hundreds of copper objects of many types, arrowheads, spearheads, celts, chisels, rings, bangles and balls, with a corresponding decline in microliths and animal bones.
  - The typical pottery is a fine, wheel-made, orange to red ware with incised designs, including dish-on-stand with a thick slip; Ganeshwar-Jodhpura ware itself is poorly fired, made of micaceous clay with a bright red slip.
  - Harappan pottery occurs on the surface at two Ganeshwar culture sites, a reserved slip ware here is otherwise known in Harappan contexts only at Banawali and a few other places, and double spiral-headed pins from Ganeshwar recur at Harappan sites. Over 80 sites of this culture are known, concentrated in Sikar with others in Jaipur and Jhunjhunu districts. Curiously, no direct evidence of smelting - furnaces or crucibles - has been reported.
