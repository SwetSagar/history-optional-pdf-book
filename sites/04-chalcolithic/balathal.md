- **Location**: Balathal, Udaipur district, Rajasthan, in the Banas-Berach river system; excavated between 1994 and 1998.
- **Period/Level**: An Ahar (Banas) culture site of about 2 hectares. Calibrated dates place the protohistoric settlement in the late 4th millennium BCE, making it contemporary with the early Harappan phase at Kot Diji and as early as the Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture of north-east Rajasthan.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Balathal is the Chalcolithic site that overturned the assumption that fortification and planned building were Harappan monopolies, since it produced a massive defensive wall in a purely non-Harappan village culture.
  - In the early phase of Period I there were small circular wattle-and-daub huts with mud-plastered floors and two plastered storage pits. In the later phase a massive mud fortification wall stood in the centre of the mound, reinforced in places with stone and with clear evidence of bastions; the walls ranged from 4.8 m to over 5 m wide and enclosed an area of over 500 sq m. A street 2 to 4.8 m wide running north-west to south-east, and a small lane, were exposed.
  - Houses in the second phase were larger rectangular units of mud, mud-brick and stone on stone foundations, in three multi-roomed complexes with identifiable kitchens and storage areas; two potters' kilns were also found.
  - The pottery includes thin red, tan, black-and-red and buff wares and a distinctive reserve slip ware, in which a thin red wash was covered with a thick dark red slip and combed while still wet. Copper artefacts are plentiful - choppers, knives, razors, chisels and barbed and tanged arrowheads - while stone microliths and blades are very few.
  - Of the large quantity of animal bones, wild animals account for only 5 per cent; cattle alone make up almost 73 per cent of the faunal remains. Plant remains include wheat, barley, at least two millets, black gram, moong, pea, linseed and jujube, stored in bins and ground on querns, with bread probably cooked on flat pans over u-shaped chulhas of the kind still used in the village.
