- **Location**: Kayatha, Ujjain district, Madhya Pradesh, on the banks of the Chhoti Kali Sindh, a tributary of the Kali Sindh which in turn joins the Chambal.
- **Period/Level**: Type site of the Kayatha culture, placed by calibrated radiocarbon dates in the second half of the 3rd millennium BCE and commonly given as about 2000-1800 BCE; it is the earliest of the three successive Malwa sequences, followed by the Ahar and then the Malwa culture. There was an abrupt break in occupation around 1800 BCE, after which the site lay deserted for about a century before being reoccupied in an Ahar-Banas phase.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Kayatha, with Eran, represents the earliest established Chalcolithic settlement of central India, and its hoards give an unusually vivid picture of a household abandoned in haste.
  - The typical Kayatha pottery is a fine, sturdy, wheel-made ware with a thick brown slip and linear designs painted in violet or deep red on the upper part of the vessel; associated wares are a thin buff ware with linear and geometric designs in red, and a red combed ware decorated with wavy and zigzag lines.
  - Houses were of mud and reed with mud-plastered floors. The stone tool kit included plentiful microliths of locally available chalcedony, and a mace head or ring stone that may have been used to turn soil.
  - Two copper axes cast in moulds, a fragmentary chisel and 28 copper bangles in two pots show command of copper technology; the axes carry indentation marks similar to Ganeshwar specimens and may well have come from there.
  - Two necklaces of agate and carnelian beads, one of 175 and the other of 160 beads, were found in pots, and another pot held 40,000 steatite micro-beads strung in threads. All of these lay in a small area of what must have been a single house, suggesting the occupants left suddenly, abandoning their valuables on the floor.
