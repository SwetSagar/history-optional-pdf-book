- **Location**: Gungeria, Balaghat district, Madhya Pradesh, in the Satpura uplands of the upper Wainganga basin.
- **Period/Level**: Chalcolithic, belonging to the Copper Hoard horizon of central India.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Gungeria produced the largest copper hoard ever found in India: 424 copper tools and weapons together with 102 thin sheets of silver objects.
  - The silver sheets are the single largest find of silver from a protohistoric Indian context, and the hoard's scale suggests either a merchant's stock, a votive deposit or the accumulated wealth of a community.
  - Copper hoards are distributed from West Bengal and Odisha in the east to Gujarat and Haryana in the west, and from Andhra Pradesh to Uttar Pradesh, but the largest concentration is in the Ganga-Yamuna doab; Gungeria is the outstanding find outside the doab.
  - The objects - flat celts, harpoons, antennae swords and anthropomorphic figures - served varied purposes, and the debate over whether they were tools, weapons or ritual objects, and over the identity of the people who made them, remains unresolved.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
