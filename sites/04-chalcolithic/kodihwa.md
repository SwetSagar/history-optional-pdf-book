- **Location**: Koldihwa, Allahabad (Prayagraj) district, Uttar Pradesh, on the banks of the Belan river on the northern fringes of the Vindhyas, close to the related site of Mahagara.
- **Period/Level**: A sequence showing cultural continuity from the Neolithic through the Chalcolithic to the Iron Age; megalithic burials occur in the Chalcolithic period.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Koldihwa is at the centre of the debate on the origins of rice cultivation in India, since remains of rice and impressions of rice husk embedded in pieces of burnt clay were found here in Neolithic levels.
  - Examination of the rice imprints on pottery suggests that the people were familiar both with wild rice and with cultivated Oryza sativa; the very early dates once claimed for this rice have been questioned, but the site remains the key reference in the argument.
  - Rice was the staple, supplemented by meat, and the sequence records the introduction of wheel-made pottery in place of the earlier handmade wares.
  - Other finds include stone blades, polished stone celts and microliths. The Belan valley setting matters: wild rice still grows in the area, which falls within the natural habitat zone of the cereal, and the Neolithic here emerged directly out of a well-established Mesolithic phase seen at Chopani Mando.
