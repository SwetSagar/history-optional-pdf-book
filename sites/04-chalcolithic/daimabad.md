- **Location**: Daimabad, on the left bank of the Pravara, a tributary of the Godavari, in Ahmednagar district, Maharashtra.
- **Period/Level**: A five-fold sequence covering the Savalda culture, a late Harappan phase (Period II), the Malwa culture and the Jorwe culture. At about 20 hectares it is the largest of the roughly 200 known Jorwe sites, and could have held around 4,000 people.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest Chalcolithic settlement of the Deccan, which with Inamgaon shows Jorwe society approaching an urban threshold; it is most famous for its bronze hoard.
  - The celebrated bronzes comprise a man standing on a chariot drawn by two bulls, a water buffalo, an elephant and a rhinoceros, solid-cast and together weighing about 60 kg; some show Harappan influence.
  - The settlement seems to have been fortified with a mud wall carrying stone rubble bastions. There were mud houses, some large and multi-roomed, with hearths, storage pits and jars, sometimes with courtyards in front, and a lane has been traced in one place.
  - Excavations yielded microliths, bone and stone artefacts, beads of shell, carnelian, steatite and terracotta, a phallus-shaped object of agate found inside a house, an elephant tusk and black-on-buff cream ware. Plant remains include wheat, barley, pea, lentil, black gram and green gram.
  - Pit burials, urn burials, symbolic burials and post-cremation burials all occur. The late Harappan Period II produced an extended grave lined with mud bricks, a fine red ware with linear and geometric designs painted in black, two button-shaped seals carrying Harappan writing and inscribed potsherds, which together make Daimabad the southernmost Harappan-related site known.
