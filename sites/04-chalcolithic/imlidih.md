- **Location**: Imlidih Khurd, on the banks of the Kuwana river in Gorakhpur district, Uttar Pradesh, in the trans-Sarayu part of the middle Ganga plain.
- **Period/Level**: Period I represents the pre-Narhan culture and goes back to about 1300 BCE; Period II belongs to the Narhan culture and is dated about 1300-800 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site provides the clearest evidence that a two-crop agricultural regime was already established in the trans-Sarayu plain in the first half of the 2nd millennium BCE.
  - Period I yielded a crude, handmade cord- or mat-impressed red ware, with spherical and pedestalled bowls, vases with flaring rims and handi-like and spouted vessels; there were wattle-and-daub houses, a storage pit 1.95 m across, a circular bin-like structure and ovens.
  - The plant remains are extremely varied - rice, barley, bread wheat, dwarf wheat, jowar, bajra, lentil, moong, field pea, grass pea, mustard and sesame, along with seeds of wild jujube, anwala and grape.
  - Faunal remains include domesticated cattle, sheep or goat and pig, with cattle bones the most numerous and bearing cut marks, plus freshwater turtle, fish and molluscs.
  - Period II saw intense structural activity with at least two successive mud floors, post-holes and ovens, and the white-painted black-and-red ware typical of Narhan, together with bone points, pottery discs, terracotta beads, a copper arrowhead, two copper beads and curious terracotta pedestals possibly used for ritual.
