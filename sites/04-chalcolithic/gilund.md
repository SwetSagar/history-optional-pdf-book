- **Location**: Gilund, Rajsamand district, Rajasthan, in the Banas valley; about 10.5 hectares in extent. Excavated in 1959-60 and again by a Deccan College team in the early 2000s.
- **Period/Level**: An Ahar or Banas culture site, broadly 2100-1500 BCE, regarded as a regional centre of that culture.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Gilund is the Banas culture site that has produced the most substantial architecture, and evidence that has been read as pointing to contact with Central Asia.
  - The structural remains include a large mud-brick complex measuring about 30.48 by 24.38 m, and part of a wall of burnt bricks resting on a foundation of stone rubble - an unusually early use of burnt brick, around 1500 BCE, in a culture that generally did not use it.
  - A series of storage pits or bins was found, along with microliths, fragments of copper, and beads of semi-precious stones; unlike Ahar, Gilund had a stone blade industry.
  - Terracotta gamesmen and animal figurines, including humped bulls with long horns, were recovered; stylised bull terracottas in Malwa and Rajasthan are taken to show that the bull was the symbol of a religious cult.
  - A group of seal impressions recovered by the Deccan College excavations shows designs comparable to those of the Bactria-Margiana complex of Central Asia, which has been used to argue for long-distance contact.
