- **Location**: Eran, Sagar district, Madhya Pradesh, on the Bina river, a tributary of the Betwa, in the Malwa-Bundelkhand borderland; ancient Airikina.
- **Period/Level**: Period I, roughly 1800-700 BCE, is Chalcolithic and belongs to the Malwa culture sequence; the site was among the earliest Chalcolithic settlements of central India and was fortified. Later occupation runs through the Saka and Gupta periods.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Eran combines an early fortified Chalcolithic village with one of the most important epigraphic sequences of the Gupta age, which is why it appears in two syllabus categories at once.
  - The Chalcolithic levels yielded stone and copper implements and Ochre-Coloured Pottery, and the settlement at Eran, like Kayatha, was established early in comparison with those of western Maharashtra and eastern India.
  - An inscription of about 339-368 CE is attributed to the Saka king Sridharavarman, and the site also carries an inscription of Samudragupta.
  - The Bhanugupta inscription of 510 CE records the death in battle of the chief Goparaja and the self-immolation of his wife, and is generally read as the earliest epigraphic record of sati in India.
  - Eran also preserves a colossal Varaha (boar) image inscribed in the reign of Toramana and a Vishnu pillar of the time of Budhagupta dated 484-85 CE.
