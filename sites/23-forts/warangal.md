- **Location**: Warangal, Telangana, on the Deccan plateau in the upper Krishna-Godavari interfluve - a plain fortified with concentric earth and stone ramparts rather than a hill fort.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Kakatiyas from the 12th century, built up under Ganapatideva, r. 1199-1262, and his daughter Rudramadevi, r. 1262-1289. Malik Kafur raided it in 1309-10 and Ulugh Khan annexed it in 1323, ending Kakatiya rule under Prataparudra II.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Kakatiya capital, with one of the very few women to rule an Indian kingdom in her own right, and a temple architecture of exceptional refinement.
  - Rudramadevi succeeded her father and ruled for nearly three decades. Inscriptions refer to her by the male name Rudradeva and depict her in male dress, and Marco Polo, who passed through her realm, praised her government. She is the standard example in questions on female sovereignty, alongside Razia and Didda.
  - The fort's four free-standing *kirti toranas*, carved gateways standing where the Svayambhusiva temple once was, are now the emblem of Telangana. The Thousand Pillar Temple at Hanamkonda, c. 1163, is a triple-shrined Kakatiya building in polished black basalt.
  - The Ramappa temple at Palampet, consecrated 1213 and inscribed by UNESCO in 2021, is the only major Indian temple named after its sculptor. It uses lightweight bricks said to float, and carries bracket figures of dancers of extraordinary quality.
  - Kakatiya rule rested on tank irrigation - Pakhal, Laknavaram and Ramappa lakes - built by the state and by local chiefs, which is the usual evidence for their agrarian base.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*.
