- **Location**: Cooch Behar, northern West Bengal, on the Torsa river in the duars below the eastern Himalaya - the plain between Bhutan, Assam and the Bengal delta. The building is a palace rather than a fort, despite this chapter's heading.
- **Period/Level**: The Koch kingdom was founded by Biswa Singha in the early 16th century and reached its height under Nara Narayan, r. 1554-1587, and his general Chilarai. The present Victor Jubilee Palace was built in 1887 by Maharaja Nripendra Narayan; the princely state acceded to India in 1949.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the Koch dynasty, and a textbook example of princely-state architecture built to advertise loyalty to the Raj.
  - Nara Narayan rebuilt the Kamakhya temple at Guwahati in 1565 after its destruction, so the Koch court is directly connected to the principal Shakta shrine of the north-east. He also patronised Shankaradeva, the Assamese Vaishnava reformer, who took refuge in Koch territory - a single kingdom sustaining both tantric Shaktism and neo-Vaishnavism.
  - Chilarai's campaigns briefly made the Koch the dominant power of the Brahmaputra valley, checking the Ahoms, before the kingdom split into Koch Bihar and Koch Hajo and fell between Mughal and Ahom pressure.
  - The palace, modelled on Buckingham Palace with Corinthian columns and a 38 m dome over the durbar hall, is Italianate Renaissance with no Indian element at all. Princely rulers adopting European classicism wholesale is the architectural signature of the post-1858 settlement, and stands in deliberate contrast to the Indo-Saracenic style the British themselves used for public buildings in India.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; ASI; *Bengal District Gazetteer*, Cooch Behar.
