- **Location**: Kangla, in the centre of Imphal, Manipur, on the west bank of the Imphal river - the historic seat of the Meitei kings, surrounded by moats within the Manipur valley.
- **Period/Level**: The traditional capital of the Ningthouja dynasty, with the *Cheitharol Kumbaba*, the Manipuri royal chronicle, beginning its record in 33 CE. Rebuilt several times, notably under Khagemba in the 17th century and Bhagyachandra in the 18th. Occupied by the British after the Anglo-Manipur War of 1891 and returned to Manipur only in 2004.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The centre of Meitei kingship, and the site of one of the last independent kingdoms absorbed into British India.
  - The *Cheitharol Kumbaba* is a continuous court chronicle maintained over centuries, comparable in kind to Kashmir's *Rajatarangini* and Odisha's *Madala Panji* - one of very few indigenous historical records of its type, and the main source for Manipur's dynastic past.
  - The Kangla Sha, paired heraldic lion-dragons guarding the enclosure, are the emblem of Manipuri sovereignty and were destroyed by the British in 1891 and later rebuilt.
  - The Anglo-Manipur War of 1891 followed a palace coup and the killing of British officers. It ended with the public hanging of Yuvraj Tikendrajit and Thangal General at Imphal and the fort's conversion into a garrison - an episode central to Manipuri political memory and generally absent from all-India narratives.
  - Manipur's Vaishnava turn under Bhagyachandra in the 18th century, which produced the *Raas Leela* dance tradition, was directed from this court.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; *Cheitharol Kumbaba*; ASI.
