- **Location**: Mehrangarh, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, on a sheer rock about 120 m above the city at the eastern edge of the Thar - the Rathore capital of Marwar, on the desert's trade frontier.
- **Period/Level**: Begun in 1459 by Rao Jodha, who moved the Rathore seat here from Mandore, and extended by successive rulers, notably Jaswant Singh and Ajit Singh in the 17th and 18th centuries. Jaswant Thada, the royal cenotaph in white marble, dates from 1899.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Rathore stronghold of Marwar, and the centre of the longest Rajput resistance to Aurangzeb.
  - When Jaswant Singh died in 1678, Aurangzeb attempted to take Marwar under direct control and to bring up the infant heir Ajit Singh at court. Durgadas Rathore smuggled the child out of Delhi and fought a thirty-year guerrilla war until Ajit Singh was restored in 1707. The Rajput war is a principal item in the case that Aurangzeb's religious and political policies cost the empire its most useful allies.
  - The fort's Loha Pol carries the handprints of Maharaja Man Singh's widows who committed *sati* in 1843 - a physical record of the practice as it persisted into the colonial period, and abolished in Marwar only afterwards.
  - Cannonball scars from a Jaipur bombardment remain in the walls, evidence of the inter-Rajput warfare that preoccupied these states as much as any imperial threat did.
  - Foundation legends - Karni Mata laying the first stone, and a man buried alive in the ramparts - attach to the site and are worth reporting as tradition.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
