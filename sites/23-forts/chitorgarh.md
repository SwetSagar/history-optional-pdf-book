- **Location**: Chittorgarh, southern Rajasthan, on an isolated plateau about 180 m above the plain between the Berach and Gambhiri rivers - the largest hill fort in India, commanding the route from Malwa into Mewar.
- **Period/Level**: The Sisodia capital of Mewar until 1568. It was stormed three times: by Alauddin Khalji in 1303, by Bahadur Shah of Gujarat in 1535, and by Akbar in 1567-68, after which Udai Singh moved the capital to Udaipur.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The central symbol of Rajput resistance, and the site of the three *jauhars* around which that tradition is built.
  - The 1303 siege is the subject of Malik Muhammad Jayasi's *Padmavat*, written in Awadhi in 1540 - two and a half centuries after the event, by a Sufi poet, as an allegory in which Padmini represents the soul. Treating it as a contemporary record of Alauddin's motives is the commonest error; Amir Khusrau, who was present, says nothing of the kind.
  - Akbar's siege of 1567-68 ended in a general massacre, and Abu'l Fazl records it. It is the hardest case against the picture of Akbar as uniformly conciliatory, and should be set beside his Rajput marriage alliances rather than omitted.
  - The Vijaya Stambha, a nine-storeyed victory tower raised by Rana Kumbha about 1448 to mark his defeat of Mahmud Khalji of Malwa, is covered in sculpture of Hindu deities and carries an inscription giving the Sisodia genealogy. The older Kirti Stambha is Jain.
  - Mirabai, the bhakti poet, belonged to this court by marriage, and her rejection of it links the fort to the chapter on saints.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I and II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*.
