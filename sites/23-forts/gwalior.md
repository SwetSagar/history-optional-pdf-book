- **Location**: Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, on an isolated sandstone plateau about 100 m above the plain in the Chambal basin - the fort that commands the route between the Ganga plain and Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: Occupied from at least the 6th century CE, when a Huna inscription of Mihirakula records a sun temple. The standing fort is largely the work of Man Singh Tomar, r. 1486-1516; the Jain colossi in the rock face are 15th century. Held afterwards by the Lodis, Mughals, Marathas and Scindias.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The home of the earliest known written zero, and of the finest pre-Mughal Hindu palace architecture in northern India.
  - An inscription of 876 CE in the Chaturbhuj temple records a land grant for a garden measuring 187 by 270 *hastas* and a daily gift of fifty garlands - and writes the 0 in both numbers. It is the oldest securely dated use of zero as a written numeral anywhere in the world, and the single most quotable fact about Indian mathematics.
  - The Man Mandir palace, faced in blue, green and yellow glazed tile with friezes of ducks, elephants and banana trees, is the high point of Tomar building and was admired by Babur, who otherwise found little in Hindustan to praise.
  - The Siddhachal Jain colossi, rock-cut tirthankaras up to 17 m high, were defaced on Babur's orders and later repaired - a visible instance of iconoclasm and restoration on one cliff.
  - Tansen, Akbar's musician, is buried at Gwalior beside his teacher Muhammad Ghaus, and Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi was killed here in June 1858 during the last phase of the revolt.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
