- **Location**: Kumbhalgarh, Rajsamand district, Rajasthan, on a spur of the Aravallis at about 1,100 m, west of Udaipur - deliberately sited on the watershed between Mewar and Marwar.
- **Period/Level**: Built by Rana Kumbha of Mewar, r. 1433-1468, largely between 1443 and 1458, with the architect Mandan. Its perimeter wall runs about 36 km, the second longest continuous wall in the world. Taken only once, by a combined Mughal, Amber and Marwar force under Akbar in 1576, and then only by poisoning the water.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The refuge fortress of Mewar - the place the dynasty retreated to when Chittor fell, and the reason it survived.
  - Rajput strategy in Mewar depended on having a second capital in the hills that no siege train could reach. Udai Singh was born here and sheltered here as an infant; Maharana Pratap was born here in 1540 and used the surrounding hills as his base after Haldighati in 1576. Kumbhalgarh is what made a policy of prolonged resistance possible.
  - Rana Kumbha built or rebuilt some thirty-two of Mewar's eighty-four forts. He was also a scholar - a commentator on the *Gita Govinda* and a writer on music - and the *Kumbhalgarh prashasti* records his works. The soldier-scholar king is a Rajput counterpart to Bhoja of Dhar.
  - The fort encloses over three hundred temples, Jain and Hindu, some predating Kumbha, showing that a fortress of this size functioned as a self-sufficient township rather than a garrison.
  - It is one of the six Hill Forts of Rajasthan inscribed by UNESCO, with Chittorgarh, Ranthambore, Amber, Jaisalmer and Gagron.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
