- **Location**: Srirangapatna, Mandya district, Karnataka, on an island formed where the Kaveri divides - a natural moat that made it the strongest position in southern Karnataka, about 15 km from Mysuru.
- **Period/Level**: Fortified in 1454 by Timmanna Nayaka under Vijayanagara; Wodeyar capital from 1610; then the capital of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. It fell to the British on 4 May 1799 in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, when Tipu was killed defending the breach.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of the state that fought the East India Company four times - the most formidable Indian opponent the Company faced in the south.
  - Tipu's Mysore was a modernising state: he reorganised revenue on a *ryotwari* basis cutting out intermediaries, established state trading houses and factories, sent embassies to France, Turkey and Persia, and introduced a new calendar and coinage.
  - The Mysorean iron-cased rockets used at Pollilur in 1780 and afterwards were effective enough that captured examples were studied at Woolwich and developed into the Congreve rocket. Indian military technology influencing European practice is the point worth making.
  - The Daria Daulat Bagh, Tipu's summer palace, carries a mural of the Mysorean victory at Pollilur - one of very few Indian paintings of a battle against the British painted from the winning side.
  - Note that the French assisted Hyder Ali and Tipu in the 18th century; they had nothing to do with the fort's construction in 1454, which was Vijayanagara work. The Ranganathaswamy temple on the island is one of the three great Kaveri island shrines with Srirangam and Shivanasamudra.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
