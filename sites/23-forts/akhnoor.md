- **Location**: Akhnoor, Jammu district, Jammu and Kashmir, on the right bank of the Chenab where the river leaves the hills for the plain - the crossing point on the old route between Punjab and Kashmir.
- **Period/Level**: The standing fort was begun by Mian Tej Singh in 1762 and completed under Raja Alam Singh about 1802. The site matters more for what lies around it: the Harappan settlement at Manda a few kilometres upstream, and the Buddhist monastic site of Ambaran, active from about the 1st century BCE to the 5th century CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The northernmost extension of the Harappan world, and a Kushana-period Buddhist establishment in the Jammu hills.
  - Manda, on the Chenab at the foot of the Pir Panjal, is the northernmost Harappan site known. Its pre-Harappan, Mature Harappan and Late Harappan levels extend the civilisation's map into the sub-Himalaya and are usually explained by access to Himalayan timber floated down the Chenab.
  - Ambaran has produced a stupa complex with a gold and silver casket containing bone relics and charred material, and a large body of terracotta heads in the Gandhara idiom - the *Akhnoor terracottas*, among the finest unbaked-clay sculpture from ancient India, now dispersed across several museums.
  - Together the two sites make Akhnoor evidence that the Jammu region was not peripheral: it was inside the Harappan network in the third millennium BCE and inside the Gandharan Buddhist one in the early centuries CE.
  - The 18th-century fort itself belongs to the Dogra rise under the Jammu rajas that culminates in Gulab Singh and the state of Jammu and Kashmir.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4 and 8; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
