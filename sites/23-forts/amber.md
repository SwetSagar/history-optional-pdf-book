- **Location**: Amer, about 11 km north of Jaipur, Rajasthan, on a ridge of the Aravallis above the Maota lake, with the older fortress of Jaigarh on the hill behind it.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Kachhwaha Rajputs, taken from the Meenas and held from the 12th century. The palace-fort as it stands was begun by Raja Man Singh I from 1592 and extended by Mirza Raja Jai Singh and Sawai Jai Singh II, who moved the capital to the new city of Jaipur in 1727.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the first Rajput house to ally with the Mughals, and the architectural record of what that alliance produced.
  - The Kachhwahas entered Mughal service in 1562 when Bharmal's daughter married Akbar. Man Singh I became one of Akbar's senior commanders, governing Kabul and Bengal and leading the imperial army at Haldighati against Mewar. Amber is therefore the counterweight to Chittorgarh: two Rajput states, alliance and resistance, and both should be given in any answer on Mughal-Rajput relations.
  - The palace shows the resulting hybrid - Rajput courtyards, *jharokhas* and painted ornament combined with Mughal *charbagh* gardens, *pietra dura* and the Sheesh Mahal's mirror work. Man Singh's Ganesh Pol and the Diwan-i-Am use Mughal arcading on Rajput plans.
  - Man Singh also built the Govind Dev temple at Vrindavan, so the same patron funded a Vaishnava temple in Braj and served a Muslim emperor - the alliance's cultural dimension.
  - Jaigarh above holds the Jaivana, cast in 1720 and among the largest wheeled cannon ever made.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
