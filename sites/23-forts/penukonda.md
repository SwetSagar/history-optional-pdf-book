- **Location**: Penukonda, Sri Sathya Sai district, Andhra Pradesh, at the foot of a steep granite hill in the dry Rayalaseema uplands - about 70 km from Anantapur, on the road between Karnataka and the Andhra plateau.
- **Period/Level**: A Vijayanagara stronghold from the 14th century, and the empire's capital after the defeat at Talikota in 1565, when Tirumala Deva Raya of the Aravidu line withdrew here from the ruined Hampi. It served as capital until about 1592, when the seat moved to Chandragiri and then to Vellore.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The proof that Vijayanagara did not end in 1565 - the standard correction to the textbook date.
  - The Aravidu dynasty ruled from Penukonda, Chandragiri and Vellore for nearly a century after Talikota, issuing grants and asserting authority over the Nayaks of Madurai, Thanjavur and Gingee. Ending the empire at the sack of Hampi misses that entire successor phase, and Penukonda is the place to anchor the correction.
  - The Gagan Mahal, built as a summer palace, shows the Deccan sultanate idiom - arches, stucco and domes - adopted by a Hindu court, the same borrowing visible in Hampi's secular buildings.
  - Jain and Hindu temples on the hill, including the Parshvanatha basadi, and the tomb of Sher Khan below, record the mixed religious landscape of a Rayalaseema garrison town.
  - Its eventual decline follows the Nayaks' assertion of independence and the Bijapur and Golconda advance into the Carnatic in the 1640s, which finished the Aravidu claim.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
