- **Location**: Raigad, Raigad district, Maharashtra, on an isolated basalt hill about 820 m high in the Sahyadris, approached by a single fortified path - inland from the Konkan coast, roughly 25 km from Mahad.
- **Period/Level**: Taken by Shivaji from the Chandrarao More of Javli in 1656, rebuilt by his engineer Hiroji Indulkar, and made the Maratha capital. Shivaji's coronation was held here on 6 June 1674; he died at Raigad in 1680. The fort fell to Aurangzeb's forces in 1689 and was destroyed by British artillery in 1818.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the Maratha state, and the setting of the coronation that turned a rebellion into a kingdom.
  - The *rajyabhisheka* of 1674 is the event that matters. Shivaji was crowned *Chhatrapati* with full Vedic ritual, conducted by Gaga Bhatta of Banaras, who first had to establish a Kshatriya genealogy for him - the resistance of the local brahmanas to crowning a man they regarded as a shudra, and the lengths gone to overcome it, is the best evidence available on caste and kingship in 17th-century Maharashtra.
  - He also instituted the *Ashta Pradhan*, an eight-member council with defined portfolios, and commissioned the *Rajyavyavahara Kosha*, a lexicon replacing Persian administrative terms with Sanskrit ones - a deliberate assertion of independence at the level of language.
  - The fort's design shows Maratha military practice: hill forts chosen for inaccessibility, held by small garrisons, used as bases for mobile cavalry rather than as centres to be defended in set-piece war. Shivaji is said to have held over two hundred such forts.
  - The Jagadishwar temple, Shivaji's samadhi and the market street survive on the summit.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
