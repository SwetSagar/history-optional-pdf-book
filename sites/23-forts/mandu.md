- **Location**: Mandu, Dhar district, Madhya Pradesh, on a Vindhyan plateau about 630 m high, cut off from the tableland by a deep ravine and looking south over the Narmada valley - a natural fortress with a 45 km circuit of walls.
- **Period/Level**: The Paramara *Mandapa-durga*, fortified from the 10th-11th centuries; capital of the Malwa Sultanate from 1401 under Dilawar Khan Ghuri and his son Hoshang Shah, r. 1405-1435. Taken by Akbar in 1561 from Baz Bahadur, the last independent sultan.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest provincial Sultanate architecture in India, and the building the Taj Mahal's architects came to study.
  - Hoshang Shah's tomb, completed about 1440, is the earliest marble building in India: a plain domed cube with latticed screens and corner cupolas. An inscription on it records the visit of Ustad Hamid and three other architects sent by Shah Jahan - documentary evidence of a Mughal masterpiece's acknowledged model.
  - The Jami Masjid, said to follow the great mosque of Damascus, and the Hindola Mahal with its steeply battered walls, show the Malwa style: massive, austere, unornamented, and using coloured stone rather than surface carving.
  - The Jahaz Mahal, a long narrow pavilion between two tanks so that it appears to float, is the best surviving example of Sultanate pleasure architecture and of the elaborate rainwater harvesting the plateau required.
  - Baz Bahadur and Rupmati, the sultan and the singer, are the subject of a romance that generated its own painting and poetry tradition after Akbar's conquest - a case of a defeated court surviving as legend.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I and II; ASI.
