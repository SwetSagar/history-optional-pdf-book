- **Location**: Ranthambore, Sawai Madhopur district, Rajasthan, on an isolated hill where the Aravallis meet the Vindhyas, between the Banas and the Chambal - now within a tiger reserve.
- **Period/Level**: Founded by the Chauhans, probably in the 10th century, and their principal stronghold after the fall of Delhi in 1192. Besieged and taken by Alauddin Khalji in 1301 after the death of Hammira Deva Chauhan; later held by Mewar under Rana Sanga, and surrendered to Akbar in 1569.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site of Alauddin Khalji's first great Rajput siege, and of the *jauhar* tradition's earliest well-documented instance.
  - Hammira Deva's refusal to surrender Mongol rebels who had taken refuge with him gave Alauddin his pretext. The year-long siege ended with Hammira killed in a final sortie and the women of the fort performing *jauhar* - the episode celebrated in Nayachandra Suri's Sanskrit *Hammira Mahakavya* a century later.
  - Amir Khusrau, who accompanied the campaign, describes the siege in the *Khazain-ul-Futuh*, so the event can be read from both the victor's Persian account and the vanquished's Sanskrit one. Setting the two against each other is a good exercise in source criticism, and a corrective to relying on either alone.
  - Alauddin's reduction of Ranthambore, Chittor, Siwana and Jalor between 1301 and 1311 broke Rajput power in eastern Rajasthan and secured the Delhi Sultanate's western flank before the Deccan campaigns.
  - It is one of the six Hill Forts of Rajasthan inscribed by UNESCO.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; Amir Khusrau, *Khazain-ul-Futuh*.
