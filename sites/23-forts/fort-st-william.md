- **Location**: Fort William, Kolkata, West Bengal, on the east bank of the Hooghly beside the Maidan - sited where the river was deep enough for ocean-going ships, about 130 km upstream from the sea.
- **Period/Level**: The old fort was built from 1696 to 1706 around the Company's factory at Sutanuti-Kalikata-Govindpur. It was stormed by Siraj-ud-Daula in June 1756; after Plassey the present star-shaped fort was built between 1758 and 1781 on a new site cleared for the purpose.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The base from which the Company conquered Bengal, and therefore India.
  - Siraj-ud-Daula's capture of the old fort, provoked by the Company's unauthorised fortification and abuse of the *dastak* trade privilege, is the immediate cause of the campaign that ended at Plassey on 23 June 1757. The Black Hole episode, as reported by Holwell, has been questioned on its numbers ever since and should be presented with that qualification.
  - Plassey brought the Company the *diwani* of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in 1765 - the right to collect revenue - which converted a trading body into a territorial government with an army paid from Indian land tax. Everything that follows in the Company's expansion rests on that revenue.
  - The new fort was built on a scale intended never to be taken: eight bastions, a vast glacis cleared of buildings, which is how Kolkata acquired the Maidan.
  - Fort William College, founded in 1800 by Wellesley to train civil servants in Indian languages, produced grammars, dictionaries and the first printed prose in Bengali and Hindustani - a lasting effect on Indian vernacular literature.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; ASI.
