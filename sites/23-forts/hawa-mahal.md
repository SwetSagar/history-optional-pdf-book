- **Location**: The Hawa Mahal, in the walled city of Jaipur, Rajasthan, forming part of the City Palace complex and fronting onto the Sireh Deorhi bazaar - a screen wall rather than a free-standing building.
- **Period/Level**: Built in 1799 by Sawai Pratap Singh, designed by Lal Chand Ustad, in red and pink sandstone. It belongs to the later phase of Jaipur, the city founded in 1727 by Sawai Jai Singh II. Despite this chapter's heading, it is a palace facade and not a fort.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Purdah made architectural - a building whose entire design exists to let women see without being seen.
  - The facade rises five storeys and is pierced by 953 small *jharokhas* with latticed screens. Women of the zenana could watch processions and street life in the bazaar below while remaining invisible and unapproachable. Few buildings anywhere state a social rule so directly in their structure, which is why it belongs in any discussion of gender and space in Indian history.
  - The screens also drive airflow, cooling the interior by the venturi effect - the *hawa* of the name - so the same device serves seclusion and climate together.
  - Jaipur itself is the more important subject: Sawai Jai Singh II laid it out in 1727 on a nine-block grid derived from the *Shilpa Shastra*, one of the earliest planned cities of the period, with the Jantar Mantar observatory at its centre.
  - Jai Singh's five observatories, his astronomical tables and his correspondence with European astronomers make Jaipur the best example of Indian scientific patronage in the 18th century.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; ASI.
