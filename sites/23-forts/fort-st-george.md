- **Location**: Fort St George, on the Coromandel shore at Chennai, Tamil Nadu, beside the mouth of the Cooum - an open surf coast with no natural harbour, chosen for politics rather than geography.
- **Period/Level**: Built by the English East India Company from 1640 under Francis Day and Andrew Cogan, on a strip granted by Damarla Venkatadri, the Nayak governing for the Vijayanagara successor at Chandragiri. Completed 1644; captured by the French under La Bourdonnais in 1746 and returned in 1749 by the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The first English fortress in India, and the model for how a trading company turned into a territorial power.
  - The sequence is the point: a factory becomes a fortified factory, the fortified factory acquires jurisdiction over the settlement around it, and the settlement becomes a base for revenue and troops. Madras grew as White Town inside the walls and Black Town outside, the earliest instance of the segregated colonial city.
  - Madras received a municipal corporation and a mayor's court in 1688, the first in India, so the institutions of Company government were tried here before Bengal.
  - St Mary's Church inside the fort, consecrated in 1680, is the oldest Anglican church east of Suez; Robert Clive and Elihu Yale were both married in it, and Clive began his career here as a writer.
  - Its loss to the French in 1746 and recovery by treaty, not by arms, opened the Carnatic Wars and demonstrated that the European struggle for India would be settled as much in Europe as in Asia.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; ASI; *Records of Fort St George*.
