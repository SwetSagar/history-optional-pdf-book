- **Location**: Bekal, Kasaragod district, northern Kerala, on a headland jutting into the Arabian Sea - the largest fort in Kerala, covering about 16 hectares of laterite headland with the sea on three sides.
- **Period/Level**: Built about 1650 by Shivappa Nayaka of Keladi, whose dynasty succeeded to Vijayanagara authority in coastal Karnataka after 1565. It passed to Mysore under Hyder Ali in the 1760s, was held by Tipu Sultan, and was taken by the British in 1799 after Tipu's death.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A purely military coastal fort, and evidence of Karnataka powers pressing south into Malabar.
  - Bekal has no palace, temple or residential quarter inside it - only ramparts, a magazine, an observation tower and water tanks. That makes it unusual among Indian forts, which were normally seats of government as well, and it is read as a garrison built specifically to control the Malabar coast and its shipping.
  - The keyhole-shaped bastion facing the sea, with tiered openings for cannon at the top and for muskets below, and the water-filled ditch cut into laterite, show the adaptation of fort design to gunpowder and to a laterite coast.
  - Shivappa Nayaka is better known for the *Shivappa Nayaka Shist*, a revenue settlement assessing land by its productivity, so the same ruler appears in questions on both fortification and agrarian administration.
  - Its later passage to Hyder Ali and Tipu ties the site into the Anglo-Mysore wars and the Company's absorption of Malabar.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
