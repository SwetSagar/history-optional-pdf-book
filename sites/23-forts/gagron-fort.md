- **Location**: Gagron, Jhalawar district, south-eastern Rajasthan, at the confluence of the Ahu and the Kali Sindh - water on three sides and a rock-cut moat on the fourth, in the Hadoti country bordering Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: Built by the Doda and later Khichi Rajputs from about the 12th century. It was besieged repeatedly from Malwa and Delhi, taken by Hoshang Shah of Malwa in 1423 and by Mahmud Khalji in 1444, held by Rana Kumbha of Mewar, and finally passed to Akbar.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The classic Indian *jaldurg* or water fort, and a frontier stronghold between Rajasthan and Malwa.
  - Indian fort typology distinguishes *giridurg* (hill), *jaldurg* (water), *vandurg* (forest), *dhanvadurg* (desert) and others, following the *Arthashastra* and later texts. Gagron is the textbook *jaldurg*: it needed no artificial defences on three sides and was reduced only by prolonged blockade.
  - Its position on the Rajasthan-Malwa border made it the object of repeated contest between Mewar and the Malwa Sultanate, and two *jauhars* are recorded there. The fort is useful for showing that Rajput warfare was as often against neighbouring sultanates over frontier ground as against Delhi.
  - The dargah of the Sufi Mitthe Shah stands below the walls, and its Muharram fair draws both communities - a shared devotional site at a contested military one.
  - It is among the six Hill Forts of Rajasthan inscribed by UNESCO in 2013.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
