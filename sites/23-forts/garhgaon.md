- **Location**: Garhgaon, Sivasagar district, upper Assam, in the Dikhow valley south of the Brahmaputra - about 13 km from Sivasagar, in the Ahom heartland.
- **Period/Level**: Built as the Ahom capital in 1540 by Suklenmung, called the Garhgaya Raja after it, and used until the seat moved to Rangpur in the 18th century. The surviving Kareng Ghar, a seven-storeyed brick palace of which four floors remain, was rebuilt by Rajeswar Singha about 1752.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Ahom capital through the century of Mughal invasion, and the base of the resistance that stopped Mughal expansion eastward.
  - Mir Jumla, Aurangzeb's governor of Bengal, took Garhgaon in 1662 and held it through the monsoon, but disease and the flooded country destroyed his army and he withdrew and died in 1663. The Treaty of Ghilajharighat that followed was reversed within a decade.
  - The decisive engagement came at Saraighat in 1671, where Lachit Borphukan destroyed a far larger Mughal fleet in a river battle on the Brahmaputra. Assam is the one region the Mughals never held, and the terrain, climate and Ahom naval capability are the reasons - a useful counter-case in any question on the limits of Mughal power.
  - The Ahom state ran on the *paik* system, under which every adult male owed rotational labour service organised in *khels*. It produced the embankments, tanks, roads and palaces without a cash revenue economy, and is the standard example of a non-monetised administrative system.
  - The *Buranjis*, Ahom chronicles first written in Tai-Ahom and later in Assamese, record all of this from the Ahom side.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; *Ahom Buranji*; ASI.
