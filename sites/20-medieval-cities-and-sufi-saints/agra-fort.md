- **Location**: Agra Fort, on the right bank of the Yamuna at Agra, Uttar Pradesh - a river-front citadel about 2.5 km upstream of the Taj Mahal, which is visible from its walls.
- **Period/Level**: A Lodi fort rebuilt in red sandstone by Akbar between 1565 and 1573; Jahangir and above all Shah Jahan replaced much of the interior in white marble in the 1630s-40s. Aurangzeb held his father prisoner here from 1658 until Shah Jahan's death in 1666. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The whole evolution of Mughal architecture inside one enclosure - the clearest single site for the Akbar-to-Shah Jahan transition.
  - The Jahangiri Mahal, built under Akbar, uses brackets, carved beams and post-and-lintel construction taken directly from Rajput and Gujarati practice, in red sandstone. Shah Jahan's Khas Mahal, Diwan-i-Khas, Sheesh Mahal and Moti Masjid a lifetime later are marble, with cusped arches, *pietra dura* inlay and bulbous domes. Trabeate and indigenous gives way to arcuate and Persianate; the two stand a courtyard apart.
  - The Musamman Burj, the octagonal marble tower looking downriver, is where Shah Jahan was confined - the episode that anchors the war of succession of 1657-58 in physical memory.
  - The Diwan-i-Am, with its rows of cusped arches, is where *jharokha darshan* was performed, the daily public appearance that made the emperor visible to his subjects as a ritual obligation.
  - As the seat of empire until Shahjahanabad was founded in 1648, the fort is where the Mughal revenue and military system was administered for eighty years.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
