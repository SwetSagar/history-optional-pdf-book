- **Location**: Fatehpur Sikri, Agra district, Uttar Pradesh, on a rocky ridge about 37 km west of Agra, above a lake that has since dried - the water supply that helped cause its abandonment.
- **Period/Level**: Built by Akbar between 1571 and 1585 and occupied as the imperial capital for roughly fourteen years, then left when the court moved to Lahore to manage the north-west frontier. Red sandstone throughout. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A complete Mughal capital frozen at one moment, and the setting of Akbar's religious experiment.
  - Akbar built it in fulfilment of a vow to Shaikh Salim Chishti, who had foretold the birth of his son Salim, the future Jahangir. The saint's marble tomb, with its serpentine brackets and pierced *jali* screens, sits in the courtyard of the Jama Masjid - a Sufi grave at the centre of an imperial city.
  - The *Ibadat Khana* housed the debates Akbar convened from 1575 among Muslim theologians, and later Hindus, Jains, Zoroastrians and Jesuits. Out of these came the *mahzar* of 1579, making Akbar the final arbiter in disputed religious questions, and eventually the *Din-i-Ilahi*. The whole of Akbar's religious policy can be taught from this one building.
  - The Diwan-i-Khas, with its single central pillar carrying a circular platform linked by radiating bridges, and the Panch Mahal, a five-storeyed open pavilion, show Gujarati, Rajput and Timurid elements combined - the architectural counterpart of *sulh-i-kul*.
  - The Buland Darwaza, added about 1576, commemorates the conquest of Gujarat and is among the tallest gateways in the world.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Abu'l Fazl, *Akbarnama*; ASI.
