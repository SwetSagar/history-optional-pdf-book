- **Location**: Vijayapura, long known as Bijapur, northern Karnataka, on the dry Deccan plateau between the Krishna and the Bhima - about 100 km from Badami and Pattadakal.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Adil Shahi sultanate from 1490, when Yusuf Adil Khan broke away from the Bahmanis, until Aurangzeb took the city in 1686. Its architectural high point falls under Ibrahim Adil Shah II, r. 1580-1627, and Muhammad Adil Shah, r. 1627-1656.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The most accomplished architecture of the Deccan sultanates, and a court whose religious eclecticism rivalled Akbar's.
  - The Gol Gumbaz, the tomb of Muhammad Adil Shah completed about 1656, carries a dome roughly 44 m across - the largest in India and among the largest anywhere - resting on intersecting pendentive arches rather than a drum. Its Whispering Gallery, where a murmur circles the base of the dome, is the best-known acoustic feature in Indian architecture.
  - The Ibrahim Rauza, the tomb and mosque of Ibrahim II, is the finer building: exact proportions, deep eaves and stonework cut like filigree. It is often said to have influenced the Taj, and its architect Malik Sandal signed it.
  - Ibrahim II called himself *Jagadguru Badshah*, and his *Kitab-i-Nauras*, a book of songs in Dakhni, opens by invoking Saraswati and Ganesha alongside the Prophet. A Muslim sultan writing Hindustani music theory addressed to Hindu deities is the sharpest Deccan example of religious accommodation.
  - The Malik-e-Maidan, one of the largest bronze cannon of the medieval world, stands on the ramparts - a reminder that these sultanates were technically formidable until Mughal artillery and money outlasted them.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
