- **Location**: Vrindavan, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh, on the right bank of the Yamuna in the Braj country - a few kilometres from Mathura, at the centre of the Krishna landscape.
- **Period/Level**: An ancient tirtha that was effectively refounded in the 16th century, when Chaitanya Mahaprabhu sent the Six Goswamis to identify and restore the Braj sites. The great temples belong to the decades around 1590: Govind Dev, Madan Mohan, Radha Raman and Jugal Kishore.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The physical centre of Krishna bhakti, and a case of a sacred landscape being deliberately reconstructed.
  - Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva and the other Goswamis located the groves, ghats and ponds of the Krishna story on the ground and wrote the theological works - Rupa's *Bhaktirasamritasindhu* above all - that gave Gaudiya Vaishnavism its doctrine of *rasa*. Vrindavan is therefore where a devotional geography was mapped as a scholarly project.
  - The Govind Dev temple was built about 1590 by Raja Man Singh of Amber, Akbar's leading Rajput commander, in red sandstone on a cruciform plan with a vaulted nave. A Mughal *mansabdar* funding a Vaishnava temple with imperial approval, in an architectural idiom that borrows from mosque construction, is the clearest single illustration of Akbar's religious policy in built form; Aurangzeb later had its upper storeys taken down.
  - Braj bhakti produced a vernacular literature to match - Surdas of the Ashtachhap and the Vallabha sampradaya, Mirabai's songs, and the poetry of Swami Haridas - which is why Vrindavan belongs in questions on regional languages as well as religion.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI.
