- **Location**: Ramdevra, also called Runicha, Jaisalmer district, Rajasthan, in the Thar desert about 12 km from Pokhran - a shrine village on the old caravan track across western Rajasthan.
- **Period/Level**: The samadhi of Baba Ramdev, a Tanwar Rajput chieftain of the early 15th century - his dates are usually given as 1352-1385 or in the 1400s, and cannot be fixed precisely. The temple over the samadhi was built later, with major additions in the 19th and 20th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A regional saint venerated across religious and caste lines - the best western Indian example of folk devotion that no orthodoxy controls.
  - Ramdev is worshipped by Hindus as an incarnation of Krishna and by Muslims as *Ramshah Pir* or *Ramapir*, and his shrine carries both a Hindu samadhi and Islamic devotional practice. That a single figure is a deity to one community and a pir to another is the standard illustration of how the Hindu-Muslim boundary worked in practice at the village level.
  - His following is strongest among Dalit and artisan communities, especially the Meghwals, and the tradition centres on his rejection of untouchability - the story of his taking food with Dali Bai and his refusal of caste exclusion at his own feast.
  - The *Kamdiya* and *terah taali* devotional performances, and the annual fair at Bhadrapada, sustain an oral tradition in Marwari with no Sanskrit text behind it - useful evidence that bhakti in western India operated largely outside the literate tradition.
  - The site belongs with Pandharpur and the Varkaris as an example of caste-inclusive regional devotion, though Ramdev's cult has no theological system comparable to theirs.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; *Rajasthan District Gazetteer*, Jaisalmer.
