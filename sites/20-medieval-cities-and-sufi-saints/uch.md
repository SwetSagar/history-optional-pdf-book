- **Location**: Uch Sharif, Bahawalpur district, Punjab, Pakistan, near the confluence of the Chenab and the Sutlej before they join the Indus - a river-island town on the Multan-Sindh route.
- **Period/Level**: An ancient site sometimes identified with an Alexandrian foundation on the Indus. It was Nasiruddin Qubacha's capital in the early 13th century before the Delhi Sultanate absorbed it, and thereafter a Sultanate frontier stronghold and a major Suhrawardi centre. The surviving tomb group is of the 14th-15th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: With Multan, the second pole of the Suhrawardi order, and the site of some of the finest glazed-tile architecture in the subcontinent.
  - Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari settled here in the 13th century and his descendant Jahaniyan Jahangasht, the "world traveller", made Uch a centre whose spiritual authority reached across Sindh and Gujarat. The Multan-Uch axis is where Islam spread along the Indus by persuasion and patronage rather than by campaign.
  - The tomb of Bibi Jawindi, c. 1493, is octagonal with sloping buttresses and faced in blue and white glazed tile, closely related to the Multan tombs and to Central Asian work at Samarkand. Half of it was carried away by flooding of the Chenab, and what remains is on the tentative World Heritage list.
  - Guru Nanak visited during his travels, and the shrine preserves relics associated with him - a reminder that Sufi khanqahs on the Indus were meeting points rather than closed communities.
  - Qubacha's brief kingdom here, squeezed between Iltutmish and the Mongols, is a useful example of how unstable the north-western frontier was in the Sultanate's first decades.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I.
