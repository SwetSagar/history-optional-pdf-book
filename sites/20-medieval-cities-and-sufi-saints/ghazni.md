- **Location**: Ghazni, eastern Afghanistan, on the Ghazni river on the high plateau between Kabul and Kandahar - astride the road from Khurasan to the Indian passes.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Ghaznavid dynasty, founded by Sabuktigin and brought to its height by Mahmud, r. 998-1030, who led some seventeen raids into India between 1000 and 1027. The city was sacked and burnt by the Ghurid Alauddin Husain in 1151, who earned the title *Jahansuz*, the world-burner.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The base from which the Ghaznavid raids on India were mounted, and an unexpected centre of scholarship funded by their proceeds.
  - Mahmud's raids - Nagarkot, Mathura, Kannauj, Somnath in 1025-26 - were expeditions for plunder rather than for territory; only the Punjab was annexed. Treating them as conquest is the commonest error, and Ghazni's own history makes the point: the wealth was carried back here to build a capital, not used to govern India.
  - Al-Biruni was brought to Ghazni from Khwarezm and travelled in India in Mahmud's train. His *Kitab-ul-Hind* is the most penetrating foreign account of Indian society, science and religion before the modern period, and is notable for studying Sanskrit sources directly and for its detachment - written, as it was, in the service of a raider.
  - Firdausi's *Shahnama* was composed for Mahmud's court, and the poet's quarrel with the sultan over payment is part of the standard picture of Ghaznavid patronage.
  - The two surviving victory minarets of Masud III and Bahram Shah, richly carved in brick, are the remains of the city Mahmud's Indian plunder built.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Al-Biruni, *Kitab-ul-Hind*.
