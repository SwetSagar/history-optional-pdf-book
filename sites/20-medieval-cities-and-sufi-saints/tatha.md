- **Location**: Thatta, Sindh, Pakistan, near the eastern edge of the Indus delta about 100 km from Karachi, with the Makli ridge rising beside it - the delta's capital before the river shifted away.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of Sindh under the Sammas, Arghuns and Tarkhans from the 14th century, then a Mughal provincial seat from 1592. The Makli necropolis spans the 14th to the 18th centuries; the Shah Jahan mosque was completed about 1647.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the largest funerary landscapes in the world, and the point where Persian and Timurid tilework meets Gujarati stone carving.
  - The Makli necropolis covers roughly ten square kilometres and holds an estimated half a million graves - kings, queens, saints, scholars and soldiers across four centuries. Nowhere else in South Asia can a single regional elite be studied so completely through its tombs.
  - The two traditions sit side by side there. Samma-period tombs, such as Jam Nizamuddin's, are carved sandstone with Gujarati-derived brackets and floral bands; Tarkhan and Mughal tombs are brick faced in blue, turquoise and white glazed tile in the Iranian manner. The shift tracks Sindh's reorientation from the Indian west coast towards Safavid Iran and Central Asia.
  - The Shah Jahan mosque, built for a city that had sheltered the emperor during a revolt, has ninety-three domes and no minarets, and is designed so that a speaker at the mihrab can be heard throughout - the most sophisticated acoustic design in Mughal architecture.
  - Thatta declined as the Indus migrated westward and Karachi took its trade, the same river-shift that had earlier stranded Debal.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; ASI/UNESCO.
