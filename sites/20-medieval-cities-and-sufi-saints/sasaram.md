- **Location**: Sasaram, Rohtas district, Bihar, at the foot of the Kaimur hills in the Son valley - on the Grand Trunk Road, the highway its most famous son rebuilt.
- **Period/Level**: The tomb of Sher Shah Suri, r. 1540-1545, was built between about 1540 and 1545 by the architect Aliwal Khan. Red sandstone, five storeys, octagonal, about 46 m high, standing on a plinth in the middle of an artificial lake. One of Ashoka's minor rock edicts is also inscribed on a hill nearby.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest tomb of the Indo-Afghan tradition, and the burial place of the administrator the Mughals copied.
  - The tomb is the culmination of the Lodi-Sur octagonal type: an octagon within a square terrace, ringed by chhatris, its dome among the largest built in India before Gol Gumbaz. Setting it in the centre of a tank makes the reflection part of the design - an effect later used at the Taj by different means.
  - Sher Shah's five years of rule produced the framework the Mughals inherited: the *zabti* revenue settlement with measured land and rates fixed by crop, later developed by Todar Mal under Akbar; the silver *rupiya*, ancestor of the modern rupee; the rebuilt Sadak-i-Azam from Bengal to the Indus with sarais and a *dak* postal relay.
  - That an Afghan interregnum supplied so much of Mughal administration is the standard corrective to treating Mughal institutions as purely Mughal inventions.
  - The Ashokan minor rock edict on the hill above makes Sasaram a rare site carrying both Mauryan and Sur-period remains.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 7; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
