- **Location**: Ahmednagar, western Maharashtra, on the Sina river in the upper Godavari basin - on the Deccan plateau inland from the Western Ghats, commanding the route between Pune and Aurangabad.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Nizam Shahi sultanate, founded in 1490 by Malik Ahmad, Bahmani governor of Junnar. It resisted Akbar's forces under Chand Bibi in 1595, was annexed by Shah Jahan in 1636, and is where Aurangzeb died in 1707.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Deccan sultanate that produced Malik Ambar - the administrator and general whose methods the Marathas inherited.
  - Malik Ambar, an Ethiopian *Habshi* brought to India as a slave, became the effective ruler of Ahmednagar from about 1600 to 1626. He introduced a revenue settlement on the Todar Mal model - measured land, assessment by crop and soil, collection in cash - which became the basis of the system the Marathas later used in the same country.
  - His military method mattered more. Denied the field against superior Mughal artillery, he used light cavalry, raiding, denial of supply and constant mobility to keep the Mughals out of the Deccan for a quarter of a century. This is the *ganimi kava* that Shivaji perfected, and Ambar is its acknowledged originator.
  - Chand Bibi's defence of the fort against Akbar's army in 1595, personally directing the repair of a breach, is among the best-documented instances of a woman commanding in the field in Indian history.
  - The fort later held Jawaharlal Nehru and other Congress leaders from 1942-45, where Nehru wrote *The Discovery of India*.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I and II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*.
