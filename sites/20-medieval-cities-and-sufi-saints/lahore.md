- **Location**: Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan, on the left bank of the Ravi in the Bari doab - the principal city between the Khyber and Delhi, and therefore the hinge of every invasion route into India.
- **Period/Level**: The Ghaznavids' Indian capital from the 11th century after Mahmud's raids; then Ghurid and Sultanate. Akbar made it the imperial capital from 1585 to 1598 while securing the north-west. Shah Jahan built the Shalimar Gardens in 1641, Aurangzeb the Badshahi mosque in 1673, and Ranjit Singh ruled the Sikh empire from here, 1799-1839.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Mughal empire's second capital, and the best place to see Mughal garden and mosque architecture at full scale.
  - Akbar's move here for thirteen years was strategic: Kabul, Kandahar and the Uzbek frontier all had to be managed, and Lahore was the only city from which that was possible. The episode shows Mughal capitals following military necessity rather than sentiment.
  - The Shalimar Gardens are the classic *charbagh* on three descending terraces with over four hundred fountains, fed by a canal cut from the Ravi - a Persian paradise-garden realised as a hydraulic engineering project.
  - The Badshahi mosque, with its vast courtyard and red sandstone inlaid with marble, is the last great imperial mosque of the Mughals and deliberately answers Shah Jahan's Jama Masjid at Delhi. The Wazir Khan mosque, 1634, is the finest surviving example of Mughal glazed-tile *kashi* work.
  - Jahangir's tomb across the river at Shahdara, with no dome at the emperor's own instruction, and the Sikh-period additions under Ranjit Singh complete a sequence running from Ghaznavid to Sikh in one city.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
