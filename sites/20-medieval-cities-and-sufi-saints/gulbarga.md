- **Location**: Kalaburagi, formerly Gulbarga, northern Karnataka, on the dry plateau of the upper Bhima basin - a fortified town in open country between Bidar and Bijapur.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Bahmani sultanate from 1347, when Alauddin Bahman Shah broke from Muhammad bin Tughlaq, until the seat moved to Bidar about 1425-32. The Jama Masjid within the fort dates to 1367; the dargah of Gesu Daraz, d. 1422, stands outside it.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The first capital of the first independent Deccan sultanate, and the place where the Chishti order moved south.
  - The Bahmani secession followed Muhammad bin Tughlaq's transfer of the capital to Daulatabad and its failure - the standard case study in Sultanate overreach, since the very officers moved south to hold the Deccan founded a kingdom there instead.
  - The Jama Masjid is unique in India for having its entire courtyard roofed, with sixty-three small domes and open arcaded sides rather than an open *sahn*. The design is traditionally attributed to a Persian architect and compared to Cordoba; whatever its source, it shows the Bahmanis importing Iranian expertise directly rather than adapting north Indian models.
  - Khwaja Bandanawaz Gesu Daraz, a disciple of the Delhi Chishti line, migrated to Gulbarga in his old age and made it a spiritual centre for the whole Deccan. His arrival marks the extension of the Chishti network beyond the Vindhyas, and he wrote in Dakhni, contributing to the language's early literature.
  - The rivalry between *Afaqi* immigrant nobles and *Dakhni* locals that later destroyed Mahmud Gawan at Bidar begins in this court.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I.
