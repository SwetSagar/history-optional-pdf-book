- **Location**: The Khanqah-e-Moula or Shah-e-Hamadan mosque, on the right bank of the Jhelum in the old city of Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir - built at the river's edge in the heart of the medieval town.
- **Period/Level**: Built in 1395 by Sultan Sikandar in memory of Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani, the Persian Kubrawi Sufi who came to Kashmir in the 1370s and died in 1384. Rebuilt after fires in the 15th and 18th centuries, always to the same design, in deodar timber.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The masterpiece of Kashmiri wooden architecture, and the monument of the Sufi credited with reshaping the valley's economy as well as its religion.
  - The structure is built of interlocking deodar beams laid without nails, on the *taq* and *dhajji* principles that give Kashmiri buildings their resistance to earthquake. The interior is finished in *papier-mache* and *khatamband* - geometric wooden ceilings assembled from small pieces without glue - and hung with painted panels.
  - Sayyid Ali Hamadani is said to have arrived with several hundred followers, many of them craftsmen, and Kashmiri tradition credits him with introducing or systematising shawl weaving, carpet knotting, papier-mache and woodcarving. Whether or not the attribution is exact, it records a real transfer of Persian craft into the valley, and is the standard example of Sufi networks moving technique as well as doctrine.
  - His son Mir Muhammad Hamadani influenced Sikandar's reign, associated with iconoclasm and conversion - so the site is also cited in the harsher account of Kashmir's Islamisation, against the accommodating reputation of Nund Rishi and the Rishi order.
  - The mosque stands opposite the Hindu ghats on the Jhelum, and both remain in use.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI.
