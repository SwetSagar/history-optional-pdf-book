- **Location**: Paisra, in the Kharagpur forests near Munger, Bihar, on the northern fringe of the Chhotanagpur plateau; excavated by Pant and Jayaswal (1991).
- **Period/Level**: A Lower Palaeolithic living and working floor, with Upper Palaeolithic material also present in the wider Munger and Rajmahal (Damin) region.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the rare Lower Palaeolithic sites in India to yield structural evidence of dwellings, showing that Acheulian groups built shelters in the open and did not depend only on caves and rock shelters.
  - Eight post-holes were found, marking places where wooden posts had been dug into the ground to support thatched huts.
  - The whole area was rich in finished and unfinished artefacts, broken pieces of stone and anvils, marking it as a stone working site as well as a habitation.
  - Large and small fireplaces occur close together, indicating organised use of space within the settlement.
