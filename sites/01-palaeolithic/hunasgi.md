- **Location**: The Hunsgi valley, Gulbarga (Kalaburagi) district, Karnataka, on the banks of the Hunsgi, a tributary of the Krishna; investigated by K. Paddayya from 1982 onwards.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic (Acheulian), with Middle Palaeolithic occupation at the associated site of Isampur, where prehistoric occupation goes back to between 500,000 and 600,000 years ago.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The classic Indian demonstration of Acheulian settlement patterns, where the size and composition of sites have been used to reconstruct how hunter-gatherer bands used a landscape.
  - Sites with very few artefact types represent places where specific activities such as tool-making or killing game were carried out; sites with tools in larger number and variety were probably temporary camps; the largest sites, with tools in great profusion, were places where groups lived for longer periods.
  - The tools were made of limestone, sandstone, quartzite, dolerite and chert, some of which were not locally available; in one excavated area huge granite blocks were arranged around a 63 sq m space, probably to support temporary shelters of branches, grass and leaves.
  - At Isampur, covering about 7,200 sq m, siliceous limestone slabs were quarried and worked: the Acheulian material consists mainly of cores, large flakes and debitage, with chopping tools, knives, handaxes, cleavers and scrapers, and hammer stones of quartzite, basalt and chert in very large numbers. Unfinished tools far outnumber finished ones, marking it as a centre of stone tool manufacture and also a habitation site.
  - The area today supports about 40 types of wild edible plants and plenty of small game, which helps explain the density of occupation.
