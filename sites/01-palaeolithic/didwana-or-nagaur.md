- **Location**: Didwana, Nagaur district, western Rajasthan, on the eastern margin of the Thar desert beside the Didwana salt lake; the principal localities are Singi Talav and Amarpura. Studied in detail by V. N. Misra and S. N. Rajaguru.
- **Period/Level**: A stratigraphic profile extending from the early to the middle Palaeolithic. The Lower Palaeolithic is dated 390,000 BP by the uranium-thorium series method, and the Middle Palaeolithic has two thermoluminescence dates of 150,000 BP and 144,000 BP.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the very few Indian sites with absolute dates for both the Lower and Middle Palaeolithic, and the anchor for reconstructing the palaeo-environment of the Thar.
  - Finds include handaxes, cleavers and choppers in stratified deposits.
  - The study shows that the present environment of the Thar is very different from that of the Pleistocene: except for a phase in the upper Pleistocene between 25,000 and 13,000 BP, surface water was always available in some quantity and the flora and fauna were far more abundant than today.
  - Sediments of the salt lakes record a significant increase in rainfall in the mid-Holocene, about 6000-4000 BP, precisely the period to which the most widespread prehistoric occupation of the area belongs.
