- **Location**: Pallavaram, near Chennai, formerly Madras, Tamil Nadu.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic (Acheulian).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: In the summer of 1863 Robert Bruce Foote, an officer of the Geological Survey of India on routine survey duty, noticed a stone embedded in a gravel pit here: a seemingly unremarkable piece of brownish quartzite with one end chipped off, which he recognised as a handaxe. It was the first Palaeolithic tool discovered in India.
  - Foote went on to find and study many more stone tools and made major contributions to research on Indian prehistory, earning the title "Father of Indian Prehistory".
  - The Pallavaram handaxe was not strictly the first prehistoric tool found in India; in 1856 Le Mesurier, a railway engineer, had found a small chert arrowhead near Nyagurhee in central India, and in 1842 Dr Primrose had reported implements at Lingsugur. Foote's find, however, was the one that was followed up.
  - He travelled to England in 1868 to inform the scholarly community of his work, and in 1873 some of the tools he had discovered were displayed at the International Exhibition at Vienna, so that within two decades the foundations of Indian prehistory had been laid and internationally recognised.
