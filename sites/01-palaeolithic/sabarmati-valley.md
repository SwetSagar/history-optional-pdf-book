- **Location**: The Sabarmati valley of north Gujarat, together with its Orsang and Karjan tributaries, and the related Bhadar valley in Saurashtra.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic, continuing into later phases.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Lower Palaeolithic tools have been found throughout these valleys, establishing the Pleistocene occupation of Gujarat and bridging the Thar sites of Rajasthan with the Narmada sequence of central India.
  - Lower Palaeolithic and later artefacts have also been found all along the Konkan coast as far as Goa, so the Gujarat valleys form the northern end of a continuous western Indian record.
  - The same valley system later carried the important Mesolithic settlement of Langhnaj, giving the region a sequence from the Palaeolithic into the microlithic and Chalcolithic phases.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2.
