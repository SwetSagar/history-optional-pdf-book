- **Location**: Nashik, on the Godavari in northern Maharashtra; the Palaeolithic material comes from the Gangawadi area on the river bank.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic tools recovered in a stratigraphic context.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Nashik contributes one of the stratified river-section profiles on which H. D. Sankalia and his successors built the Palaeolithic sequence of the Deccan, alongside those of the Mula-Mutha, Pravara and Tapi.
  - Palaeolithic tools have been found in many places along the Maharashtra coast and in the Wardha-Wainganga valleys, with Lower and Middle Palaeolithic material in stratigraphic contexts in the Dattawadi area of the Mutha at Pune.
  - In the historic period the Pandavleni caves above the town became a major Hinayana Buddhist centre: the Cave 10 inscription of Ushavadata, viceroy and son-in-law of the Western Kshatrapa ruler Nahapana, dated about 120 CE, is among the earliest known uses of Sanskrit in western India, and the Cave 3 inscription of Gautamiputra Satakarni, issued by his mother Gautami Balashri, records his military conquests and his feud with Nahapana.
