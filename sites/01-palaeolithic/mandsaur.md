- **Location**: Mandsaur district, western Madhya Pradesh, on the Chambal-Malwa plateau; the Palaeolithic evidence comes from the Daraki Chattan cave in the Indragarh hill.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic, the rock art dated to between 200,000 and 500,000 years ago; the site is also a major non-Ashokan inscription and fort site of the historic and medieval periods.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: At Daraki Chattan more than five hundred pieces of rock art, cup-shaped depressions pecked into the quartzite, have been found together with hammer stones, making it among the oldest rock art sites in India and in the world.
  - With the cupules of the Auditorium Cave at Bhimbetka, these petroglyphs push the origins of symbolic and ritual expression in India back into the Lower Palaeolithic.
  - Mandsaur, ancient Dasapura, later carried the sixth-century Sanskrit inscription of Yasodharman marking his victory over the Huna king Mihirakula, and a fifth-century inscription of the time of Kumaragupta recording the migration of silk weavers from Lata to Dasapura, who organised themselves into a guild and built a sun temple.
  - The fort was built by Hoshang Shah of the Malwa Sultanate, was occupied by Rana Sanga in 1519, and was the place where the treaty of 1818 bringing Malwa under British control was signed.
