A site in Madhya Pradesh with remains spanning several periods.

- At Daraki Chattan more than five hundred pieces of rock art have been found together with hammer stones; dated to between 200,000 and 500,000 years ago, it is among the oldest such sites in India.
- The site also carries non-Ashokan inscriptions: one in Sanskrit, dated to the sixth century, was issued by Yasodharman to mark his victory over the Huna king Mihirakula; another, of Kumaragupta in the fifth century, records the migration of silk weavers from Lata to Dasapura, who organised themselves into a guild and built a sun temple.
- The fort was built by Hoshang Shah of the Malwa Sultanate and was occupied by Rana Sanga in 1519.
- The treaty of 1818, by which Malwa passed under British occupation, was signed here.
