- **Location**: The Kurnool cave complex, including Muchchatla Chintamanu Gavi and the Billa Surgam caves, Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh, in the limestone hills of the Nallamalai range.
- **Period/Level**: Upper Palaeolithic, with two electron spin resonance dates of 19,224 BP and 16,686 BP.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Kurnool and Muchchatla Chintamanu Gavi are the only places in the subcontinent where tools made of animal bone have been found in an Upper Palaeolithic context; in one of the caves as many as 90 per cent of the excavated tools were of bone.
  - Animal teeth found in one cave have grooves suggesting they were strung and worn as ornaments; ash deposits show familiarity with fire, and painted rock shelters occur alongside the naturally formed caves.
  - The faunal list is exceptionally long, including bat, nilgai, four-horned antelope, gazelle, chital, sambar, barking deer, mouse deer, wild boar, tiger, leopard, jungle cat, spotted hyena, civet, freshwater fish, mongoose, sloth bear, porcupine, hare, grey langur, horse, ass, rhinoceros and giant pangolin.
  - Besides recording the animals that Upper Palaeolithic people shared their landscape with, this range indicates that thick forests and far more humid conditions prevailed in the area than today.
