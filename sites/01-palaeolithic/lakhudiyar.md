- **Location**: Lakhudiyar, on the bank of the Suyal river about 20 km from Almora on the Almora-Barechhina road, Uttarakhand - a rock shelter in the Kumaon hills. The name means "a hundred thousand caves".
- **Period/Level**: Rock paintings assigned broadly to the Mesolithic and later prehistoric periods, with some figures argued to be Chalcolithic. The shelter carries superimposed layers in three colours, the black generally earlier, then red ochre, with white over both.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal prehistoric rock art site of the central Himalaya - the northern counterpart to Bhimbetka.
  - The paintings show stick-like human figures holding hands in rows, read as dancing or as a group ritual, along with animals - long-snouted quadrupeds, foxes, lizards - and geometric and wavy patterns.
  - Human figures greatly outnumber animals, which is the opposite of the pattern at Bhimbetka and in the Vindhyan shelters. That difference is the site's analytical value: Indian rock art is usually discussed through the central Indian material, and Lakhudiyar shows a distinct regional tradition with its own subject preferences.
  - The three-colour superimposition allows a relative sequence to be worked out even without absolute dates, which is how most Indian rock art is dated.
  - Other Kumaon and Garhwal shelters - Falseema, Petshala, Kasar Devi - belong to the same group, so the region has a cluster rather than an isolated site, and it should be read with the Garhwal Neolithic settlement at Thapli.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
