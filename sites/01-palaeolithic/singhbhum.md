- **Location**: Singhbhum district, on the Chhotanagpur plateau of Jharkhand, in the Subarnarekha basin.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic, with continuity of occupation in the region into the Neolithic and Chalcolithic phases.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The river valleys and foothills of the Chhotanagpur plateau and the adjoining areas of West Bengal have yielded Lower Palaeolithic tools, including handaxes and bifacial chopping tools.
  - Barudih in the same district represents the later Neolithic-Chalcolithic occupation, with cultivated pulses such as chickpea, green pea and horse gram and an increase in faunal remains, showing unbroken use of the landscape.
  - Singhbhum holds the richest copper mines in India, in a belt about 130 km long, which supplied the copper used by Chalcolithic and Harappan-period cultures, so the district connects the Palaeolithic occupation of eastern India to the metal-using cultures that followed.
