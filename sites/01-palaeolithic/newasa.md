- **Location**: Nevasa, Ahmednagar district, Maharashtra, on the Pravara river, a tributary of the Godavari; the associated living and factory site of Chirki lies close by. Excavated by H. D. Sankalia of Deccan College.
- **Period/Level**: One mound with two widely separated occupations. The deeper levels carry a stratified Middle Palaeolithic industry, with a uranium-thorium date of about 350,000 BP; above them lies a Jorwe culture Chalcolithic settlement of roughly 1400-700 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site of the Indian Middle Palaeolithic, and one of the principal sources for Chalcolithic burial and belief in the Deccan.
  - Sankalia found Middle Palaeolithic artefacts here in a stratified context for the first time, and the "Nevasan" is accordingly the name given to the Middle Palaeolithic of central and peninsular India. The industry is flake-based, dominated by scrapers of every kind - convex, concave, side, end and angle - with points and borers.
  - The tools are worked in fine-grained agate, jasper and chalcedony rather than the coarse quartzite of the Lower Palaeolithic, and that shift in raw material is the diagnostic marker of the Middle Palaeolithic in India. Chirki nearby yielded some two thousand tools from a living and factory site.
  - In the Chalcolithic levels, children were buried in twin urns set mouth to mouth beneath house floors - the characteristic Jorwe practice of keeping the dead within the home rather than in a separate cemetery.
  - Some child burials at Nevasa and Chandoli carry copper necklaces while others have only pots, a difference read as emerging social inequality. Baked and unbaked female figurines point to a fertility or mother-goddess cult, the unbaked ones apparently made for a single ritual use.
  - Cotton and semal silk threads from the site show cloth manufacture in Chalcolithic Maharashtra.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2 and 5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; H. D. Sankalia, *Prehistory and Protohistory of India and Pakistan*.
