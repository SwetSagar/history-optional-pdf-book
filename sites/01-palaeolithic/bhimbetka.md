- **Location**: Bhimbetka, Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh, about 45 km south of Bhopal and 30 km north of Hoshangabad, on a sandstone and quartzite hillside of the Vindhyas overlooking the Narmada; brought to notice and excavated by V. S. Wakankar, and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003.
- **Period/Level**: An exceptionally long sequence running from the Lower Palaeolithic through the Mesolithic to the historic period, across hundreds of rock shelters.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The single most important prehistoric site in India, combining the longest occupation sequence with the richest body of rock art.
  - Most tools were made of a yellowish quartzite available locally, with a grey quartzite obtained from further away; five floors paved with flat stone slabs belong to the Lower Palaeolithic. No bones have been found, probably because of the acidic soil.
  - Cave III F-24, the "Auditorium Cave", stands on the borderline of the Lower and Middle Palaeolithic. A large rock in the middle of the hall carries seven cupules up to 16.8 mm deep, and a second rock has a single large cup mark with a meandering line; these were most likely made deliberately as part of a community ritual.
  - A chalcedony disc was found in an Acheulian context, and ostrich eggshell beads about 6 mm across came from an Upper Palaeolithic burial, lying at the neck of the skull of a buried man.
  - The Mesolithic paintings, executed in green and dark red, show hunters in red and dancers in green, along with large animal figures of bison, elephant, tiger and rhinoceros and geometric patterns; the shelters were attractive because of three perennial springs, about 30 plant types yielding edible fruits, tubers and roots, and abundant game.
