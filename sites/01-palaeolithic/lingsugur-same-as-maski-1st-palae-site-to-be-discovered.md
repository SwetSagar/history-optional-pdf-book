- **Location**: Lingsugur, Raichur district, Karnataka, in the Krishna-Tungabhadra doab, close to Maski.
- **Period/Level**: Palaeolithic surface finds, in a tract better known for its Neolithic ash-mound settlements.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: In 1842 Dr Primrose reported stone implements from Lingsugur, the earliest recorded discovery of a prehistoric stone tool in India, some two decades before Robert Bruce Foote picked up the Pallavaram handaxe in 1863.
  - Primrose's find attracted no scholarly follow-up, which is why Foote's Pallavaram discovery is conventionally treated as the starting point of systematic Indian prehistory.
  - The same tract, at Maski, later yielded the minor rock edict that is one of the very few Ashokan inscriptions to name the emperor as "Ashoka" rather than only by his titles, and the Raichur doab remained a contested frontier between Vijayanagara and the Bahmanis.
