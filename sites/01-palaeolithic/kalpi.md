- **Location**: Kalpi, Jalaun district, Uttar Pradesh, on the southern bank of the Yamuna, where the finds lie embedded in a 20 m thick cliff section.
- **Period/Level**: Middle Palaeolithic; the tool-bearing level has been dated to about 45,000 years ago.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Kalpi preserves the earliest trace of human occupation in the Ganga plain, a region where deep alluvium normally conceals or destroys Palaeolithic evidence.
  - Vertebrate fossils recovered here include an elephant tusk and shoulder blade and the molars of Equus and bovids.
  - The stone tools include pebble tools, points and side scrapers, and were found together with bone tools such as end scrapers, points and burins, an uncommon association in the Indian Middle Palaeolithic.
