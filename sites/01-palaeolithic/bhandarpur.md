- **Location**: Bhandarpur, in the lower Narmada basin of eastern Gujarat, in the tract where the Orsang and Karjan join the main river.
- **Period/Level**: Middle to Upper Palaeolithic, with a microblade horizon.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The microblade assemblage recovered here belongs to the class of evidence used to trace the dispersal of anatomically modern humans out of Africa into South Asia.
  - Microblade industries of this kind, dated across western and central India, are central to the debate on when fully modern human populations entered the subcontinent and how that relates to the Toba super-eruption of about 75,000 years ago.
  - Gujarat's Lower Palaeolithic is otherwise known from the valleys of the Sabarmati and its Orsang and Karjan tributaries, and from the Bhadar valley in Saurashtra, so Bhandarpur extends that record into the later Palaeolithic.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2 (the Gujarat valleys and the Toba eruption; the Bhandarpur microblade finds are from the author's notes).
