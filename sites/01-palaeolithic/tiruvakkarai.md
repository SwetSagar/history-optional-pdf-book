- **Location**: Tiruvakkarai, Villupuram district, Tamil Nadu, about 20 km from Puducherry, the site of the National Fossil Wood Park.
- **Period/Level**: Palaeolithic surface finds within a Miocene fossil-bearing formation.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The locality is best known for petrified wood fossils roughly 20 million years old, the trunks being thought to have been transported here by water before they were petrified.
  - Palaeolithic tools occur in the same tract as neighbouring Odai, whose hominid remains and microliths make this stretch of the South Pennar basin one of the richest prehistoric landscapes in Tamil Nadu.
  - The juxtaposition of a Miocene fossil bed with Palaeolithic occupation makes the site a convenient illustration of the difference between geological and archaeological time scales.
  - *Sources*: Geological Survey of India, National Fossil Wood Park, Tiruvakkarai, a National Geological Monument since 1951; the Palaeolithic finds are from the author's notes.
