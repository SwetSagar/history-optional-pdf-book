- **Location**: The Soan or Sohan river in the Potwar plateau, between the Pir Panjal and the Salt ranges, in the Punjab province of Pakistan.
- **Period/Level**: The Soanian pebble tool tradition of the Lower Palaeolithic, contemporary with the Acheulian, with abundant Middle and Upper Palaeolithic material; Riwat near Rawalpindi has given a palaeomagnetic date of 2.01 million years ago, Dina and Jalalpur in the Jhelum basin about 700,000-500,000 years ago, and site 55 at Riwat 45,000 BP for the Upper Palaeolithic.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type region of the Soanian industry and the setting of the first systematic palaeo-environmental study in Indian prehistory.
  - In 1935 H. de Terra and T. T. Paterson found a large number of tools here, mostly of the Middle and Upper Palaeolithic with some of the Lower Palaeolithic, and identified five tool-bearing river terraces. The assemblage is dominated by choppers, chopping tools and edged pebble tools rather than handaxes.
  - They tried to correlate these terraces with a four-fold glacial cycle in Kashmir and further with a four-fold European glacial cycle, and extended the framework to the Narmada and the area around Chennai. Most of their correlations and conclusions are no longer accepted, but the study marked an important stage in the development of Indian prehistoric research.
  - Fossils of gazelle, rhinoceros, crocodile and giraffe from the region help reconstruct the Pleistocene environment of the north-west.
