- **Location**: Chopani Mando, in the Belan valley on the Vindhyan fringe of the Allahabad (Prayagraj) and Mirzapur tract, Uttar Pradesh; excavated by G. R. Sharma of Allahabad University.
- **Period/Level**: A habitation site with a cultural sequence running from the Upper Palaeolithic (Period I) through the Mesolithic (Periods II and III, with geometric microliths in Period IIB) to the Neolithic; the Belan Upper Palaeolithic is dated between 25,000 and 19,000 years ago.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The classic Indian site for the transition from hunting and gathering to settled food production, set within a valley whose sequence runs unbroken from the Lower Palaeolithic to the Neolithic.
  - The Palaeolithic levels yielded tools of chert, a stone available in the nearby Vindhyas.
  - Period III produced handmade pottery with cord-impressed patterns, among the earliest pottery known in India, along with querns and mullers for processing wild grains.
  - Wild rice was recovered, though whether it was domesticated remains debated; the huts were beehive-shaped in the earlier phase and oval, clustered and wattle-and-daub later, evidenced by burnt clay bearing reed impressions.
  - Bones of sheep and goat, animals not indigenous to the area, suggest either contact with the north-west or an early stage of animal domestication.
