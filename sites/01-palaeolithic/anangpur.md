- **Location**: Anangpur, in the Badarpur hills of the Aravalli quartzite ridge immediately south of Delhi (Faridabad district, Haryana).
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic, with both early and late Acheulian assemblages.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Excavations revealed thousands of early and late Acheulian tools together with traces of several palaeo-channels of the Yamuna, marking this as a large Lower Palaeolithic habitation-cum-factory site.
  - A systematic survey of south Delhi and adjoining areas by Chakrabarti and Lahiri (1986) identified 43 stone age sites ranging from the Lower Palaeolithic to the microlithic, including finds on the Delhi Ridge near Delhi University and a late Acheulian handaxe on the JNU campus in 1983.
  - The site is a standard illustration that stone age sites are not remote curiosities but lie under areas that are today densely urbanised.
  - The same village later carried early medieval fortifications and a stone masonry dam associated with the Tomara king Anangapala, giving the place its name.
