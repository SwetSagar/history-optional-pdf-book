- **Location**: Located in the Potwar region (West Punjab, Pakistan), lying between the Pir Panjal and Salt ranges along the Sohan (Soan) River Valley.
- **Period/Level**: Lower Palaeolithic (associated with the Sohanian / Sohan Valley pebble tool tradition).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site yielded characteristic Lower Palaeolithic core tools, including handaxes, choppers, and edged pebble tools.
  - It is one of the localities of the Soan terrace sequence surveyed by H. de Terra and T. T. Paterson in 1935, who identified five tool-bearing river terraces in the Potwar and attempted to correlate them with a four-fold glacial cycle in Kashmir; most of those correlations are no longer accepted.
  - The dominance of choppers and chopping tools over handaxes is the defining trait of the Soanian, long contrasted with the Acheulian handaxe traditions of peninsular India, though sites such as Ghumarwin where the two occur together have weakened that sharp division.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2 (the Soan valley and the de Terra-Paterson survey; Adiyala itself and the Ghumarwin comparison are from the author's notes).
