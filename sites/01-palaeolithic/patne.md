- **Location**: Patne, Jalgaon district, Maharashtra, in the Tapi valley.
- **Period/Level**: A stratigraphic sequence of Middle Palaeolithic, Upper Palaeolithic and Mesolithic tools; the ostrich eggshell from the Upper Palaeolithic level is dated to about 25,000 BP.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Patne is the single most important Indian site for Upper Palaeolithic art and personal ornament.
  - A piece of ostrich eggshell engraved with two panels of criss-cross designs was recovered here. Most patterned eggshell fragments from India turn out under the microscope to be naturally weathered; the Patne fragment is clearly engraved by human hands.
  - Four perforated beads and one incomplete bead of ostrich eggshell, about 10 mm in diameter, came from the Upper Palaeolithic level. Such beads are known from about 41 Indian sites in Pleistocene contexts ranging from 39,000 to 25,000 BP, and from Siberia, inner Mongolia, China and Africa, linking India to a wider Upper Palaeolithic world of bead-making.
  - A gradual reduction in tool size can be traced here from the late Pleistocene onwards, with microliths of that age, documenting the technological drift from the Upper Palaeolithic into the Mesolithic.
