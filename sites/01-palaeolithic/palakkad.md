- **Location**: Palakkad (Palghat) district, Kerala, at the Palghat gap, the major natural break in the Western Ghats linking the Tamil plains with the Kerala coast.
- **Period/Level**: Palaeolithic surface finds.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Choppers and scrapers made of quartz have been found in the district, among the few Palaeolithic finds reported from Kerala, where heavy rainfall and thick laterite cover make prehistoric sites scarce and hard to locate.
  - The Palghat gap itself explains the importance of the district, since it is the corridor through which people, and later trade and armies, moved between the Coromandel and Malabar coasts.
  - The fort at Palakkad, in its present form the work of Hyder Ali, later served as a base for operations directed at Srirangapatna.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; Thapar, *Early India*, on the Palghat Gap route (the fort is from the author's notes).
