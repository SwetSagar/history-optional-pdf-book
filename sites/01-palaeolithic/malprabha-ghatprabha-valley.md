- **Location**: The Malaprabha and Ghataprabha valleys, tributaries of the Krishna in the Belagavi and Bagalkot districts of northern Karnataka.
- **Period/Level**: A stratigraphic sequence of Lower and Upper Palaeolithic tools has been identified in these valleys.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the few stratified rather than surface Palaeolithic sequences in the Deccan, and the evidence that broke the old regional divide in Indian prehistory.
  - Acheulian handaxes and cleavers occur together with pebble tools such as choppers and chopping tools.
  - It was once believed that the Lower Palaeolithic industry of the south, then named "Madrasian", differed from that of the rest of the country because it supposedly lacked pebble tools. Research of the past few decades, here and in the Hunsgi-Baichbal and Krishna valleys, has shown this to be incorrect.
