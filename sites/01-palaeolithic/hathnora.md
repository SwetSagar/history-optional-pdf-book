- **Location**: Hathnora village, on the northern bank of the Narmada about 40 km north-east of Hoshangabad, Sehore district, Madhya Pradesh.
- **Period/Level**: Middle Pleistocene, in a late Acheulian to Middle Palaeolithic context; estimated dates for the hominid finds range between 0.5 and 0.2 million years ago.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only well-authenticated Pleistocene hominid fossil locality in India, which places the subcontinent within the global story of human evolution.
  - In 1982 Arun Sonakia of the Geological Survey of India found a fossilised cranial fragment embedded in thick, closely packed sandy, pebbly gravel, along with fossils of proboscideans and bovids and a few late Acheulian tools.
  - The skull fragment belonged to a woman of about 30. Sonakia named her Homo erectus narmadensis, treating her as an advanced variety of Homo erectus because of the larger cranial capacity range of 1155 to 1421 cc; other scholars regard the cranium as that of an early or archaic Homo sapiens, and its date remains disputed.
  - In 1997 A. R. Sankhyan reported a hominid clavicle from the same boulder conglomerate deposit, together with animal fossils and several middle or late Palaeolithic tools, following the Anthropological Survey of India's intensive search of the central Narmada valley between 1983 and 1992.
