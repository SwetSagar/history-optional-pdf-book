A Palaeolithic site on the northern bank of the Narmada in Madhya Pradesh, which yielded the hominid fossil named Homo erectus narmadensis, a female, together with late Acheulian tools.

- Cranial fragments, collar bones and animal bones of the Middle to Late Palaeolithic were recovered.
- Tools were made of limestone, sandstone, quartzite, dolerite and chert, and huge granite blocks were used to support temporary shelters.
- Subsistence rested on wild edible plants and wild cattle such as deer.
- At nearby Isampur there is evidence of Acheulian and Middle Palaeolithic occupation, with chopping tools, knives, handaxes, cleavers and flake tools; a specialised reddish-brown chert was used for hunting.
- The site served both for stone working and habitation, and the presence of non-indigenous stone points to a band society in contact with others.
