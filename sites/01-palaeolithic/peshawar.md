- **Location**: The Peshawar valley, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan; the principal site is the Sanghao cave in the hills of the adjoining Mardan district.
- **Period/Level**: Middle and Upper Palaeolithic; occupation is commonly cited at about 30,000 years ago, with radiocarbon dates from Sanghao ranging from 41,825 +/- 4,120 BCE to 20,660 +/- 360 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Sanghao provides the best stratified cave sequence for the Middle and Upper Palaeolithic in the north-west of the subcontinent.
  - The deposit is over 3 m thick and yielded thousands of stone tools, including chipped stone, scrapers, blades and flakes, almost all made of quartz that is easily available around the site.
  - Hearths were found along with bones of animals and some possibly of humans, and with what appear to be burials, pointing to organised and repeated cave habitation.
  - Peshawar, as ancient Purushapura, later became the Kushana capital, where Kanishka I is said to have enshrined the Buddha's relics in a stupa that became the centre of a major monastery.
