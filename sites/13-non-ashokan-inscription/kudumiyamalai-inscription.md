- **Location**: Kudumiyanmalai, Pudukkottai district, Tamil Nadu, in the dry country between the Kaveri delta and the Pandya lands; the inscription is cut on a rock face beside the Shiva cave temple.
- **Period/Level**: A musical inscription of about the 7th century CE, in Sanskrit in the Pallava-Grantha script, attributed to Mahendravarman I, r. c. 600-630 CE - though on script and style rather than a signature. The hill stayed in use under the later Cholas and Pandyas, who left inscriptions of their own.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The oldest treatise on Indian music surviving anywhere in the world - and a notation, not a description.
  - It is arranged in seven sections corresponding to seven parent scales, each giving sequences of svara syllables in groups of four - a systematic working through of permutations, in effect a teaching manual for students of music.
  - Its value is that it is a dated physical witness to the svara system from a period that has left no musical manuscripts at all, bridging Bharata's *Natyashastra* and the medieval treatises. Almost everything else known about early Indian music is theory transmitted through later copies; this is contemporary and fixed in stone.
  - The attribution to Mahendravarman I is plausible on other grounds: he wrote the farce *Mattavilasa Prahasana*, bore the titles Vichitrachitta and Chitrakarapuli, and is credited in the Mamandur inscription with work on music - a Pallava king who cultivated drama, painting and music together.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI, *South Indian Inscriptions*; *Pudukkottai State Inscriptions*.
