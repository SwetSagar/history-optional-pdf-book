- **Location**: Chandragiri, the smaller of the two hills at Shravanabelagola, Hassan district, Karnataka, in the Hemavati basin; Vindhyagiri, with the colossal Bahubali (Gommateshvara), stands opposite across the tank.
- **Period/Level**: More than five hundred inscriptions on Chandragiri alone, from about the 6th century CE to modern times - Ganga, Rashtrakuta, Hoysala and Vijayanagara - with the hill's floruit in the Western Ganga period, c. 9th-11th centuries. The Bhadrabahu and Chandragupta record belongs to an inscription of c. 600 CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest single body of Jaina inscriptions in India, and the epigraphic basis for the Digambara account of the great schism.
  - They record that Bhadrabahu, the last shrutakevalin, foresaw a twelve-year famine in Magadha and led a migration of Jaina monks south to Karnataka; a disciple who accompanied him, identified in later tradition with Chandragupta Maurya, performed *sallekhana* - ritual fasting to death - on this hill, at the spot shown as the Bhadrabahu cave.
  - That migration is the standard explanation for the split between the Digambaras, who followed Bhadrabahu and kept to nudity, and the Svetambaras, who stayed in Magadha under Sthulabhadra and convened the Pataliputra council to fix the canon.
  - The claim that the emperor abdicated and died here as a Jaina ascetic rests on a tradition first recorded some nine centuries after the event. Present it as tradition and weigh the evidence - that is what the question tests.
  - The hill carries Ganga-period basadis, among them the Chandragupta basadi and the Chamundaraya basadi of c. 982 CE, built by the minister who raised the Bahubali colossus opposite.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7 and 10; *Epigraphia Carnatica* II; Thapar, *Early India*.
