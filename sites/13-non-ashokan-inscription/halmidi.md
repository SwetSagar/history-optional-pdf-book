- **Location**: Halmidi, Belur taluk, Hassan district, Karnataka, in the upper Hemavati basin on the eastern flank of the Western Ghats.
- **Period/Level**: A stone pillar inscription of c. 450-500 CE, in the Kannada language and an early Kannada script, of the Kadamba period and referred to the reign of Kakusthavarman - the high point of Kadamba power at Banavasi. The original is now in the Government Museum, Bengaluru, with a replica at the site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest full-length inscription in the Kannada language - the point at which Kannada becomes a written language.
  - Sixteen lines on a small pillar record a grant of villages to a warrior named Vija Arasa (Vijaya) in recognition of valour in battle, made under Kadamba authority - a service grant, not a religious one, which makes it useful evidence for how land was used to reward military followers.
  - The text is Kannada but heavily Sanskritised, and it opens with a Sanskrit invocation to Vishnu, so it documents the transition itself: a regional language taking over the epigraphic function from Sanskrit and Prakrit while still borrowing their formulae.
  - It anchors the standard sequence of "earliest inscription in language X" - the Tamil-Brahmi cave records from about the 2nd century BCE, Halmidi for Kannada in the 5th century CE, and the later emergence of Telugu and Marathi.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9-10; *Epigraphia Carnatica*; D. C. Sircar, *Indian Epigraphy*.
