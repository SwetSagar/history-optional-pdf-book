- **Location**: Bhitari, Saidpur tehsil, Ghazipur district, Uttar Pradesh, in the tract between the Ganga and the Gomti east of Varanasi.
- **Period/Level**: A red sandstone pillar carrying a Sanskrit inscription of Skandagupta in Gupta Brahmi, c. 455-467 CE - the closing phase of Gupta power. A brick temple of cruciform plan with a curvilinear shikhara stands at the site, which also yielded a sealing of Kumaragupta; excavated by the ASI.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The record of the two crises that broke the Gupta empire - the Pushyamitra war and the first Huna invasion.
  - It gives the Gupta genealogy from Gupta and Ghatotkacha through Chandragupta I, Samudragupta, Chandragupta II and Kumaragupta to Skandagupta, and is a standard source for the dynastic sequence.
  - Skandagupta is said to have restored his family's fallen fortunes by defeating the Pushyamitras, and to have made the earth shake when he met the Hunas - the earliest Indian reference to a Huna conflict, a generation before they broke through under Toramana and Mihirakula. The verse describing how he then passed a night sleeping on the bare earth is the detail always quoted to show how close-run the defence was, and the evidence used to argue that Gupta decline began in his reign rather than after it.
  - It closes by recording his installation of a Vishnu image and the endowment of a village for its upkeep. Read it with Skandagupta's Junagadh inscription of 455-458 CE on the repair of the Sudarshana dam: the two give the military and administrative faces of one reign.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; J. F. Fleet, *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* III; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*.
