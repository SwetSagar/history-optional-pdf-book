- **Location**: Uthiramerur, Kanchipuram district, Tamil Nadu, in the Palar basin south-west of Kanchipuram - a planned *brahmadeya* village laid out on a grid, with temples at its cardinal points. The inscriptions are cut on the walls of the village sabha mandapa and the Vaikunta Perumal temple.
- **Period/Level**: Founded as a brahmadeya under the Pallavas in the 8th century; the earliest record is of Dantivarman, c. 795-846 CE. The two celebrated inscriptions are of the Chola Parantaka I, in his twelfth and fourteenth regnal years, c. 919 and 921 CE, in Tamil. Vijayanagara additions complete the complex.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The most detailed surviving account of village self-government in the ancient world - effectively the written constitution of the Chola village assembly, cut into a temple wall that served as the village's permanent statute book.
  - Parantaka's inscriptions set out the *kudavolai*, election by pot-ticket: the village was divided into thirty wards (kudumbus), each putting up candidates whose names went on palm-leaf tickets, into a pot, and were drawn out by a young boy - selection by lot from a pre-qualified pool, not open voting.
  - Qualifications are stated precisely: a quarter veli of tax-paying land, a house on one's own site, age between thirty-five and seventy, knowledge of a Veda with a bhashya. So are the disqualifications - anyone who had served on a committee in the previous three years without submitting his accounts was barred, with his close relatives. That accountability clause is the detail worth quoting.
  - Those elected formed the *variyams* - tottavariyam for gardens, eri-variyam for tanks, pon-variyam for gold - the machinery by which a brahmadeya ran irrigation, land and temple funds.
  - The second inscription amends the first, showing the scheme revised in light of practice; Dantivarman's earlier endowment shows the sabha already working under the Pallavas, so the Cholas codified an inherited institution.
  - The Vaikunta Perumal temple that carries the text is a modest Dravida structure dedicated to Vishnu with Lakshmi - the standard illustration of the south Indian temple as archive, treasury and civic centre rather than only a place of worship.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, *The Colas*; ASI, *South Indian Inscriptions* VI.
