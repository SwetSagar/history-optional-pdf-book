- **Location**: Banskhera, Shahjahanpur district, Uttar Pradesh, in the Rohilkhand plain between the Ganga and the Gomti, near the Garra.
- **Period/Level**: A copper-plate grant of Harshavardhana dated in his regnal year 22, c. 628 CE - the middle of his reign, 606-647 CE - in Sanskrit in the northern Brahmi of the day, and carrying the king's signature in his own hand.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of only two known charters of Harsha, and the rarest kind of document in Indian history - a royal autograph.
  - The plate records the grant of a village to two brahmanas, Bhatta Vatasvamin and Bhatta Shivadevasvamin, with its revenues and the usual exemptions. That makes it a primary document for the brahmadeya and for the land-grant economy whose spread is central to the debate over early Indian feudalism.
  - It gives the Pushyabhuti genealogy - Naravardhana, Rajyavardhana I, Adityavardhana, Prabhakaravardhana, Rajyavardhana II and Harsha - and records Rajyavardhana's victory over the ruler of Malava, which independently confirms and dates what Bana narrates in the *Harshacharita*. Epigraphy checking a court biography is exactly the use examiners want to see.
  - The charter closes with the words *sva-hasto mama*, "this is my own hand", followed by Harsha's signature, written by the king himself rather than by a scribe.
  - It is read alongside the Madhuban copper plate of the same reign, which records a comparable grant and repeats the genealogy.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; *Epigraphia Indica* IV.
