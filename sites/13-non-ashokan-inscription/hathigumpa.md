- **Location**: The Hathigumpha, or Elephant Cave, a natural cavern on Udayagiri hill about 6 km west of Bhubaneswar, Odisha, facing the Khandagiri caves; the Daya and the fortified city at Sisupalgarh lie close by.
- **Period/Level**: A seventeen-line inscription of Kharavela, king of Kalinga of the Mahameghavahana (Chedi) line, in Prakrit in Brahmi, generally assigned to the 1st century BCE - Kalinga's revival after Ashoka. The date is contested, argued between the 2nd century BCE and the 1st century CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The fullest royal autobiography from ancient India - a year-by-year account of a reign, with no parallel in the period.
  - It runs through the regnal years in order: year 1, repairing the gates and ramparts of Kalinganagara after a storm; year 2, an army sent west against the Satavahana Satakarni; year 5, extending a canal first cut by a Nanda king three hundred years earlier - the only epigraphic reference to Nanda rule in Kalinga, and the basis for dating the Nandas backwards from Kharavela; years 8 and 12, attacks on Magadha, forcing its king to submit and recovering the Jina image a Nanda had carried off.
  - He calls himself a worshipper of all sects but a Jaina by allegiance, and records excavating caves on the Kumari hill for Jaina monks - the Udayagiri and Khandagiri groups - which makes this the principal record of royal Jaina patronage in eastern India.
  - It also notes the rajasuya, remission of taxes, public entertainments, and his training as a prince in writing, coinage, accountancy and law.
  - The stone is damaged, and both the Magadhan king's name and the date are disputed readings - a useful illustration of how much early Indian chronology rests on contested epigraphy.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Early India*.
