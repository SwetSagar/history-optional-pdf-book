- **Location**: Aihole (ancient Ayyavole), Bagalkot district, Karnataka, on the left bank of the Malaprabha, with Badami and Pattadakal within 35 km - the first seat of the Early Chalukyas.
- **Period/Level**: Temple building spans c. 450-750 CE, the site's floruit under the Early Chalukyas of Badami. The Aihole Prashasti, cut on the east wall of the Meguti temple, was composed by Ravikirti, court poet of Pulakesin II, and dated Shaka 556 = 634 CE, in Sanskrit in old Kannada script.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The cradle of Indian temple architecture - the workshop in which Nagara and Dravida forms were tried out - carrying a dated inscription that fixes Deccan chronology.
  - The prashasti records Pulakesin II's victories over the Kadambas, Gangas, Malavas and Gurjaras, his capture of Vatapi, and his defeat of Harshavardhana on the Narmada, with Ravikirti's pun that Harsha's *harsha*, his joy, melted away in fear. It is the principal source for the one check Harsha's empire received.
  - Its double date, in the Shaka and Kali eras, is a fixed point of early Deccan dating; Ravikirti's comparison of himself to Kalidasa and Bharavi is the earliest datable reference to Kalidasa anywhere.
  - The Meguti temple, 634 CE, is the earliest dated structural temple in Karnataka. The Durga temple - named from *durga*, a fort, not the goddess - is apsidal with an ambulatory, a Nagara shikhara over a Dravida-derived mandapa; the flat-roofed Lad Khan derives from timber hall construction.
  - Aihole was home to the Ayyavole Five Hundred, a great south Indian merchant guild, making it a trade centre as well as a capital.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI.
