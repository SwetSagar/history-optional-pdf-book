- **Location**: Chausa, Buxar (formerly Bhojpur) district, Bihar, on the south bank of the Ganga near the Uttar Pradesh border - better known as the field where Sher Shah defeated Humayun in 1539.
- **Period/Level**: A hoard of eighteen Jaina bronzes found in 1931 during digging near the village, spanning roughly the 2nd to the 6th centuries CE - the earlier pieces Kushana in feeling, the later ones Gupta. Now in the Patna Museum.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest group of Jaina metal images in India, and the eastern counterpart to Akota.
  - Eighteen bronzes: Tirthankaras, yakshis, a makara and a kalpavriksha. The kalpavriksha and the dharmachakra are among the very few surviving Jaina cult objects that are not images of a Tirthankara, and show that the Jaina shrine held symbols as well as icons.
  - The physique is masculine and heavily modelled, and Adinatha (Rishabhanatha) is shown with long hair locks falling over the shoulders - the iconographic mark that distinguishes him from the other twenty-three Tirthankaras, here in one of its earliest datable appearances.
  - The earliest pieces stand close to Kushana Mathura in their modelling and the later ones show Gupta restraint, so the hoard records in bronze the same stylistic transition that Mathura records in stone.
  - It establishes that Jainism had a developed image cult in cast metal well before the Gupta period, against the older view that Jaina art begins with the medieval temple.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8-9; Patna Museum catalogue; ASI.
