- **Location**: Dhanesar Kheda, Banda district, Uttar Pradesh, in the Bundelkhand tract between the Yamuna and the Ken - a minor mound, not an excavated city.
- **Period/Level**: Bronze images of the Gupta and post-Gupta period, c. 5th-6th centuries CE, the principal one an inscribed standing Buddha. The site is known for this group of metal sculpture rather than for a stratified sequence.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: An inscribed Gupta bronze that doubles as a historical document for a region with no dynastic record of its own.
  - The Buddha stands in abhaya mudra, the right hand raised in reassurance. The sanghati is wrapped over both shoulders and gathered in the left hand, and the modelling is deliberately thin and clinging, so the cloth reads as transparent over the body - the Sarnath convention of the fifth century translated into metal, and cast by the lost-wax process.
  - The dedicatory inscription on the image records the gift by the wife of one Hariraja - a named lay woman as donor, evidence that Gupta-period metal images were commissioned privately and not only by rulers or monasteries.
  - Because it is inscribed, the image supplies names and a date for local Gupta-period history where no copper plates or coins survive; its value is epigraphic as much as artistic.
  - Read it against the 2.3 m Sultanganj Buddha for the scale Gupta bronze casting could reach, and against the Akota and Chausa hoards for the parallel Jaina tradition in metal.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; Vidya Dehejia, *Indian Art*; ASI.
