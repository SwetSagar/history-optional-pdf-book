- **Location**: Akota (ancient Ankottaka), a mound now inside Vadodara on the west bank of the Vishvamitri, Gujarat - on the route linking the Gujarat ports to Malwa and the Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: A hoard of sixty-eight Jaina bronzes recovered in 1951 when the mound was being levelled, ranging from the late 5th or 6th century to the 12th century CE - Maitraka, Gurjara-Pratihara and Chaulukya (Solanki) work. Published by U. P. Shah as *Akota Bronzes* (1959); the images are chiefly in the Baroda Museum.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest and earliest sequence of Jaina metal images in western India - the dated series against which all later Svetambara bronzes are measured.
  - Sixty-eight images of Tirthankaras with their attendant yakshas and yakshis, cast in brass and bronze by the lost-wax (cire perdue) method, many with silver and copper inlay for the eyes and the srivatsa mark on the chest.
  - The principal images are of Jivantasvami - Mahavira shown as a crowned prince before renunciation, standing in kayotsarga with dhoti, crown and ornaments - and of Parshvanatha under his serpent hood. The Jivantasvami type is rare in Indian art, and Akota has its earliest known examples.
  - Many pieces carry donative inscriptions naming the donor, which is why the group functions as a dated sequence rather than a stylistic guess; the donors are lay men and women and nuns, not kings.
  - With the Chausa bronzes it establishes that Jaina metal sculpture in the Gupta and post-Gupta centuries was a western and eastern Indian speciality funded by mercantile patronage - a tradition running parallel to, and independent of, the Buddhist bronzes.
  - *Sources*: U. P. Shah, *Akota Bronzes* (1959); Upinder Singh, ch. 9; ASI.
