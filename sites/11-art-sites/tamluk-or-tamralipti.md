- **Location**: Tamluk (ancient Tamralipti), Purba Medinipur district, West Bengal, on the west bank of the Rupnarayan near its meeting with the Hooghly - the great eastern seaport of early India, and the seaward end of the Uttarapatha.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated from 1954-55 by the ASI and Calcutta University. A Chalcolithic black-and-red ware level at the base; NBPW from c. 400 BCE; the port at its height from the Mauryan to the Gupta period, c. 300 BCE-600 CE; decline after the 8th century as the river silted and shifted.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The exit point of the Mauryan trade route to the south and south-east, and one of the two great terracotta centres of early Bengal.
  - Its terracottas date from the Mauryan period and are hand modelled, with dress and accessories added separately afterwards - a technique that precedes the single and multiple moulds used at Chandraketugarh, so the two sites together give the sequence from modelling to mass production.
  - Pre-Mauryan pieces represent mother goddesses and fertility figures; the later range covers yakshas, toys, animals, birds, erotic motifs, nagas, naginis and apsaras - a repertoire of popular cult rather than court art.
  - Roman Arretine ware, rouletted ware and red polished ware confirm the Indian Ocean trade, alongside coins, beads and semi-precious stones.
  - It was the port from which Ashoka's mission under Mahendra and Sanghamitra is said to have sailed for Sri Lanka; Faxian spent two years here copying texts before sailing for Sri Lanka, and Xuanzang describes it. By land it was tied to Taxila along the Uttarapatha - the road that crossed the subcontinent from the north-west frontier to the Bay of Bengal.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-9; Faxian, *Fo-kuo-chi*; ASI, *IAR*.
