- **Location**: Begram, Parwan province, Afghanistan, at the junction of the Ghorband and Panjshir rivers north of Kabul - the Kushana summer capital Kapisa, where the routes from China, India and the Roman west converged.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by the Delegation Archeologique Francaise en Afghanistan under Joseph Hackin and Roman Ghirshman, 1936-40. The sealed Begram hoard was found in 1937 and 1939 in two walled-up rooms of the palace and dates to the 1st-2nd centuries CE, the Kushana peak under Kanishka.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The richest single assemblage of long-distance trade goods from the ancient world - the Silk Road as an object rather than an argument.
  - The two rooms held, stacked side by side: Indian carved ivories and bone furniture fittings; Roman and Alexandrian bronzes, plaster medallions and painted, gilded and millefiori glass; Chinese lacquerware; and alabaster vessels. Three civilisations' luxury goods in one sealed deposit, datable to within a century.
  - The ivories are the largest surviving group of early Indian ivory carving anywhere. They were carved, smoothed and lightly polished, with red and blue pigment used for colour, and show women at the toilet, standing at gateways and under trees, in an idiom close to Mathura and Sanchi - proof that the Kushana empire carried Indian craft north, not only Indian religion.
  - Whether the rooms were a royal treasury sealed in an emergency or a merchant's bonded warehouse is still argued; either reading makes Kapisa an entrepot rather than a consumer.
  - Much of the collection was looted from the Kabul Museum after 1992; a part had been hidden and was recovered in 2003.
  - *Sources*: Joseph Hackin, *Recherches archeologiques a Begram* (1939, 1954); Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*.
