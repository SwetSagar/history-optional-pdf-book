- **Location**: Herat, western Afghanistan, on the Hari Rud in the province of Khurasan - the junction of the roads north to Merv and Bukhara, west into Iran, east to Balkh and Kabul, and south to Kandahar and the Indian passes.
- **Period/Level**: An ancient city, sacked by the Mongols in 1221, and restored as the capital of the Timurids under Shah Rukh and his queen Gauhar Shad in the 15th century. Its cultural peak, the Herat school, falls between about 1400 and 1507, when the Uzbeks took the city.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Timurid capital whose artistic tradition the Mughals brought to India - the aesthetic ancestor of Mughal painting.
  - The Herat atelier under Sultan Husain Bayqara, with Bihzad as its greatest master, brought Persian miniature painting to its height: individualised faces, controlled recession, and everyday incident within formal composition.
  - Humayun recruited two Herat-trained painters, Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd us-Samad, during his exile at the Safavid court and brought them to India. They founded the imperial atelier under Akbar, so the Mughal school begins as a Herati transplant before absorbing Indian colour and naturalism. The line from Herat to the *Hamzanama* and the *Akbarnama* is the point to make.
  - Babur was a Timurid prince who visited Herat and admired it, and his sense of inheritance - the *Baburnama*'s constant comparisons between Kabul, Herat and Hindustan - is why the Mughals called themselves Timurids rather than Mughals at all.
  - Gauhar Shad's musalla and minarets, and the Friday Mosque, are the surviving monuments of that period.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; Babur, *Baburnama*.
