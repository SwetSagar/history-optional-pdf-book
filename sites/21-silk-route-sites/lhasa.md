- **Location**: Lhasa, in the valley of the Kyi Chu, a tributary of the Brahmaputra (Yarlung Tsangpo), on the Tibetan plateau at about 3,650 m - the political and religious centre of Tibet.
- **Period/Level**: Made the capital by Srongtsen Gampo in the 7th century CE, who founded the Jokhang and the first structure on the Potala hill. Buddhism's "first diffusion" belongs to this period; the "second diffusion" follows Atisha Dipankara's arrival from India in 1042. The Potala as it stands is a 17th-century rebuilding under the Fifth Dalai Lama.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The destination of the Indian Buddhist transmission, and the reason much of Indian Buddhist literature survives at all.
  - Srongtsen Gampo sent Thonmi Sambhota to India to devise a script for Tibetan; the alphabet he brought back derives from an Indian Gupta-period script, so Tibetan writing is an Indian export. The king's Nepali and Chinese queens, Bhrikuti and Wencheng, are credited with bringing the images around which the Jokhang was built.
  - Atisha Dipankara Shrijnana, abbot of Vikramashila in Bihar, went to Tibet in 1042 and reformed its monastic Buddhism. The Bengal-to-Tibet connection under Pala patronage is the key link for questions on the later phase of Indian Buddhism.
  - Indian Buddhist texts were translated into Tibetan on a vast scale and collected as the *Kangyur* and *Tengyur*. A large number of works whose Sanskrit originals were lost after Buddhism declined in India survive only in these translations, so Tibetan sources are now primary evidence for Indian intellectual history.
  - The route through Nepal and the Chumbi valley also carried trade in wool, salt, borax and gold.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; Taranatha, *History of Buddhism in India*.
