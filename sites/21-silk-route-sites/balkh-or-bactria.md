- **Location**: Balkh, ancient Bactra, in northern Afghanistan on the Balkh river a little south of the Oxus (Amu Darya) - the oasis where the roads from Iran, the Central Asian steppe, China and India converge.
- **Period/Level**: An Achaemenid satrapal seat, then the core of the Greco-Bactrian kingdom from c. 250 BCE, the base from which the Yuezhi became the Kushanas, and later a great Buddhist and then Islamic centre. Destroyed by Genghis Khan in 1220 and never fully recovered. The Arabs called it *Umm al-Bilad*, the Mother of Cities.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The single most important crossroads on the northern route - the place where Greek, Iranian, Indian and Chinese worlds met.
  - The Greco-Bactrian kings, Diodotus, Euthydemus and Demetrius among them, ruled here before pushing into India as the Indo-Greeks. Their portrait coinage and Hellenistic sculptural conventions fed directly into Gandhara art, so Balkh stands behind the Buddha image itself.
  - The *Nava Vihara*, the great Buddhist monastery outside the city, was described by Xuanzang in the 7th century. Its hereditary abbots, after conversion, became the Barmakid family, viziers of the Abbasid caliphs at Baghdad - the channel through which Indian mathematics, astronomy and medicine reached the Arab world in the 8th-9th centuries.
  - Zoroastrian tradition places Zoroaster's death at Balkh, and the city was a major centre of that faith before Buddhism and Islam - three religions in succession at one site.
  - Rumi was born here in 1207, a few years before the Mongol destruction that drove his family west.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 8.
