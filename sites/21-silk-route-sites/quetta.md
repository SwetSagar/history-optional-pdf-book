- **Location**: Quetta, Balochistan, Pakistan, in a high valley ringed by mountains at the head of the Bolan pass - the second great gateway from the Iranian plateau into the Indus valley, after the Khyber.
- **Period/Level**: The Quetta valley carries one of the longest cultural sequences in South Asia. Kili Gul Mohammad and Damb Sadaat give a Neolithic-to-Chalcolithic succession from about the 5th millennium BCE, and Mehrgarh on the Kachi plain at the foot of the Bolan runs from about 7000 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The corridor through which farming, and later people and goods, entered the subcontinent.
  - Mehrgarh, excavated by Jean-Francois Jarrige, is the earliest farming settlement known in South Asia: mud-brick houses, domesticated wheat, barley, cattle, sheep and goat, and in its later levels cotton, the earliest anywhere. It shows agriculture developing locally in the Indus borderlands rather than arriving fully formed from West Asia - the central point in any question on the origins of the Harappan civilisation.
  - Mehrgarh has also produced drilled human molars, evidence of dentistry around 7000 BCE, and fine bead-work in turquoise and lapis that anticipates Harappan craft.
  - The Quetta valley sites give the ceramic sequence - Kili Gul Mohammad, Kechi Beg, Damb Sadaat wares - by which the Baluchistan Chalcolithic is dated and linked to Iranian and Central Asian material.
  - In historic times the Bolan carried the same traffic: Achaemenid and Greek armies, and the caravan trade between Kandahar and Sindh.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Jarrige, *Mehrgarh: Field Reports*.
