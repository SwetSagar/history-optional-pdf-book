- **Location**: Jaisalmer, western Rajasthan, on the Trikuta hill rising some 75 m above the Thar desert - built of the local yellow sandstone that turns gold at sunset, on the caravan track from Gujarat and Delhi towards Multan and Central Asia.
- **Period/Level**: Founded in 1156 CE by Rawal Jaisal of the Bhati Rajputs, who moved the seat from Lodurva. The fort was besieged by Alauddin Khalji in the early 14th century, with *jauhar* performed, and again under Firoz Shah Tughlaq. The Jain temples within the walls date from the 12th to the 15th centuries; the merchant *havelis* are 18th and 19th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A desert caravan city, one of the very few forts anywhere still inhabited as a town, and the keeper of one of India's most important manuscript collections.
  - Jaisalmer's wealth came entirely from transit. Caravans crossing between the Indian plains and the Indus carried opium, indigo, silk, spices and horses, and the Bhati rulers taxed them. When sea trade through Bombay and then the railways redirected that traffic in the 19th century, the town simply stopped growing - which is why its medieval fabric survives so completely.
  - Ninety-nine bastions ring the hill, and the defence depended on the Ghadisar tank and on the desert itself: an attacker had to bring water across the Thar, which is why sieges here were rare and short.
  - Roughly a quarter of the old town still lives inside the walls, with continuous drainage, wells and habitation rather than a preserved ruin. That is also its main conservation problem - modern water use has saturated the foundations and caused bastion collapses.
  - The Jain temples inside hold the *Jaisalmer Bhandar*, a library of palm-leaf and paper manuscripts preserved by the dry desert air, some from the 11th century, including texts that survive nowhere else. It is a principal source for medieval Indian literature and science.
  - The havelis, Patwon ki Haveli above all, document the Marwari commercial families whose networks later financed trade across northern India.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India*; ASI.
