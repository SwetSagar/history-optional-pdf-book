- **Location**: Barbarikon, the port at the mouth of the Indus in Sindh, Pakistan. The *Periplus* places it on the middle of seven mouths; it is generally sought in the delta east of modern Karachi, and is sometimes identified with the site later known as Debal.
- **Period/Level**: Active in the 1st century CE, when the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* describes it, under Indo-Scythian (Saka) and then Indo-Parthian rule, with the Kushanas controlling the hinterland shortly after.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The one port where the maritime trade of the Indian Ocean and the overland Silk Route demonstrably meet.
  - The *Periplus* says ships anchored at Barbarikon but cargo was taken upriver to the royal capital - Minnagara - for the king's inspection, a rare description of customs procedure. Imports listed are clothing, figured linens, topaz, coral, storax, frankincense, glass vessels, silver plate and wine.
  - The exports are what matter: *costus*, bdellium, *lycium*, nard, turquoise, lapis lazuli, Chinese pelts, cotton cloth, silk yarn and indigo. Turquoise comes from Khurasan, lapis from Badakhshan, silk from China - none of it Indian. The goods had come down the Indus from Bactria and beyond, so this is the documentary proof that the Central Asian caravan routes emptied into the Arabian Sea trade here.
  - That makes Barbarikon the standard answer to how the Silk Route connected to maritime commerce, rather than running only overland to the Mediterranean.
  - Note that the port is often loosely equated with modern Karachi. The two are near neighbours on the same delta but not the same place, and Karachi has its own separate entry as Krokola.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
