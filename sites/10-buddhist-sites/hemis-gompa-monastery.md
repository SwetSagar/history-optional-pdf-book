- **Location**: Hemis, Leh district, Ladakh, in a side gorge on the west bank of the Indus about 45 km south-east of Leh, hidden from the valley floor - which is why it survived raids that destroyed other gompas.
- **Period/Level**: A monastery existed on the site before the 11th century; the present establishment was founded in 1672 by King Sengge Namgyal of Ladakh for Stagsang Raspa Nawang Gyatso, and belongs to the Drukpa Kagyu lineage.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The wealthiest and largest monastery in Ladakh, and the standard example of royal patronage creating a monastic order's dominance.
  - Sengge Namgyal's endowment made the Drukpa Kagyu the state church of Ladakh; Hemis held revenue villages and controlled subsidiary gompas - monastic landholding as an instrument of Himalayan kingship.
  - It holds a copper-gilt Buddha, gold and silver stupas, and a thangka of Padmasambhava of several storeys, unfurled once every twelve years.
  - The Hemis Tse-chu, held on Padmasambhava's birth anniversary, is the best-known cham masked dance festival of the western Himalaya.
  - *Sources*: Luciano Petech, *The Kingdom of Ladakh*; Snellgrove and Skorupski, *The Cultural Heritage of Ladakh*.
