- **Location**: Sanghol (Uchcha Pind), Fatehgarh Sahib district, Punjab, on an old course of the Sutlej between the Sutlej and the Ghaggar - on the route from Mathura to Taxila.
- **Period/Level**: A multi-period mound with late Harappan, PGW and NBPW levels; its great phase is Kushana, 1st-2nd centuries CE, when the stupa and monastery were built. Excavated by the Punjab Department of Archaeology from 1968 under S. S. Talwar and R. S. Bisht, with the decisive discovery by G. B. Sharma in 1985.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest single hoard of Mathura sculpture ever found, and the proof that Mathura workshops exported finished stone hundreds of kilometres north.
  - In 1985 a pit beside the stupa yielded 117 carved railing pillars, crossbars and coping stones in Mathura's spotted red sandstone, evidently buried for safety - complete with yakshi and salabhanjika figures at the toilet, with mirrors, birds and trees, in the fully modelled sensuous Mathura idiom.
  - The stupa was built on a wheel-shaped plan with a bone relic casket at its centre, and a monastery stood beside it; Kushana inscriptions, pottery and coins were recovered from the complex.
  - Because the stone is Mathura sandstone, the find settles a long argument about whether provincial Kushana sculpture was locally made or imported - here it was quarried, carved and shipped from Mathura.
  - Xuanzang's She-to-tu-lu is usually identified with Sanghol.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; S. P. Gupta, *Kushana Sculptures from Sanghol* (1985); ASI, *IAR*.
