- **Location**: Jagaddala, Dhamoirhat upazila, Naogaon district, north-west Bangladesh - in Varendra, the Pala heartland between the Karatoya and the Mahananda.
- **Period/Level**: A Pala mahavihara founded by Ramapala, c. 1077-1120 CE, flourishing into the early 13th century; destroyed in the Ghurid raids of c. 1207. Excavated by the Department of Archaeology, Bangladesh, from 1996.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The last of the five great Pala mahaviharas to rise, and the bridge by which Vajrayana learning passed to Tibet.
  - It came into prominence as Nalanda and Vikramashila declined, and specialised in Vajrayana; Vibhutichandra, Danashila and Mokshakaragupta taught here, and Shakyashribhadra fled from here to Tibet in 1204, carrying texts that shaped the Tibetan canon.
  - Vidyakara compiled the *Subhashitaratnakosha*, the great Sanskrit verse anthology, at Jagaddala - the main literary product of the Pala monasteries.
  - Excavation has exposed a large brick monastery with cells around a courtyard and ornamental terracotta plaques, on the standard Pala plan shared with Somapura and Vikramashila.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Taranatha, *History of Buddhism in India*; Department of Archaeology, Bangladesh.
