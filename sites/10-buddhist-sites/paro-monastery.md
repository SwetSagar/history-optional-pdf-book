- **Location**: Paro Taktsang, the Tiger's Nest, in the Paro valley of western Bhutan, clinging to a granite cliff about 900 m above the valley floor at roughly 3,120 m.
- **Period/Level**: The Taktsang Senge Samdup cave is held to be where Padmasambhava meditated in the 8th century CE. The monastery around it was built in 1692 by Gyalse Tenzin Rabgye, the fourth Druk Desi; gutted by fire in 1998 and restored by 2005. Nyingma and Drukpa Kagyu.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The defining monument of Bhutanese Buddhism, and the eastern end of the Padmasambhava tradition that began in Indian Vajrayana.
  - Tradition holds that Padmasambhava - Guru Rinpoche, a tantric master associated with Uddiyana in the north-west of the subcontinent - flew here on the back of a tigress and meditated for three years, three months, three weeks, three days and three hours, subduing the local deities; this is the founding story of Buddhism in Bhutan.
  - The complex of four temples and residences is built into the rock face at several levels, connected by stairways cut in the cliff - a building whose whole design is an act of devotion to a cave.
  - Its importance for the syllabus is as evidence of the outward transmission of Indian Vajrayana into the Himalaya, alongside Tabo, Alchi and Tawang.
  - *Sources*: Michael Aris, *Bhutan: The Early History of a Himalayan Kingdom*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10 (for the Vajrayana background).
