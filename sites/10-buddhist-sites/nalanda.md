- **Location**: Bargaon, Nalanda district, Bihar, about 10 km north of Rajgir in the Magadhan plain - a mahavihara on the road between Rajagriha and Pataliputra.
- **Period/Level**: Founded by Kumaragupta I in the 5th century CE (c. 415-455), enlarged under Harshavardhana and the Palas, and functioning until it was sacked by Bakhtiyar Khalji c. 1199-1203 - some 700 years of continuous teaching. Identified by Cunningham in 1861 and excavated by the ASI, 1915-37 and 1974-82.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The first residential university in the world, and the institution that separates monastic learning from the individual-teacher tradition of Taxila.
  - Eleven monasteries and six brick temples stand in a planned row along a central alley, the monasteries built to a uniform plan of cells around a courtyard with a shrine opposite the entrance - deliberate architecture for an institution, not an accumulation of donations.
  - Temple Site 3, the Sariputta stupa, was rebuilt seven times, each casing enclosing the last, with stucco Buddha and bodhisattva figures in its corner towers - a stratigraphy of Gupta-to-Pala sculpture in one monument.
  - Seals reading "Sri-Nalanda-Mahavihariya-Arya-Bhikshu-Sanghasya" confirm the identification; the Nalanda copper plate of Devapala records a grant made at the request of the Shailendra king of Suvarnadvipa, evidence of international endowment.
  - Xuanzang studied here in the 630s under Shilabhadra and Yijing in 673-685; both describe an admissions examination at the gate, a curriculum of Mahayana with grammar, logic, medicine and Samkhya, and residential students. Dharmapala, Shantarakshita and Dharmakirti taught here; tradition also links Nagarjuna and Aryabhata to it. UNESCO World Heritage, 2016.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9-10; Xuanzang and Yijing's accounts; ASI, *Nalanda* excavation reports.
