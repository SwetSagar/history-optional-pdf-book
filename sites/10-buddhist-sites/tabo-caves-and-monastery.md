- **Location**: Tabo, Spiti valley, Lahaul and Spiti district, Himachal Pradesh, on the left bank of the Spiti river at about 3,280 m, with meditation caves cut into the cliff above the village.
- **Period/Level**: Founded in 996 CE by Yeshe-O, the king of Guge, with his teacher Rinchen Zangpo; renovated in 1042, the year Atisha is said to have visited. In continuous use since - the oldest Buddhist establishment in India and the Himalaya still functioning without a break. Gelugpa; ASI protected.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Called the Ajanta of the Himalayas - and the best-preserved monument of the second diffusion of Buddhism from India into Tibet.
  - Nine temples, twenty-three chortens and the monks' quarters are built entirely of mud, sand and timber, with no stone - the survival of 10th-century mud architecture in the cold desert is itself remarkable.
  - The Tsuglakhang, the assembly hall, holds thirty-three life-size clay stucco bodhisattvas projecting from the walls, arranged as a three-dimensional mandala of Vairochana, with murals of the 10th-11th centuries below them in a Kashmiri-derived style.
  - The caves above the monastery served as assembly halls and retreats for monks; traces of hearths, cut seats and grain storage survive in them, evidence of how the community actually lived.
  - The 14th Dalai Lama has conducted the Kalachakra initiation here (1983 and 1996) and has spoken of retiring to Tabo, which keeps the site a living institution rather than a monument.
  - *Sources*: Deborah Klimburg-Salter, *Tabo: A Lamp for the Kingdom* (1997); Tucci, *Indo-Tibetica*; ASI.
