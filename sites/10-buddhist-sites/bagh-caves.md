- **Location**: Bagh, Dhar district, Madhya Pradesh, cut into a sandstone hill on the left bank of the Baghani, a Narmada tributary, on the route between Malwa and the Gujarat coast.
- **Period/Level**: Nine rock-cut Buddhist caves of the late Gupta period, c. 5th-6th centuries CE, of which five survive; an inscription mentions Maharaja Subandhu, who ruled the region in the 5th-6th centuries. Contemporary with the second phase at Ajanta and abandoned by the 7th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best evidence outside Ajanta for Gupta-age mural painting, and the surviving link in the chain west to Gujarat.
  - Cave 4, the Rang Mahal, carried the principal murals - processions, dancers and musicians, a royal cavalcade - painted in tempera on a mud-plaster ground, technically identical to Ajanta but more secular in subject, with Jataka and Avadana scenes.
  - The caves are viharas with a stupa chamber at the rear rather than true chaityas, showing how the vihara absorbed the shrine in the later phase.
  - The rock is friable and the paintings decayed early; copies made in 1921-22 are preserved in the Gwalior museum and are now the main record of what was there.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; ASI; Marshall, Ghosh and others, *The Bagh Caves* (1927).
