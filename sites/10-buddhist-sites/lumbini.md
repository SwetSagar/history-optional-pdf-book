- **Location**: Lumbini, Rupandehi district, Nepal, in the Terai below the Churia hills near the Telar stream, between Kapilavastu and Devadaha.
- **Period/Level**: The birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, c. 563 BCE by the conventional reckoning. Ashoka visited in his twentieth regnal year, c. 249 BCE, and set up the Rummindei pillar. The pillar was found by A. Fuhrer and Khadga Shumsher in 1896; excavations by Debala Mitra, by the Japanese Buddhist Federation, and by Robin Coningham's team, which in 2013 reported a timber structure under the Mayadevi temple dated to the 6th century BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The first of the four great pilgrimage places, and the only Ashokan inscription that records a tax concession.
  - The Rummindei pillar inscription states that Ashoka came in person and worshipped because "here the Buddha Sakyamuni was born" (hida budhe jate sakyamuniti), and that he made the village of Lumbini free of bali and liable to only one-eighth of the produce as bhaga - the single most direct evidence we have of Mauryan revenue terms.
  - The pillar also fixes the site itself, and is the earliest surviving epigraphic confirmation that the Buddha was a historical person.
  - The Mayadevi temple, the marker stone said to locate the exact birthplace, the Puskarini tank and the surrounding monastic remains complete the complex. UNESCO World Heritage, 1997.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
