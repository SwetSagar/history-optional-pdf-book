- **Location**: Junnar, Pune district, Maharashtra, on the Kukadi river below the Naneghat pass - the gateway from the Konkan ports of Kalyan and Sopara to the Satavahana capital at Pratishthana.
- **Period/Level**: More than two hundred caves in five groups - Tulja Lena, Manmodi, Lenyadri (Ganesh Lena), Shivneri and Bhutling - cut between the 1st century BCE and the 3rd century CE, Hinayana, under the Satavahanas and the Kshatrapa Nahapana.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest concentration of rock-cut caves in India, and the clearest case of Buddhist establishments following a trade route rather than a capital.
  - Tulja Lena has a circular chaitya with a stupa ringed by twelve plain octagonal pillars - a domed circular plan found nowhere else in the western caves, and the rock-cut counterpart of the structural circular shrine at Bairat.
  - The inscriptions name the donors: Yavana (Greek-named) traders, the Kshatrapa Nahapana's officers, guilds of bamboo workers and braziers, and Saka settlers - a cross-section of the western Deccan's commercial society in the 1st-2nd centuries CE.
  - Lenyadri Cave 7 was converted into a Ganesha shrine and is now one of the Ashtavinayaka temples; Shivneri fort above is Shivaji's birthplace, so the hill carries three religious and political layers.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
