- **Location**: Kanheri, in the basalt hills of Salsette island inside Sanjay Gandhi National Park, Mumbai, Maharashtra - within reach of the ports of Sopara, Kalyan and Chaul.
- **Period/Level**: About 110 caves cut continuously from the 1st century BCE to the 10th century CE - the longest unbroken occupation of any Buddhist cave site in western India, spanning Satavahana, Kshatrapa, Traikutaka, Rashtrakuta and Shilahara patronage.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A complete Buddhist university-monastery in rock, and the one site where the full sequence Hinayana to Mahayana to Vajrayana is visible in a single complex.
  - Cave 3 is the great chaitya of the 2nd century CE, from the reign of Yajna Sri Satakarni, with thirty-four pillars and colossal standing Buddhas over 6 m at the entrance - the last of the great chaitya halls, and the point at which the Buddha image enters a form that had been aniconic.
  - Cave 41 has an eleven-headed Avalokiteshvara, unique in India and paralleled only in China and Japan; Cave 90 carries the Litany of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva saving devotees from eight perils - the mature Mahayana iconography.
  - Rock-cut cisterns, channels and a dam supplied the settlement, an engineering system as impressive as the sculpture; over a hundred inscriptions record gifts by merchants of Sopara and Kalyan, and later Pahlavi inscriptions of 1009 CE mark visits by Parsis.
  - A large gallery of memorial stupas over the ashes of resident monks lies on the hillside.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8 and 10; S. Gokhale, *Kanheri Inscriptions*; ASI.
