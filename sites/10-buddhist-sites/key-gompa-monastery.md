- **Location**: Key (Ki) Gompa, Spiti valley, Lahaul and Spiti district, Himachal Pradesh, on a conical hill at about 4,150 m above the Spiti river, 12 km from Kaza.
- **Period/Level**: Founded c. 1100 CE by Dromton, a pupil of Atisha, as a Kadampa monastery; it passed to the Gelugpa after the 14th century. Sacked repeatedly - by Mongol raiders in the 17th century, by Ladakhi and Mongol forces in the 1630s, by Zorawar Singh's Dogra army in 1841, and damaged by fire and by the 1975 earthquake.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest monastery in Spiti and its principal training centre for lamas, and a building whose form was dictated by warfare.
  - Its architecture is fort-like, a pasada of low boxes piled irregularly up the hill with windowless outer walls - the result of repeated sacking, and quite unlike the courtyard plan of Tabo below it in the same valley.
  - It preserves Buddhist manuscripts and thangkas, wall paintings and stucco images, and a collection of wind instruments used in cham performance; the murals show Chinese influence alongside Tibetan.
  - Its history is the political history of Spiti in miniature - Kadampa foundation, Gelugpa takeover, Mongol and Dogra raids - and is the usual example of how the western Himalayan monasteries changed sect with the fortunes of their patrons.
  - *Sources*: Snellgrove and Skorupski, *The Cultural Heritage of Ladakh*; ASI; Tucci, *Indo-Tibetica*.
