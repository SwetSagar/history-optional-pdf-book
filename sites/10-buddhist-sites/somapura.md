- **Location**: Paharpur, Badalgachhi, Naogaon district, Bangladesh, in ancient Varendra between the Karatoya and the Mahananda - Somapura Mahavihara.
- **Period/Level**: Built by the Pala king Dharmapala, c. 781-821 CE, whose name appears on seals from the site; the principal monastery of the Palas through the 9th-11th centuries, damaged in the 11th century and abandoned by the 13th. Excavated by K. N. Dikshit for the ASI, 1923-34. UNESCO World Heritage, 1985.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest Buddhist monastery south of the Himalayas, and the source of a building form that spread across South-East Asia.
  - A square of 281 m a side, with 177 monastic cells opening on to a courtyard, and at its centre a cruciform temple rising in three receding terraces to about 21 m - the monastery and the temple are a single designed composition, not a shrine added to a residence.
  - About 2,000 terracotta plaques and 63 stone sculptures are set into the terrace walls - Buddhas and bodhisattvas alongside Shiva, Krishna, Balarama, animals and scenes of village life, evidence of how thoroughly Pala Buddhism shared a visual world with its Hindu neighbours.
  - The cruciform terraced plan travelled: it is the acknowledged model behind the temples of Bagan in Myanmar, and is compared with Chandi Loro Jonggrang in Java and with the Cambodian temple mountains.
  - Atisha Dipankara Shrijnana, who took Buddhism to Tibet in 1042, was associated with Somapura, which links it to Vikramashila and Jagaddala in the same network.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; K. N. Dikshit, *Excavations at Paharpur* (ASI Memoir 55); UNESCO.
