- **Location**: Vala, Bhavnagar district, Gujarat, in eastern Saurashtra near the Gulf of Khambhat - a port-linked capital on the trade route from the western seaboard to Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Maitrakas, c. 475-775 CE, founded by Bhatarka, a general of the Guptas who made himself independent as the Gupta empire weakened. Destroyed in an Arab raid from Sindh c. 775 CE. Known principally from over a hundred Maitraka copper-plate grants.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The great centre of Hinayana learning, and the western counterweight to Mahayana Nalanda.
  - Its mahavihara taught the Sthaviravada and Sammitiya doctrines along with nitishastra (statecraft), law and philosophy, and drew Brahmana students as well as monks - graduates went into royal service, which is why it was funded. Gunamati and Sthiramati taught here.
  - Xuanzang visited c. 640 and Yijing in the later 7th century; both rank it with Nalanda, and Xuanzang counts some hundred monasteries with six thousand monks - the pairing Nalanda/Vallabhi is the standard contrast of Mahayana east and Hinayana west.
  - The second Jaina council of the Svetambaras met at Vallabhi under Devarddhi Kshamashramana, c. 453 or 466 CE, and there the Agamas were committed to writing - the moment the Svetambara canon became a fixed text.
  - The Maitraka copper plates are among the richest sources for land grants, administrative titles and religious endowment in western India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9-10; Xuanzang's account; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*.
