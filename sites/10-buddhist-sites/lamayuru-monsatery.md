- **Location**: Lamayuru (Yuru Gompa), Leh district, Ladakh, on a crag at about 3,510 m on the Srinagar-Leh road, above the eroded lake-bed badlands known as the moonland.
- **Period/Level**: One of the oldest and largest gompas in Ladakh, founded in the 11th century in the period of Rinchen Zangpo, with the Naropa cave at its core; originally Kadampa, and now Drikung Kagyu.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The western Himalayan monastery that best preserves the memory of the "second diffusion" of Buddhism from India into Tibet.
  - The oldest surviving building, the Seng-ge-sgang, is attributed to Rinchen Zangpo, the translator sent by the Guge kings to bring Indian texts and craftsmen north - the channel through which late Indian Buddhism entered Tibet as Indian Buddhism itself was ending.
  - The cave where the siddha Naropa is said to have meditated is enclosed within the complex; Naropa, a scholar of Nalanda and Vikramashila, is the link between the Pala mahaviharas and the Kagyu lineage.
  - It holds thangkas, murals and a large library, and hosts the Yuru Kabgyat masked dance festival.
  - *Sources*: Snellgrove and Skorupski, *The Cultural Heritage of Ladakh*; Luciano Petech, *The Kingdom of Ladakh*.
