- **Location**: Bharhut, Satna district, Madhya Pradesh, in the Baghelkhand uplands on the old road from Kaushambi towards the Deccan, midway between the Ganga plain and Vidisha-Sanchi.
- **Period/Level**: A brick stupa of the Mauryan period, probably Ashokan in origin. The stone railing (vedika) and gateways are Shunga, c. 125-100 BCE; an inscription on the eastern torana records its erection during the rule of the Shungas by Vatsiputra Dhanabhuti. Discovered by Alexander Cunningham in 1873.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest extensive narrative relief sculpture in India, and the only one that labels its own scenes.
  - Jataka and life-of-the-Buddha scenes are carved on the railing medallions in shallow, flat, frontal relief, each with a Brahmi label naming the story - which is how the Jataka repertoire of early Buddhist art was first identified at all.
  - The Buddha is never shown in human form: he is indicated by the empty throne, the footprints, the bodhi tree, the wheel and the stupa. Bharhut is the standard example of aniconic representation.
  - Large yaksha and yakshi figures stand on the railing uprights - Chulakoka Devata, Sudarshana, Sirima Devata - folk deities absorbed into a Buddhist monument, and among the first monumental stone figures of Indian art.
  - Donative inscriptions record gifts by monks, nuns, merchants and villagers rather than by a king - collective, non-royal patronage. Most of the remains are now in the Indian Museum, Kolkata.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Cunningham, *The Stupa of Bharhut* (1879); Basham, *The Wonder That Was India*.
