- **Location**: Karla, near Lonavala in Pune district, Maharashtra, on the Bhor Ghat above the Indrayani valley, a few kilometres from Bhaja - on the same Konkan-to-Deccan trade road.
- **Period/Level**: Developed from the 2nd century BCE to the 5th century CE. The Great Chaitya (Cave 8) is c. 50-120 CE, in the period of the Kshatrapa Nahapana and Gautamiputra Satakarni; associated with the Mahasamghika sect, and taken over for Hindu worship in later centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest and most perfectly finished rock-cut chaitya hall in India - the form at its height, just before the Buddha image displaces the stupa.
  - The hall is about 45 m long and 14 m high, with thirty-seven octagonal pillars carrying bell-shaped Persepolitan capitals surmounted by kneeling elephants ridden by mithuna couples; the original wooden ribbed vault and the wooden umbrella over the stupa survive, an extraordinary case of timber lasting two thousand years.
  - The facade carries a great horseshoe sun window and panels of donor couples, the most monumental figure sculpture in the western caves; a free-standing lion pillar stands in front.
  - The dedicatory inscription names Bhutapala, a setthi of Vaijayanti (Banavasi), and calls it the most excellent rock mansion in Jambudvipa. Other inscriptions record Nahapana's gifts and Gautamiputra Satakarni's grant of the village of Karajika to the monks, and name Yavana donors from Dhenukakata.
  - The alternation of Nahapana's and Gautamiputra's donations here is primary evidence for the Satavahana-Kshatrapa struggle over the western Deccan.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
