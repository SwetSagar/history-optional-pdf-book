- **Location**: Kesariya, East Champaran district, Bihar, on an old bank of the Gandak (Narayani), on the ancient road from Vaishali to Kushinagar.
- **Period/Level**: A stupa with a possibly Mauryan core, its surviving terraced structure built and rebuilt between about the 2nd and the 8th centuries CE, in the Gupta and Pala periods. Reported by Cunningham in 1861-62 and excavated by the ASI under K. K. Muhammed from 1998.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The tallest Buddhist stupa in India, and the closest Indian parallel to the terraced mandala stupas of South-East Asia.
  - It stands about 31.5 m high on a base roughly 400 ft across, rising in polygonal terraces ringed with niches holding life-size Buddha images in bhumisparsha mudra - a plan compared with Borobudur, which it precedes.
  - Tradition holds that the Buddha, on his last journey, gave his alms bowl to the Licchavis here when they would not let him go on, and that they raised a stupa on the spot; Xuanzang notes a stupa at this stage of the route.
  - It shows the Pala-period transformation of the stupa from a solid relic mound into an ascending image-bearing structure, the architectural expression of the bodhisattva path.
  - *Sources*: ASI, *IAR*; Cunningham, *ASI Reports* vol. I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
