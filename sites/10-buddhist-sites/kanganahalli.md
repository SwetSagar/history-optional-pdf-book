- **Location**: Kanganahalli, near Sannati village, Chitapur taluk, Kalaburagi (Gulbarga) district, Karnataka, on the left bank of the Bhima - a bend of the river in the northern Karnataka plain.
- **Period/Level**: A mahastupa, called the Adholoka Maha Chaitya in its inscriptions, built c. 3rd century BCE and encased with carved limestone slabs in the Satavahana period, 1st century BCE-3rd century CE. Excavated by the ASI under K. P. Poonacha, 1994-98.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only known portrait of Ashoka, and a royal gallery of the Satavahana dynasty in stone.
  - A limestone slab shows a king under a parasol flanked by female attendants, labelled in Brahmi "Ranyo Ashoka" - "King Ashoka". No other representation of Ashoka identified by name exists anywhere.
  - Other slabs carry labelled portraits of Satavahana rulers including Simuka and Pulumavi, making the site a primary source for the dynasty's own self-representation rather than a later account of it.
  - About sixty carved drum and dome slabs survive, with Jataka scenes, stupa worship panels and both aniconic and iconic Buddhas, in a limestone idiom close to Amaravati.
  - Satavahana lead coins and donative inscriptions were recovered; the Ashokan Major Rock Edicts and both Separate Edicts found at Sannati, 3 km away, tie the complex to the Mauryan south.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-8; K. P. Poonacha, *Excavations at Kanaganahalli* (ASI Memoir, 2011).
