- **Location**: Bhaja, near Lonavala in Pune district, Maharashtra, cut into a spur above the Indrayani valley beside the Bhor Ghat - the pass carrying goods from the Konkan ports of Kalyan and Sopara up to Junnar and Paithan.
- **Period/Level**: Twenty-two caves of c. 2nd-1st centuries BCE, Hinayana, under the Satavahanas - among the earliest rock-cut Buddhist architecture in India. Later additions run to the 5th century CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest surviving chaitya hall that still keeps its wooden fittings - the direct transcription of a timber building into rock.
  - Cave 12 is an apsidal chaitya griha with a horseshoe-arched sun window, twenty-seven plain octagonal pillars raked inward as timber posts would be, and actual wooden ribs set into the vault, with a wooden umbrella surviving over the stupa. Nothing shows the timber origins of the form more plainly.
  - A group of fourteen stupas, five inside and nine outside, are funerary memorials to resident monks; several carry inscriptions naming the theras commemorated - among the earliest evidence for the cult of the monastic dead.
  - The reliefs in Cave 19 - a figure on an elephant and another driving a four-horse chariot over a demon, usually read as Indra and Surya - are the earliest large narrative sculptures in the Deccan caves.
  - Patronage is recorded from merchants and householders, not kings: the caves sit on a trade route, and the donors are the people who used it.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
