- **Location**: Mahasthangarh, Bogura (Bogra) district, Bangladesh, on the west bank of the Karatoya - ancient Pundranagara, capital of Pundravardhana and the oldest urban site in Bangladesh.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by K. N. Dikshit from 1928-31 and since 1993 by a Bangladesh-France mission. Occupation runs from the 3rd century BCE (Mauryan, with NBPW) to the 18th century CE, through Shunga, Gupta, Pala and Sena levels.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The eastern limit of Mauryan administration on the ground, proved by an inscription rather than inferred from a map.
  - The Mahasthan stone plaque inscription, Prakrit in Brahmi of about the 3rd century BCE, is the earliest epigraph found in Bangladesh. It is an order to the mahamatra stationed at Pundranagara directing that people in distress be relieved by distributing paddy (dhanya) from the state granary (koshthagara) and by a loan in gandaka coins, the stock to be replenished afterwards.
  - That single text gives us a Mauryan provincial officer, a state granary, famine relief as an administrative duty and a coined loan - the best independent check on the Arthashastra's account of famine policy.
  - The citadel is a rampart-enclosed rectangle roughly 1.5 by 1.4 km rising 5 m above the plain, with suburban mounds around it including Govinda Bhita temple and Vasu Vihara.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Department of Archaeology, Bangladesh.
