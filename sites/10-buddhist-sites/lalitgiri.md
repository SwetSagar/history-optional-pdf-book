- **Location**: Lalitgiri, Cuttack district, Odisha, on the Birupa river in the Assia range - with Ratnagiri and Udayagiri it forms the "Diamond Triangle" of Odishan Buddhism.
- **Period/Level**: The earliest Buddhist complex in Odisha, occupied from about the 1st century BCE to the 13th century CE, with Kushana-period Brahmi inscriptions in its lowest levels. Excavated by the ASI, 1985-92.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The rare case of an excavated stupa yielding what are claimed as corporeal relics of the Buddha.
  - The hilltop mahastupa produced a khondalite stone casket containing a steatite box, which held a silver casket and inside that a gold casket with bone relics - one of only a handful of intact multiple-casket deposits found in India.
  - A large brick apsidal chaitya-griha and four monasteries have been exposed, with colossal standing Buddha images and Mahayana and Vajrayana sculpture - Tara, Avalokiteshvara, Jambhala - showing the Odishan transition from Mahayana to Tantric Buddhism under the Bhaumakaras and Somavamshis.
  - The complex is one of the candidates for the Pushpagiri mahavihara that Xuanzang describes in Odra, though the identification is unsettled.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI, *IAR* 1985-92; Debala Mitra, *Buddhist Monuments*.
