- **Location**: Ghantasala, Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh, near the lower Krishna a short way from the coast - Ptolemy's port of Kantakossyla, on the sea route to South-East Asia.
- **Period/Level**: A Satavahana and Ikshvaku Buddhist centre, c. 2nd century BCE-3rd century CE. Reported by Boswell in 1870, examined by Alexander Rea and excavated by Gabriel Jouveau-Dubreuil in 1919-20.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A stupa of unique plan, and the clearest link between Andhra Buddhism and the maritime trade that paid for it.
  - The mahastupa is built of solid brick with a wheel-shaped internal plan - radiating spoke walls dividing the drum into compartments filled with earth - a structural device found in the Andhra stupas and not in the north; a separate vihara stood beside it.
  - A carved slab with the twelve zodiac signs was recovered here, evidence of Hellenistic astronomical ideas reaching the Andhra coast, along with relics including a lotus, limestone sculpture in the Amaravati manner, and Roman coins.
  - As a port it exported the Buddhism of the Krishna valley eastwards; the Andhra stupa plan reappears in South-East Asia.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Jouveau-Dubreuil, *L'archeologie du sud de l'Inde*; ASI.
