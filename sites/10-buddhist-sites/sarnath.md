- **Location**: Sarnath - ancient Isipatana, the Migadaya or Deer Park - 10 km north-east of Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, near the meeting of the Varuna with the Ganga.
- **Period/Level**: The site of the first sermon, c. 528 BCE. Ashoka built the Dharmarajika stupa and the pillar with the lion capital in the 3rd century BCE; the Dhamekh stupa in its present form is Gupta, c. 500 CE, as is the Sarnath school of sculpture; the monasteries were destroyed by Qutb-ud-din Aibak's forces in 1194. Excavated by Jagat Singh and Duncan (1794), Cunningham (1835), F. O. Oertel (1904-05, who found the lion capital), and Marshall, Hargreaves and Daya Ram Sahni after.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Where the Sangha came into being, and where the two most reproduced objects in modern India were made.
  - The Buddha preached the Dhammachakkappavattana Sutta to the five ascetics here - the Middle Path, the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path - and the ordination of Kondanna and his companions founded the Sangha, the third of the three jewels.
  - Ashoka's lion capital in polished Chunar sandstone - four addorsed lions on an abacus carrying lion, elephant, bull and horse separated by wheels, over an inverted lotus - is India's state emblem, and its dharmachakra is on the national flag. The shaft carries the Schism Edict, warning that any monk or nun who splits the Sangha will be made to wear white and expelled, evidence of Ashoka's intervention in monastic discipline.
  - The Dhamekh stupa, 43.6 m of brick with a carved stone drum of Gupta floral and geometric ornament, marks the spot of the sermon; the Chaukhandi stupa nearby marks the meeting with the five disciples and carries a Mughal octagonal tower of 1588.
  - The Sarnath school of the 5th century CE - the seated Buddha preaching the first sermon, in cream Chunar sandstone with a plain transparent robe and no folds - is the classic Gupta Buddha, and the deliberate contrast to Mathura's heavy pleated drapery.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7 and 9; Daya Ram Sahni, *Catalogue of the Museum of Archaeology at Sarnath*; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
