- **Location**: The Bamiyan valley in the Hazarajat of central Afghanistan, at 2,500 m on the Bamiyan river in a pass of the Hindu Kush, on the Silk Road link between Balkh, Gandhara and the Tarim basin.
- **Period/Level**: A monastic complex of the 3rd-8th centuries CE under Kushana and Hephthalite patronage. The two colossal Buddhas carved into the cliff are radiocarbon-dated to c. 544-595 CE (the 38 m eastern figure) and c. 591-644 CE (the 55 m western figure). They were destroyed by the Taliban in March 2001.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest standing Buddha images ever carved, and the meeting point of Gandharan, Sasanian and Gupta form.
  - The figures were cut from the conglomerate cliff, their robes modelled in mud-and-straw stucco over wooden armatures and painted; the folds follow Gandharan convention but the scale and the niche setting are local.
  - About fifty caves survive around them, twelve with wall paintings - and analysis after 2001 showed some used oil-based binders in the 7th century, the earliest known oil painting anywhere, made by or for travellers on the silk road.
  - Xuanzang visited c. 630 CE, described the two figures and a third, reclining Buddha of some 300 feet not yet found, and recorded several thousand monks of the Lokottaravada school.
  - *Sources*: Xuanzang, *Da Tang Xiyu Ji*; UNESCO World Heritage documentation (2003); Upinder Singh, ch. 8.
