- **Location**: Antichak, Bhagalpur district, Bihar, on a bluff above the right bank of the Ganga near Kahalgaon - sited on the river so that it commanded the route into Bengal.
- **Period/Level**: Founded by the Pala king Dharmapala, c. 783-820 CE, and functioning until destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khalji c. 1203. Excavated jointly by Patna University under B. P. Sinha and by the ASI, 1960-69 and 1972-82.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The leading centre of Vajrayana learning in India, and the source from which Buddhism was re-established in Tibet.
  - The plan is a square monastery of 208 cells around a courtyard, with a cruciform brick stupa on a terraced plinth at the centre, faced with terracotta plaques - the same Pala scheme as Somapura, and the fullest excavated example in India.
  - Its organisation is recorded in Tibetan sources: six colleges each with its own gate, a dvarapandita or gate-scholar appointed to examine entrants at each, and a mahasthavira over all - the most formally structured of the mahaviharas.
  - Atisha Dipankara Shrijnana was its abbot before leaving for Tibet in 1041 at the invitation of the Guge king - the single most consequential export of Indian Buddhism, and the foundation of the Kadampa and later Gelugpa lines. Ratnakarashanti, Jnanapada and Jetari also taught here.
  - A separate library building with a cooling water channel was identified in excavation, along with a tank; the destruction of Vikramashila and Nalanda within a few years effectively ends organised Buddhism in India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Taranatha, *History of Buddhism in India*; ASI, *IAR* 1972-82.
