- **Location**: Belum, Kolimigundla mandal, Nandyal (formerly Kurnool) district, Andhra Pradesh - a limestone cave system in the Erramala hills of the Nallamala country, dissolved out by an underground stream of the Chitravati drainage.
- **Period/Level**: Used by Jaina and Buddhist monks in the early centuries BCE-CE; vessels recovered from the caves have been assigned to around 4500 BCE. Brought to notice by the British geologist H. B. Foote in 1884 and surveyed by a German team under H. D. Gebauer in 1982-84.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The second longest natural cave system in India, at 3,229 m, and a rare instance of a karst cavern taken over as a monastic retreat.
  - Buddhist relics and earthen vessels were found in the chambers, now in the museum at Anantapur; the caves have stalactite and stalagmite formations throughout, with a "meditation hall" (Dhyan Mandir) named from the seats cut near the entrance.
  - It illustrates the pattern of the Andhra Buddhist landscape, where natural caves and hill sites near the Krishna-Penna corridor housed monks before masonry viharas were built.
  - *Sources*: ASI; Geological Survey of India records; Upinder Singh, ch. 8 (for the Andhra Buddhist context).
