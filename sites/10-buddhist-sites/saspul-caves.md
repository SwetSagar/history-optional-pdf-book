- **Location**: Saspol (Saspochhe) caves, Leh district, Ladakh, cut into a cliff on the right bank of the Indus opposite Alchi, above the village of Saspol.
- **Period/Level**: Five man-made caves painted in the 13th-15th centuries CE, in the period after the Guge kingdom and during the rise of the Ladakhi Namgyal house.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A fusion of the Indian-Kashmiri manner of Alchi with the Tibetan style that replaced it - the transition caught mid-way.
  - The walls were plastered with clay and painted in bright mineral colours, with mandalas, Shakyamuni, Avalokiteshvara, Padmasambhava and the wheel of existence, along with small miniature-scale panels.
  - The caves were meditation retreats rather than a monastery, so they preserve a private rather than an institutional iconography - unusual survival for the western Himalaya.
  - Placed on the World Monuments Watch because of severe deterioration; documentation rather than excavation is the work being done here.
  - *Sources*: Snellgrove and Skorupski, *The Cultural Heritage of Ladakh*; World Monuments Fund documentation.
