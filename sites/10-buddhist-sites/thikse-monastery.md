- **Location**: Thiksey, Leh district, Ladakh, on a hill on the right bank of the Indus about 19 km south-east of Leh at 3,600 m.
- **Period/Level**: Founded in the 15th century, c. 1430, by Palden Sherab Zangpo, in the line of Tsongkhapa's disciples; Gelugpa. The largest gompa in central Ladakh.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The clearest surviving example of Tibetan tiered monastic architecture in India, and the seat of the Gelugpa in Ladakh.
  - Twelve storeys of white-washed buildings climb the hill in ascending rank, monks' quarters below and temples at the summit - a plan modelled on the Potala at Lhasa, and the standard illustration of how Tibetan monastic hierarchy is expressed in building.
  - The Maitreya (Chamba) temple, built in 1970 to mark the fourteenth Dalai Lama's visit, holds a 15 m seated Maitreya occupying two storeys - the largest such image in Ladakh.
  - It holds stupas, thangkas, wall paintings, swords and an assembly hall with a Tara temple of twenty-one images; the Gustor festival with its cham dances is held annually.
  - *Sources*: Snellgrove and Skorupski, *The Cultural Heritage of Ladakh*; Petech, *The Kingdom of Ladakh*.
