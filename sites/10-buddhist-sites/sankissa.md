- **Location**: Sankisa Basantapur, Farrukhabad district, Uttar Pradesh, on the Kali Nadi - the ancient Ikshumati - in the central Ganga-Yamuna doab.
- **Period/Level**: An early historic city mound with NBPW, occupied from about the 6th century BCE. The Ashokan pillar is 3rd century BCE. Identified by Alexander Cunningham in 1842 on the strength of Faxian's and Xuanzang's itineraries; the mound itself remains largely unexcavated.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the eight great places of Buddhist pilgrimage, and the only Ashokan pillar to carry an elephant capital.
  - The site marks the Buddha's descent from the Trayastrimsa heaven, where he had gone to preach the Abhidhamma to his mother Maya, returning by a triple ladder with Brahma and Indra at his sides - the "Descent at Sankissa", one of the eight great events (ashtamahapratiharya) of Buddhist art.
  - The surviving Ashokan capital is a seated elephant on a bell-shaped lotus; the shaft has gone and no inscription survives, but it is the only elephant capital still standing at its pillar site.
  - Faxian and Xuanzang both describe the ladders and the monasteries; today a Bisari Devi temple stands on the stupa mound, a common instance of a Buddhist site being absorbed into local Hindu worship.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; Cunningham, *The Ancient Geography of India*; Faxian and Xuanzang's accounts.
