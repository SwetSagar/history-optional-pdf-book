- **Location**: Rangiri Dambulla, Matale district, central Sri Lanka, on a 160 m granite outcrop overlooking the dry-zone plain, near the ancient road between Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa.
- **Period/Level**: The caves were monastic shelters with drip-ledges and Brahmi inscriptions from the 3rd-2nd centuries BCE. King Valagamba (Vattagamani Abhaya) sheltered here for fourteen years and, on regaining Anuradhapura in the 1st century BCE, converted them into a temple. Enlarged and gilded by Nissanka Malla in the 12th century; the surviving paintings are largely Kandyan, 18th century, under Kirti Sri Rajasinha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest and best-preserved cave temple complex in Sri Lanka, and a continuous record of Theravada image-making over two millennia.
  - Eighty documented caves, five of them developed as shrines, holding 153 Buddha statues along with images of Sri Lankan kings, Vishnu and Saman - the absorption of Hindu deities into a Theravada setting.
  - About 2,100 sq m of painted ceiling and wall, following the rock's natural contour; the 14 m reclining Buddha in Cave 1 is cut from the living rock.
  - Read with Sigiriya and Ajanta, it completes the picture of a shared painting tradition across the Bay of Bengal. UNESCO World Heritage, 1991.
  - *Sources*: UNESCO World Heritage documentation; Senake Bandaranayake, *Sinhalese Monastic Architecture*; the *Mahavamsa*.
