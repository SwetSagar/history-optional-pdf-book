- **Location**: Bodh Gaya, Gaya district, Bihar, on the west bank of the Niranjana (Phalgu/Lilajan) - the ancient Uruvela, where Sujata is said to have offered the milk-rice.
- **Period/Level**: Ashoka built the Vajrasana (diamond throne) and a shrine around the bodhi tree in the 3rd century BCE - a structure depicted in a Bharhut relief. The stone railing is Shunga, 1st century BCE. The present Mahabodhi temple is Gupta, 5th-6th centuries CE, with Pala and Burmese repairs and a restoration by Cunningham and Beglar in the 1880s.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The place of the enlightenment - the first of the four great pilgrimage sites, and the oldest brick temple in India still in use.
  - The Mahabodhi temple rises about 55 m in a straight-edged pyramidal shikhara over a high plinth, with four small corner towers - a form with no close parallel in India, and the model copied at Bagan, Chiang Mai and Beijing.
  - Ashoka's Vajrasana slab, polished sandstone with a palmette and geese frieze, survives between the temple and the bodhi tree - one of the very few Mauryan objects still in its original position.
  - The Shunga railing pillars carry medallions of yakshas, Surya in a chariot and Lakshmi, in the same flat idiom as Bharhut; much of the original is in the site museum and in Kolkata.
  - Faxian and Xuanzang both describe the site; the Sinhalese king Meghavarna built a monastery here in the 4th century with Samudragupta's leave, and later Pala kings and Burmese missions repaired it. UNESCO World Heritage, 2002.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-9; Cunningham, *Mahabodhi* (1892); Thapar, *Early India*.
