- **Location**: Junagadh, Gujarat, at the foot of the Girnar hills in Saurashtra, on the route from the Kathiawar ports to Malwa - three separate cave groups plus the inscribed rock on the Girnar road.
- **Period/Level**: Khapra Kodiya, cut into living rock near the Sudarshana lake, is the oldest, Mauryan, c. 3rd century BCE. Baba Pyara comprises thirteen caves of the 1st-2nd centuries CE, Buddhist with later Jaina use. The Uparkot caves, three storeys with a pillared court and cistern, are 2nd-3rd centuries CE, Kshatrapa.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The caves are Gujarat's Buddhist record; the rock beside them carries three inscriptions that between them span seven centuries of Indian history on one surface.
  - The Girnar rock holds Ashoka's fourteen Major Rock Edicts; Rudradaman I's Sanskrit inscription of c. 150 CE; and Skandagupta's of 455-458 CE - the only place where Mauryan, Shaka-Kshatrapa and Gupta records stand together.
  - Rudradaman's record is the first long inscription in Sanskrit and in ornate kavya prose, and it states that the Sudarshana reservoir was built by Pushyagupta, provincial governor of Chandragupta Maurya, and provided with conduits by Tushaspha the Yavana under Ashoka - our best evidence for Mauryan provincial administration and public works.
  - The Uparkot caves are unusual for their carved pillars with Kshatrapa-period motifs and an elaborate water-management system, showing Buddhist monasticism in a region better known for its Jaina establishments on Girnar above.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-8; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI.
