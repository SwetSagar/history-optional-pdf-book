- **Location**: Sankaram, comprising the twin hills of Bojjannakonda and Lingalakonda, near Anakapalli in Visakhapatnam district, Andhra Pradesh, a few kilometres inland from the coast on the Sarada river.
- **Period/Level**: Occupied from about the 3rd century BCE to the 9th century CE, with the main rock-cutting of the Satavahana to Vishnukundin period, c. 1st-7th centuries CE. Excavated by Alexander Rea for the ASI in 1907.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the few sites where all three phases of Buddhism - Hinayana, Mahayana and Vajrayana - can be seen on a single hill.
  - A large brick stupa crowns Bojjannakonda, surrounded by rock-cut cells and chaityas with seated Buddha figures carved in the rock face; Lingalakonda is covered with rows of monolithic votive stupas cut from the living rock, one of the densest such groups in India.
  - Relic caskets in earthen pots, Satavahana lead coins and stucco fragments were recovered; the name Sankaram derives from Sangharama.
  - Its interest is regional: it shows the Andhra coast maintaining Buddhist establishments long after the Krishna valley centres at Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda had declined.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8 and 10; Alexander Rea, *ASI Annual Report* 1907-08.
