- **Location**: Tawang, Arunachal Pradesh, on a spur at about 3,050 m above the Tawang Chu valley, close to the Bhutan and Tibet borders on the old trade and pilgrimage road to Lhasa.
- **Period/Level**: Founded in 1680-81 by Merak Lama Lodre Gyatso at the instance of the fifth Dalai Lama; Gelugpa. Occupied by Chinese forces for some months during the war of 1962.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest monastery in India and the second largest in the world after the Potala - and the eastern anchor of the Gelugpa order's reach.
  - The complex is a walled monastic town: a three-storeyed assembly hall (Dukhang) with an 8 m gilded seated Buddha, some sixty-five residential buildings, and the Parkhang library holding the Kangyur and Tengyur, part of it in gold lettering.
  - The sixth Dalai Lama, Tsangyang Gyatso, was born nearby at Urgelling in 1683 - the reason the monastery holds the position it does in the Gelugpa hierarchy.
  - The fourteenth Dalai Lama crossed into India at Tawang in 1959; the monastery's history is inseparable from the politics of the eastern Himalayan frontier, which is the angle worth flagging in an answer.
  - *Sources*: Michael Aris, *Hidden Treasures and Secret Lives*; ASI; Government of Arunachal Pradesh records.
