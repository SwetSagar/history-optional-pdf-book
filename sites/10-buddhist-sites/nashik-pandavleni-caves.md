- **Location**: The Trirashmi hills, about 8 km south of Nashik, Maharashtra, above the Godavari - on the route from the Konkan ports through the ghats to Pratishthana.
- **Period/Level**: Twenty-four caves cut between the 1st century BCE and the 3rd century CE, Hinayana, with later Mahayana additions to the 6th century. Patrons were the Satavahanas, the Kshatrapa house of Nahapana, and merchants and monks.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The single most important epigraphic site of the Deccan - the caves are read for their inscriptions more than for their architecture.
  - The Nasik prashasti of Gautami Balashri in Cave 3 praises her son Gautamiputra Satakarni as the destroyer of Shakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas, as ekabrahmana, as the one who crushed the pride of the Kshatriyas and stopped the mixing of the four varnas - the fullest statement of Satavahana royal ideology, and the key text on the brahmanical claims of a dynasty that patronised Buddhism.
  - Ushavadata (Rishabhadatta), son-in-law of Nahapana, records gifts of villages, of 3,000 cows, of money invested with weavers' guilds at fixed interest to feed and clothe the monks, and of a free ferry on the Barnasa - among the earliest land grants and the earliest recorded permanent endowments in India.
  - Cave 10 (Nahapana vihara), Cave 3 (Gautamiputra vihara) and Cave 18, the chaitya of the 1st century BCE, are the principal excavations; a rock-cut water management system of cisterns serves the whole group.
  - The alternation of Kshatrapa and Satavahana donations on the same hill records the see-saw of control over the western Deccan in the 1st-2nd centuries CE.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Early India*.
