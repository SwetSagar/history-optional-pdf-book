- **Location**: Capital of the Shakya gana, claimed by two sites: Tilaurakot in Kapilvastu district, Nepal, on the Banganga, and Piprahwa-Ganwaria in Siddharthnagar district, Uttar Pradesh, 15 km south. The map dot follows the Nepal identification.
- **Period/Level**: Tilaurakot, excavated by Debala Mitra (1962) and later Nepali, Japanese and Durham teams, is a fortified town with NBPW from about the 6th century BCE. Piprahwa, opened by W. C. Peppe in 1898 and excavated by K. M. Srivastava for the ASI in 1971-77, has a stupa and monastery of the 5th century BCE onwards.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Buddha's home for his first twenty-nine years, and the one major Buddhist site whose location is still genuinely in dispute.
  - The Piprahwa relic casket of 1898 carries a Brahmi inscription describing it as the receptacle of the relics of the Buddha of the Shakyas, deposited by his kinsmen - very probably the Shakya share of the eight divisions of the relics.
  - Srivastava's excavation recovered monastic sealings reading "Om devaputra vihare Kapilavastu bhikshu sanghasa", which he took as proof that Piprahwa is Kapilavastu; Tilaurakot's defences, NBPW and position relative to the Ashokan pillars at Niglihawa and Gotihawa support the Nepali claim. Faxian's and Xuanzang's distances have been read both ways.
  - The Shakyas were a gana-sangha, not a kingdom - Shuddhodana was an elected chief, not a king, a point often got wrong in answers.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6-7; K. M. Srivastava, *Excavations at Piprahwa and Ganwaria*; Debala Mitra, *Excavations at Tilaura-kot*.
