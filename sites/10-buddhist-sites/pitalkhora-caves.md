- **Location**: Pitalkhora, in a ravine of the Satmala range of the Western Ghats near Kannad, Aurangabad district, Maharashtra - Ptolemy's Petirgala, on the road from Pratishthana to the Tapi valley.
- **Period/Level**: Fourteen caves - four chaityas and ten viharas - of c. 2nd century BCE, among the earliest rock-cut excavations in the Deccan, Hinayana; Mahayana images and paintings were added in the 5th-6th centuries CE. The rock is friable and much of the site has collapsed.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest monumental sculpture in the western Deccan caves, older than Karle and contemporary with Bhaja.
  - The great yaksha dvarapalas, the elephant frieze supporting the terrace, a gaja-lakshmi and a horseman in high relief are the site's chief works, and are among the first attempts at large-scale figure carving in the western caves; the best pieces are now in the National Museum, Delhi.
  - An inscription records the gift of a physician (vaidya) of Pratishthana - a professional donor from the Satavahana capital, showing where the patronage came from.
  - Rock-cut channels carry rainwater through the complex, an early example of the water engineering that made long occupation of these sites possible.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; M. N. Deshpande, *The Rock-Cut Caves of Pitalkhora* (Ancient India 15, 1959); ASI.
