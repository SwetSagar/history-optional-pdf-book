- **Location**: Kuda, near Mandad in Raigad district, Maharashtra, in a hill above the Rajapuri creek of the Konkan coast - beside the ancient port of Mandagora named in the *Periplus*.
- **Period/Level**: Twenty-six small caves of c. 1st century BCE-3rd century CE, Hinayana, under the Mahabhojas as feudatories of the Satavahanas; Mahayana images were added after the 5th-6th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Modest caves with first-rate inscriptions - the record of a Konkan feudatory house and of the ordinary people who paid for Buddhist monuments.
  - The caves are small viharas and chaityas with stupas, carrying reliefs of the Buddha, the lotus, the wheel, elephants and nagas.
  - The inscriptions record gifts by the Mahabhoja family - Mandava Khandapalita, Sadageri Vijaya and their kin - and by physicians, blacksmiths, gardeners and merchants with their wives, one of the best cross-sections of donor society in the western Deccan.
  - Their position beside a Konkan creek port shows the same pattern as Kanheri and Karle: Buddhist establishments sit on the trade arteries, and their donors are the traders.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; S. Nagaraju, *Buddhist Architecture of Western India*; ASI.
