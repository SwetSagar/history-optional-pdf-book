- **Location**: Alchi, Leh district, Ladakh, on the left bank of the Indus about 70 km downstream of Leh - the one major Ladakhi gompa built on the valley floor rather than a crag.
- **Period/Level**: Founded in the late 10th or early 11th century, its temples built and painted c. 1000-1200 CE in the Kashmiri idiom; inscriptions name the founder as Kal-dan Shes-rab and the tradition credits Rinchen Zangpo, the Great Translator of the Guge kingdom. Abandoned as a working monastery and now maintained by Likir; Gelugpa.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The oldest surviving wall paintings in Ladakh, and the last substantial body of Kashmiri Buddhist art anywhere, Kashmir's own having been destroyed.
  - The Sumtsek, a three-storeyed temple, holds three colossal clay bodhisattvas - Maitreya, Avalokiteshvara and Manjushri - whose painted dhotis carry miniature scenes of palaces, pilgrimage and courtly life, a documentary record of 12th-century dress and architecture.
  - The Dukhang (assembly hall) has a Vairochana mandala and a carved wooden doorway; the third temple, the Manjushri lhakhang, contains a wood-carved Buddha.
  - The style is Indian and Kashmiri rather than Tibetan - slim modelled figures, rich reds and blues - which is why Alchi is used as the type site for the "second diffusion" of Buddhism into the western Himalaya.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Snellgrove and Skorupski, *The Cultural Heritage of Ladakh*; Roger Goepper, *Alchi* (1996).
