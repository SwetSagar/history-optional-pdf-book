- **Location**: Thanale (also called Nadsur), Sudhagad taluka, Raigad district, Maharashtra, in a Sahyadri ravine above the Konkan - on the ghat route from the Konkan creek ports up to the Deccan plateau.
- **Period/Level**: Twenty-three caves of c. 2nd-1st centuries BCE, Hinayana, contemporary with Bhaja and Kondane; partly unfinished, and little studied.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the oldest and least disturbed of the early Konkan cave groups - the plain monastic architecture that the great sites grew out of.
  - The chaitya has a stupa carved in relief on the rear wall rather than a free-standing one, an early and unusual solution; the viharas open off a long common verandah with a sculpted facade.
  - Several caves were abandoned part-cut, which leaves the working method visible - excavation proceeding from the roof downwards, so that no scaffolding was needed.
  - Like Kuda and Bhaja, its interest is that it sits on a trade pass: the early Buddhist caves of the western ghats map the routes linking the Konkan ports to Junnar, Nashik and Paithan.
  - *Sources*: S. Nagaraju, *Buddhist Architecture of Western India*; ASI; Upinder Singh, ch. 8.
