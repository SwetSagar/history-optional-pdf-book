- **Location**: Runkata, near Agra on the Yamuna in Uttar Pradesh, is one of the places named as Surdas's birthplace; his working life was spent at Gaughat and then at Parasauli near Govardhan in the Braj country.
- **Period/Level**: Surdas, c. 1478-1583 by tradition, was blind from birth or early childhood. He became a disciple of Vallabhacharya about 1510 and was one of the *Ashtachhap*, the eight poets of the Pushtimarg who sang before the image of Shrinathji at Govardhan.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The greatest poet of Braj Bhasha, and the figure through whom Krishna devotion became a literature of childhood and maternal love.
  - The *Sursagar*, attributed to him, is an enormous body of *pada* verse on Krishna's infancy and youth at Gokul and Vrindavan - butter-stealing, the games with the cowherds, and Yashoda's love. That the emotional centre of the poetry is *vatsalya*, parental affection, rather than erotic longing distinguishes it from the Radha-centred tradition.
  - The *Bhramargeet* section, in which the gopis rebuke Krishna's messenger Uddhava for offering them abstract philosophy in place of the god they love, is the most famous statement in Hindi of *bhakti*'s superiority to *jnana* - devotion answering back to metaphysics.
  - His verse fixed Braj Bhasha as the standard literary dialect of north India for the next three centuries, used by poets far outside the Vaishnava fold.
  - Textual scholarship has shown the *Sursagar* grew hugely by accretion, from a few hundred verses in early manuscripts to many thousands later, so the corpus is a tradition rather than one man's work. A few of his verses also appear in the Guru Granth Sahib.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II.
