- **Location**: Basavana Bagewadi, Vijayapura district, northern Karnataka, in the dry Deccan plateau between the Krishna and the Bhima - the birthplace of Basavanna, some 80 km from Kalyana where he worked.
- **Period/Level**: Basavanna, c. 1105-1167 CE, served as treasurer and chief minister to the Kalachuri king Bijjala II at Kalyana. The Veerashaiva or Lingayat movement he led belongs to the second half of the 12th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The origin of the most thoroughgoing social critique produced by any Indian devotional movement.
  - Basavanna rejected caste, image worship, temple ritual, pilgrimage and the authority of the Vedas, and replaced them with the *ishtalinga*, a personal linga worn on the body, so that each devotee carried the shrine and needed no priest. Abolishing the intermediary is what distinguishes the Lingayats from most bhakti movements, which softened caste without dismantling its ritual basis.
  - The *Anubhava Mantapa* at Kalyana was an assembly where *sharanas* of every background debated - Allama Prabhu presiding, Akka Mahadevi among them, along with cobblers, washermen and tanners whose *vachanas* survive under their own names.
  - The *vachanas* are short, unmetred, direct utterances in Kannada, deliberately plain against Sanskrit ornament. They are a major literary corpus and the earliest sustained body of prose-poetry by non-elite authors in any Indian language.
  - The movement's crisis came around 1167 with a marriage between a brahmana girl and an untouchable boy arranged by the sharanas, the violence that followed and the dispersal of the community. It is the best-documented case of a bhakti movement tested in practice and defeated.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; A. K. Ramanujan, *Speaking of Siva*.
