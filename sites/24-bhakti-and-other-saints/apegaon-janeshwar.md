- **Location**: Apegaon, Paithan taluk, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar district, Maharashtra, on the Godavari - the ancestral village of Jnaneshwar's family, a short distance from Paithan and from Alandi near Pune where he took *samadhi*.
- **Period/Level**: Jnaneshwar, or Dnyaneshwar, c. 1275-1296, wrote under the Yadavas of Devagiri. His *Dnyaneshwari* is dated 1290. He died at twenty-one, having taken *sanjeevan samadhi* at Alandi.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The founder of the Varkari tradition and the writer who made Marathi a language of philosophy.
  - The *Dnyaneshwari* is a commentary on the *Bhagavad Gita* in Marathi *ovi* verse, some nine thousand stanzas. Writing Vedantic philosophy in a regional language, for an audience that could not read Sanskrit, was a deliberate act: he says so in the text. It is the foundation of Marathi literature and the model for later vernacular commentary across India.
  - His *Amrutanubhav* is an independent philosophical work rather than a commentary, arguing a non-dual position derived from the Nath tradition in which he was initiated by his brother Nivrittinath.
  - He and his siblings were outcaste, their father having returned from sannyasa to family life, and were refused the sacred thread by the brahmanas of Paithan. The exclusion is the biographical fact behind the movement's later openness - the Varkari sampradaya centred on Pandharpur admitted all castes, and Namdev, Chokhamela, Eknath and Tukaram follow from this beginning.
  - The annual *palkhi* carrying his sandals from Alandi to Pandharpur still draws hundreds of thousands.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
