- **Location**: Dehu, Pune district, Maharashtra, on the Indrayani river about 30 km from Pune - a village on the same river as Alandi, where Jnaneshwar's samadhi lies.
- **Period/Level**: Tukaram, c. 1608-1650, a grain trader of the Kunbi caste, wrote during the last years of the Nizam Shahi and the rise of Shahaji and Shivaji. He is the last of the four principal Varkari saints and the movement's greatest poet.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The peak of Marathi devotional poetry, and the sharpest voice against ritual and caste privilege in the tradition.
  - His *abhangas*, some four and a half thousand of them, are direct, colloquial and often bitter - about hunger, debt, the death of his family in the famine of the 1620s, and his own doubt. That autobiographical honesty is unusual in Indian devotional literature and is what gives the poems their standing.
  - Tradition records brahmana opponents, Rameshwar Bhatt among them, forcing him to sink his manuscripts in the Indrayani because a shudra had no right to expound scripture; the notebooks are said to have surfaced after thirteen days. Whatever happened, the story preserves the real conflict over who could speak for religion.
  - He is said to have declined Shivaji's invitation to court and sent him back to his duties, while his contemporary Ramdas accepted the role of royal preceptor. The contrast between the two is the standard question on the relationship of Maharashtra's saints to Maratha politics.
  - The *palkhi* from Dehu joins the Alandi procession to Pandharpur each year, binding the two founding villages of the Varkari tradition.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Dilip Chitre, *Says Tuka*.
