- **Location**: Associated with Tiruvathavur near Melur, Madurai district, Tamil Nadu, in the Vaigai basin east of Madurai - Pandya country, with the saint's later life spent at Madurai and Chidambaram.
- **Period/Level**: Manikkavachakar is generally placed in the 9th century CE, serving as chief minister to a Pandya king before renouncing office. His *Tiruvasagam* and *Tirukkovaiyar* together form the eighth *Tirumurai* of the Shaiva canon.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest devotional poet in Tamil, and one of the four *Samayacharyas* who shaped Shaiva Siddhanta.
  - The four teachers of Tamil Shaivism are Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar and Manikkavachakar. Note that the first three are counted among the sixty-three Nayanmars and he is not - a distinction frequently got wrong, and worth stating precisely.
  - The *Tiruvasagam* is built on the experience of grace rather than on ritual or scholarship: the soul's helplessness, its seizure by Shiva, and the alternation of union and abandonment. G. U. Pope's remark that those unmoved by it must have hearts of stone is the standard quotation.
  - Shaiva Siddhanta, the philosophical system that grew from this poetry, holds *pati*, *pashu* and *pasha* - god, soul and bond - to be eternally distinct, with release consisting in the soul's union with Shiva by grace rather than in identity with him. It is the dominant theology of Tamil Shaivism and the counterpoint to Advaita.
  - The story of his diverting royal funds meant for horses into temple building, and the divine intervention that followed, is the best-known episode of his life, and marks the renunciation of office for devotion.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Tiruvasagam*.
