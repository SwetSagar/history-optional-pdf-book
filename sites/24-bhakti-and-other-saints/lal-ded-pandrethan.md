- **Location**: Pandrethan, ancient Puranadhisthana, on the outskirts of Srinagar beside the Jhelum, Jammu and Kashmir - the old capital site, where a small 10th-century stone Shiva temple stands in a tank.
- **Period/Level**: Lal Ded, also Lalleshwari or Lalla Arifa, is placed in the 14th century, roughly 1320-1392, a generation into the Shah Mir sultanate and contemporary with the arrival of Sayyid Ali Hamadani and the spread of Islam in the valley.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The founding voice of Kashmiri literature, and a figure claimed by both Hindus and Muslims.
  - Her *vakhs*, four-line utterances, are the earliest substantial composition in the Kashmiri language and remain in oral circulation - proverbs as much as poetry. The Kashmiri literary tradition effectively begins with a woman.
  - Her thought is Kashmir Shaivism, the non-dual Trika system of Abhinavagupta, expressed without Sanskrit apparatus: the self and Shiva are one, the search outside oneself is futile, and ritual, caste and scripture are useless without realisation.
  - She left her marriage and wandered, by tradition naked, refusing both household and social respectability. Hindus call her Lalleshwari and Muslims Lalla Arifa, and she is associated with the Rishi order of Nund Rishi (Sheikh Nooruddin), whose verse echoes hers. She is the standard example of Kashmir's shared devotional culture, and of the Rishi tradition as a Kashmiri counterpart to Sufism.
  - Her position at the moment of religious transition makes her the best single source on how that change was experienced from inside the valley rather than from court chronicles.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
