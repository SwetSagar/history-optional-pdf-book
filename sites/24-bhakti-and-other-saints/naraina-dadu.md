- **Location**: Naraina, Jaipur district, Rajasthan, in the semi-arid country south-west of Jaipur near the Sambhar salt lake - the place where Dadu Dayal settled and where his principal seat remains.
- **Period/Level**: Dadu Dayal, 1544-1603, a cotton-carder by trade, probably of Muslim birth, taught in Rajasthan during Akbar's reign and is recorded as having met the emperor at Fatehpur Sikri in 1585. Note that some note-sets place him in the 6th century; he is a 16th-century figure.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The founder of the Dadupanth, and one of the clearest cases of a *nirguna* teacher building a lasting order.
  - Dadu preached a formless god beyond the categories of Hindu and Muslim, rejecting scripture, caste, image worship and circumcision alike. He called his followers simply *parabrahmi*, of the supreme reality, refusing a sectarian name - a deliberate attempt to found a community without a religious identity.
  - His verses were collected by his disciple Rajjab as the *Dadu Anubhav Vani*, and the Dadupanthi scribes assembled anthologies of *nirguna* poetry - the *Panch Vani* and the Sarvangi collections - which preserve the earliest datable manuscripts of Kabir, Namdev and Ravidas. The Dadupanthi archive is therefore a principal source for the whole north Indian bhakti corpus, independent of the Sikh and Bijak traditions.
  - His recorded audience with Akbar belongs with the Ibadat Khana debates as evidence of how far the emperor's religious enquiry extended beyond the formal ulama.
  - The order later maintained an armed *naga* wing that took military service with Jaipur - a reminder that devotional sects in this period could become political and military actors.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II.
