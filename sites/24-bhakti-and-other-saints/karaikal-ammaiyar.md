- **Location**: Karaikal, a coastal enclave of the union territory of Puducherry on the Arasalar, a distributary of the Kaveri, surrounded by Tamil Nadu - in the delta, close to Nagapattinam.
- **Period/Level**: The home of Karaikkal Ammaiyar, born Punithavathi, usually placed in the 6th century CE - among the earliest of the sixty-three Nayanmars, and earlier than Appar and Sambandar, so her verse is some of the oldest Tamil devotional poetry to survive.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of only three women among the Nayanmars, and the author of the earliest datable Shaiva poetry in Tamil.
  - Her *Arputhat Tiruvantadi* and *Tiru Irattai Manimalai* are included in the eleventh *Tirumurai*. Because they precede the great Shaiva hymnists, they are the starting point for the Tamil bhakti movement, which is itself the earliest large-scale devotional movement in India and the model for later north Indian bhakti.
  - The mango story defines her: having given a fruit meant for her husband to a Shiva devotee, she produced another by prayer, and her husband, recognising her as more than human, left her. Freed of marriage, she asked Shiva not for beauty but for the form of a *pey*, a ghoul, and is depicted as an emaciated skeletal figure with bulging eyes, playing cymbals at Shiva's dance.
  - That request is the point worth making. Where male saints are shown as ideal devotees, she achieves sanctity by deliberately abandoning the feminine ideal - the sharpest instance in Indian religious literature of a woman escaping the roles available to her by refusing the body itself.
  - Her image appears in Chola bronze and in the Nayanmar friezes at Darasuram.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Periya Puranam*.
