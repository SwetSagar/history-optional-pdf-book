- **Location**: Varanasi, on the left bank of the Ganga where the river turns north, Uttar Pradesh. For the ancient city, the Kashi mahajanapada and its NBPW levels, see the separate Varanasi entry in the NBPW chapter; this entry concerns the bhakti teachers associated with the city.
- **Period/Level**: The 15th and early 16th centuries, under the Sharqi sultans of Jaunpur and then the Lodis and early Mughals. Ramananda belongs to the late 14th and early 15th centuries; Kabir to c. 1440-1518; Ravidas to roughly the same span.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The place where north Indian bhakti became a movement that crossed caste and religion, and where the *nirguna* tradition took shape.
  - Ramananda, a Ramanandi teacher in the Ramanuja line, is credited with opening initiation to disciples of every caste and preaching in Hindi rather than Sanskrit. Tradition gives him twelve disciples including Kabir the weaver, Ravidas the leather worker, Sena the barber and Dhanna the farmer - the list itself, whatever its accuracy, states the movement's social claim.
  - Kabir's verse attacks the ritual of Hindus and Muslims alike, the authority of Veda and Quran, pilgrimage, caste and image worship, holding to a formless god. His work survives in three distinct recensions - the *Bijak*, the Guru Granth Sahib and the Rajasthani manuscripts - which differ enough that establishing what Kabir actually said is a genuine textual problem worth citing.
  - Ravidas, a *chamar* by birth, taught an equally uncompromising *nirguna* devotion and imagined *Begampura*, a city without sorrow, tax or hierarchy - one of the very few utopias in pre-modern Indian literature, and central to modern Dalit thought. Forty of his verses are in the Guru Granth Sahib.
  - Their shared vocabulary with Sufi teaching makes them the standard evidence in questions on syncretism, though Kabir himself refused both labels.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I and II.
