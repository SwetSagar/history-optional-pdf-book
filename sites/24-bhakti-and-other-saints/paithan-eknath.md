- **Location**: Paithan, on the north bank of the Godavari, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar district, Maharashtra - the old Satavahana capital of Pratishthana, for which see the separate entry in the capitals chapter. This entry concerns Eknath, who lived and wrote here.
- **Period/Level**: Eknath, c. 1533-1599, worked at Paithan during the Nizam Shahi and early Mughal period in the Deccan. He is the third of the four great Varkari figures, between Jnaneshwar and Namdev before him and Tukaram after.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The saint who recovered the Varkari textual tradition and pushed it furthest on caste.
  - His first act of scholarship was to produce a corrected text of the *Dnyaneshwari*, which had become corrupt in the two and a half centuries since Jnaneshwar wrote it. Without that edition the central text of Marathi devotion might not have survived in usable form.
  - The *Eknathi Bhagavata*, his Marathi commentary on the eleventh book of the *Bhagavata Purana*, was attacked by the pandits of Banaras for expounding scripture in a vernacular. That episode is the standard illustration of the resistance vernacular religious writing met from the Sanskrit establishment.
  - His *bharuds* - dramatic songs written in the voices of a washerwoman, a Muslim faqir, an untouchable - carried devotion into street performance, and his recorded acts of eating with Mahars and drawing water for them were public breaches of caste practice by a brahmana, not merely statements against it.
  - He is usually described as the bridge between the earlier and later Varkari saints, and his *samadhi* is at Paithan on the Godavari.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; R. C. Dhere, *Rise of a Folk God*.
