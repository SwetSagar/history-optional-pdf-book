- **Location**: Tirthahalli, Shivamogga district, Karnataka, on the Tunga in the Malnad hills below the Western Ghats - one of the places named as Purandaradasa's birthplace, with Hampi the centre of his later life.
- **Period/Level**: Purandaradasa, c. 1484-1564, was by tradition a wealthy gem merchant who gave away his property and became a wandering singer, taking initiation from Vyasatirtha. He lived and sang at Vijayanagara in its last decades before the defeat at Talikota.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Called the father of Carnatic music - the man who turned a performing art into a teachable system.
  - He devised the graded exercises still used to begin instruction: the *sarali varisai*, *janti varisai* and *alankaras*, set in the Mayamalavagowla raga, which every Carnatic student in south India starts with to this day. Codifying pedagogy is what earns him the title *Sangita Pitamaha*, and it is the concrete reason his name matters beyond devotion.
  - He composed *kirtanas* in Kannada, said to number in the thousands though far fewer survive, joining a devotional text to a fixed raga and tala - the structural ancestor of the Carnatic *kriti* that Tyagaraja and his contemporaries later perfected.
  - His songs belong to the Haridasa movement, the Kannada devotional stream flowing from the Madhva mathas at Udupi, and they attack caste pride, hypocrisy and ritualism in plain language while remaining within a Dvaita framework.
  - That a merchant's renunciation and a court philosopher's initiation lie behind the standard music syllabus of half the subcontinent is the point worth making about Vijayanagara's cultural reach.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; B. N. K. Sharma, *History of the Dvaita School of Vedanta*.
