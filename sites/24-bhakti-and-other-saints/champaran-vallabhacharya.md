- **Location**: Champaran, also called Champajhar, in Raipur district, Chhattisgarh, on the Mahanadi about 60 km from Raipur. Note that this is not the Champaran of Bihar: Vallabhacharya's birthplace is the Chhattisgarh village, where his family was travelling when he was born.
- **Period/Level**: Vallabhacharya, 1479-1531, a Telugu brahmana by descent, taught during the Lodi and early Mughal period. He established the Shrinathji shrine at Govardhan in the Braj country; the image was later moved to Nathdwara in Mewar to keep it from Aurangzeb.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The founder of the Pushtimarg, the most organised of the Krishna devotional sects, and of the *Shuddhadvaita* philosophy.
  - *Shuddhadvaita*, pure non-dualism, holds that the world is a real transformation of Brahman and not *maya* - an explicit rejection of Shankara's position, preserving the reality of the world so that devotion to an embodied god makes sense.
  - *Pushtimarg*, the way of grace, teaches that liberation comes from divine favour rather than from works or knowledge, and that the devotee serves Krishna through *seva* - the daily cycle of bathing, dressing, feeding and putting the image to rest. The sect's ritual life is unusually elaborate and domestic.
  - It drew its following from the trading and banking communities of Gujarat and Rajasthan, which gave it wealth and durability, and it generated its own painting tradition in the *pichhwai* hangings of Nathdwara.
  - The *Ashtachhap*, eight poet-musicians including Surdas, sang in its temples, so the sect is also the institutional setting for much of the finest Braj literature.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II.
