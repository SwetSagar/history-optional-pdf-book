- **Location**: Bannur, Mysuru district, Karnataka, on the Kaveri in the southern Deccan - the birthplace of Vyasatirtha, whose working life was spent at the Vijayanagara court and at Tirupati.
- **Period/Level**: Vyasatirtha, or Vyasaraja, 1460-1539, headed the Madhva Dvaita order and served as *rajaguru* to Krishnadevaraya, r. 1509-1529. He administered the Tirupati temple for some years before moving to the capital.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The philosopher-minister of Vijayanagara at its height, and the link between Dvaita philosophy and the Haridasa devotional movement.
  - His *Nyayamrita*, *Tarkatandava* and *Tatparyachandrika* are the most rigorous statements of Dvaita logic, written against Advaita. The *Nyayamrita* provoked Madhusudana Sarasvati's *Advaitasiddhi* in reply, and that exchange is the last great philosophical debate of the Indian scholastic tradition.
  - He was Krishnadevaraya's preceptor, and the emperor is recorded as seating him on the throne during an inauspicious conjunction. A Dvaita pontiff holding that position illustrates how Vijayanagara kingship drew authority from religious institutions, as the Sringeri matha had done for the dynasty's founders.
  - He was guru to Purandaradasa and Kanakadasa, the two great Haridasa poets, so the technical philosophy of the mathas and the popular Kannada devotional songs of the *dasa* tradition run through one teacher.
  - He endowed and administered temples across the Vijayanagara territories, and inscriptions record his grants - useful evidence for the temple economy of the period.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; B. N. K. Sharma, *History of the Dvaita School of Vedanta*.
