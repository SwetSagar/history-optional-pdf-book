- **Location**: Bordowa in Nagaon district, Assam, on the south bank of the Brahmaputra in the central valley - Shankaradeva's birthplace, with his later work centred at Barpeta and at Patbausi in Koch territory.
- **Period/Level**: Srimanta Shankaradeva, 1449-1568, taught through the late Ahom and Koch period. His disciple Madhavadeva, 1489-1596, carried the movement forward; the Koch king Nara Narayan gave it protection after Ahom hostility.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The founder of Assamese neo-Vaishnavism, and the most complete case of a religious movement creating a regional culture whole.
  - *Ekasarana Dharma*, shelter in one name, rejected image worship, sacrifice and caste distinction in favour of *nama-kirtana*, the chanting of Krishna's name. The *sattras*, monastic establishments, and the *namghars*, village prayer houses, gave it an institutional base that still organises rural Assamese life.
  - What distinguishes Shankaradeva from other bhakti teachers is the sheer range of forms he created: *Borgeet* devotional songs, *Ankiya Naat* one-act dramas in Brajavali, *Sattriya* dance now recognised as a classical form, the *Kirtan Ghosha* verse anthology, and the *Vrindavani Vastra*, a woven silk tapestry depicting Krishna's life, fragments of which are in the British Museum and the Musee Guimet.
  - He composed in Assamese and in Brajavali, an artificial literary dialect, rather than Sanskrit, and his movement admitted Muslims, tribal groups and low castes - Chandsai, a Muslim, was among his close disciples.
  - The Ahom court's suspicion and the Koch court's patronage show how much these movements depended on finding a protector.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; *Kirtan Ghosha*.
