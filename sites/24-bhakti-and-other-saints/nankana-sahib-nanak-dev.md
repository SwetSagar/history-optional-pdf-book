- **Location**: Nankana Sahib, formerly Talwandi, in Sheikhupura district, Punjab, Pakistan, about 75 km west of Lahore in the Ravi-Chenab doab.
- **Period/Level**: The birthplace of Guru Nanak, 1469-1539. His life spans the late Lodi Sultanate, Babur's invasion of 1526 and the first Mughal decade; he settled finally at Kartarpur on the Ravi.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The origin of Sikhism, and of a religious teaching that was institutional as well as devotional from the start.
  - Nanak taught a formless god - *Ik Onkar* - reached through *nam simran*, honest work and sharing, and rejected caste, ritual, idol worship and asceticism. What separates him from Kabir, with whom he shares much vocabulary, is organisation: he established *sangat*, the congregation, *pangat* and *langar*, the common kitchen where all eat seated together regardless of caste, and appointed a successor. A named line of succession is why the teaching became a community and Kabir's did not.
  - His *udasis* took him across India and, by tradition, to Mecca, Baghdad and Sri Lanka; his hymns were later collected by Guru Arjan in the *Adi Granth* in 1604, alongside those of Kabir, Ravidas, Namdev and Sufi Shaikh Farid - a scripture that includes writers of other faiths and low caste, which is unique.
  - His *Babarvani* verses describe Babur's invasion and the suffering of the Punjab - rare contemporary comment on a political event from a religious teacher, and a useful non-courtly source on 1526.
  - Kartarpur, where he farmed and taught, and Nankana Sahib are both now in Pakistan, which is why the corridor opened in 2019 matters.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I and II; *Guru Granth Sahib*.
