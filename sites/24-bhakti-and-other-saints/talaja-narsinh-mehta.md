- **Location**: Talaja, Bhavnagar district, Gujarat, on the Shetrunji river near the Gulf of Khambhat coast of Saurashtra - Narsinh Mehta's birthplace, with his later life spent at Junagadh below Girnar.
- **Period/Level**: Narsinh Mehta, c. 1414-1481, a Nagar brahmana, lived under the Gujarat Sultanate. He is counted the *Adi Kavi*, the first poet, of Gujarati literature.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The founder of Gujarati literature, and the author of the hymn that became the anthem of the national movement.
  - *Vaishnava Jana To* defines a Vaishnava entirely by conduct - one who feels another's pain, helps the suffering, is free of pride, covets nothing, treats all women as mothers, speaks no untruth. Gandhi made it a daily prayer at his ashrams, and it is the clearest instance of a 15th-century devotional lyric being carried into 20th-century politics.
  - His *prabhatiya* morning hymns and the *pada* cycles on Krishna and Radha established Gujarati as a literary language, and his autobiographical narrative poems - the *Hundi*, the *Mameru*, the *Shraddha* - are among the earliest sustained first-person narratives in an Indian vernacular.
  - He was excommunicated by his own Nagar caste for singing and eating with untouchables at Junagadh, and the episodes of divine intervention that follow in his verse are framed as vindication against that exclusion. Caste conflict recorded by the excluded party himself is rare and worth citing.
  - His devotion is *saguna*, to a personal Krishna, placing him with Mirabai and Surdas rather than with Kabir.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II.
