- **Location**: Kudki, or Kurki, in Pali district, Rajasthan, in the arid Marwar country of the Luni basin - traditionally Mirabai's birthplace, with Merta nearby as the home of her Rathore family.
- **Period/Level**: Mirabai, c. 1498-1547, was a Rathore princess of Merta married to Bhojraj, son of Rana Sanga of Mewar. Her life spans the collapse of Rajput power at Khanwa in 1527 and the early Mughal decades.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-known woman in the north Indian bhakti tradition, and a case of devotion used to refuse the obligations of rank and marriage.
  - She took Krishna as her husband and refused the duties owed to her marital family, including, by tradition, *sati* on Bhojraj's death and the seclusion expected of a Sisodia widow. The persecution that followed - the poisoned cup, the basket with a snake - is the standard material of her legend and of her own verse.
  - Her *padas*, in a Rajasthani-Braj mixture, are sung across northern and western India. As *saguna* devotion to a personal Krishna they sit opposite Kabir's formless god, and the pairing is the usual way of laying out the two streams of north Indian bhakti.
  - The gender argument is the point to develop: for a woman of her rank, bhakti was the one available exit from the duties of caste and household, and Mirabai's refusal - like Akka Mahadevi's in Kannada and Andal's in Tamil - shows devotion functioning as social escape as much as religious practice.
  - The textual position is uncertain: few of the songs attributed to her can be traced to her own century, and the corpus grew by accretion. Say so rather than treating the whole body as hers.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II.
