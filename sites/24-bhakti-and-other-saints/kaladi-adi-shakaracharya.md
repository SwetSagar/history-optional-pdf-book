- **Location**: Kalady, Ernakulam district, Kerala, on the bank of the Periyar about 45 km from Kochi - in the Chera country, on the river that also carried the Muziris trade.
- **Period/Level**: The traditional birthplace of Adi Shankaracharya, usually dated c. 788-820 CE, though the dates are disputed and some scholars place him earlier. His floruit falls in the early medieval period, after the Tamil bhakti movement and during the decline of Buddhism in the peninsula.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The birthplace of the philosopher who gave Advaita Vedanta its systematic form and gave Hinduism an institutional structure.
  - Shankara's commentaries on the *Brahma Sutras*, the ten principal Upanishads and the *Bhagavad Gita* are the foundation of Advaita: Brahman alone is real, the world as perceived is *maya*, and the individual self is not different from Brahman. The *Sri Bhashya* of Ramanuja and Madhva's Dvaita were both written against this position, so Indian philosophy for the next five centuries is largely a conversation with Shankara.
  - He organised the *Dashanami* monastic order and, by tradition, founded four mathas at the cardinal points - Sringeri, Dwarka, Puri and Jyotirmath at Badrinath. A network of monasteries with defined territories and a succession of heads gave Hinduism a durable institutional form it had lacked.
  - His debate with Mandana Mishra, and Ubhaya Bharati's adjudication of it, is the best-known story of the scholastic disputation culture of the period.
  - He is often credited with the decline of Buddhism in India. The claim needs care: Buddhism was already weakening for reasons of patronage and institutional concentration, and Shankara's own debt to Buddhist logic was noted by his opponents, who called him a crypto-Buddhist.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
