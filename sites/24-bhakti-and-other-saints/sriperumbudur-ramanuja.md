- **Location**: Sriperumbudur, Kanchipuram district, Tamil Nadu, in the flat country between Kanchipuram and the coast, in the upper drainage of the Adyar - about 40 km west of Chennai.
- **Period/Level**: The birthplace of Ramanuja, traditionally 1017-1137 CE. He studied at Kanchipuram under Yadava Prakasha, later headed the Srirangam temple, and spent years at Melkote in Hoysala territory after conflict with a Chola ruler.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The origin of Vishishtadvaita - the philosophy that gave devotional religion an intellectual foundation - and of a practical challenge to caste exclusion.
  - Ramanuja's *Sri Bhashya* on the *Brahma Sutras*, with the *Vedarthasangraha* and his *Gita Bhashya*, argue for qualified non-dualism: souls and matter are real and form the body of Brahman, distinct from it yet inseparable. Against Shankara's impersonal absolute this allows a personal god who can be loved, which is what made *bhakti* philosophically respectable.
  - He brought the Tamil hymns of the Alwars into temple liturgy alongside the Sanskrit Veda, a decision with large consequences: it gave a vernacular devotional corpus canonical standing, and it is the basis of the Srivaishnava claim to an *ubhaya vedanta*, a dual scripture.
  - Tradition records him admitting groups excluded by caste to temple worship, and proclaiming the secret *Tirumantra* from a temple tower so that all could hear it. Present it as tradition, but the Srivaishnava community's later division into Vadakalai and Tenkalai turned partly on exactly these questions.
  - At Melkote he is said to have converted the Hoysala Vishnuvardhana from Jainism - the link between his teaching and the Hoysala temple programme at Belur and Halebidu.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
