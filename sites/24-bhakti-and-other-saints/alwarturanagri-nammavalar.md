- **Location**: Alwarthirunagari, Thoothukudi district, Tamil Nadu, on the Thamirabarani river in the southern Pandya country - near Tirunelveli, in the belt of nine Vaishnava shrines along that river.
- **Period/Level**: The home of Nammalwar, generally placed in the 9th century CE, the foremost of the twelve Alwars. His four works - the *Tiruviruttam*, *Tiruvasiriyam*, *Periya Tiruvandadi* and above all the *Tiruvaymoli* - make up over a third of the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The central figure of Tamil Vaishnava bhakti, and the poet whose work Srivaishnavism treats as scripture.
  - The *Tiruvaymoli*, 1,102 verses, is called the *Dravida Veda* or Tamil Veda, and Ramanuja's tradition gives it standing equal to the Sanskrit Upanishads. A vernacular text admitted to canonical parity with the Veda is the single most important development in the history of Indian devotional religion, and Nammalwar is where it happens.
  - His poetry speaks in many voices - lover, mother, friend - using the grammar of Tamil *akam* love poetry for religious longing. The Sangam secular tradition converted into a devotional idiom is the clearest instance of continuity between classical Tamil literature and bhakti.
  - The Alwar movement, with the Shaiva Nayanmars, is the earliest mass devotional movement in India, running from roughly the 6th to the 9th centuries, and precedes the north Indian bhakti movement by several hundred years - the point examiners most often want established.
  - Nathamuni is credited with collecting and setting to music the scattered Alwar hymns in the 10th century, which is how the corpus survived.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*.
