- **Location**: Jamb near Jalna, Maharashtra, in the Godavari basin of Marathwada, is Ramdas's birthplace; his working life was spent at Sajjangad near Satara, the fort where he died.
- **Period/Level**: Samarth Ramdas, 1608-1681, was an exact contemporary of Tukaram and lived through the rise of Shivaji. His principal work, the *Dasbodh*, was composed in the Marathi *ovi* metre at Shivthar Ghal.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The saint of activist devotion - the counter-model to the Varkari tradition, and the figure invoked to give Maratha power a religious sanction.
  - Ramdas was a devotee of Rama and Hanuman rather than of Vitthala, and he stands outside the Varkari succession. His teaching joins *bhakti* to worldly capability: the *Dasbodh* discusses statecraft, organisation, discipline and leadership as spiritual duties, which is why he is read as advocating an engaged rather than a withdrawn piety.
  - He founded a network of Hanuman temples and *mathas* across Maharashtra, giving the movement an institutional reach the Varkaris never sought.
  - His role as Shivaji's guru is the contested part. The relationship is asserted strongly in later Maratha tradition and was emphasised again by nineteenth and twentieth-century nationalist writers; contemporary evidence for it is thin, and historians treat the *guru-shishya* claim with caution. The honest answer sets the tradition out and notes the doubt rather than repeating either version flat.
  - The contrast with Tukaram - one declining the court, the other associated with it - is the usual frame for discussing religion and politics in 17th-century Maharashtra.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; ASI.
