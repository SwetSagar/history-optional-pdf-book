- **Location**: Srivilliputhur, Virudhunagar district, Tamil Nadu, at the foot of the Western Ghats in the dry Pandya country near the Vaippar basin - about 75 km from Madurai.
- **Period/Level**: The home of Andal, also called Kodhai, generally placed in the 8th-9th centuries CE. She is the only woman among the twelve Alwars, the Tamil Vaishnava saint-poets whose hymns were later collected as the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The one female voice in the Alwar canon, and the author of a text still recited by millions every year.
  - The *Tiruppavai*, thirty verses on the *pavai* vow, is sung daily through the Tamil month of Margazhi in temples and households across the south. Few devotional compositions anywhere have that continuity of ritual use, and it is the single most widely known piece of Alwar poetry.
  - Her *Nachiyar Tirumozhi* is more striking for the historian: it speaks of longing for Krishna in frankly erotic terms, in a woman's own voice, and uses the conventions of Tamil love poetry for devotion. It is the key text in any question on gender and bhakti, beside Akka Mahadevi in Kannada and Mirabai in Rajasthani.
  - Tradition holds that she refused a human marriage and merged into the image of Ranganatha at Srirangam. That refusal - devotion as an alternative to marriage - is the recurring pattern in the lives of women saints, and Karaikal Ammaiyar and Mirabai show the same shape.
  - The temple's eleven-tiered gopuram is the emblem of the Government of Tamil Nadu.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*.
