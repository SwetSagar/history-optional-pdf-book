- **Location**: Armamalai, near Vellore in the Palar basin of northern Tamil Nadu - a natural cave in a granite hill in the tract between Kanchipuram and the Karnataka border.
- **Period/Level**: A Jaina cave shrine with paintings assigned to about the 8th century CE, in the Pallava-Pandya period, and Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions on the walls that belong to a considerably earlier phase of Jaina occupation.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A rare surviving example of Jaina cave painting in northern Tamil Nadu, and evidence of long monastic use of one shelter.
  - The murals were executed in fresco and tempera technique on a lime ground, showing Jaina themes and decorative motifs. With Sittanavasal and the Malayadipatti fragments they make up almost the entire surviving corpus of early Tamil painting.
  - The Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions place Jaina ascetics here in the early historic centuries, long before the paintings. Jainism reached the Tamil country by the last centuries BCE, and the cave shelters of Madurai, Pudukkottai and the northern districts are the material record of that.
  - Rock art and petroglyphs reported at the site suggest use of the shelter before the Jaina occupation, so the sequence runs from prehistoric to monastic to painted.
  - Jainism's retreat in the Tamil country before Shaiva and Vaishnava bhakti from the 7th century is the context for why these sites are ruins rather than living temples, and the persecution narratives in the *Periya Puranam* are the literary side of the same story - to be treated as hagiography rather than record.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8 and 10; ASI.
