- **Location**: The Dharashiv caves, about 8 km from Dharashiv town, formerly Osmanabad, in the Balaghat hills of Marathwada, Maharashtra - basalt country on the Deccan plateau between the Godavari and the Bhima.
- **Period/Level**: Seven caves excavated between roughly the 5th and 7th centuries CE. They began as Buddhist and were converted to Jaina use, the principal shrines now holding tirthankara images, Parshvanatha among them.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A rock-cut group whose conversion from Buddhist to Jaina use is legible in the excavation itself.
  - Cave 2, the largest, follows the Buddhist vihara plan - a pillared hall with cells around it and a shrine at the rear - but the shrine now holds a seated tirthankara with attendant figures. The plan is Buddhist, the occupancy Jaina, and the sequence can be read directly off the stone.
  - The layout is often compared with Ajanta, and the caves are treated as a provincial member of the western Deccan rock-cut tradition that runs from Bhaja and Karle through Ajanta and Ellora.
  - They matter for the wider question of what became of Deccan Buddhism. Rather than sites being destroyed, several were taken over by Jaina and Brahmanical communities as patronage shifted - the same process visible at Ellora, where Buddhist, Hindu and Jaina excavations sit on one cliff.
  - Jaina tradition connects the caves with Karandai and the Jaina presence in Marathwada, and local legend attaches them to the Mahabharata, as the many "Pandav Leni" names across the Deccan do.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
