- **Location**: Kambadahalli, Mandya district, Karnataka, in the Kaveri basin near Shravanabelagola - the name means "village of the pillar", from the free-standing column that stands before the temple.
- **Period/Level**: The Panchakuta Basadi, built under the Western Gangas between about the 9th and 10th centuries CE, with later Hoysala renovation. Dravida idiom in granite and soapstone.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest Ganga-period Jaina temple in Karnataka, and a rare example of the five-shrined *panchakuta* plan.
  - The complex joins five shrines - a *trikuta* group of three and a *dvikuta* pair - around shared halls, dedicated principally to Adinatha with Neminatha and Shantinatha. Multi-shrined temples are common in the Deccan but a five-celled Jaina arrangement of this quality is unusual.
  - The vimanas are stepped Dravida towers in tiers, showing the Ganga adaptation of the southern form, against the Nagara shikharas of contemporary Jaina building in Rajasthan and Gujarat. The regional split in Jaina architecture follows the regional split in Hindu architecture, which is the point to make.
  - The *Brahmadeva pillar* in front, a monolithic column crowned by a seated figure, is characteristic of Karnataka Jaina sites and recurs at Shravanabelagola.
  - The Western Gangas were the great Jaina patrons of the southern Deccan for six centuries - Chamundaraya's Bahubali colossus is the same dynasty's work - and Kambadahalli is their architectural counterpart to that statue.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
