- **Location**: Sittanavasal, Pudukkottai district, Tamil Nadu, in the granite uplands between the Kaveri delta and the Pandya country. The name is read as *Siddhanavasal*, the abode of siddhas. A duplicate record exists in the rock-cut caves chapter and the two should be merged.
- **Period/Level**: Two phases. The *Eladipattam* rock shelter with polished stone beds and Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions belongs to about the 2nd century BCE to the 1st century CE; the *Arivar Koil* rock-cut temple and its paintings are of the 7th century, Pandya period, with a 9th-century inscription of Srivallabha recording renovation.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best surviving Jaina paintings in India, and evidence for Jaina monasticism in the far south from the last centuries BCE.
  - The paintings, in fresco-secco on lime plaster, show a lotus pond with elephants, buffaloes, fish, geese and men gathering flowers, with dancing figures and celestial beings on the ceiling and pillars. They are read as the *samavasarana*, the assembly where a tirthankara preaches, with the pond as the *khatika bhumi* that the liberated soul crosses.
  - Almost nothing else of south Indian painting survives between Ajanta and the Vijayanagara period, so with the fragments at Malayadipatti and Kanchipuram these murals carry the whole intervening tradition.
  - The shelter above, with seventeen polished rock beds each with a raised stone pillow and Tamil-Brahmi donative inscriptions naming monks, documents the austere Jaina monasticism of the Tamil country - the community that produced the *Tirukkural*'s intellectual milieu and later receded before Shaiva bhakti.
  - Those inscriptions are among the earliest written records in Tamil.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8 and 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
