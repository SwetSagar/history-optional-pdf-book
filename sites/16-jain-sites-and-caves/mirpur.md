- **Location**: Mirpur, Sirohi district, southern Rajasthan, near Abu Road in the Aravalli foothills - a short distance from Mount Abu and the Dilwara group.
- **Period/Level**: The Parshvanatha temple, built in white marble and usually assigned to the 9th-10th centuries CE under Rajput patronage. It was damaged, by tradition in Mahmud Begada's Gujarat campaigns, and rebuilt in the 15th century; further restoration followed in the modern period.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Claimed as the oldest marble monument in Rajasthan, and the precursor to Dilwara.
  - If the 9th-10th century dating holds, Mirpur precedes the Vimala Vasahi at Dilwara by more than a century and represents the first use of the Makrana marble tradition that western India later made its own. That claim to priority is the reason the site matters, and it should be stated with the dating caveat attached.
  - The carving - pillared halls, figured brackets, deeply cut ceilings and a richly worked *torana* - is directly comparable to Dilwara, and the two are best studied together as one workshop tradition either side of the Gujarat-Rajasthan border.
  - The temple is dedicated to Parshvanatha, the 23rd tirthankara, whose historicity is generally accepted and who is placed roughly two and a half centuries before Mahavira.
  - Its cycle of destruction and rebuilding is typical of western Indian Jaina temples, and the Jaina practice of *jirnoddhara*, the meritorious restoration of decayed temples, is why so few survive in original fabric.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; ASI.
