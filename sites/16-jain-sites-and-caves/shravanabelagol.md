- **Location**: Shravanabelagola, Hassan district, Karnataka, between the two hills of Vindhyagiri and Chandragiri with a tank - the *belagola*, white pond - between them, in the Hemavati basin of the southern Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: A Jaina centre from about the 3rd century BCE in tradition and certainly from the Western Ganga period. The Bahubali colossus was raised about 981 CE by Chamundaraya, minister and general of the Ganga king Rachamalla IV. Some five hundred inscriptions on the two hills run from about the 6th century to modern times.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal Digambara Jaina site in India, and home to the tallest free-standing monolithic statue in the world.
  - The Gommateshvara or Bahubali image on Vindhyagiri stands about 57 feet, carved from a single block of light grey granite and polished to a high finish. It shows Bahubali in *kayotsarga*, standing in meditation so long that creepers have twined up his legs - the story of a prince who won a war and renounced its fruits.
  - The *Mahamastakabhisheka*, the anointing of the image, is performed roughly every twelve years and is one of the largest Jaina observances anywhere.
  - The inscriptions are the largest single body of Jaina epigraphy in India, recording grants, *sallekhana* deaths and dynastic history from the Gangas, Rashtrakutas, Hoysalas and Vijayanagara. The Bhadrabahu tradition on Chandragiri - the migration from Magadha and Chandragupta Maurya's death by ritual fasting - is treated in the Chandragiri Hill entry in the non-Ashokan inscriptions chapter.
  - Chamundaraya also commissioned the *Chavundaraya Purana* in Kannada prose, so the same patron funded sculpture and literature.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7 and 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Epigraphia Carnatica* II.
