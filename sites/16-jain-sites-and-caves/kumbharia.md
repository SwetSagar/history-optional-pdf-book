- **Location**: Kumbharia, Banaskantha district, northern Gujarat, at the foot of the Aravallis near Ambaji and close to the Rajasthan border - about 25 km from Mount Abu.
- **Period/Level**: Five Jaina temples in white marble built under the Chaulukya (Solanki) kings between the 11th and 13th centuries CE. The Mahavira temple carries an inscription of 1062; the others are dedicated to Parshvanatha, Shantinatha, Neminatha and Sambhavanatha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Solanki marble tradition at its earliest and best, and the immediate context for the more famous temples at Dilwara.
  - The temples are contemporary with the Vimala Vasahi at Dilwara, begun in 1031, and were built by the same regional workshops. Kumbharia is where the Maru-Gurjara marble idiom can be seen taking shape rather than in its finished form.
  - The ceilings are the achievement: concentric rings of carved lotus petals with deeply undercut pendant bosses, and narrative panels of scenes from the lives of the tirthankaras and of Krishna. Comparing a Kumbharia ceiling with a Luna Vasahi ceiling of 1230 shows two centuries of development in one craft.
  - The patrons were merchants and ministers of the Jaina mercantile community rather than kings, following the pattern of Vimala Shah and of Vastupala and Tejpala at Abu. Commercial capital, not royal revenue, paid for western India's finest architecture.
  - The site's proximity to the Shakta shrine at Ambaji, one of the major goddess temples of Gujarat, makes it a good illustration of adjacent and unrelated pilgrimage traditions.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; ASI.
