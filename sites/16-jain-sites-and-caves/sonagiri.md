- **Location**: Sonagiri, Datia district, Madhya Pradesh, on a low hill in the Bundelkhand plateau between Gwalior and Jhansi - the name means "golden peak".
- **Period/Level**: A Digambara Jaina temple hill with some seventy-seven temples on the summit and about twenty-six more in the village below. The earliest shrines are assigned to around the 9th-10th centuries, in the Pratihara and Chandela period, with most of the present buildings of the 17th century onward.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A *siddha-kshetra* of the Digambara tradition, and a clear example of the Indian temple-hill type.
  - Tradition holds that Nanganang Kumar and five and a half crore ascetics attained liberation here, which makes it a place of liberation rather than a place associated with a single teacher - the same category as Shatrunjaya and Shikharji.
  - The principal temple, number 57, holds an image of Chandraprabha, the eighth tirthankara, about 11 feet high. The temples are plain whitewashed structures with tall *shikharas*, deliberately austere compared with the marble work of Dilwara and Kumbharia.
  - The three great Digambara temple-hills - Sonagiri and Sammed Shikhar in the north and east, with Shatrunjaya as the Svetambara counterpart in the west - map the sectarian geography of Jainism, and the split between Digambara and Svetambara is best taught through which communities hold which sites.
  - Bundelkhand's Jaina heritage runs from here through the Chandela Jain group at Khajuraho to the colossi at Gwalior, so the region has a continuous Jaina record from the 9th century.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI.
