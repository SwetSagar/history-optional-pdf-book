- **Location**: Parasnath hill, also called Shikharji or Sammed Shikhar, Giridih district, Jharkhand, rising to about 1,365 m - the highest peak in the state, on the Chhotanagpur plateau.
- **Period/Level**: A Jaina tirtha of the greatest antiquity in tradition, associated with the historical Parshvanatha, the 23rd tirthankara, generally placed in the 8th century BCE. The *tonks* or shrines along the summit ridge are mostly of the 18th and 19th centuries, rebuilt repeatedly on older sites.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The holiest Jaina pilgrimage site of all - where twenty of the twenty-four tirthankaras are held to have attained *moksha*.
  - The concentration of liberations makes Shikharji a *siddha-kshetra* of the first rank, ahead of even Shatrunjaya for many communities. Pilgrims walk a circuit of about 27 km over the ridge, visiting each tonk.
  - Its position matters historically: the hill lies in the old Magadhan sphere where Jainism first took shape, and the association with Parshvanatha connects it to the pre-Mahavira phase of the religion. Parshvanatha's four vows, to which Mahavira added celibacy as the fifth, are the standard evidence that Jainism predates Mahavira rather than beginning with him.
  - Both Digambaras and Svetambaras hold the site sacred and have long disputed its control, a conflict that has generated litigation into the present.
  - The surrounding forest and the Santal communities of the plateau give the hill a second, unrelated significance in discussions of tribal Jharkhand.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
