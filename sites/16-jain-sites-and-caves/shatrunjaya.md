- **Location**: Shatrunjaya hill above Palitana, Bhavnagar district, Gujarat, rising some 600 m from the Saurashtra plain near the Shetrunji river - a ridge crowned entirely by temples.
- **Period/Level**: A tirtha from early centuries in tradition, with the surviving temples built and rebuilt from the 11th century onward. Most of what stands is of the 16th to 19th centuries, following destruction attributed to Sultanate and later raids; the Adinatha temple was rebuilt in 1530.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest temple city in the world - some 863 shrines on one hill - and the supreme tirtha of the Svetambara Jains.
  - Jaina tradition holds that Rishabha, the first tirthankara, preached his first sermon here, and that countless ascetics attained liberation on the hill. It is a *siddha-kshetra*, a place of liberation, which is what gives it precedence over sites associated with a single teacher.
  - No one lives on the summit. The temples are locked at dusk and the priests descend, so the hill is a city of gods rather than of people - an arrangement that has no parallel and is the detail worth citing.
  - The temples are grouped in nine walled enclosures, *tuks*, each funded by a merchant family or community, in Maru-Gurjara marble work descended from the Solanki idiom seen at Dilwara and Kumbharia.
  - The scale of it is evidence for the wealth of Gujarati Jaina mercantile communities and for their practice of converting commercial profit into religious endowment - the same pattern that built Dilwara and Ranakpur.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; ASI.
