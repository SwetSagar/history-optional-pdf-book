- **Location**: Shri Mahavirji, Karauli district, eastern Rajasthan, on the bank of the Gambhir river in the tract between the Chambal and the Banas - roughly midway between Jaipur and Agra.
- **Period/Level**: A modern Digambara pilgrimage complex grown around an image of Mahavira said to have been recovered from a mound at the village of Chandangaon in the 18th century. The main temple and the five associated shrines date from the 18th and 19th centuries, with later additions.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal Digambara pilgrimage centre of Rajasthan, and an example of a tirtha created in the modern period rather than inherited from antiquity.
  - Most of the sites in this chapter are ancient, and Shri Mahavirji is useful precisely because it is not. It shows the mechanism by which Indian sacred places come into being: a chance find, a local cult, a community's endowment, and within two centuries a major pilgrimage with its own fair and institutions.
  - The complex is built in red sandstone with *shikharas* in the north Indian idiom, and includes the Kamal Mandir, the Shantinatha temple and the Bhagwan Mahavira temple with the recovered image.
  - It is a Digambara site, and its growth in eastern Rajasthan from the 18th century tracks the prosperity of Jaina trading communities in the region under Jaipur and Karauli patronage.
  - The annual Mahavir Jayanti fair draws pilgrims from across northern India, and local Meena and other communities participate - the same cross-community pattern seen at Ramdevra elsewhere in Rajasthan.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; *Rajasthan District Gazetteer*, Karauli.
