- **Location**: Pawapuri, Nalanda district, Bihar, in the plain south of the Ganga between Rajgir and Bihar Sharif - in the heartland of ancient Magadha, close to Nalanda and Rajagriha.
- **Period/Level**: The place where Mahavira attained *nirvana*, traditionally in 527 BCE by Jaina reckoning, though most modern scholars place his death nearer 468 BCE. The Jal Mandir, standing in the middle of a lotus tank, marks the cremation site; the present structure is a later rebuilding.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site of Mahavira's death, and the eastern anchor of the Jaina sacred geography of Magadha.
  - Jaina tradition holds that so much earth was taken from around the cremation site by devotees that a tank formed, which is why the principal temple stands on an island reached by a causeway. Whatever its origin, the arrangement is unusual and memorable.
  - Mahavira's life joins the Magadhan sites: born at Kundagrama near Vaishali, he renounced, attained *kevala jnana* at Jrimbhikagrama on the Rijupalika, preached at Rajagriha and died here. That circuit is the geography of early Jainism, and it overlaps almost exactly with the Buddha's, in the same kingdoms and the same decades.
  - His death is the fixed point of the Jaina calendar, from which the Vira Nirvana Samvat era is reckoned - the era still used in Jaina inscriptions and manuscripts, beginning 527 BCE.
  - Tradition also holds that Pawapuri is where the Jaina community divided on the succession, and the councils that followed at Pataliputra and Valabhi attempted to fix the canon.
  - *Sources*: R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Upinder Singh, ch. 6.
