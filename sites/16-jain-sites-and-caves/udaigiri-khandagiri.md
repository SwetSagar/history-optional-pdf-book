- **Location**: The twin hills of Udayagiri and Khandagiri, about 6 km west of Bhubaneswar, Odisha, facing each other across a ravine near the ancient city of Sisupalgarh.
- **Period/Level**: Thirty-three rock-cut caves, eighteen on Udayagiri and fifteen on Khandagiri, excavated for Jaina ascetics under Kharavela of the Mahameghavahana line, generally 1st century BCE, with some later medieval additions and Jain images of the 10th-12th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest Jaina rock-cut monastic complex in India, and the physical expression of Kharavela's patronage.
  - The caves are *lena* - austere dormitories for monks, with low ceilings, sloping floors and stone pillows cut into the rock, quite unlike the spacious Buddhist viharas of the western Deccan. Comfort was not the point, and the contrast with Ajanta or Karle is worth drawing.
  - The Ranigumpha, Cave 1, is the largest and the exception: two storeyed, with sculpted friezes of processions, hunts, dancers and a scene often read as the abduction of a woman, and with pillared verandahs. It is the finest early sculpture in eastern India.
  - The Hathigumpha on Udayagiri carries Kharavela's seventeen-line inscription, the fullest royal autobiography from ancient India. See the Hathigumpha entry in the non-Ashokan inscriptions chapter for its content.
  - The Ganesha Gumpha, Bagha Gumpha and Sarpa Gumpha take their names from the animal forms carved at their entrances. Khandagiri's later caves hold tirthankara reliefs showing continued Jaina use for a thousand years.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
