- **Location**: Kozhikode, the Calicut of European and Arabic sources, on the Malabar coast of Kerala between the Ghats and the Arabian Sea, with the open roadstead at Kappad a little to the north.
- **Period/Level**: A Chera-period settlement that became the seat of the Zamorin (Samoothiri) from about the 13th century and the dominant Malabar port through the 14th-16th centuries. Vasco da Gama landed at Kappad on 17 May 1498; Portuguese conflict with the Zamorin runs from 1500 to the 1560s.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The port where the European age in Indian history begins, and the best-documented case of an Indian state resisting it at sea.
  - Ibn Battuta, visiting in the 1340s, ranked it among the greatest ports in the world and described Chinese junks wintering in its harbour - evidence that the Indian Ocean was a Chinese as well as an Arab and Indian sea before the Portuguese arrived. Zheng He's fleets called here.
  - The Zamorin's prosperity rested on pepper and on his guarantee of security to the Muslim Mappila merchants who handled it. His refusal to exclude them is what made conflict with the Portuguese *cartaz* system inevitable, rather than any religious quarrel.
  - The Kunjali Marakkars, the Zamorin's admirals, fought the Portuguese at sea for nearly a century - the one sustained Indian naval resistance of the period, and the standard counter to the claim that Indian states ignored sea power.
  - The city's cotton cloth gave English the word *calico*.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*.
