- **Location**: Muziris, Sangam-age Muchiri, on the Periyar near Kodungallur (Cranganore), Ernakulam district, Kerala. The archaeological site is at Pattanam, north of the present river mouth; the harbour is thought to have been destroyed by the Periyar flood of 1341.
- **Period/Level**: The premier Chera port of the Sangam age, active from about the 1st century BCE to the 4th century CE and referred to into the medieval period. Pattanam excavations have yielded Roman amphorae, glass and semi-precious stone beads, and a wharf with a dugout canoe.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The western terminus of the Roman pepper trade, and the subject of the single most informative document in Indian economic history.
  - The *Muziris Papyrus*, a 2nd-century CE contract now in Vienna, records a loan between an Alexandrian financier and a merchant for a cargo carried from Muziris on the ship *Hermapollon* - pepper, ivory and nard, valued at nearly seven million sesterces, with duty and security terms spelled out. It converts a trade known from anecdote into figures.
  - Pliny called Muziris *primum emporium Indiae* and complained that India drained Rome of its gold; the Peutinger Table marks a Temple of Augustus here, implying a resident Roman community.
  - Sangam poems describe *yavana* vessels arriving with gold and departing with pepper, churning the Periyar's water - literary confirmation of the same traffic.
  - Kerala's early Jewish, Christian and later Muslim communities are tied to this coast, so the port also anchors questions on the entry of those religions into India.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
