- **Location**: Debal or Daybul, at the mouth of the Indus in Sindh, Pakistan, generally identified with the excavated port of Banbhore east of Karachi on the Gharo creek - a channel the Indus has long since abandoned.
- **Period/Level**: Founded in the early centuries CE and prominent through the 7th-13th, it was the landing point of Muhammad bin Qasim's expedition in 711-712 CE and remained Sindh's chief port under Arab rule before silting and the river's shift left it stranded.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The port at which the Arab conquest of Sindh began, and the site of the earliest dated mosque in the subcontinent.
  - The *Chachnama* records that the seizure of Arab ships off Debal - carrying, in the account, gifts and Muslim women from Sri Lanka - gave Hajjaj the pretext for the invasion. The campaign opened with the storming of Debal's great temple, whose flagstaff the Arabs brought down.
  - Banbhore has yielded a fortified town with a congregational mosque bearing an inscription of 727 CE, the earliest dated mosque in South Asia, alongside Sassanian, Islamic and Chinese pottery and a working industrial quarter.
  - Its exports were cotton cloth, indigo and leather, and the town was known for sword-making; the route inland carried goods to Multan and on to Khurasan, so Debal joins the sea and the Central Asian land routes.
  - The site is also standard evidence in the argument that the Arab conquest of Sindh was a commercially motivated frontier operation with limited wider consequence, rather than the beginning of Muslim rule in India proper.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; *Chachnama*.
