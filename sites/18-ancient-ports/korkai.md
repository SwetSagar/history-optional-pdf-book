- **Location**: Korkai, Thoothukudi district, Tamil Nadu, on the Thamirabarani river near the Gulf of Mannar. Silting has left the site some 6 km inland from the present shore, so the ancient harbour is now dry land.
- **Period/Level**: The earliest Pandya capital and port, flourishing c. 3rd century BCE to 3rd century CE. Excavation has yielded Black-and-Red Ware, potsherds of c. 200 BCE-200 CE, and thick deposits of pearl-oyster shell. Known to the *Periplus* and Ptolemy as Kolkhoi.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The centre of the ancient pearl fishery - a port built on one commodity, and the Pandya kingdom's origin.
  - The Gulf of Mannar pearl banks made Korkai famous across the ancient world. The *Periplus* records pearls fished by condemned criminals, and Sangam poems return repeatedly to Korkai's pearls; Roman demand for them is part of the same trade that brought gold to the Tamil coast.
  - Beds of oyster shell in the excavated levels confirm the literary claim directly, which is why Korkai is cited as a case where Sangam poetry can be checked against material evidence.
  - It was the Pandya capital before the move inland to Madurai, with Kayal serving later as the port. The sequence Korkai, Kayal, Tuticorin traces one harbour system retreating seaward as the Thamirabarani silted.
  - Marco Polo and the Arab geographers describe the same fishery continuing into the medieval centuries, giving the industry a documented life of well over a thousand years.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*.
