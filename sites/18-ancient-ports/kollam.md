- **Location**: Kollam, the Quilon of European sources, on the Ashtamudi backwater near the Arabian Sea in southern Kerala - a lagoon harbour sheltered from the open coast.
- **Period/Level**: Refounded in 825 CE, the year from which the Malayalam *Kollam* era is reckoned. It was the chief port of the Venad rulers and a great emporium through the 9th-15th centuries, raided by Malik Kafur's forces in 1311 and visited by Marco Polo, Ibn Battuta and the Chinese fleets.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The port that produced the finest surviving document of multi-religious commerce in early medieval India.
  - The *Tharisapalli* copper plates of c. 849 CE, issued by Ayyanadikal Thiruvadikal of Venad, grant land and privileges to a Syrian Christian church and place it under the protection of the merchant guilds *Anjuvannam* and *Manigramam*, with Jewish and Muslim witnesses signing in Hebrew, Pahlavi and Kufic Arabic. One plate records Christians, Jews and Muslims guaranteeing a Hindu ruler's grant.
  - That the Kollam era is still used in Kerala shows how decisively the port's refounding reorganised regional life.
  - Arab writers record Chinese vessels transshipping cargo here rather than continuing west, making Kollam the pivot between the eastern and western halves of the Indian Ocean trade.
  - The trading guilds named in the plates are the same corporations attested across south India and Southeast Asia, so the document is used to argue for organised merchant capital in early medieval India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; *Travancore Archaeological Series* (Tharisapalli plates).
