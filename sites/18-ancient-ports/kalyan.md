- **Location**: Kalyan, Thane district, Maharashtra, at the head of the Ulhas creek on the Konkan coast - the inland end of a tidal inlet, reached from the Deccan through the Naneghat and Bhor passes.
- **Period/Level**: The *Kalliena* of the *Periplus*, active from the early centuries CE under the Satavahanas and Western Kshatrapas, still notable in the 6th century, and later held in turn by the Sultanate, the Portuguese, the Mughals, the Marathas and the British.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A port whose classical description records commercial warfare between two Indian dynasties - the sharpest such passage in ancient Indian sources.
  - The *Periplus* states that Kalliena was a lawful market in the time of Saraganus the elder - Satakarni of the Satavahanas - but that after Sandanes, the Western Kshatrapa power, took possession, Greek ships putting in there were seized and taken under guard to Barygaza. It is direct evidence that control of ports was a weapon in the Satavahana-Kshatrapa struggle.
  - Its hinterland routes tie it to the Naneghat inscription and the Buddhist caves at Kanheri, Karle and Bhaja, all of which were endowed by merchants and guilds using this corridor. Port, pass and cave monastery form a single economic unit.
  - Cosmas Indicopleustes in the 6th century records Kalliana as a place of trade with a resident Christian community under a bishop consecrated in Persia - early evidence of the Church of the East on the Indian coast.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
