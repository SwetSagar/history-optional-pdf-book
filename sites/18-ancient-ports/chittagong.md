- **Location**: Chattogram, on the Karnaphuli river near its mouth on the Bay of Bengal, south-eastern Bangladesh - the only deep-water harbour on the deltaic coast, where the hills reach the sea.
- **Period/Level**: A port from the early medieval period, described by Ibn Battuta in the 1340s. Contested from the 15th century between the sultans of Bengal, the Arakanese kings of Mrauk-U and Portuguese freebooters, until Shaista Khan, Aurangzeb's governor of Bengal, took it in 1666 and renamed it Islamabad.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Bay of Bengal's principal eastern port, and the frontier where Mughal authority finally met the sea.
  - The Portuguese called it *Porto Grande*, against the *Porto Pequeno* of Satgaon, and the pair is the standard way of describing Bengal's two medieval outlets to the sea.
  - From the late 16th century the Arakanese and the Portuguese *Feringhi* operated a slave-raiding trade up the Bengal rivers, depopulating parts of the delta. Shaista Khan's campaign of 1665-66, using a purpose-built river fleet, ended it - one of the few Mughal operations that was genuinely naval.
  - Its capture completed Mughal control of Bengal's coastline and secured the revenue-richest province of the empire, which is why the episode appears in questions on Aurangzeb's eastern policy rather than only in local history.
  - The port linked Bengal cotton and rice to Arakan, Burma, Malacca and beyond, and remained the outlet for the eastern delta under the British.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*.
