- **Location**: Kannur, the Cannanore of European sources, on the north Malabar coast of Kerala, with the harbour at Ayikkara and the headland later crowned by St Angelo fort.
- **Period/Level**: The port of the Kolathiri rajas of Kolathunadu, prominent in the 12th-15th centuries in trade with Persia and Arabia. The Portuguese built St Angelo in 1505 under Francisco de Almeida; the Dutch took it in 1663 and sold it to the Arakkal family in 1772; the British held it as their western-coast military headquarters until 1887.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The horse port of the Malabar coast, and the seat of Kerala's only Muslim ruling house.
  - Medieval Indian states had no domestic supply of cavalry horses, and Kannur was one of the principal points at which Arabian and Persian horses were landed for the Deccan and south Indian kingdoms. Marco Polo remarks on the traffic. The horse trade is a standard theme in questions on why coastal control mattered to inland powers.
  - The Arakkal Ali Rajas, based here, were the only Muslim dynasty to rule in Kerala, held the Lakshadweep islands, and maintained a fleet - an unusual case of a Muslim maritime principality within a Hindu regional order.
  - The Kolathiri's rivalry with the Zamorin of Kozhikode gave the Portuguese their opening: Almeida was welcomed at Kannur precisely because Calicut resisted him. European penetration exploited existing Indian rivalries rather than creating them.
  - Pepper, cardamom and sandalwood were the outbound cargoes.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I and II; ASI.
