- **Location**: Arikamedu, on the Ariyankuppam river about 4 km south of Puducherry town, on the Coromandel coast - a sandbar site at a river mouth, the standard setting for an east-coast anchorage.
- **Period/Level**: Occupied from about the 2nd century BCE to the 2nd century CE, with later phases. Excavated by Jouveau-Dubreuil, then Mortimer Wheeler in 1945, and later by Casal and Vimala Begley. Generally identified with the *Poduke* of the *Periplus* and Ptolemy.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of Indo-Roman trade, and the site whose reinterpretation changed how that trade is understood.
  - The Roman material is unambiguous: Arretine ware, the red-gloss Italian tableware stamped with its potters' names, amphorae that carried wine and olive oil, Roman lamps, glass and engraved gems - the largest such assemblage in India.
  - Alongside it lies a fully local industry. Brick-built dyeing vats, tanks and warehouse structures point to muslin being dyed and finished for export, and the site produced the rouletted ware and Black-and-Red Ware of the Tamil country.
  - Wheeler read it as a Roman trading station, almost a colony. Begley's later work showed a substantial Indian settlement that predated the Roman contact and continued after it, trading with Rome rather than serving it. That correction - from Roman outpost to Indian port with Roman customers - is the point to make, and a good example of how colonial-era archaeology overstated external agency.
  - Sangam poems describing *yavana* ships and the wine they brought corroborate the material record, letting literature and excavation be read against each other.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; Vimala Begley, *The Ancient Port of Arikamedu*.
