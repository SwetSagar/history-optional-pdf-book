- **Location**: Karachi, Sindh, Pakistan, on the Arabian Sea west of the Indus delta, where the coast turns towards the Makran - the last sheltered anchorage before the desert shore.
- **Period/Level**: Identified with the *Krokola* from which Alexander's admiral Nearchus began his voyage back along the coast in 325 BCE. The Indus-mouth ports of the *Periplus* lie close by. The modern city grew from the fishing settlement of Kolachi in the 18th century; the British took it in 1839 and made it the port of the Indus.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The maritime gateway of the north-west, where the Indus route meets the sea and the overland Iranian routes meet both.
  - Nearchus's voyage, recorded by Arrian, is the earliest detailed account of navigation on the Indian coast and fixes the site in the historical record from the 4th century BCE.
  - The *Periplus* describes this stretch as the outlet for goods brought down the Indus and overland from Bactria - turquoise from Khurasan and lapis lazuli from Badakhshan passing through to Egypt, with indigo, cotton and silk going west and topaz, coral, glass and silver plate coming in.
  - Because lapis and turquoise reach the Mediterranean through here, the port ties Indian maritime trade to the Central Asian caravan system, and is the natural companion to Barbarikon in discussing the Indus estuary's trade.
  - The nineteenth-century development, with rail links to Punjab, made it the outlet for wheat and cotton and set the pattern of colonial port cities built to drain a hinterland.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
