- **Location**: Nirppeyarru, a Pallava port town of the Tamil coast known from inscriptions rather than from surviving remains. It is generally placed at the mouth of the Palar, in the Kanchipuram-Chengalpattu tract, with the excavated coastal site of Vasavasamudram the most-cited candidate.
- **Period/Level**: Pallava period, c. 7th-9th centuries CE, the same phase in which Mamallapuram was the dynasty's principal harbour. It appears in Pallava and later Chola records as a port and a centre of revenue administration.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Evidence that the Pallavas worked a coastline of several harbours rather than a single port - and a caution about how much of that coastline is now lost.
  - Pallava maritime activity is usually reduced to Mamallapuram. The inscriptions name other landing places on the same stretch, of which Nirppeyarru is one, indicating a network of small anchorages at river mouths serving the capital at Kanchipuram.
  - Its identification is unsettled, and the honest position is that the name is secure while the place is not. Shifting river mouths and a prograding coast have buried or washed away most early Tamil harbour sites, which is why so many are known only from texts.
  - Vasavasamudram, at the Palar mouth, has yielded rouletted ware and Roman-period material, showing the estuary was in use; whether it is Nirppeyarru remains argued.
  - The wider point is methodological: for early port history the epigraphic and literary record runs well ahead of the archaeological one, and a map entry must say so rather than pretend to a fixed location.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI, *South Indian Inscriptions*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
