- **Location**: Kuntasi, Morbi district, Gujarat, on the Phulki river near the head of the Gulf of Kutch - a small mound on the Saurashtra coast, not in Maharashtra as some note-sets have it.
- **Period/Level**: Harappan, with a Mature phase of c. 2200-1900 BCE and a Late Harappan reoccupation to c. 1700 BCE. Excavated by M. K. Dhavalikar; the site covers barely two hectares.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A specialised Harappan manufacturing and shipping outpost - evidence that the civilisation's coastal network included small industrial stations, not only cities.
  - The settlement is dominated by workshops rather than housing: bead-making debris in carnelian, agate and steatite, shell-working, copper smelting, and grinding and storage areas, with a boundary wall and a watchtower.
  - A stone platform and a projecting structure by the creek have been read as a jetty or loading point. With Lothal's basin, it is part of the argument that the Harappans maintained deliberate maritime infrastructure on the Gujarat coast.
  - Raw materials came from far inland and beyond - agate from the Rajpipla hills, copper from Rajasthan or Oman - and finished beads went out by sea towards the Gulf. The site therefore documents a full production chain compressed into one small place.
  - Its abandonment and reduced Late Harappan reoccupation, with coarser pottery and no workshop output, is a compact illustration of the deurbanisation visible across the Harappan world after c. 1900 BCE.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; M. K. Dhavalikar, *Kuntasi: A Harappan Emporium*.
