- **Location**: Mylapore, now a quarter of Chennai, on the Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu just south of the Cooum mouth - an open shore with no natural harbour, worked by surf boats.
- **Period/Level**: An ancient settlement and port, active under the Pallavas and Cholas and trading into the medieval centuries; the Portuguese founded San Thome here in 1523, well before the English built Fort St George at Madras in 1639.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The focus of the St Thomas tradition in India, and the Portuguese settlement that Madras grew up beside.
  - Tradition places the martyrdom and burial of the apostle Thomas at Mylapore, and the San Thome basilica is one of only three churches in the world built over an apostle's reputed tomb. Whatever its historicity, the tradition is central to the self-understanding of the Syrian Christian community and to arguments about Christianity's antiquity in India.
  - As a Chola-period port it exported cotton textiles and muslin eastward to Malacca and Southeast Asia, and earlier traded with the Greek world; Ptolemy's coastal list is usually taken to include it.
  - The Kapaleeshwarar temple marks it as a Shaiva centre of the Nayanmar tradition, and Tiruvalluvar, author of the *Tirukkural*, is by tradition associated with the town.
  - Mylapore is a useful corrective to the idea that European port cities were founded on empty coast: Madras was built next to a settlement already more than a thousand years old.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; ASI.
