- **Location**: Machilipatnam, also Masulipatnam or Bandar, Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh, on the Coromandel coast just north of the Krishna delta.
- **Period/Level**: A port from the Satavahana period, named *Maisolia* by Ptolemy and *Masalia* in the *Periplus*. Its great age is the 15th-17th centuries as the principal seaport of the Qutb Shahi kingdom of Golconda; the Dutch established a factory in 1605 and the English their first Coromandel factory in 1611. It declined after the cyclone of 1779 and the rise of Madras.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The export point for the Coromandel textile trade - the industry that drew European companies to India in the first place.
  - The *Periplus* already singles out Masalia for its fine muslin, and the region's cloth is the probable origin of the word. Two thousand years of continuous textile export from one stretch of coast is the site's main claim.
  - *Kalamkari*, hand-painted and block-printed cotton using natural mordants and dyes, was produced in its hinterland for markets in Persia and Southeast Asia. Designs were made to order for particular foreign markets, which is early evidence of production tailored to export demand.
  - As Golconda's port it handled diamonds from the Kollur mines along with cloth, and its customs revenue was a major item in Qutb Shahi finances.
  - The Dutch and English factories here predate Madras and Calcutta, so Machilipatnam is where European commercial settlement on the east coast begins.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
