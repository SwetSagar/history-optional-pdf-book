- **Location**: Puhar or Kaveripattinam, Mayiladuthurai district, Tamil Nadu, where the Kaveri enters the Bay of Bengal - much of the ancient town now lies under the sea and the shifting river mouth.
- **Period/Level**: Port and capital of the early Cholas in the Sangam age, c. 2nd century BCE to 3rd century CE. Known to the *Periplus* as Camara and to Ptolemy; excavation has recovered brick structures, a wharf and Roman material, with offshore remains found by marine survey.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-described city of early Tamilakam - a place where literature gives a town plan that archaeology can then test.
  - The *Pattinappalai* describes the harbour's warehouses, the customs officers who stamped bonded goods with the tiger seal of the Cholas, and foreign merchants living in their own quarter. The tiger-seal detail is the standard evidence for organised customs administration in the Sangam period.
  - The fifth book of the *Silappadikaram* divides the city into Maruvurpakkam by the shore, where the yavana and other foreign traders lived, and Pattinappakkam inland, the royal and brahmana quarter - a documented segregation of commercial and courtly space.
  - The *Manimekalai* is set here too, and records Buddhist and Jain establishments, so the city supports questions on religion in the Tamil country as well as on trade.
  - Its submergence, attributed in the epics to the sea's anger, is corroborated by coastal change, and Puhar is the usual example of an ancient Indian port lost to the sea rather than to conquest.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Upinder Singh, ch. 8.
