- **Location**: Bharuch, ancient Barygaza or Bhrigukachchha, on the north bank of the Narmada near its mouth on the Gulf of Khambhat, Gujarat - the outlet of the whole Narmada corridor into the Arabian Sea.
- **Period/Level**: An international port from pre-Mauryan times through the Satavahana, Western Kshatrapa, Gupta and Gurjara periods, and still important under the Sultanate and the Mughals. Its classical description is in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*, c. 1st century CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal western port of ancient India, and the sea end of the two great overland routes.
  - Two arms of the Uttarapatha converged here: one from Taxila through the Punjab, down the Yamuna to Mathura, then Ujjain to Bharuch; the other from Taxila down the lower Indus and along the coast. A port fed by inland highways is the clearest illustration of how India's overland and maritime trade were one system.
  - The *Periplus* gives unusual navigational detail - the treacherous approach through shoals and the Narmada's tidal bore, with royal pilots sent out to bring ships in. It also records the exchange of gifts with the local king, evidence of state supervision of foreign trade.
  - Exports were cotton cloth, spices, ivory, agate and carnelian; imports wine, copper, tin, lead, coral, glass and Roman coin. Bead-making and ring-well construction are attested archaeologically.
  - It changed hands between the Satavahanas and the Western Kshatrapas repeatedly, so control of Bharuch is a proxy for who held the Deccan trade in the early centuries CE.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
