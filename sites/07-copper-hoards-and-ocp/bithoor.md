- **Location**: Bithur, Kanpur district, Uttar Pradesh, on the right bank of the Ganga in the central doab.
- **Period/Level**: The findspot of 1822 - the first copper hoard object ever recorded in India, a copper harpoon. The hoards as a class are placed between the mid-3rd and the 2nd millennium BCE, but almost all were chance finds without stratigraphy.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The discovery that created the category: every discussion of the copper hoards begins here.
  - The 1822 harpoon was followed by flat celts, shouldered celts and further harpoons from the same tract, part of a large hoard.
  - Since 1822 over 1,300 copper objects of this range have been found at about 90 sites, from the upper Ganga valley to Bengal and Odisha, with outliers in Haryana, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu. The greatest concentration is the Uttar Pradesh doab; hoards contain 1 to 47 objects, the sole exception being Gungeria in Madhya Pradesh with 424 copper objects weighing over 200 kg and 102 silver plates.
  - Because the finds are unstratified, dating rests on the rare excavated cases - Saipai in Etawah district, where copper hoard objects came from an OCP level, and Sanauli, where two antennae swords occurred in a late Harappan grave.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; ASI, *IAR*.
