- **Location**: Kallur, Raichur district, northern Karnataka, in the Raichur doab between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra - the southernmost copper hoard findspot on the standard distribution map.
- **Period/Level**: A neolithic-megalithic settlement of the Karnataka plateau whose copper hoard is placed in the late 2nd to early 1st millennium BCE, at the neolithic-megalithic overlap where the region's earliest metal appears.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Carries the copper hoard tradition a thousand kilometres south of the doab, and so defines the outer limit of the phenomenon.
  - Famous for its antennae swords, the longest exceeding 36 inches - the same distinctive type found in the Uttar Pradesh and Haryana hoards, and recently in a late Harappan grave at Sanauli with a copper sheath.
  - The presence of doab types this far south is the main evidence for Upinder Singh's conclusion that the upper Ganga valley emerged as a distinct copper-manufacturing area between the mid-3rd and 2nd millennia BCE, with interactions reaching Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, the Deccan, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.
  - Typology varies by region: flat, shouldered and bar celts and double axes dominate the eastern zone of Bihar, Bengal and Odisha; these occur with anthropomorphs, antennae swords, hooked swords and harpoons in Uttar Pradesh and Haryana; Rajasthan yields mainly flat and bar celts.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (Map 5.3); ASI, *IAR*.
