- **Location**: Bargaon, Saharanpur district, western Uttar Pradesh, in the upper Ganga-Yamuna doab beside Ambakheri and Hulas, in the Hindon-Krishni drainage.
- **Period/Level**: Late Harappan to Ochre Coloured Pottery, c. 1900-1300 BCE. The OCP phase as a whole carries thermoluminescence dates of 2650-1180 BCE from Atranjikhera, Lal Qila, Nasirpur and Jhinjhina, making it a late contemporary of the mature and late Harappan cultures.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type locality for the late Harappan-OCP overlap, and the centre of the densest OCP concentration in the subcontinent.
  - Bargaon and Ambakheri are the two sites where late Harappan and OCP levels overlap, which is the main evidence for those who read OCP as a degenerate late Harappan ware rather than an independent tradition.
  - Bargaon anchors the western OCP zone, which retains Harappan links (with Jodhpura, Siswal, Mitathal, Bara and Ambakheri), as against the eastern zone of Lal Qila, Atranjikhera and Saipai, which shows none.
  - Over 80 OCP sites have been identified in Saharanpur district alone, spaced 4-6 km apart - a dense village landscape. Finds include a fragmentary copper chisel and rings, chert blades, querns, bone points, terracotta cakes, carts and wheels, and beads of steatite, agate, carnelian and faience.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4-5; Mughal and Dikshit on the Saharanpur OCP survey.
