- **Location**: Bisauli, Budaun district, western Uttar Pradesh, in the Ganga-Ramganga tract of the upper doab.
- **Period/Level**: With Rajpur Parsu (Bijnor), the site at which Ochre Coloured Pottery was first recognised, in 1950-51 by B. B. Lal. The copper hoard objects are attributed to the 2nd millennium BCE, though most were recovered without stratigraphic context.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Joint type site of the OCP culture, and the source of the anthropomorph that defines the copper hoard debate.
  - Ill-fired wheel-made pottery of fine to medium fabric with a thick red slip, decorated with black bands, carrying incised designs and post-firing graffiti.
  - The Bisauli copper anthropomorph: cast and then hammered, with in-curved arms sharpened on the outer edge and plain outstretched legs, the head thickened by beating. Anthropomorphs run 25-45 cm in length and 30-43 cm in breadth and weigh up to 5 kg; length normally exceeds breadth, and the Bisauli piece is the recorded exception.
  - The function of the anthropomorph is unsettled: D. P. Agrawal proposed a throwing weapon with a whirling boomerang effect for killing birds, while others argue for a ritual object - tiny figures of the same shape are still worshipped in north India as Shani. The 2001 find of 31 anthropomorphs at Madarpur (Moradabad), stacked in situ in a deposit with OCP, favours the second reading.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; B. B. Lal, *Ancient India* 1950-51; D. V. Sharma et al. (2001-02).
