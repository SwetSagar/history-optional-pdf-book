- **Location**: Rajpur Parsu in Bijnor district, western Uttar Pradesh, on the upper Ganga where the river leaves the Siwaliks - the northern edge of the OCP distribution, which runs up to Bahadarabad near Hardwar.
- **Period/Level**: With Bisauli, one of the two sites where Ochre Coloured Pottery was first identified, in 1950-51, in B. B. Lal's excavations. The OCP phase runs c. 2000-1500 BCE within the wider thermoluminescence bracket of 2650-1180 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A type site of the OCP culture - the ware that gives the whole post-Harappan doab its archaeological name.
  - The pottery is ill-fired and wheel-made, of fine to medium fabric, with a thick red slip sometimes decorated in black bands, and incised designs and post-firing graffiti on some sherds. Rubbed, it leaves an ochre smear on the fingers - from waterlogging, wind action, poor firing, or all three - which is what named it.
  - Rajpur Parsu also yielded a copper hoard, including nine flat celts and harpoons, making it one of the few places where the two defining traits of the culture - OCP and copper hoard objects - occur together.
  - B. B. Lal noted the resemblance of this ware to the ceramic industry of the lowest level at Hastinapura, where OCP is followed by a break in occupation and then Painted Grey Ware - the sequence on which doab chronology rests.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; B. B. Lal, *Ancient India* 1950-51 and 1954-55.
