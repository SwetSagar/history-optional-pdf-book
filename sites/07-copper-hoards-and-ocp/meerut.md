- **Location**: Meerut district, western Uttar Pradesh, in the Ganga-Yamuna doab on the Hindon; one of the four districts - with Saharanpur, Muzaffarnagar and Bulandshahr - holding the densest OCP concentration.
- **Period/Level**: Ochre Coloured Pottery, c. 2000-1500 BCE, overlapping the late Harappan occupation of the same district at Alamgirpur. OCP deposits here are shallow, 0.5-1.5 m thick, and the settlements small, usually 200-300 sq m.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The core OCP district, and the tract that yields both the anthropomorph hoards and the late Harappan graves that date them.
  - Wattle-and-daub houses with mud floors and post-holes are the norm; at Atranjikhera the posts were of babul, sissoo, sal and chir pine, with an unlined well. Lal Qila in Bulandshahr district, the largest excavated OCP settlement at 632 sq m, gave oblong wattle-and-daub structures, a few sun-dried bricks with mud mortar, five copper objects, bone tools and terracotta anthropomorphic and animal figurines.
  - Lal Qila yielded wheat, barley and rice, and Atranjikhera rice, barley, gram and khesari - two crops a year, rice in summer and barley and legumes in winter. Circular fire pits at Lal Qila held charred bones of cattle, buffalo, goat, sheep, pig, horse, dog and wild deer, many with cut marks.
  - At Sanauli in the neighbouring Baghpat district, D. V. Sharma's excavations found a late Harappan cemetery with two copper hoard-type antennae swords, one in situ in a grave with a copper sheath - the firmest link yet between the copper hoards and a datable culture.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; D. V. Sharma et al., Sanauli reports; ASI, *IAR*.
