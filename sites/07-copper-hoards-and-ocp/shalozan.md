- **Location**: Shalozan, in the upper Kurram valley on the Pakistan-Afghanistan frontier (Kurram district, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa), on the route from the Indus plains through the Peiwar Kotal into Afghanistan.
- **Period/Level**: A copper hoard findspot of the 2nd millennium BCE, recovered as a chance find without stratigraphy, like most hoards of this class.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Marks the westernmost extent of the copper hoard distribution, and the point at which the Indian series meets the Iranian and Central Asian one.
  - The diagnostic objects are trunnion celts - axes cast with lateral lugs or trunnions for hafting. This is a West Asian and Central Asian type, not a doab type, and its appearance here is the argument for contact across the Hindu Kush rather than for an extension of the Gangetic tradition.
  - The contrast with the doab hoards is instructive: those run to flat, shouldered and bar celts, harpoons, antennae swords and anthropomorphs, all largely hunting equipment, and about 46 per cent of copper hoard objects carry up to 7 per cent arsenic alloying against only 8 per cent of analysed Harappan artefacts - a different metallurgical tradition altogether.
  - The find bears on the open question Upinder Singh leaves standing: whether the upper Ganga valley was an independent centre of copper working, or an extension of the older centre in north-eastern Rajasthan at Ganeshwar-Jodhpura.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; B. B. Lal on the north-western copper hoard types.
