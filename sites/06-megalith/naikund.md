- **Location**: Naikund, about 40 km north-east of Nagpur in Vidarbha, Maharashtra, near Ramtek in the Kanhan-Wainganga tract.
- **Period/Level**: Vidarbha megalithic, with calibrated ranges of 800-420 BCE and 785-410 BCE - the firmest chronology available for the whole group. Excavated by S. B. Deo and A. P. Jamkhedkar (1978-80).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Supplies the dates for the Vidarbha megaliths and the clearest iron-smelting workshop of the Indian iron age.
  - An iron-smelting furnace built of small curved bricks with a cylindrical terracotta pipe (tuyere); iron ore was traced to a nala about a kilometre away. This is the standard citation for local smelting rather than traded iron.
  - Stone-circle graves containing horse skeletons with iron bits and copper ornaments, as at Mahurjhari. The iron repertoire is wide - hoes, ladles, nails, dagger blades, arrowheads, knives, chisels, spikes, axes, double-edged adzes, bars, fishhooks, bangles, tridents, a sword and a cauldron.
  - The habitation had circular huts with wooden posts, black-and-red ware, and barley, rice and lentil grains lying on house floors - a prosperous agricultural village behind the cemetery. Iron hoes here are the direct evidence for iron in agriculture.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; Deo & Jamkhedkar, *Excavations at Naikund* (1982).
