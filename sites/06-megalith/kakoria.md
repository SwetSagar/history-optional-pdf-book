- **Location**: Kakoria, on the northern fringe of the Vindhyas in south-eastern Uttar Pradesh; the habitation lies on both banks of the Chandraprabha river, immediately north-west of the megalithic cemetery at the base of a hillock.
- **Period/Level**: Pre-iron megalithic. A range from the 2nd millennium BCE (or earlier) to the 7th century CE has been suggested for pre-iron Kakoria, against the beginning of the 1st millennium BCE for the neighbouring Jang Mahal group and c. 800-300 BCE for Kotia.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-documented habitation-plus-cemetery pairing in the pre-iron megalithic province of the Vindhyas.
  - The megaliths are cairns and stone circles; some graves show fractional burial, others are associated with animal burials, and many contain no skeletal remains at all - memorials rather than graves.
  - Pottery from both habitation and burials is black-and-red ware, black-slipped ware and red ware, mostly wheel-made, with dishes, bowls, perforated vessels, lids, pedestalled cups and elliptical and globular jars.
  - Microliths of agate, chalcedony and chert in quantity; beads of terracotta and semi-precious stone; sling balls, grinding stones and a few copper objects - and no iron, which is the defining negative evidence of this province.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5.
