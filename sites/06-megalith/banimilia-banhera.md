- **Location**: Banimilia-Banhera, on the northern fringe of the Vindhyas in the Varanasi-Chandauli tract of south-eastern Uttar Pradesh, in the Chandraprabha-Karamnasa drainage.
- **Period/Level**: Part of the south-eastern Uttar Pradesh megalithic province - the group spread over Allahabad, Banda, Varanasi and Mirzapur districts, with Kakoria, Jang Mahal and Kotia. Most megaliths of this province are pre-iron: the Jang Mahal group is placed at the beginning of the 1st millennium BCE and pre-iron Kakoria is given a range from the 2nd millennium BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Belongs to the one Indian megalithic zone that is substantially pre-iron - the standing exception to the equation of megaliths with the iron age.
  - The megalith types are cairns and stone circles. Many of them contain no skeletal remains of any kind and are best understood as memorials to the dead rather than graves.
  - Fractional burials occur in some graves, and separate animal burials in others; the associated pottery is black-and-red ware with black-slipped and plain red wares.
  - Since iron is absent here while the peninsular megaliths are defined by it, this province is the key qualification in any answer on megalithic chronology: the megaliths are a burial practice of wide date range, not a single culture.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (Vindhyan megaliths).
