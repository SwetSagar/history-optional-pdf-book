- **Location**: Nartiang, West Jaintia Hills district, Meghalaya, on a ridge above the Myntdu valley near Jowai - the old summer capital of the Jaintia rajas.
- **Period/Level**: A living megalithic tradition rather than a prehistoric one: the stones of the monolith park were raised by Jaintia chieftains from about the 16th century CE onwards, and the practice continued into the 19th century. The site is protected by the ASI.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest single collection of standing monoliths in India, and the standing proof that megalith building is not confined to prehistory.
  - Menhirs - moo shynrang or 'male stones', the tallest about 8 m and attributed to the chieftain U Mar Phalyngki - set beside flat table stones, moo kynthai or 'female stones', raised as memorials and to mark victories and market rights.
  - The Khasi-Jaintia custom of erecting memorial stones over ossuaries holding cremated bone makes the north-eastern group the ethnographic key to what megaliths were for.
  - It anchors the crucial distinction between commemorative and sepulchral megaliths, and supports Upinder Singh's warning that the megaliths cannot be treated as a single, homogeneous or contemporaneous culture.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (megalith typology); ASI, Shillong circle.
