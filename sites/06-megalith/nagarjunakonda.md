- **Location**: Nagarjunakonda, Palnadu (formerly Guntur) district, Andhra Pradesh, in a valley on the right bank of the Krishna, now an island in the Nagarjunasagar reservoir.
- **Period/Level**: A sequence running from the palaeolithic, through a neolithic-chalcolithic farming village of the 3rd millennium BCE, to a megalithic phase of the 1st millennium BCE, and then to the Ikshvaku capital Vijayapuri of the 3rd-4th centuries CE. Salvage-excavated by R. Subrahmanyam (ASI, 1954-60) before submergence.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The one site where the megalithic phase can be read directly against both a neolithic village below it and an early historic city above.
  - Megalithic cists and pit burials with black-and-red ware, iron objects and beads - the Andhra group of megaliths, with Kadambapur, Yelleswaram, Gallapalli and Tadapatri.
  - Its palaeolithic levels have yielded palaeo-climatic data, and Thapar lists it among the early farming settlements of the Krishna-Tungabhadra tract alongside Hallur, Kupgal and Maski.
  - Above the megaliths lie the mahachaityas, viharas, an amphitheatre and Brahmanical temples of Vijayapuri, with the Ikshvaku inscriptions - treated under the Buddhist sites.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 and ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI Nagarjunakonda memoirs.
