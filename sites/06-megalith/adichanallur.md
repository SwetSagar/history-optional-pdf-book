- **Location**: Adichanallur, Thoothukudi district, Tamil Nadu, on the north bank of the Tamiraparani river about 24 km from its mouth.
- **Period/Level**: An urn-burial field excavated by Alexander Rea (ASI, 1899-1905) and again by T. Satyamurthy (ASI, 2003-05). Iron age burials from c. 1000-900 BCE, continuing into the early historic Sangam age; a radiocarbon date for the terminal megaliths here runs as late as the 12th century CE, which Upinder Singh cites as proof that the megaliths are not one homogeneous culture.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest and earliest-excavated urn-burial site in Tamil Nadu, and the bridge between the iron age and the Sangam age.
  - Hundreds of large black-and-red burial urns containing extended and fractional skeletons, husked paddy and grain. Cairn circles - the standard marker further north - are absent here.
  - Iron implements in quantity: swords, daggers, tridents and a trident with a cock finial, read by some as an early Murugan emblem; bronze objects; and gold diadems and ornaments. Gold is not local to the area, so its presence implies external contact.
  - An urn sherd carrying a Tamil-Brahmi inscription, dated by the excavator to c. 500 BCE, is central to the debate on the antiquity of Tamil literacy.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; Rea, ASI *Annual Reports*; ASI, *IAR* 2004-05.
