- **Location**: Sanur, near Tindivanam in the old Chingleput district of northern Tamil Nadu, in the Palar-Ponnaiyar interfluve.
- **Period/Level**: Iron age megalithic, placed by its excavators in the last centuries BCE and the early centuries CE, within the Tamil Nadu megalithic range of c. 1000 BCE to the early centuries CE. Excavated by N. R. Banerjee and K. V. Soundara Rajan in 1950 and 1952.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The standard published Tamil Nadu megalithic excavation, and the site that defined the dolmenoid cist with a sarcophagus - the illustration reproduced in most textbooks, including Upinder Singh's.
  - Dolmenoid cists set inside stone circles, each holding a legged terracotta sarcophagus within the chamber; several orthostats carry the characteristic port-hole cut into the slab.
  - Black-and-red ware and russet-coated painted ware, iron implements and beads; the excavation established the local sequence of cist, urn and pit-circle types.
  - With Adichanallur, Amritamangalam and Kunnattur it sets the Tamil megalithic typology, and its sarcophagus form connects the Tamil country to the jar burials of Karnataka.
  - *Sources*: N. R. Banerjee & K. V. Soundara Rajan, 'Sanur 1950 and 1952', *Ancient India* 15 (1959); Upinder Singh, ch. 5.
