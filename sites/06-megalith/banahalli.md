- **Location**: Banahalli, in the granite uplands of eastern Karnataka (the Kolar-Chikkaballapur tract), in the upper drainage of the north Pennar, close to the Andhra border.
- **Period/Level**: A neolithic-chalcolithic settlement whose upper levels merge into a megalithic phase, the pattern Upinder Singh notes for most South Indian sites of this class. The megalithic burials belong to the first half of the 1st millennium BCE; excavated by the Karnataka Directorate of Archaeology and Museums in the 1980s.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Shows the neolithic-to-megalithic continuum of the Karnataka plateau in a single section, where the earliest iron of the region appears in the overlap.
  - Pit burials and sarcophagus burials in high-necked jars - a form that links the Karnataka megaliths to the legged sarcophagi of Tamil Nadu.
  - Black-and-red ware as the diagnostic pottery, alongside the wheel-made grey and buff wares of the terminal neolithic phase.
  - Iron objects including agricultural implements, which is the significant point: in the Deccan megaliths iron appears as farming equipment - hoes, sickles - as much as weaponry, implying an agrarian rather than purely martial economy.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (Map 5.5); ASI, *IAR*.
