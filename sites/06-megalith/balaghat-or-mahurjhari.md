- **Location**: Mahurjhari lies about 15 km north-west of Nagpur on the Vidarbha uplands; the map dot falls in the adjoining Balaghat district of Madhya Pradesh, the copper-bearing tract of the upper Wainganga that also yielded the Gungeria hoard.
- **Period/Level**: Vidarbha megalithic, c. 800-400 BCE on the calibrated dates for the group (Naikund gives 800-420 BCE), with bead manufacture continuing from the megalithic into the early historic phase. Excavated by S. B. Deo in the 1970s and by R. K. Mohanty (Deccan College) from 1998.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The richest stone-circle cemetery of Vidarbha and the bead-making capital of the megalithic Deccan.
  - Bead manufacture on a large scale in carnelian, agate and jasper - which Mohanty (1999) connects directly to the exceptional richness of the grave goods here. Production spans the megalithic-to-early-historic transition.
  - Horse remains with iron bits and copper ornaments occur in almost every stone circle, as at Naikund. One burial held the complete skeleton of a horse whose cut marks show it was sacrificed and buried with its owner.
  - Two burials speak of a warrior tradition: an adult male with his mouth gaping and an arrow embedded near the collarbone, and the upper half of a male with an iron-bladed, copper-hilted dagger resting on his chest.
  - Balaghat, in the same tract, carries the copper-hoard connection: the Gungeria hoard from this district - 424 copper objects with 102 thin silver plates - is the largest copper hoard found in India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; Thapar, *Early India*; Mohanty (1999).
