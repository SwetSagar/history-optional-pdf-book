- **Location**: Junapani, about 10 km north-west of Nagpur in Vidarbha, Maharashtra, on the Nagpur plateau in the upper Kanhan drainage.
- **Period/Level**: First reported by J. H. Rivett-Carnac in 1879, among the earliest megaliths ever recorded in India, and later excavated by the ASI and Nagpur University (S. B. Deo). Vidarbha megalithic, c. 1000-400 BCE, the burials commonly dated around 1000 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site of the Vidarbha stone-circle burial and the principal Indian site for cup-marked megalithic stones.
  - About three hundred stone circles of packed boulders. Several of the stones carry cup marks pecked into them, sometimes interpreted as alignment or astronomical markers - a trait that sets Vidarbha apart from the cists and urns of the far south.
  - Horse burials with iron horse bits and copper ornaments; black-and-red ware; copper and iron objects. Thapar links these cairn burials, with their BRW and horse furniture, to megalithic burials further south.
  - The associated habitation deposits show that these were flourishing agricultural settlements, not the camps of pastoral nomads - the standard correction to older views of megalith builders.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; Thapar, *Early India*.
