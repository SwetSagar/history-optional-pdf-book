- **Location**: Kuntitoli, on the Chotanagpur plateau of Jharkhand, in the western uplands drained by the North Koel.
- **Period/Level**: Eastern Indian megalithic - burials of the 1st millennium BCE in a region where megalith building survived as a living practice among the Munda-speaking peoples into modern times.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Represents the eastern Indian megalithic province, where archaeology and ethnography can be read together.
  - Dolmens, cists and standing stones of the type massed on the same plateau at Chokahatu, the 'place of mourning' in Ranchi district - one of the largest megalithic burial grounds in India.
  - Burials of the class the Mundas still call sasandiri, flat slabs raised over the collected bones of a clan's dead, which is why the eastern group is treated as evidence for what megaliths were for, not merely when they were built.
  - Associated black-and-red ware and iron objects tie the plateau to the Vindhyan group to the west and the Vidarbha group to the south.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (eastern India); ASI, *IAR* (Chotanagpur megaliths).
