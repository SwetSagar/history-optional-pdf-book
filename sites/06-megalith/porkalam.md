- **Location**: Porkalam, near Kunnamkulam in Thrissur district, Kerala, on the laterite uplands between the Bharathappuzha and Chalakudy drainages.
- **Period/Level**: Kerala megalithic (iron age), broadly c. 1000-100 BCE - the range established for the Kerala group at Mangadu in Kollam district. Excavated by B. K. Thapar in 1948, the first scientific excavation of a Kerala umbrella-stone megalith.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site for the distinctive laterite megalithic forms of Kerala, found nowhere else in India.
  - A kudakkal or umbrella stone: four clinostatic laterite slabs capped by a dressed hemispherical stone, set over a pit holding the burial urn; topikals or hat stones stand nearby.
  - The urn contained bones with iron implements and black-and-red ware; the iron objects include daggers and a tripod.
  - Kerala's repertoire - kudakkal, topikal, rock-cut chambers and hood stones, all cut from soft laterite - is a regional adaptation of the peninsular megalithic and is the standard illustration of megalithic regional variation.
  - *Sources*: B. K. Thapar, 'Porkalam 1948', *Ancient India* 8 (1952); Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (megalith types).
