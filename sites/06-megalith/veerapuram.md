- **Location**: Veerapuram, Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh, on the right bank of the Krishna near Nandikotkur, downstream of the Tungabhadra confluence.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by the Birla Archaeological and Cultural Research Institute (T. V. G. Sastry and others, 1977-80). A neolithic level, a megalithic-iron age level of the earlier 1st millennium BCE, and an early historic Satavahana level above.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A type site for the cultural sequence of the Krishna valley, chosen and published precisely for its unbroken column.
  - The neolithic-megalithic overlap, in which the earliest iron objects of the region appear - the transition Upinder Singh treats as characteristic of South India, where iron enters at the join between the neolithic and megalithic phases.
  - Megalithic burials with black-and-red ware, iron implements and beads, belonging to the Andhra group with Kadambapur, Nagarjunakonda, Yelleswaram and Gallapalli; habitation deposits with circular huts.
  - Satavahana-period levels above, with coins, beads and brick structures, carry the site into the early historic period and allow the megalithic phase to be dated by what follows it.
  - *Sources*: T. V. G. Sastry et al., *Veerapuram: A Type Site for Cultural Study in the Krishna Valley* (Hyderabad, 1984); Upinder Singh, ch. 5.
