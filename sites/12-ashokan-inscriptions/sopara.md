An important port in Maharashtra under Ashoka and later, carrying sea trade with the Romans in the pre- and post-Mauryan age and with the Arabs in the early medieval period.

- It is associated with the Satavahanas and then the Guptas, and is mentioned in the Periplus.
- Arab sources describe it as an important centre of artisanal activity.
- It bears the eighth and ninth Major Rock Edicts, portions of Major Rock Edicts 13 and 14 in Prakrit and Brahmi, and the separate edicts 1–2 found on a temple stone; the first inscribed portrait of Ashoka was found in the stupa.
- In post-Mauryan times it was a major terracotta sculpture centre.
- It is known in the older sources as Surparaka or Suppara.
