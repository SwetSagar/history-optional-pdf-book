- **Location**: Nala Sopara, Palghar district, Maharashtra, on the Konkan coast north of Mumbai - ancient Shurparaka, the Souppara of the *Periplus* and Ptolemy's Soupara, the chief port of the western Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: Fragments of Major Rock Edicts 8 and 9, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 257-256 BCE. The edict 9 fragment was found in the stupa mound by Bhagwanlal Indraji in 1882 and a further fragment in 1956. The stupa itself is Mauryan in origin with later rebuilding; the port flourished from the Mauryan period through the Satavahanas and Guptas and is still named in Arab sources.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only Major Rock Edict site on the western seaboard - the empire's window on the Arabian Sea trade.
  - Major Rock Edict 8 records that Ashoka gave up the royal pleasure tour (vihara-yatra) of hunting and replaced it with the dhamma-yatra, visiting shramanas and brahmanas, giving gifts and instructing the countryside - the single clearest statement of what changed in his practice of kingship.
  - Major Rock Edict 9 attacks the ceremonies performed at births, marriages and journeys, especially those performed by women, as of little fruit, and sets against them the ceremony of dhamma - proper treatment of slaves and servants, respect for elders, restraint towards living beings.
  - Indraji's excavation of the stupa recovered a stone coffer holding relic caskets and eight bronze images of the Buddha of a later date, confirming continuous Buddhist use.
  - The Sri Lankan chronicles record that Ashoka's mission to Aparanta under Dharmarakshita went to this coast, and in later centuries Sopara was the port serving Kanheri, Kalyan and the ghat caves inland.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-8; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI.
