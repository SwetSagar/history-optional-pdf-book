- **Location**: Kalsi, Dehradun district, Uttarakhand, on a quartzite boulder near the confluence of the Yamuna and the Tons at the foot of the Siwaliks - where the Doab route enters the hills.
- **Period/Level**: All fourteen Major Rock Edicts, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 257-256 BCE. Discovered by John Forrest in 1860.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The northernmost Major Rock Edict site in India, and the one that carries a carved symbol alongside the text.
  - An elephant is carved beside the inscription with the word "gajatame" - the supreme elephant - cut beneath it. The elephant is a standard symbol of the Buddha, referring to Maya's dream, and this is a rare instance of Ashoka using an image rather than words to make a point.
  - The Major Rock Edicts occur at only about nine places, all of them peripheral - Kalsi, Girnar, Sopara, Dhauli, Jaugada, Yerragudi, Sannati, Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra. Their distribution outlines the empire, while the Minor Rock Edicts fill its interior along the roads.
  - Kalsi commands the route into the hills towards the Tibetan plateau, and is usually cited as the marker of the empire's northern reach in the central Himalaya.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI.
