- **Location**: Jaugada, Ganjam district, Odisha, on the Rishikulya river inside the earthen ramparts of an early historic fort - the southern administrative centre of conquered Kalinga.
- **Period/Level**: Major Rock Edicts 1-10 and 14, plus the two Separate Kalinga Edicts, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 257-256 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of only two sites - with Dhauli - where Ashoka replaced the standard edicts with a text written specially for a conquered people.
  - Rock Edicts 11, 12 and 13 are omitted here and at Dhauli. Edict 13 is the one describing the Kalinga war and Ashoka's remorse: he did not put an account of the conquest in front of the conquered. That deliberate omission is the single most revealing editorial decision in the corpus.
  - In their place stand the two Separate Edicts. The first tells the mahamatras that "all men are my children", that they must avoid harshness, envy and arbitrary imprisonment, and orders a quinquennial inspection tour to check their conduct. The second addresses the unsubdued forest peoples of the frontier, promising them that the king wishes them no harm and will forgive what can be forgiven.
  - Together they are the clearest evidence of Mauryan provincial administration under supervision - and of Ashoka's awareness that his own officers were the problem to be managed.
  - The edicts are addressed to the mahamatras at Samapa, the local capital, as Dhauli's are to those at Tosali.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
