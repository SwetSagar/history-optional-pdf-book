- **Location**: Sanchi, Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh, on a hill above the plain 10 km from Vidisha - beside the route from the Ganga plain to Ujjayini and the western ports.
- **Period/Level**: Stupa 1 was commissioned by Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE as a brick mound; the Shungas encased it in stone and doubled its size in the 2nd century BCE, adding the balustrade and stairway; the four carved toranas were added under the Satavahanas, c. 1st century BCE. An Ashokan pillar with a Schism Edict stands beside it, and a Gupta-period temple (Temple 17) and inscription belong to the 5th century CE. Rediscovered in 1818; restored by John Marshall, 1912-19. UNESCO World Heritage, 1989.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-preserved and most complete early Buddhist stupa in India, and the standard case study of how a Mauryan mound became a Satavahana picture gallery.
  - The Ashokan pillar beside the southern gateway is of finely polished sandstone with a four-lion capital - the same design as Sarnath, here weathered - and carries the Schism Edict, warning that a monk or nun who divides the Sangha is to be expelled in white robes. A Gupta inscription was later added to the same shaft.
  - The four gateways are the great achievement: architraves and uprights covered with Jataka scenes, the Buddha's life, the war over the relics, and Ashoka's own visit to the bodhi tree. The Buddha is still shown aniconically here - by the wheel, the tree, the empty throne, the footprints - so Sanchi is the fullest surviving statement of the aniconic phase.
  - An inscription on the southern gateway records that it was the work of the ivory carvers of Vidisha, and the several hundred short donative records on the railing name monks, nuns, artisans and villagers - collective, non-royal funding, which is the point to make about early Buddhist patronage.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-8; John Marshall, *A Guide to Sanchi*; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*.
