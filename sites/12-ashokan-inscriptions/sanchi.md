A site famous for the earliest surviving stupa and for a pillar edict of Ashoka, with four lions and the Schism Edict.

- The stupa was originally commissioned by the emperor in the third century BC, expanded by the Shungas with stone slabs, and its gateways were improved in the Satavahana period.
- The pillar is of finely polished sandstone and carries an inscription of Ashoka and another of the Gupta period.
- It is known in the older sources as Kakanaya.
