- **Location**: Two Ashokan pillars in Delhi: the Delhi-Topra pillar at Firoz Shah Kotla, brought from Topra in Yamunanagar district, Haryana, and the Delhi-Meerut pillar on the northern Ridge, brought from Meerut. Both were moved by Firoz Shah Tughlaq in 1356.
- **Period/Level**: Polished Chunar sandstone shafts of c. 243-242 BCE carrying Pillar Edicts in Brahmi and Prakrit, cut in Ashoka's twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh regnal years. Topra carries Pillar Edicts I-VII; Meerut carries I-VI.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Topra is the only pillar in India to carry Pillar Edict VII - Ashoka's own review of his reign, and the last thing he is known to have inscribed.
  - Pillar Edict VII looks back over what he had done for dhamma: banyan trees and mango groves planted along the roads, wells dug and rest houses built at intervals, the appointment of dhamma-mahamatras, and his conclusion that dhamma grows better by persuasion than by regulation. It is the only edict in which he assesses his own policy.
  - The transport is itself a documented event. Shams-i-Siraj Afif's *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi* describes the pillars being wrapped in silk cotton, lowered onto a carriage of forty-two wheels, hauled by thousands of men and floated down the Yamuna on boats - the earliest detailed account of moving an ancient monument in India. Firoz Shah took them for their beauty; nobody could then read Brahmi.
  - The script was deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837, working from these and the Sanchi and Girnar texts, which is the moment Ashoka re-entered Indian history after sixteen centuries.
  - Delhi also holds the Bahapur Minor Rock Edict, the only Ashokan inscription in the city still on its original rock, and is later the seat of the Chishti saints Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki, Nizamuddin Auliya and Nasiruddin Chirag Dehlavi.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; Shams-i-Siraj Afif, *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi*; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*.
