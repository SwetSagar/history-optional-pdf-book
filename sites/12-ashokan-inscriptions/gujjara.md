- **Location**: Gujarra, Datia district, Madhya Pradesh, in the Bundelkhand uplands - on the old route from Pataliputra through Ujjayini to Bharuch (Broach) on the Gujarat coast.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edict I, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Discovered in 1953.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the handful of inscriptions that give Ashoka's personal name - without which the edicts would belong to an anonymous "Devanampiya Piyadasi".
  - The Gujarra text reads "Devanampiya Piyadasi Asoka raja", combining the titles with the name. Only five sites do this - Gujarra, Maski, Nittur, Udegolam and Panguraria (Saru Maru) - and the first of them, Maski, found in 1915, is what finally allowed the Piyadasi of the edicts to be identified with the Ashoka of the Buddhist chronicles.
  - Before that identification the edicts were an orphan corpus; after it, the chronicles of Sri Lanka and the Puranic king lists could be checked against contemporary epigraphy. This is the standard example in historiography answers of inscriptions correcting and anchoring literary tradition.
  - Its position on the Pataliputra-Bharuch road again shows the Minor Rock Edicts following routes rather than marking boundaries.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
