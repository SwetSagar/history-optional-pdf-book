- **Location**: Ahraura, Mirzapur district, Uttar Pradesh, on a rock in the Vindhyan scarp south of the Ganga near Varanasi.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edict I, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE - the Minor Rock Edicts belong to Ashoka's twelfth or thirteenth regnal year and precede the Major Rock Edicts. Discovered in 1961.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The one version of Minor Rock Edict I that appears to mention relics of the Buddha - which is why a short provincial inscription carries weight out of all proportion to its length.
  - Minor Rock Edict I is Ashoka's personal statement: he has been a lay follower (upasaka) for more than two and a half years, was not zealous at first, but has since drawn close to the Sangha and been zealous - the most intimate thing he ever put on stone, and the text always quoted on the sincerity of his conversion.
  - The Ahraura version adds a clause absent elsewhere, read as referring to the relics (salila) of the Buddha being made an object of worship. If that reading holds, the edict dates the spread of the relic cult to Ashoka's own reign rather than after it.
  - Its position in the Vindhyan borderland, away from any city, illustrates the placement rule for the Minor Rock Edicts: they sit on routes and at frontier points where people passed, not in capitals.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
