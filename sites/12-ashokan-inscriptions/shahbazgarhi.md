- **Location**: Shahbazgarhi, Mardan district, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, on two boulders at the foot of a hill in the Peshawar valley - in the heart of Gandhara, near Takht-i-Bahi, on the road from the Khyber to the Indus.
- **Period/Level**: All fourteen Major Rock Edicts, cut in the Kharoshthi script in Prakrit, c. 257-256 BCE - the largest and best-preserved Kharoshthi version of the edicts.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The north-western limit of the Major Rock Edicts, and the fullest evidence that the empire published in the local script.
  - The text is divided between two rocks on the hillside, the first carrying edicts 1-12 and the second 13 and 14, written right to left in Kharoshthi, a script derived from Aramaic and used only in Gandhara and the north-west.
  - Major Rock Edict XIII, the Kalinga edict, is best preserved in the north-western versions. It gives the casualty figures, Ashoka's remorse, his declaration that the chief conquest is conquest by dhamma, and the naming of the five Hellenistic kings - Antiochus, Ptolemy, Antigonus, Magas and Alexander - to whom he claims to have sent missions. Those names are what allow the Mauryan chronology to be fixed against Greek dates, and they are the foundation of all ancient Indian dating.
  - Gandhara was an Achaemenid satrapy before it was Mauryan, and the choice of script here, as at Mansehra, records that inheritance.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
