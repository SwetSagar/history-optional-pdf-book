- **Location**: Lampaka - the Sanskrit name for the Laghman region of eastern Afghanistan, on the Alishang and Alingar rivers north of Jalalabad, between the Kabul valley and the Khyber.
- **Period/Level**: Aramaic inscriptions of c. 260-250 BCE, the same group recorded under Laghman; found on rock faces beside the highway in 1969-70 and 1973.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Fixes the north-western administrative frontier of the Maurya empire, in the province the Sanskrit sources call Lampaka.
  - Lampaka appears in the Puranas and in Sanskrit geographical literature as a country of the far north-west, and Xuanzang later describes it as Lan-po; the Aramaic inscriptions show it inside Mauryan control in the 3rd century BCE.
  - This is the tract Chandragupta obtained from Seleucus Nikator in the treaty of c. 303 BCE, along with Gandhara, Arachosia and Gedrosia, in exchange for 500 elephants - the north-western inscriptions are the physical evidence that the cession was real and held.
  - Aramaic here, Greek and Aramaic at Kandahar, Kharoshthi at Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra, Brahmi everywhere else: the north-west is where the empire's multilingual character is visible.
  - Note for the maps: this entry and the Laghman Minor Rock Edict entry refer to the same site under two names, and should be merged or plotted as one dot.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Early India*.
