- **Location**: Bairat (Viratnagar), Jaipur district, Rajasthan, in a valley of the Aravallis on the route from the doab to Gujarat - the Matsya country.
- **Period/Level**: Two Minor Rock Edicts, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. One is cut on a boulder at the site; the other, the Bhabru or Calcutta-Bairat edict, was removed by Cunningham in 1871 and is now in the Indian Museum, Kolkata.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Bhabru edict is the only Ashokan inscription that cites Buddhist scripture by name - the strongest evidence for his personal Buddhism.
  - It is addressed to the Sangha of Magadha, opens with Ashoka's salutation and his declaration of reverence and faith in the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha, and then recommends seven passages of scripture that monks, nuns and lay followers should hear and reflect on repeatedly.
  - That list separates the private Buddhist from the public ruler: in the Major Rock Edicts Ashoka preaches a non-sectarian dhamma of tolerance and social duty and never names a Buddhist text. The Bhabru edict is the counter-evidence, and the two together are the whole debate over whether Ashoka's dhamma was Buddhism.
  - The site also holds a Mauryan circular brick-and-timber chaitya on Bijak-ki-Pahari, the earliest free-standing structural Buddhist shrine in India, and a monastery - so the edict sits beside a Buddhist establishment Ashoka's period built.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
