- **Location**: Rupnath, Katni district, Madhya Pradesh, at the foot of the Kaimur range near a spring and waterfall below the Vindhyan scarp - on the route from the Ganga plain to the Narmada valley and the Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edict I, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Reported by Captain James Franklin in 1834.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the three texts James Prinsep worked on when he deciphered Brahmi - the site belongs to the history of Indology as much as to Ashoka's.
  - Prinsep's decipherment of Brahmi in 1837, using Rupnath, Sahasram, Bairat and the Delhi and Girnar texts, recovered a king called Devanampiya Piyadasi whose identity was then unknown. Only the Maski edict of 1915 supplied the name Ashoka.
  - The edict is addressed to the local sangha and records Ashoka's declaration of faith in the Buddha and the Dhamma, his admission that he was at first not zealous, and his instruction that the message be spread widely and cut on rock wherever rock is found.
  - It also carries the line that through exertion even the lowly can reach heaven, and closes with a dating formula counted in nights spent on tour - the sort of detail that lets the edicts be sequenced against one another.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
