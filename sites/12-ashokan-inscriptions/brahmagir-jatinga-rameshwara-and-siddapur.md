- **Location**: Three rock sites within a few kilometres of one another at Brahmagiri, Jatinga-Rameshwara and Siddapura, Chitradurga district, Karnataka, in the granite country between the Tungabhadra and the Pennar.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edicts I and II, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Brahmagiri is also a Neolithic and megalithic type site excavated by Mortimer Wheeler in 1947, whose three-period sequence became the frame for South Indian prehistory.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The southernmost limit of the Mauryan empire on the ground, and the clearest statement of how a Mauryan province was actually run.
  - The edicts are issued in the name of the aryaputra - the prince viceroy - and the mahamatras at Suvarnagiri, and addressed to the mahamatras of Isila, which is Brahmagiri itself. This gives the chain of command whole: king, prince at the provincial capital, mahamatras at the district town.
  - The scribe Chapada signs the Brahmagiri edict in Kharoshthi characters at the end of a Brahmi text - a north-western clerk working in the far south, and incidental proof of how the Mauryan bureaucracy moved men across the subcontinent.
  - Minor Rock Edict II sets out dhamma in practical terms: obedience to parents and elders, respect for teachers, kindness to living creatures and to servants - the social morality Ashoka actually preached.
  - The southern edict sites stop here. Below them lie the Cholas, Pandyas, Cheras and Satiyaputas, named in Major Rock Edicts II and XIII as neighbours rather than subjects - so the map, not the text, fixes the empire's edge.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Wheeler, *Ancient India* 4 (1947-48).
