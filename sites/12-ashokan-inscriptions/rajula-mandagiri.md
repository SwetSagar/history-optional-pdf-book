- **Location**: Rajula-Mandagiri, near Pattikonda in Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh, on a granite hill in the Rayalaseema uplands between the Tungabhadra and the Krishna.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edicts I and II, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Discovered in 1953.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the southern pair of sites - with Yerragudi, 40 km away - that carries both Minor Rock Edicts together.
  - Minor Rock Edict II is the more important of the two for the content of dhamma. It is cast as an instruction relayed through officials and lists the duties: obedience to mother and father, respect for elders, kindness to living creatures, truthfulness, and proper treatment of teachers, relatives, slaves and servants.
  - Its phrasing is close to the household duties of the Sigalovada Sutta, and it is the text that best supports reading dhamma as a civic and social ethic drawn from a shared moral vocabulary rather than a Buddhist creed - the central question in any Ashoka answer.
  - The concentration of Minor Rock Edicts across Kurnool, Bellary, Raichur, Koppal and Chitradurga marks the Mauryan south as a governed province, not a nominal claim, run from Suvarnagiri.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI, *Epigraphia Indica*.
