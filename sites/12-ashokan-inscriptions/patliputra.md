- **Location**: Pataliputra, modern Patna, Bihar, on the strip between the Ganga and the Son - the excavated mounds are Kumrahar, Bulandi Bagh and Agam Kuan.
- **Period/Level**: Founded as a fort by the Magadhan king Udayin c. 460 BCE and made the capital in place of Rajagriha. Imperial capital of the Nandas and Mauryas, c. 345-185 BCE, then of the Shungas and the Guptas. Excavated by D. B. Spooner (1912-15, funded by the Tatas), L. A. Waddell, and A. S. Altekar and S. Mishra (1951-55).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of the first Indian empire - and the one place where Megasthenes' description can be checked against the ground.
  - At Bulandi Bagh, a double row of enormous upright sal-wood beams was found, the remains of the wooden city wall. Megasthenes, as quoted by Arrian and Strabo, described Palibothra as 9.5 miles long and 1.75 miles wide, enclosed by a timber palisade with 570 towers and 64 gates, and fronted by a moat - the excavated palisade is that wall, and is the most direct confirmation of a Greek account of India anywhere.
  - At Kumrahar, an eighty-pillar hall was found: rows of monolithic sandstone pillars with the Mauryan polish, set on a wooden platform, with a wooden approach channel. It is generally identified as a Mauryan assembly or audience hall, the only Mauryan palace structure excavated.
  - Ashoka replaced the timber city with stone, according to both the excavated evidence and Faxian, who saw the palace still standing in the 5th century CE and thought it the work of spirits rather than men.
  - The Third Buddhist Council met here c. 250 BCE under Moggaliputta Tissa, in Ashoka's seventeenth regnal year; it compiled the Kathavatthu and dispatched the dhamma missions, including Mahendra's to Sri Lanka. Sher Shah Suri later rebuilt the city as Patna.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6-7; Megasthenes via Arrian and Strabo; ASI, Kumrahar excavation reports.
