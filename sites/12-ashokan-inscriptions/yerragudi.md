- **Location**: Yerragudi (Erragudi), near Gooty in Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh, on a granite outcrop in the Rayalaseema uplands south of the Tungabhadra.
- **Period/Level**: All fourteen Major Rock Edicts, c. 257-256 BCE, and Minor Rock Edicts I and II, c. 258-257 BCE - all in Brahmi script and Prakrit.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only site in the empire carrying both the Major and the Minor Rock Edicts - which is what lets the two series be dated relative to each other.
  - Because both sets appear on one rock, their sequence can be established directly: the Minor Rock Edicts are the earlier, and the Major Rock Edicts follow in the twelfth to fourteenth regnal years. Every relative dating of the Ashokan corpus rests ultimately on sites like this.
  - The engraving is famously irregular. The lines run in varying directions, some right to left, the text crowded and fitted to the rock's surface rather than to a ruled plan - taken as evidence of a scribe working from dictation or from a Kharoshthi-trained hand unused to Brahmi, and a reminder that the edicts were cut by ordinary craftsmen, not a uniform imperial workshop.
  - Ashoka is named here as Piyadasi, with the title Devanampiya, not by his personal name.
  - Yerragudi is the southernmost Major Rock Edict site, and with Rajula-Mandagiri 40 km away it anchors the Andhra end of the southern edict cluster.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI, *Epigraphia Indica*.
