- **Location**: Sannati, Chitapur taluk, Kalaburagi (Gulbarga) district, Karnataka, on the left bank of the Bhima - the northernmost Deccan plain, with the Kanganahalli stupa immediately adjacent.
- **Period/Level**: Major Rock Edicts 12 and 14 and both Separate Kalinga Edicts, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 257-256 BCE. Found in 1986 when a slab serving as the pedestal of the image in the Chandralamba temple was lifted and proved to be inscribed; published from 1989.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only place outside Kalinga where the two Separate Edicts have been found - a discovery that overturned what the Separate Edicts were thought to be.
  - They had been read as a special message written for the Kalingans after the war, and their presence at Dhauli and Jaugada alone supported that. Sannati shows the same text in the Deccan, so they are better understood as a standing instruction to mahamatras in any newly acquired or frontier province - general administrative policy, not a one-off act of contrition.
  - Major Rock Edict 12 is the tolerance edict: honour other sects, restrain praise of your own, for whoever extols his own sect while disparaging others injures his own sect - the text always quoted on Ashoka's religious policy.
  - The find also extends the Major Rock Edict distribution into the Deccan interior, and it came from a temple pedestal, not an excavation, which is the standard example of how much of the corpus surfaces by accident.
  - The Kanganahalli stupa beside it yielded the "Ranyo Ashoka" relief portrait, so the two sites together give both Ashoka's words and his only known likeness.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; K. V. Ramesh, *Epigraphia Indica*; ASI.
