- **Location**: A granite boulder beside the road from Junagadh to the Girnar hills, Gujarat, in Saurashtra - on the route from the Kathiawar ports inland to Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: All fourteen Major Rock Edicts, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, cut in Ashoka's twelfth to fourteenth regnal years, c. 257-256 BCE. Noticed by James Tod in 1822. Two later inscriptions were added to the same rock: Rudradaman I's, c. 150 CE, and Skandagupta's, 455-458 CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only surface in India carrying Mauryan, Shaka-Kshatrapa and Gupta records together - seven centuries of imperial history on one stone.
  - The fourteen Major Rock Edicts here are the fullest and best-preserved set: Edict I bans animal sacrifice and the samajas; II provides medical care for humans and animals, and wells and roadside trees, in his realm and in the Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputa, Keralaputa and Tamraparni lands; XII urges restraint in praising one's own sect; XIII is the Kalinga edict, with its remorse over the 150,000 deported, 100,000 killed and many more dead, and the claim of dhamma-vijaya over the five Yona kings.
  - Rudradaman's inscription of c. 150 CE is the first long record in Sanskrit and in ornate kavya prose - the moment Sanskrit displaces Prakrit as the language of public inscription. It records that the Sudarshana reservoir was built by Pushyagupta, provincial governor of Chandragupta Maurya, and fitted with conduits by the Yavana Tushaspha under Ashoka, and that Rudradaman repaired it from his own treasury without a benevolence (pranaya) or forced labour (vishti) on his subjects.
  - Skandagupta's record of 455-458 CE reports the dam's breach and repair again - three dynasties maintaining one irrigation work, the best single case study of continuity in public works in ancient India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-9; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* II.
