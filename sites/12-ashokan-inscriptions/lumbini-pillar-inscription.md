- **Location**: The pillar standing in the sacred garden at Lumbini, Rupandehi district, Nepal, beside the Mayadevi temple - the Rummindei pillar.
- **Period/Level**: Erected in Ashoka's twentieth regnal year, c. 249 BCE, in Brahmi and Prakrit, to mark his pilgrimage to the Buddha's birthplace. Found by A. Fuhrer and Khadga Shumsher Rana in 1896.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only Ashokan inscription that records a specific tax concession - the single most concrete piece of evidence for Mauryan land revenue.
  - The text states that Ashoka came in person, worshipped because "here the Buddha Sakyamuni was born", had a stone wall and a stone pillar set up, and made the village of Lumbini free of bali and liable to pay only an eighth share (atha-bhagiya) of the produce as bhaga.
  - Bali and bhaga are the two terms the whole discussion of Mauryan revenue rests on. Bhaga is the king's share of the produce, the normal rate being a sixth, reduced here to an eighth. Bali is a separate levy, usually read as a tax tied to the area of land cultivated, and it is remitted outright.
  - Because a religious site is being rewarded with a fiscal privilege, the inscription is also the earliest evidence in India of tax exemption used as an instrument of royal patronage - the practice that later becomes the agrahara and the land grant.
  - Note for the maps: this entry and the Rummindei entry describe the same pillar and should be merged or plotted as one dot.
