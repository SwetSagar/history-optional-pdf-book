- **Location**: The Dhauli rock, on a low hill beside the Daya river about 8 km south of Bhubaneswar, Odisha - overlooking the plain traditionally identified as the battlefield of the Kalinga war, with the fortified city of Sisupalgarh a few kilometres away.
- **Period/Level**: Major Rock Edicts 1-10 and 14, together with the two Separate Kalinga Edicts, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 257-256 BCE. The elephant carved above the inscription is of the same date. The modern Shanti Stupa on the hill was built in 1972.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest rock-cut sculpture in India stands above the edicts that are Ashoka's answer to the war he fought here.
  - Major Rock Edicts 11, 12 and 13 are omitted, as at Jaugada. Edict 13 is the one that narrates the Kalinga conquest and his remorse - it was not shown to the Kalingans themselves. In its place stand the two Separate Edicts addressed to the mahamatras at Tosali.
  - Separate Edict I contains the line "all men are my children", instructs officers against harshness, jealousy and arbitrary imprisonment, and orders an inspection tour every five years to see that they comply. Separate Edict II addresses the unconquered forest peoples of the frontier, telling them the king wishes them no harm.
  - The forepart of an elephant is carved out of the living rock above the text, emerging from the boulder - the earliest example of rock-cut sculpture in India, and a Buddhist symbol placed deliberately over an edict about conquest and its aftermath.
  - Sisupalgarh nearby, almost certainly the Tosali the edicts address, is the excavated square fortified city of the same period, with eight gateways and a grid of streets.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
