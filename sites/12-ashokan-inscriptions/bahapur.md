- **Location**: Bahapur, near Kalkaji in south Delhi, on a rocky outcrop of the Delhi ridge - on the Uttarapatha where it crosses the Yamuna-Aravalli gap.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edict I, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Discovered in 1966, the latest major Ashokan find in north India.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Extends the Minor Rock Edict distribution into the Delhi region, and confirms the rule that these edicts follow trade roads.
  - The text is Ashoka's first personal message - his own account of becoming an upasaka, his initial lack of zeal, his drawing near to the Sangha, and his call that people should exert themselves in dhamma - not an administrative order.
  - Its setting is the point: Bahapur is not a city or a capital but a rock beside the Uttarapatha, the trunk road from the north-west to the Ganga plain. The Minor Rock Edicts map the empire's communications, whereas the Major Rock Edicts mark its frontiers.
  - Delhi holds two further Ashokan monuments, the Topra and Meerut pillars, but those were brought here by Firoz Shah Tughlaq in 1356; Bahapur is the only Ashokan inscription standing where Ashoka's officers put it.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI.
