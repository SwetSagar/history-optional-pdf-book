- **Location**: Mansehra, Hazara division, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, on three large boulders beside the road through the Pakhli plain - on the route from Kashmir and the upper Indus down to Taxila.
- **Period/Level**: All fourteen Major Rock Edicts, cut in the Kharoshthi script in Prakrit, c. 257-256 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: With Shahbazgarhi, one of only two Ashokan sites written in Kharoshthi - the evidence that Mauryan administration adapted to the script its subjects already used.
  - Kharoshthi runs right to left and derives from Aramaic, the chancery script of the Achaemenid empire, which held Gandhara until Alexander. Its use here is direct evidence of a century and a half of Persian administrative habit surviving under the Mauryas.
  - Brahmi, by contrast, runs left to right and is used everywhere else in the empire. That one emperor issued the same fourteen edicts in two scripts, at the same time, is the standard illustration of Mauryan administrative pragmatism.
  - The edicts are cut on three separate boulders rather than one face, the text distributed across them - a practical response to the rock available.
  - Mansehra sat on a live trade route and its edicts mark the empire's north-western frontier zone, along with Shahbazgarhi, Kandahar and Laghman.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Early India*.
