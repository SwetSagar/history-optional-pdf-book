- **Location**: Two pillars about 25 km apart in West Champaran district, Bihar - Lauriya Nandangarh and Lauriya Araraj - standing in the plain north of the Gandak on the royal road from Pataliputra to Nepal.
- **Period/Level**: Polished Chunar sandstone shafts carrying Pillar Edicts I-VI, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, cut in Ashoka's twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh regnal years, c. 243-242 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-preserved Ashokan pillar still standing on its original site, and the model for what a complete pillar looked like.
  - The Lauriya Nandangarh shaft is a single piece of sandstone about 10 m high, tapering, with the celebrated Mauryan polish, carrying a bell-shaped lotus capital, a circular abacus with a row of geese (hamsa), and a seated lion on top - the only pillar where shaft, capital and animal all survive in place.
  - Nandangarh beside it is a large brick stupa mound, excavated by Theodor Bloch and N. G. Majumdar, with a series of earthen burial mounds nearby - so the pillar stands in a monumental landscape, not alone.
  - The Lauriya Araraj pillar carries the same six edicts but has lost its capital.
  - Pillar Edicts I-VI set out the working of dhamma: Ashoka's concern for the next world, the appointment of officers, the list of animals protected from slaughter, the three-day respite granted to condemned prisoners, and his instruction that officials review and instruct the people every five years.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI.
