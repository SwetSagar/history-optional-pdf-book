- **Location**: The Allahabad (Prayagraj) pillar, now standing inside the Akbar fort at the Ganga-Yamuna confluence, Uttar Pradesh. It was not made for this spot: it originally stood at Kaushambi and was moved here, probably in the Mughal period.
- **Period/Level**: Polished Chunar sandstone, c. 240s BCE. It carries Pillar Edicts I-VI (twenty-sixth to twenty-seventh regnal years, c. 243-242 BCE), the Schism or Kaushambi Edict, and the Queen's Edict - all in Brahmi and Prakrit. Later inscriptions were cut on the same shaft in the 4th century CE and the 17th.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The most heavily inscribed object in Indian history - one pillar carrying Mauryan, Gupta and Mughal records, eighteen centuries apart.
  - The Queen's Edict is the only Ashokan record to name a queen: it directs that the gifts of the second queen Karuvaki, mother of Tivara, be credited to her - evidence both of a queen's independent religious endowment and of an administration careful about attribution.
  - The Schism Edict, addressed to the mahamatras of Kaushambi, orders that any monk or nun who divides the Sangha be made to wear white and expelled - the state intervening directly in monastic discipline.
  - The Prayaga Prashasti of Samudragupta, composed by Harishena in polished Sanskrit kavya, is cut on the same surface: it lists the kings of Aryavarta uprooted, the Dakshinapatha kings captured and released, and the frontier states paying homage - the single most important source for Gupta political history. That a Gupta panegyric was added to an Ashokan pillar is itself an argument about how the Guptas saw their inheritance.
  - Jahangir added a Persian inscription recording his accession, completing the sequence.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7 and 9; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* I and III.
