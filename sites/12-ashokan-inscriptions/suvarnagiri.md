- **Location**: Suvarnagiri, the "golden mountain" - the southern provincial capital of the Maurya empire, generally identified with Kanakagiri near Maski in the Raichur-Koppal tract of Karnataka, between the Krishna and the Tungabhadra.
- **Period/Level**: A Mauryan provincial headquarters of the 3rd century BCE. It is named not by an inscription of its own but in the Minor Rock Edicts at Brahmagiri, Siddapura and Jatinga-Rameshwara, c. 258-257 BCE, which are issued from it.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The clearest evidence for the structure of Mauryan provincial government - a capital known entirely from being quoted in other people's edicts.
  - The Brahmagiri group opens with an order sent from the aryaputra and the mahamatras at Suvarnagiri to the mahamatras at Isila. Aryaputra means a prince of the blood, so the southern province was governed by a kumara of the royal house - as were Tosali in Kalinga, Ujjayini in Avanti and Taxila in the north-west, with Pataliputra as the centre. That is the whole administrative map of the empire, and it comes from this formula.
  - Its name and its location in the Raichur doab tie it to the gold-bearing country around the Hatti mines, which is the usual explanation for why the Mauryas pushed this far south and held it with a prince rather than an officer.
  - Kanakagiri is a Sanskrit calque of Suvarnagiri, which is the main argument for the identification; the site has not been excavated to confirm it, so treat the location as probable rather than settled.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
