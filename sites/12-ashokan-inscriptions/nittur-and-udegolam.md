- **Location**: Nittur and Udegolam, two rock sites a few kilometres apart in Bellary district, Karnataka, in the granite country north of the Tungabhadra - close to the Raichur doab gold belt.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edicts I and II, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Discovered in 1977-78, the last major addition to the Ashokan corpus.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Two of the five inscriptions that name Ashoka outright, and a reminder that the corpus is still growing.
  - Both texts read "Devanampiya Asoka", giving the personal name beside the title. With Maski, Gujarra and Panguraria these are the only places that do so, and they are the basis on which the Piyadasi of the edicts is identified with the Ashoka of the Buddhist tradition.
  - Minor Rock Edict I is the personal statement about his becoming a lay follower and his zeal; Minor Rock Edict II gives dhamma its practical content - obedience to mother and father, respect for elders and teachers, compassion to living beings, truthfulness.
  - The Minor Rock Edicts are earlier than the Major Rock Edicts, and their dense concentration in northern Karnataka and the Raichur-Kurnool tract - Nittur, Udegolam, Maski, Palkigundu, Gavimath, Brahmagiri, Siddapura, Jatinga-Rameshwara, Rajula-Mandagiri, Yerragudi - is usually explained by the gold and other mineral resources of the region, which gave the Mauryas a reason to hold it.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI, *Epigraphia Indica*.
