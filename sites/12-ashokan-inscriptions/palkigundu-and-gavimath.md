- **Location**: Palkigundu and Gavimath, two rock sites near Koppal in Koppal district, Karnataka, among granite hills north of the Tungabhadra - Palkigundu on a hilltop boulder, Gavimath on a low rock beside the plain.
- **Period/Level**: Minor Rock Edict I, Brahmi script, Prakrit language, c. 258-257 BCE. Recorded in the late 19th century and edited by Buhler.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Part of the dense southern cluster of Minor Rock Edicts, and a pair that shows how the same text was cut twice within sight of each other.
  - Minor Rock Edict I preaches dhamma and the duty of exerting oneself in it. Ashoka states how long he has been an upasaka, admits he was not zealous at first, and declares that through his effort even the humble can attain heaven - the levelling claim that is the most quoted line of the edict.
  - He also orders that the proclamation be carried far, engraved on rock where rock is available, and that officers tour the countryside to spread it - the edicts themselves describe the mechanism of their own distribution, and the southern cluster is that instruction carried out.
  - Koppal is ancient Kopana, a Jaina centre of note in later centuries, and the tract belongs to the mineral-rich Raichur doab that drew Mauryan attention southward.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; ASI, *Epigraphia Indica*.
