- **Location**: Panguraria, at the foot of the Saru Maru hills in Sehore district, Madhya Pradesh, on the north bank of the Narmada in the Vindhyas - on the road from Vidisha towards Ujjayini.
- **Period/Level**: Two inscriptions in Brahmi and Prakrit. One is Minor Rock Edict I, c. 258-257 BCE; the second is a separate royal record cut in a rock shelter, referring to Ashoka's visit here while he was still a kumara, before his accession.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only Ashokan inscription that looks back to the emperor's life before he was emperor.
  - The second text records that the king, "when he was a prince, came to this place on a pleasure tour" from Ujjayini - where Ashoka served as viceroy for Bindusara. No other edict mentions his pre-accession career, which is otherwise known only from the Buddhist chronicles.
  - The Minor Rock Edict here names him as Asoka, one of the five sites in the whole corpus to give the personal name, alongside Maski, Gujarra, Nittur and Udegolam.
  - Natural caves and rock shelters around the site were used by Buddhist monks, and stupas stand nearby; some shelters carry prehistoric paintings, so the hill has a Mesolithic and a Mauryan layer together.
  - Note for the maps: Panguraria and Saru Maru are the same site under two names, and the two entries should be merged or plotted as one dot.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Harry Falk, *Asokan Sites and Artefacts*.
