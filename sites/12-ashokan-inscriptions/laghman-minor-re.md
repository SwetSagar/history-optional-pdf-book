- **Location**: Laghman province, eastern Afghanistan, on the Alishang and Alingar rivers north of Jalalabad - on the road linking Bactria and the Kabul valley to the Khyber and the Indus.
- **Period/Level**: Aramaic inscriptions of Ashoka's period, c. 260-250 BCE, found in 1969-70 (Laghman I) and 1973 (Laghman II), cut on rock beside the route.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The furthest north-west that Mauryan authority is attested in writing, and the only Ashokan texts that read like road markers.
  - The Laghman inscriptions are in Aramaic, the Achaemenid administrative language still in use across the region a century after Persian rule ended - so Mauryan officials here worked in the script and language their predecessors had left behind.
  - Laghman I records a distance measured in a local unit along the road and mentions Tadmor (Palmyra), an extraordinary reference linking a Mauryan wayside inscription to the caravan cities of the Syrian desert.
  - Their content is thin on dhamma and heavy on administration, which supports reading the north-western texts as the work of a provincial chancery rather than direct royal proclamations.
  - Note for the maps: Lampaka is the Sanskrit name for Laghman, so this site and the Lampaka entry are the same place.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
