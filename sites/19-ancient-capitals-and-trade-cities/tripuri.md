- **Location**: Tewar, ancient Tripuri, near Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh, on the north bank of the Narmada - in the Dahala country, the corridor between the Gangetic plain and the Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: An early historic settlement with Satavahana and later occupation, and from the 8th to the 13th centuries the capital of the Kalachuris of Chedi. The dynasty's peak falls under Gangeyadeva and his son Karnadeva, r. 1041-1073 CE; Tripuri fell to the Chandellas and then to the Delhi Sultanate's expansion.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Kalachuri capital, and the origin of an era used across central India.
  - The Kalachuri or Chedi era, reckoned from 248 CE, was used in inscriptions over a wide swathe of central and western India for a thousand years - one of the major Indian dating systems, alongside the Vikrama, Shaka and Gupta eras, and the practical reason the dynasty matters for chronology.
  - Gangeyadeva took the title *Vikramaditya* and struck the gold *seated Lakshmi* coins that later circulated widely and were imitated by neighbouring dynasties. Karnadeva campaigned as far as Bengal and built the temple group at Amarkantak at the Narmada's source.
  - Tripuri's position made the Kalachuris a permanent third party in the wars of the Paramaras, Chandellas and Chalukyas, and the Bilhari and Karanbel inscriptions are the main sources for that history.
  - Excavation at Tewar has exposed early historic levels with punch-marked coins and Satavahana material, showing the site was a town long before it was a capital.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; ASI.
