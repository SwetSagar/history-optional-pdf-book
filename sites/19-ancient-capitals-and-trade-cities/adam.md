- **Location**: Adam, Kuhi tehsil, Nagpur district, Maharashtra, in the Wainganga basin of eastern Vidarbha - the plateau country between the Godavari system and the Chhattisgarh plain.
- **Period/Level**: A long sequence from the Chalcolithic through the megalithic and early historic periods to the Satavahana and Vakataka centuries. Excavated by the ASI from the 1980s; the fortified early historic town yielded Northern Black Polished Ware, punch-marked and Satavahana coins, and a large body of seals and sealings.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site that gave the Asmaka *mahajanapada* a physical location - the only one of the sixteen lying in the Deccan.
  - A sealing from the site reads *Asikasa janapadasa*, "of the Asika janapada". Asmaka or Assaka is listed among the sixteen mahajanapadas in the Buddhist *Anguttara Nikaya* but is the only southern member, and until this find it had no securely identified centre. Epigraphy fixing a textual polity on the ground is exactly the kind of evidence examiners want cited.
  - The defences, the NBPW and the coinage together show a fortified urban centre in Vidarbha contemporary with the second urbanisation of the Ganga plain, which complicates the usual picture of urbanism spreading outward from the north.
  - Megalithic burials in the vicinity link the early historic town to the preceding Vidarbha megalithic culture of Mahurjhari and Naikund, giving an unusually continuous local sequence.
  - Later Satavahana and Vakataka material shows the settlement surviving as a regional town well into the historic period.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6 and 8; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
