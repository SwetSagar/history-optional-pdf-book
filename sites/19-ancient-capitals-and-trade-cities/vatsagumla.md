- **Location**: Washim, ancient Vatsagulma, Washim district, Maharashtra, on the Penganga watershed in southern Vidarbha - inland Deccan, between the Godavari and the Wardha.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Vatsagulma branch of the Vakatakas, founded by Sarvasena, son of Pravarasena I, in the later 4th century CE and ruling until the early 6th. The senior Nandivardhana-Pravarapura branch ruled from near Ramtek; Harishena, r. c. 475-500, was the last great Vatsagulma king.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The court that paid for the later caves at Ajanta - the most consequential act of patronage in Indian art history.
  - The second, Mahayana phase of Ajanta, Caves 16, 17, 19 and 26 among them, was excavated under Harishena and his feudatories; Cave 16 was the gift of his minister Varahadeva, whose inscription records it. The paintings that define classical Indian art are therefore the product of this dynasty and effectively stop when it falls.
  - Walter Spink's chronology compresses the whole later Ajanta programme into roughly two decades around Harishena's reign, arguing that work was abandoned mid-chisel when the dynasty collapsed. Unfinished caves at the site support the argument, which is the standard reference for dating Ajanta.
  - The Vatsagulma branch patronised Sanskrit literature: Sarvasena wrote the Prakrit *Harivijaya*, and Pravarasena II of the senior line is credited with the *Setubandha*. The Vakatakas are the bridge between Satavahana Prakrit culture and Gupta-age Sanskrit.
  - Their marriage alliance with the Guptas, through Prabhavatigupta, daughter of Chandragupta II, who ruled as regent, is the best-documented case of a queen exercising sovereign authority in the period.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; Thapar, *Early India*; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*.
