- **Location**: Awantipora, Pulwama district, Jammu and Kashmir, on the right bank of the Jhelum between Srinagar and Anantnag - on the valley floor, commanding the river route through Kashmir.
- **Period/Level**: Founded by Avantivarman of the Utpala dynasty, r. 855-883 CE, who built two temples here: the Avantisvamin, dedicated to Vishnu, and the larger Avantisvara, to Shiva. Both are in the Kashmiri order - colonnaded courtyards, trefoil arches, pediments and stone roofs - and both were ruined by earthquake and iconoclasm.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Utpala capital, and the setting of the most celebrated feat of engineering in Kashmir's history.
  - Kalhana's *Rajatarangini* records that Avantivarman's engineer Suyya regulated the Jhelum where it was choked at Baramulla, drained the marshes, shifted the confluence of the Jhelum and Sindh, and reclaimed vast tracts for cultivation - so that the price of rice fell dramatically. The town of Sopore is named after him.
  - That episode is the best-documented instance of state-directed hydraulic works in early medieval India, and it is usually cited against the view that Indian kingship was ritual rather than administrative.
  - The temples continue the Kashmiri style established at Martand under the Karkotas a century earlier, and the two sites are normally taken together as the surviving evidence of that tradition.
  - Kalhana's chronicle, written in 1149-50, gives Kashmir a continuous historical narrative unmatched anywhere else in India, and Awantipora is one of the places where it can be matched to standing remains.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; Kalhana, *Rajatarangini* V.
