- **Location**: Pawaya, ancient Padmavati, Gwalior district, Madhya Pradesh, at the confluence of the Sindh and Para rivers - in the Chambal-Sindh tract between Malwa and the Ganga plain.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Nagas of Padmavati in the 3rd-4th centuries CE, in the interval between Kushana decline and Gupta consolidation. The mound has yielded Naga coins in quantity, a brick temple platform, and an inscribed Manibhadra yaksha image.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of one of the regional powers that filled the gap between two empires, and the dynasty Samudragupta had to remove.
  - Samudragupta's Allahabad pillar inscription names Ganapatinaga among the kings of Aryavarta violently uprooted. Padmavati is therefore one of the few places where a defeated dynasty from that famous list can be located on the ground and its coinage handled.
  - The Naga rulers - Bharashivas in some readings - are credited in Puranic tradition with reviving Vedic sacrifice and the *ashvamedha* after the Kushana period, so the site features in arguments about Brahmanical resurgence before the Guptas.
  - Padmavati is the setting of Bhavabhuti's Sanskrit play *Malatimadhava*, written in the 8th century, which describes the city and its river confluence in enough detail to be read against the excavated topography.
  - The Manibhadra yaksha image is an important early example of yaksha worship, the popular cult stratum that runs beneath classical Hinduism and Buddhism alike.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8 and 9; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
