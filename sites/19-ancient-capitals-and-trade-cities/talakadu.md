- **Location**: Talakad, Mysuru district, Karnataka, on the left bank of the Kaveri where the river turns sharply east - the town now lies largely buried beneath sand dunes thrown up by the river.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Western Gangas from about the 6th century CE. Taken by Rajaraja Chola in 1004 and held as Rajarajapura for over a century; recovered by the Hoysala Vishnuvardhana in 1117, who took the title *Talakadugonda*. It declined under the Vijayanagara and Wodeyar periods as the sand advanced.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Western Ganga capital, and the pivot of the long contest between Chola and Hoysala for southern Karnataka.
  - The Western Gangas ruled Gangavadi for some six centuries and are best known for their Jain patronage, above all Chamundaraya's colossal Bahubali at Shravanabelagola. Talakad is the political centre behind that religious monument.
  - Its capture by Rajaraja I marks the high-water mark of Chola expansion into the Kannada country, and Vishnuvardhana's recovery of it is the event that established Hoysala power - the title he took from the victory is how the dynasty dated its rise.
  - Five Shiva temples, the *Pancha Linga*, are exposed and reburied by the moving sand, and are worshipped together in a festival held when the astronomical conditions recur.
  - The sanding-over is explained locally by the curse of Alamelamma, whose lands the Mysore Wodeyars seized in 1610. The legend is a good example of how environmental change gets narrated as moral history, and should be presented as such.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Epigraphia Carnatica*.
