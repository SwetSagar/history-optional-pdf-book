- **Location**: Pragjyotishpura, identified with Guwahati, Assam, on the Brahmaputra where the river narrows between hills - the natural crossing and control point of the whole valley.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Kamarupa kingdom under the Varman dynasty, c. 350-650 CE, whose best-known ruler is Bhaskaravarman, r. c. 600-650, the ally of Harshavardhana. Later Kamarupa dynasties - the Mlechchhas and the Palas of Kamarupa - ruled from Haruppeshvara and Durjaya nearby.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the earliest historical kingdom of the north-east, and the point at which Assam enters the mainstream Indian record.
  - Bhaskaravarman's Nidhanpur copper plates, issued from a military camp, grant land to more than two hundred brahmanas and are the principal document for Kamarupa's administration and for the settlement of brahmanas in the Brahmaputra valley - the mechanism by which Sanskritic culture spread eastward.
  - Xuanzang visited his court at Harsha's invitation and describes the kingdom, its people and its distance from China. Bhaskaravarman's alliance with Harsha against Shashanka of Gauda places Kamarupa inside north Indian politics rather than outside it.
  - The *Mahabharata* and *Ramayana* refer to Pragjyotisha and its kings Naraka and Bhagadatta, so the site carries an epic identity alongside the historical one - the standard example of a frontier region being written into the pan-Indian tradition.
  - The Da-Parbatia doorframe at Tezpur and the Kamakhya shrine on Nilachal hill are the surviving material remains of this period and its religion.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Nidhanpur* copper plates.
