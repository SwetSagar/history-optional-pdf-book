- **Location**: Kolar, Karnataka, on the eastern edge of the Mysore plateau near the Andhra and Tamil borders, in the upper Palar basin - dry upland country underlain by the ancient greenstone belt that carries its gold.
- **Period/Level**: An early capital of the Western Gangas before they moved to Talakad, and later contested by the Cholas, who held it in the 10th-11th centuries, and by the Hoysalas and Vijayanagara. The Kolaramma temple is Chola work of the 10th-11th centuries with later additions.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: India's principal gold field, worked from antiquity, and an early Ganga seat.
  - The Kolar Gold Fields were mined long before the shafts of the colonial period. Ancient workings, and lead-isotope studies matching gold objects from Harappan and later contexts to Kolar ore, are the basis for arguing that peninsular gold reached the north from the third millennium BCE onward.
  - That makes Kolar the standard answer to where early Indian gold came from - a question usually left vague - and ties the Deccan interior into the exchange networks of the Harappan and early historic worlds.
  - The Western Gangas took their early name from this region, and Kolar's Jain and Shaiva monuments record the succession of powers that fought over it.
  - Under the Cholas the area was organised as Nikarilicholamandalam, and the inscriptions of the Kolaramma temple are used for Chola provincial administration in the Kannada country.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4 and 10; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Geological Survey of India.
