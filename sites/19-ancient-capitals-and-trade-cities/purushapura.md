- **Location**: Peshawar, ancient Purushapura, in the Vale of Peshawar near the Bara and Kabul rivers, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan - at the Indian end of the Khyber pass, where the routes from Bactria and Central Asia debouch onto the plain.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Kushana empire under Kanishka I, r. from c. 127 CE, who moved the seat here from Bactria; the city remained the centre of Gandhara through the later Kushana and Kidarite periods until the Hephthalite disruption.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital from which the Kushanas ruled an empire spanning Central Asia and northern India, and the centre of Gandhara Buddhism.
  - The great stupa Kanishka raised at Shah-ji-ki-Dheri was described by Faxian and Xuanzang as the tallest structure they had seen anywhere. Excavation in 1908-09 recovered the Kanishka casket from its relic chamber, inscribed in Kharoshthi and bearing the king's own name with Buddha figures - among the few Indian reliquaries that can be tied to a named ruler.
  - Purushapura is where the Fourth Buddhist Council is traditionally placed under Kanishka's patronage, with Vasumitra and Ashvaghosha, marking the formal emergence of Mahayana - though the council's historicity is disputed and should be presented as tradition.
  - Its position made it the exchange point of the Silk Route with India, which is why Gandhara sculpture fuses Hellenistic form with Buddhist subject; the first anthropomorphic Buddha images belong to this milieu.
  - Kanishka's coinage, naming Greek, Iranian and Indian deities together, expresses the same cosmopolitanism from the same capital.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*.
