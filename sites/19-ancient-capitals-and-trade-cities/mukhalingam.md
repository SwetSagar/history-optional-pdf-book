- **Location**: Mukhalingam, Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh, on the Vamsadhara river close to the Odisha border - in the tract that was historically Kalinga rather than Andhra.
- **Period/Level**: Kalinganagara, capital of the Eastern Gangas from about the 8th century until Anantavarman Chodaganga shifted the seat to Cuttack in the 12th. Three principal temples survive: the Madhukeshwara of about the 8th-9th centuries, the Someshwara of the 10th, and the Bhimeshwara of the 11th.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of the dynasty that built Puri and Konark, and the place where the Kalinga temple style takes shape.
  - The Eastern Gangas ruled from here for four centuries before their great coastal phase. Anantavarman Chodaganga, who began the Jagannath temple at Puri c. 1135, belonged to this line, so Mukhalingam is where the architectural tradition that produced Puri and Konark was first worked out.
  - The Madhukeshwara temple shows the *rekha deul* with its curvilinear tower and the *jagamohana* porch already formed, with dense figural carving on the outer walls - the Kalinga order in an early but fully recognisable state.
  - The site sits on the cultural boundary between Odisha and Andhra, and its sculpture and inscriptions are used to argue about where that line lay in the early medieval period - a reminder that modern linguistic state borders map poorly onto historical regions.
  - The Ganga era, dated from 1038 CE and used in eastern Indian inscriptions for centuries, was instituted by this dynasty.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
