- **Location**: Bhokardan, ancient Bhogavardhana, Jalna district, Maharashtra, on the Kelna river in the upper Godavari basin - on the route between Pratishthana and the western ghat passes to the Konkan ports.
- **Period/Level**: A Satavahana-period trade and craft town, flourishing c. 2nd century BCE to 3rd century CE. Excavated jointly by Nagpur University and the ASI; the site has also produced Kshatrapa-period material.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A Deccan manufacturing town caught in the act of producing for the Roman trade.
  - The ivory carvings recovered here are the site's best-known finds: female figures and toilet articles in a style close to the celebrated ivory statuette found at Pompeii, and to the Begram ivories from Afghanistan. Bhokardan is the strongest candidate for the kind of Deccan workshop that supplied both.
  - Beads in carnelian, agate, crystal and glass, terracotta figurines, and Roman glass and pottery fragments show a settlement making high-value goods for export while consuming imports in return.
  - Its position matters: goods moved from Bhokardan and Paithan through the Naneghat and other passes to Kalyan and Sopara, so the town belongs to the same commercial system as the merchant endowments recorded in the western Deccan cave inscriptions.
  - Donative inscriptions naming residents of Bhogavardhana appear at Sanchi and in the western caves, which ties the excavated town to named individuals funding religious architecture elsewhere.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
