- **Location**: Madurai, Tamil Nadu, on the south bank of the Vaigai in the plain between the Palani hills and the Pandya country - walled on three sides with the river on the fourth, as the Sangam poems describe it.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Pandyas from the Sangam age, c. 3rd century BCE onward, through their early medieval revival to the 14th century; then the Madurai Sultanate, Vijayanagara, and from 1529 the Nayakas, whose greatest ruler was Tirumalai Nayaka, r. 1623-1659. This entry covers the city; for the temple see the Madurai Meenakshi entry in the temple chapter.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the very few Indian cities that has been a capital continuously for over two thousand years, and the seat of the Tamil literary academy.
  - Tamil tradition places the third *Sangam* here under Pandya patronage, and the surviving Sangam corpus is the earliest literature in any Indian language other than Sanskrit. The *Maduraikkanchi* describes the city's night markets, its quarters by trade, and its festivals in enough detail to be used as social evidence.
  - Megasthenes reports the kingdom of *Pandaia*, and the Pandyas sent an embassy to Augustus recorded by Strabo - among the earliest Indian diplomatic contacts with the Mediterranean world.
  - It was a manufacturing centre for textiles, goldwork, ivory and inlay, and the inland counterpart to the Pandya pearl ports at Korkai and Kayal.
  - Malik Kafur's raid of 1310-11 and the short-lived Madurai Sultanate that followed brought Delhi's power to the far south, and Vijayanagara's recovery of the region is what installed the Nayakas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I.
