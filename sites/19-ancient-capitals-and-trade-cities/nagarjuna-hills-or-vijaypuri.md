- **Location**: Nagarjunakonda, ancient Vijayapuri, in a valley of the Nallamala hills on the right bank of the Krishna, Palnadu district, Andhra Pradesh. The valley was flooded by the Nagarjunasagar reservoir in the 1960s; selected monuments were dismantled and re-erected on an island museum.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Ikshvakus, c. 225-325 CE, who succeeded the Satavahanas in the eastern Deccan. The site was excavated in the 1920s and then again, on a vast scale, in a salvage operation before submergence.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The most completely excavated early historic city in India - and the most completely lost.
  - More than thirty Buddhist establishments were recorded, with *mahachaityas*, *viharas* and limestone relief sculpture of the Amaravati school, alongside Brahmanical temples, a palace, and a stone-built amphitheatre unique in India.
  - Inscriptions record monasteries endowed for monks from Sri Lanka, Kashmir, Gandhara and even, in one reading, from beyond - the *Sihala vihara* among them. Nagarjunakonda is the best epigraphic evidence that Indian Buddhism was an international institution with resident foreign communities.
  - The endowments were overwhelmingly made by royal women - Chamtisri, sister of the founder Chamtamula, above all - while the kings performed *ashvamedha* and other Vedic sacrifices. Women funding Buddhism while men sacrificed to the Vedic gods, within one family, is the sharpest available illustration of plural patronage.
  - The name commemorates Nagarjuna, the Madhyamaka philosopher, whose association with the site is traditional rather than documented.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
