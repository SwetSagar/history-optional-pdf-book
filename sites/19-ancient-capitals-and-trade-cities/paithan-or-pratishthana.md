- **Location**: Paithan, ancient Pratishthana, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar district, Maharashtra, on the north bank of the Godavari - the head of navigation and the natural centre of the Deccan plateau.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Satavahanas from about the 1st century BCE to the 2nd century CE, and a major market long after. Ptolemy names it *Baithana*; the *Periplus* pairs it with Tagara as the two great inland marts of the Deccan; Ashoka's Rock Edict V and XIII name the *Petenikas* among the peoples of his realm.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The southern terminus of the Dakshinapatha, and the capital of the dynasty that made the Deccan a political unit.
  - The *Periplus* describes caravans bringing onyx from Pratishthana and cotton cloth from Tagara down to Barygaza over difficult country - a rare classical description of Indian internal trade, and the reason Paithan appears in every answer on the Dakshinapatha.
  - As the Satavahana seat it is the political counterpart to Naneghat, Nashik and Karle: the inscriptions in those caves record the dynasty's sacrifices and the merchants' endowments, while Paithan is where the court that received both actually sat.
  - The *Gatha Saptashati*, an anthology of seven hundred Prakrit verses attributed to the Satavahana king Hala, is associated with this court. That a royal house patronised Prakrit rather than Sanskrit lyric is a standard point on early Deccan literary culture.
  - Paithani silk weaving, with its zari borders, is a craft tradition the town has carried continuously.
  - *Sources*: R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*.
