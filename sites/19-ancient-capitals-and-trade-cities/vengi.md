- **Location**: Pedavegi, ancient Vengi, Eluru district, Andhra Pradesh, in the fertile doab between the Krishna and the Godavari - the agricultural core of coastal Andhra.
- **Period/Level**: A centre from the Mauryan period, then capital successively of the Salankayanas (4th-5th centuries CE), the Vishnukundins (5th-6th), and from 624 CE the Eastern Chalukyas, founded by Kubja Vishnuvardhana, brother of Pulakesin II, after the Chalukya conquest of the region.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the Eastern Chalukyas, whose dynastic marriage into the Cholas reshaped south Indian politics and created Telugu literature.
  - Vengi's fertility made it a permanent object of contest - between the Pallavas and Chalukyas, then between the Cholas and the Western Chalukyas of Kalyani for two centuries. Control of the Krishna-Godavari doab is the recurring strategic question of early medieval peninsular history.
  - The contest was resolved dynastically: Rajaraja Narendra of Vengi married a Chola princess, and their son ascended the Chola throne as Kulottunga I in 1070, uniting the two lines. A succession dispute settled by marriage rather than conquest is a useful counter-example to purely military narratives.
  - Nannaya, at Rajaraja Narendra's court in the 11th century, began the Telugu *Mahabharata* - the beginning of Telugu literature, and the reason Vengi belongs in answers on regional languages as well as on dynasties.
  - Excavation at Pedavegi has exposed brick structures, a Buddhist stupa and Satavahana and later coinage, showing the site's depth well before the Chalukyas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
