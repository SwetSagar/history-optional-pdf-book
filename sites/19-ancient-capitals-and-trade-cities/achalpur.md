- **Location**: Achalpur, formerly Ellichpur, Amravati district, Maharashtra, on the Sapan river at the foot of the Melghat hills - the historic capital of Berar, the cotton plain between the Tapti and the Wardha.
- **Period/Level**: A regional centre under the Yadavas of Devagiri, annexed by the Delhi Sultanate in the early 14th century and made a provincial seat; then Bahmani. It became the capital of the independent Imad Shahi sultanate of Berar from 1490 until Ahmadnagar absorbed it in 1572, after which it was the Mughal subah capital of Berar.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of Berar through the sultanate centuries - the least studied of the five Deccan successor states.
  - When the Bahmani kingdom broke up, Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk, governor of Berar, declared independence here in 1490. The Imad Shahis were the weakest of the five Deccan sultanates and the first to be swallowed, which is why Achalpur seldom appears beside Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar and Bidar - though it completes the set.
  - Berar's cotton made it the revenue prize of the eastern Deccan. Its transfer to the Mughals and later its assignment to the Nizam, and then its lease to the British in 1853, make the province a recurring item in Deccan politics down to the nineteenth century.
  - The town retains the Achaleshwar Mahadev and Kembhaswami temples, the fort, and Sultanate-period tombs and mosques.
  - Note that some note-sets make Achalpur a Rashtrakuta or Paramara capital. It was neither: the Rashtrakuta seat was Manyakheta and the Paramara seat Dhar.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; ASI.
