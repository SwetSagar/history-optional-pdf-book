- **Location**: Uraiyur, now a quarter of Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu, on the south bank of the Kaveri where the river divides around Srirangam island - the head of the Kaveri delta.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the early Cholas through the Sangam age, c. 3rd century BCE to 3rd century CE, and still significant later. Excavation has produced Black-and-Red Ware, Roman pottery and coins, terracottas and a dyeing vat. Ptolemy names it *Orthoura*.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the early Cholas before Thanjavur, and the cotton town of the Sangam economy.
  - Sangam poems describe Uraiyur as fortified, with burial grounds outside the walls, and celebrate its cotton cloth as fine as the slough of a snake - the literary source for Tamil textile manufacture that the excavated dyeing vat then corroborates.
  - Roman material here, as at Arikamedu and Puhar, places the early Chola capital inside the Indo-Roman trade network, with Uraiyur the inland manufacturing centre and Puhar its port at the Kaveri mouth.
  - The early Cholas of Karikala, remembered for building the Kallanai dam across the Kaveri, belong to this phase. The dynasty then disappears from view for some six centuries before Vijayalaya's revival in the 9th century - a gap that is itself a standard question.
  - Uraiyur, Puhar and Thanjavur in sequence trace the Chola centre of gravity moving down and across the delta over a millennium.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*.
