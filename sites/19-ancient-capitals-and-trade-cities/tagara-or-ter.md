- **Location**: Ter, ancient Tagara, Dharashiv (Osmanabad) district, Maharashtra, on the Terna river in the Marathwada uplands - inland, on the caravan route between the Godavari valley and the western ghats.
- **Period/Level**: A trade town of the Satavahana period, c. 2nd century BCE to 3rd century CE, with occupation continuing later. Excavation has produced ivory, beads, terracottas, Roman material and a brick apsidal structure now enclosed within the Trivikrama temple.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the two inland markets the *Periplus* names for the whole Deccan, and the source of a famous piece of Indian export art.
  - The *Periplus* states that from Tagara came great quantities of common cotton cloth, carried by wagon over difficult roads to Barygaza. A Greek text naming a Marathwada town is striking evidence of how deep into the interior the Roman trade reached.
  - The ivory work found here is stylistically close to the Indian ivory statuette recovered at Pompeii, buried in 79 CE. Linking an excavated Deccan workshop to an object found in a Roman town is the most concrete demonstration available of Indian manufactured exports reaching Italy.
  - The brick apsidal structure is an early Buddhist *chaitya* form later converted to Brahmanical use, so the site also documents religious change at an ordinary commercial town rather than at a royal centre.
  - Ter and Paithan together show that the Deccan's wealth rested on textile manufacture and transit trade, not only on land revenue.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
