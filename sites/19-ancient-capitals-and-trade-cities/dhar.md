- **Location**: Dhar, Madhya Pradesh, on the Malwa plateau south-west of Indore, between the Narmada and the upper Chambal - the agricultural core of Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Paramaras from the 9th century, at its height under Bhoja, r. c. 1010-1055 CE. Taken by the Delhi Sultanate in 1305, it became part of the Malwa Sultanate before the seat moved to Mandu. A centre of the 1857 revolt in central India.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The court of the scholar-king Bhoja, and the site of a documented medieval college.
  - Bhoja is credited with works across an extraordinary range - the *Samarangana Sutradhara* on architecture and mechanical devices, the *Sarasvati Kanthabharana* on poetics, and texts on grammar, medicine and astronomy. Whether he wrote them all or presided over a workshop of scholars, the corpus is the standard example of royal patronage of learning in early medieval India.
  - The *Bhojashala* was a Sanskrit college attached to a temple of Vagdevi, Sarasvati. Slabs there carry Sanskrit and Prakrit grammatical rules engraved in serpentine coils, including a *Nagabandha* arrangement - teaching material cut into stone, which exists nowhere else.
  - The Vagdevi image associated with the school was removed in the colonial period and is in the British Museum; the building has since been contested between Hindu and Muslim communities, so the site also illustrates how medieval monuments become present-day disputes.
  - The Lat Masjid and Kamal Maula mosque, built with re-used temple material, mark the Sultanate phase.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Thapar, *Early India*.
