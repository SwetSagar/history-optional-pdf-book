- **Location**: Bhita, on the left bank of the Yamuna about 18 km south-west of Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh - a mound beside the river a short distance above the Ganga-Yamuna confluence.
- **Period/Level**: Occupied from about the 5th-4th centuries BCE through the Mauryan, Shunga, Kushana and Gupta periods. Excavated by John Marshall in 1911-12, one of the earliest stratigraphic excavations of an Indian urban site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The clearest excavated plan of an ordinary early historic Indian town - not a capital, but a working commercial settlement.
  - Marshall exposed a main street with houses opening off it, courtyards, drains and shops, and worked out the sequence of rebuilding levels. Because the town was neither a great capital nor a religious centre, it is the standard reference for how early historic urban India actually looked.
  - Seals and sealings found in the houses name individuals, guilds and corporate bodies, using the term *nigama*. They are among the best evidence for organised commercial associations - guilds acting as recognised legal entities - in the centuries around the turn of the era.
  - Northern Black Polished Ware, punch-marked and Kushana coins, terracotta figurines and ring wells anchor the chronology and connect it to the wider second urbanisation of the Ganga plain.
  - Bhita is usually set beside Kaushambi, its larger neighbour, to contrast a fortified regional capital with a subordinate market town in the same stretch of the Yamuna.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7 and 8; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; John Marshall, *ASI Annual Report* 1911-12.
