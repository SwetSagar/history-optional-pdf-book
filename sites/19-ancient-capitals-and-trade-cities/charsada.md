- **Location**: Charsadda, ancient Pushkalavati, at the confluence of the Swat and Kabul rivers in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan - about 30 km north-east of Peshawar, in the heart of Gandhara.
- **Period/Level**: The capital of Gandhara before Purushapura, occupied from about the 6th century BCE. The mounds of Bala Hisar and Shaikhan Dheri carry the sequence: Achaemenid satrapal, Macedonian, Mauryan, Indo-Greek, Saka-Parthian and Kushana. Excavated by Mortimer Wheeler in 1958 and later by Pakistani and British teams.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site for the north-west's long exposure to successive foreign powers, and the stratigraphy against which that sequence is dated.
  - Gandhara was the twentieth satrapy of the Achaemenid empire, its tribute recorded by Herodotus and its people named in Darius's inscriptions. Charsadda is where that administration was seated, making it the physical evidence for Persian rule in the subcontinent.
  - Alexander's forces besieged the city for thirty days in 327 BCE under Hephaestion, according to Arrian - one of the few episodes of the Indian campaign that can be attached to an excavated site.
  - Wheeler's work established a ceramic and stratigraphic sequence for the north-west that is still the reference framework, and the site has produced Indo-Greek and Saka coinage in quantity.
  - Under the Mauryas it was a provincial centre on the road to Taxila; the two cities together define the north-western frontier of Mauryan administration.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6 and 8; Thapar, *Early India*; R. E. M. Wheeler, *Charsada*.
