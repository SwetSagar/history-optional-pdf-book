- **Location**: Vizhinjam, Thiruvananthapuram district, Kerala, on a rocky headland at the southern end of the Malabar coast - one of the few natural deep-water anchorages on India's western seaboard.
- **Period/Level**: Capital and principal port of the Ay dynasty, prominent from about the 8th to the 10th centuries CE, and repeatedly contested by the Pandyas and the Cheras. The rock-cut cave temple with its unfinished Shiva and Parvati panel belongs to the Ay period, c. 8th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of the Ays, the third power of the far south, and the harbour that its geography made worth fighting for.
  - The Ay kingdom held the coast between the Cheras and the Pandyas, and Vizhinjam was its capital. The prolonged Pandya campaigns against it - the Vizhinjam war recorded in inscriptions and in the Sangam-successor literature - are the main evidence for the politics of the far south between the Sangam age and the Chola expansion.
  - Its deep water needed no river mouth and did not silt, which is precisely why the modern transshipment terminal has been built on the same spot. A harbour chosen in the 8th century and again in the 21st is a neat demonstration that port geography is a physical constant.
  - The cave temple, left unfinished, shows the rock-cut tradition of the Tamil country reaching the west coast, and its sculpture is among the earliest in Kerala.
  - The Ays were patrons of both Jainism and Hinduism, and their territory is where the Tamil and Malayalam linguistic regions began to separate.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Travancore Archaeological Series*.
