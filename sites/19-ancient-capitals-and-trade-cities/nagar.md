- **Location**: Nagar, also called Karkota Nagar, Tonk district, Rajasthan, in the Banas basin east of the Aravallis - a large mounded site in the tract between Rajasthan and Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: An early historic settlement of roughly the 3rd century BCE to the 4th century CE, identified with a principal seat of the Malava people. It is best known for the enormous quantity of Malava coinage recovered from it.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site for the tribal republics - the *ganas* and *sanghas* that were not monarchies, known mainly through their coins.
  - Many thousands of small copper coins bearing the legend *Malavanam jaya*, "victory of the Malavas", have been found here. Coinage issued in the name of a people rather than a king is the central piece of evidence for non-monarchical polities in early India, and Nagar is where most of it comes from.
  - The Malavas appear in the Greek accounts of Alexander's campaign as the *Malloi* of the Punjab, and later in Rajasthan and Malwa, which gives the region its name. Their migration south-east is traced through exactly this coin distribution.
  - Samudragupta's Allahabad pillar inscription lists the Malavas among the frontier peoples who submitted and paid tribute, so the republics survived into the 4th century before Gupta absorption. Their disappearance after that is the standard endpoint for the history of the ganas.
  - The Vikrama era, from 58 BCE, was called the *Malava-gana* era in early usage, tying India's most widely used calendar to this people.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
