- **Location**: Sialkot, ancient Sagala or Sakala, in Punjab, Pakistan, between the Chenab and the Ravi at the foot of the Jammu hills - on the route from the Khyber into the Punjab doabs.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Madra kingdom in Mahabharata tradition, then of the Indo-Greek king Menander, r. c. 155-130 BCE; later held by the Sakas, the Kushanas and the Hunas under Mihirakula, who is remembered for persecuting Buddhists there.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of the most successful Indo-Greek king, and the setting of the one text in which a Greek ruler debates Indian philosophy.
  - The *Milindapanha*, the Questions of Milinda, records a dialogue at Sagala between Menander and the Buddhist monk Nagasena on the nature of the self, using the famous simile of the chariot that is nothing over and above its parts. It is a Pali classic and the best evidence for Greek engagement with Indian thought.
  - Menander's coins, bilingual in Greek and Kharoshthi and found in great numbers across the north-west, some carrying the Buddhist wheel, support the tradition that he adopted or at least patronised Buddhism. Plutarch reports that his relics were divided between cities after his death, in the manner of a Buddha.
  - The Indo-Greeks matter beyond Menander for what they transmitted: the use of portrait coinage with legends, Hellenistic sculptural form feeding into Gandhara, and Greek astronomical terms entering Sanskrit.
  - Sialkot was also a textile centre; the *Mahabharata* and later sources refer to the fine cloth of the Madra country.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; *Milindapanha*.
