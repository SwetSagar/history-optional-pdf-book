- **Location**: Sambhar, Jaipur district, Rajasthan, on the shore of Sambhar Salt Lake - the largest inland salt lake in India, in the shallow basin east of the Aravallis.
- **Period/Level**: Ancient Shakambhari, capital of the Chahamanas (Chauhans) from about the 7th century until the seat moved to Ajmer under Ajayaraja in the 12th. The Chahamanas of Shakambhari end with Prithviraj III, defeated by Muhammad of Ghor at the second battle of Tarain in 1192.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Chauhan homeland, and a salt resource that explains why the site was worth holding.
  - The lake is the single largest source of inland salt in India, producing by solar evaporation in shallow pans. Control of salt meant control of a commodity every household needed and which could be taxed at the point of production - an early and easily collected revenue base, and the reason a dynasty rose at an otherwise unpromising spot.
  - The Chahamanas' move from Sambhar to Ajmer, and the later shift to Delhi under Prithviraj, traces the dynasty's transformation from a regional house on the desert margin into the principal power facing the Ghurid invasions.
  - Sambhar has yielded terracottas and early historic material showing occupation well before the Chahamanas, and the goddess Shakambhari, from whom the capital took its name, remains the lineage deity.
  - Salt from Sambhar was still a major item of Rajput and later Mughal revenue, and the lake's administration under the British is part of the wider colonial salt monopoly story.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I.
