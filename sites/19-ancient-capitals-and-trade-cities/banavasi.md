- **Location**: Banavasi, Uttara Kannada district, Karnataka, on a loop of the Varada river in the Malnad country below the Western Ghats - the oldest recorded settlement in Karnataka.
- **Period/Level**: A centre under the Satavahanas and the Chutus, who left a Prakrit inscription here, and from c. 345 CE the capital of the Kadambas, founded by Mayurasharma. The Madhukeshwara temple dates from about the 9th century with Chalukya and Hoysala additions; Pulakesin I of the Chalukyas absorbed the region in the 6th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of the first indigenous dynasty of the Kannada country - the point at which Karnataka acquires a political history of its own.
  - The Kadambas are the earliest ruling house native to the region, as against the Mauryas, Satavahanas and Chutus who ruled it from outside. Mayurasharma was, by the Talagunda inscription's account, a brahmana student slighted at Kanchi who took up arms - one of the more vivid dynastic origin stories in Indian epigraphy.
  - Ashoka's Rock Edict XIII names *Vanavasi* among the places to which missionaries were sent, and the Ceylonese chronicles record the monk Rakkhita being despatched here, so the site enters the record in the Mauryan period.
  - Kadamba rule is the setting for the Halmidi inscription, the earliest full-length Kannada text, referred to Kakusthavarman. Capital and inscription belong to the same polity, and are best answered together.
  - The Madhukeshwara temple preserves the Kadamba *shikhara*, a stepped pyramidal form distinct from both Nagara and Dravida, and the dynasty's name survives in that architectural label.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Thapar, *Early India*.
