- **Location**: Kannauj, ancient Kanyakubja, on the right bank of the Ganga in Kannauj district, Uttar Pradesh - commanding the doab where the river routes of the upper and middle Ganga plain meet.
- **Period/Level**: A Maukhari capital in the 6th century; Harshavardhana moved his seat here from Thanesar and ruled 606-647 CE. It was the prize of the tripartite struggle in the 8th-9th centuries, then the Gurjara-Pratihara capital, sacked by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1018, and finally lost by the Gahadavala Jayachandra after Chandawar in 1194.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The city that stood for paramountcy in northern India - whoever held it claimed to be the successor of Harsha.
  - The tripartite struggle between the Palas of Bengal, the Gurjara-Pratiharas of Malwa-Rajasthan and the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan was fought explicitly for Kannauj across the 8th and 9th centuries. Three dynasties from three corners of India contesting one city is the standard illustration of early medieval political fragmentation and of what legitimacy was worth.
  - Xuanzang, who spent time at Harsha's court, describes a long, prosperous city on the Ganga with Buddhist and Brahmanical establishments side by side, and records the assemblies Harsha held at Kannauj and Prayaga.
  - Bana's *Harshacharita* supplies the court narrative, which the Banskhera and Madhuban copper plates then confirm - one of the rare cases where a Sanskrit biography can be checked against epigraphy.
  - Its perfume industry, distilling attar from local roses and khus, survives from the medieval period and is still the town's trade.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I.
