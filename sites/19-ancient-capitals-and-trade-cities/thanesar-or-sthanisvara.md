- **Location**: Thanesar, ancient Sthanvishvara, Kurukshetra district, Haryana, on the bank of the Sarasvati in the Ghaggar system - within the Kurukshetra tract, between the Sutlej and the Yamuna.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Pushyabhuti dynasty from the 6th century CE: Prabhakaravardhana, then Rajyavardhana, then Harshavardhana, who succeeded in 606 CE and soon moved his seat to Kannauj. Raided by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1014.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat from which Harsha's empire began, and a tirtha whose sanctity long predates any dynasty.
  - Bana's *Harshacharita* opens here, describing Prabhakaravardhana's court, his death, Rajyavardhana's killing by Shashanka of Gauda, and Harsha's accession - the fullest narrative account of a royal succession surviving from early India, and the reason Thanesar carries weight beyond its size.
  - Harsha's transfer of the capital to Kannauj is itself the examinable point: it moved the centre of power from the Sarasvati region to the Ganga, following the shift of agricultural wealth eastward, and Kannauj remained the prize for four centuries afterwards.
  - The surrounding Kurukshetra is the *Mahabharata* battlefield and one of the great tirthas of northern India, with Brahma Sarovar and a long tradition of eclipse-bathing that Xuanzang already records.
  - Mahmud's raid of 1014, aimed at the wealth of the temples, belongs with Somnath in the sequence of Ghaznavid expeditions, and the Sheikh Chilli tomb later marks the town's Sufi phase.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
