- **Location**: Kodungallur, the Cranganore of European sources, Thrissur district, Kerala, near the mouth of the Periyar at the north end of the Vembanad backwaters - the same estuary as ancient Muziris, a few kilometres away.
- **Period/Level**: Mahodayapuram, capital of the Chera Perumals from about the 9th century to the early 12th, when the kingdom broke up into the Venad, Kolathunadu and other successor states. The earlier port of Muziris, active in the Sangam and Roman periods, lies in the same estuary; see the Muziris entry in the ports chapter.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Chera Perumal capital, and the single stretch of Indian coast where Judaism, Christianity and Islam each claim their first foothold.
  - Tradition places the arrival of Jewish settlers here after 70 CE, the landing of the apostle Thomas in 52 CE, and the building of the Cheraman Juma Masjid in 629 CE, held to be the first mosque in India and founded in the Prophet's lifetime. The traditions are of varying strength and should be presented as traditions - but the underlying fact, that all three communities entered India through Kerala's trade rather than by conquest, is solid and is the point worth making.
  - The Jewish copper plates and the Tharisapalli grants issued under the Cheras record privileges to these communities and to the merchant guilds, giving documentary support to the trading context.
  - Kulasekhara Alwar and Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, saints of the Vaishnava and Shaiva bhakti traditions, are associated with this court, so the capital matters for religious history as well as commerce.
  - The Kodungallur Bhagavathy temple, with its Bharani festival, preserves an older goddess cult of the region.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; *Travancore Archaeological Series*.
