- **Location**: Thapli, in the Garhwal Himalaya of Uttarakhand, on a terrace above the Alaknanda-Ganga system near Srinagar (Garhwal) - one of the few excavated prehistoric sites in the Indian central Himalaya.
- **Period/Level**: A Neolithic settlement with later occupation, broadly assigned to the 2nd millennium BCE, excavated by the ASI and Garhwal University. It belongs with Malari, Ranihat and the Kumaon rock shelters in the small corpus of Himalayan prehistory.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Evidence that the Himalayan valleys were settled by farming communities, not merely crossed by them.
  - The site has yielded ground and polished stone tools, including the shouldered and harvester types that link the central Himalaya to the north-eastern Neolithic rather than to the Gangetic or southern traditions, together with handmade cord-impressed and burnished pottery.
  - That tool typology is the analytical point. The Indian Neolithic is usually taught as three or four regional groups - the north-west, the Vindhyan-Gangetic, the southern and the north-eastern - and the Himalayan sites sit awkwardly across them, sharing traits with Kashmir's Burzahom and Gufkral and with the shouldered celts of Assam and Meghalaya.
  - Burzahom's pit dwellings and bone tools make the Kashmir group the best known; Thapli and Malari extend the picture eastward along the mountain belt and show the Garhwal valleys were occupied continuously from prehistory.
  - The region's rock shelters at Lakhudiyar and elsewhere belong to the same broad horizon and are the art to set beside this settlement evidence.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
