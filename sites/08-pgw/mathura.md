- **Location**: Mathura, on the right bank of the Yamuna in western Uttar Pradesh - the capital of the Surasena mahajanapada, at the crossing of the Uttarapatha and the southern route to Ujjain.
- **Period/Level**: PGW occurring with BRW, dated c. 800-400 BCE, followed by the NBPW phase and then the great Kushana city. Investigated by the ASI at the city mounds and at Sonkh nearby.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the largest PGW sites, and the clearest case of a PGW village growing without a break into a metropolis.
  - The sequence shows gradual growth from a village settlement rather than a founded city: PGW with BRW in the earliest levels, and steady expansion through the NBPW phase into a fortified early historic town.
  - Its position on the Yamuna at a junction of the great trunk routes made it the river port of the doab, which is why a modest PGW village became the southern capital of the Kushanas.
  - The later city produced the Mathura school of sculpture in spotted red sandstone, the earliest datable Buddha images in the round, the Kankali Tila Jain stupa, and the Mat devakula with its statues of the Kushana kings - all treated under the art sites.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5-6; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI, *IAR*.
