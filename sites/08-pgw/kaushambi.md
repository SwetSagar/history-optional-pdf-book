- **Location**: Kaushambi (Kosam), near Prayagraj in Uttar Pradesh, on the left bank of the Yamuna - the capital of the Vatsa mahajanapada, and the eastern limit of the PGW distribution.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by G. R. Sharma (Allahabad University, 1949-66). Iron is present in the pre-PGW BRW level here; the PGW phase is followed by NBPW from c. 600 BCE, and the city runs on into the Kushana and Gupta periods.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest PGW settlement of the eastern doab, and the place where the PGW village landscape visibly turns into a city.
  - In Erdosy's settlement survey of Allahabad district, Kaushambi stands at 10 ha against fifteen other sites of 0.42-2.80 ha averaging 1.72 ha - an unmistakable two-tier hierarchy. Its siting on poor soil and rugged ground suggests it was placed to command the mineral resources of the Vindhyas rather than the best farmland.
  - Early houses of timber give way to brick. The city was planned and enclosed by a massive mud rampart with a burnt-brick revetment, bastions and gateways, which G. R. Sharma dated extremely early - a dating widely disputed.
  - Iron agricultural implements and weapons in quantity; later, the Ghositarama monastery and an Ashokan pillar carrying the schism edict. Puranic tradition brings the Kurus here from flooded Hastinapura under Nichakshu, which is why the two sites are always read together.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5-6; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Erdosy (1988).
