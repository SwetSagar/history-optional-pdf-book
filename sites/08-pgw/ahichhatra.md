- **Location**: Ahichchhatra (modern Ramnagar), Bareilly district, Uttar Pradesh, in the Ramganga basin of the upper Ganga plain - the capital of North Panchala.
- **Period/Level**: The site at which Painted Grey Ware was first identified, in the ASI excavations of the 1940s (K. N. Dikshit and A. Ghosh), although its significance was only grasped after Hastinapura. Here the OCP level is followed by a break in occupation and then PGW, c. 1100-500/400 BCE; NBPW, Kushana and Gupta levels follow.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type site of the PGW culture and one of its largest settlements, with a sequence running unbroken into the early historic city.
  - The PGW sherds from Ahichchhatra and Hastinapura are the standard published illustrations of the ware: a very fine, thin, even-coloured grey fabric, wheel-turned to eggshell thickness and painted in black with simple geometric designs.
  - Houses in the PGW levels are of wattle and daub and mud; burnt brick appears only in the later periods, when the site is enclosed by a great rampart and becomes one of the largest fortified mounds in the doab.
  - As the Ahichchhatra of the Mahabharata it is the capital of North Panchala - the half of the kingdom ceded to Drona, with Kampilya holding the south. This pairing of the PGW map with the epic geography is the core of the archaeology-and-Mahabharata argument.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; Thapar, *Early India*; K. N. Dikshit, *Ancient India* 1 (1946).
