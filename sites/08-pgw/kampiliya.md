- **Location**: Kampilya (modern Kampil), Farrukhabad district, Uttar Pradesh, on an old course of the Ganga in the central doab - the capital of South Panchala.
- **Period/Level**: Occupation begins in the PGW phase, c. 1100-600 BCE, and runs on through NBPW into the early historic period. Upinder Singh lists Kampil among the excavated sites giving important evidence of PGW material culture; the early excavations exposed wattle-and-daub structures.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A Mahajanapada capital whose occupation demonstrably begins in the PGW phase - the archaeological test of the epic and Vedic geography.
  - Wattle-and-daub and mud structures in the PGW levels, the normal architecture of the culture; burnt brick arrives only with the NBPW phase and the second urbanisation.
  - Panchala was divided along the Ganga into North Panchala, with its capital at Ahichchhatra, and South Panchala, with its capital here. The Mahabharata sets Draupadi's svayamvara at Kampilya, at Drupada's court.
  - Kampil is also a Jain tirtha, traditionally the birthplace of the thirteenth Tirthankara Vimalanatha, and the later Panchala tribal coins bearing the names of local rulers come from this area.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (Map 5.7) and ch. 6; ASI, *IAR*.
