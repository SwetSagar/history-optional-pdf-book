- **Location**: The Bahawalpur region of Punjab (Pakistan), in the Cholistan tract along the dry Hakra-Ghaggar - the western limit of the Painted Grey Ware distribution.
- **Period/Level**: PGW, c. 1100-500/400 BCE. The north-western sites are held to be earlier than those of the Ganga valley, so this is where the culture begins rather than where it peters out.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Fixes the western extent of PGW and supplies one of the three regional settlement studies on which PGW social history rests.
  - M. R. Mughal's survey identified 14 PGW sites here, ranging from 0.5 to 13.7 ha; apart from Satwali at 13.7 ha, all are under 5 ha. This is the same two-tier hierarchy - one large site among many small ones - found in northern Haryana (one site of 9.6 ha, none other above 4.3 ha) and in Allahabad district, where Kaushambi at 10 ha stands over fifteen sites averaging 1.72 ha.
  - PGW occurs here on the dry Hakra, in the very tract that had earlier carried the densest Kot Dijian and mature Harappan concentration - the same river system holding two settlement systems a millennium apart.
  - PGW sites in the Ghaggar-Hakra area, Bhagwanpura included, have yielded no iron artefacts at all, unlike those of the doab. This is the standing regional qualification to any equation of PGW with the iron age.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (Map 5.7); Mughal, *Ancient Cholistan* (1997).
