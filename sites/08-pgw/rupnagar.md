- **Location**: Rupnagar (Ropar), on the left bank of the Sutlej in Punjab, where the river leaves the Siwaliks - the north-western end of the PGW distribution in the Sutlej basin.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Y. D. Sharma (ASI, 1953-56). Period II is PGW, following the Harappan level after a break in occupation; Period III is NBPW, c. 600-200 BCE, when urbanisation appears.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Fixes the PGW-after-a-break pattern that stands against the Bhagwanpura overlap - the two together frame the Harappan-Vedic argument.
  - The break between the Harappan and PGW levels here is shared with Sanghol in Punjab, Daulatpur in Haryana, and Alamgirpur and Hulas in western Uttar Pradesh. Against it stand Bhagwanpura, Dadheri, Katpalon and Nagar, where the two cultures overlap - so the relationship differs from valley to valley and cannot be settled by one site.
  - Signs of urbanisation come with the NBPW phase, c. 600-200 BCE: burnt-brick structures, punch-marked coins, uninscribed cast copper coins and a seal with a Mauryan Brahmi legend.
  - Its position in the Sutlej basin puts it in the Punjab-Haryana group of PGW sites, which is taken to be earlier than the Ganga valley group; the site's full six-period sequence from Harappan to medieval is treated in the Harappan chapter.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; ASI, *IAR* 1953-56.
