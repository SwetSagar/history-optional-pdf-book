- **Location**: Atranjikhera, Etah district, Uttar Pradesh, on the left bank of the Kali Nadi, a tributary of the Ganga in the central doab.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by R. C. Gaur (Aligarh Muslim University, 1962-68). Period I OCP; Period II Black and Red Ware; Period III PGW, c. 1200-600 BCE; Period IV NBPW. PGW here is preceded by a BRW phase with a break in between - one of the four stratigraphic patterns in which PGW occurs.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site at which a distinct BRW phase in the doab was first recognised, and the best evidence anywhere for PGW-age iron technology.
  - BRW lay sandwiched between the OCP and PGW levels. The same sequence was later found at Noh and Jodhpura in Rajasthan, and it is the basis for treating BRW as an independent doab phase rather than a stray ware.
  - Chemical analysis of the iron from the PGW levels showed wrought iron that had then been carburised, probably by long heating on a charcoal bed. The composition of the objects and of the slag matches the iron-rich rocks of the Agra-Gwalior hills, which identifies the ore source - rare precision for this period.
  - Rice, barley, gram and khesari came from the OCP levels and rice and moong from the BRW levels: two crops a year. Deep circular pits outside the habitation are read as kachcha wells of the kind still used locally for irrigation.
  - Jakhera, in the same district, carries the culture to a proto-urban stage - a 60 m water channel with a bund, potsherd-paved roads and lanes, storage bins implying surplus, gold and copper ornaments, 106 semi-precious stone beads, and iron sickle, hoe and ploughshare.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; R. C. Gaur, *Excavations at Atranjikhera* (1983).
