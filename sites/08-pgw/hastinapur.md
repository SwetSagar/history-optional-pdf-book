- **Location**: Hastinapura, Meerut district, Uttar Pradesh, on an old right bank of the Ganga in the upper doab.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by B. B. Lal in 1950-52 and published in *Ancient India* 10-11 (1954-55). Period I OCP; a break in occupation; Period II PGW, c. 1100-800 BCE; Period III NBPW, c. 600-200 BCE; Period IV c. 200 BCE-300 CE; Period V, 11th-15th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The excavation that established what PGW meant, and the classic attempt to tie Indian archaeology to the Mahabharata.
  - PGW occurs with black-slipped ware and plain red and red-slipped wares. Houses were of mud, mud brick and reed; unbaked bricks and a single baked brick were found. Also chert and jasper weights, bone needles, glass bangles, and iron slag in the upper levels.
  - Charred grains of rice and bones of pig, horse and cattle; the horse bones from Hastinapura are among the few securely reported from PGW levels.
  - Lal found that Period II ended in a heavy Ganga flood, and connected this with the Puranic tradition that Nichakshu abandoned Hastinapura and moved the Kuru capital to Kaushambi - the most-cited correlation of archaeology with the epic, and a much-contested one.
  - The site also gives the OCP-break-PGW pattern (shared with Ahichchhatra and Jhinjhana), one of the four stratigraphic contexts in which PGW is found.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; B. B. Lal, *Ancient India* 10-11 (1954-55).
