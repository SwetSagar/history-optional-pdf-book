- **Location**: Chak 86, Sri Ganganagar district, northern Rajasthan, on the dry Ghaggar close to the Pakistan border and the Bahawalpur PGW cluster.
- **Period/Level**: A Painted Grey Ware findspot of c. 1100-600 BCE in the Ghaggar-Hakra belt, recorded in exploration rather than by excavation. The north-western PGW sites are taken to be earlier than those of the Ganga plains.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: With Bahawalpur across the border, one of the two westernmost points on the standard PGW distribution map.
  - It belongs to the Ghaggar-Hakra group of PGW sites which, like Bhagwanpura, have produced no iron - so at the western end the culture is defined by its pottery alone.
  - The ware itself is a very fine, thin, wheel-turned grey pottery painted in black with rows of lines, dots, dashes, circles, spirals, swastikas and sigmas. It forms only 3-10 per cent of the assemblage at the levels where it occurs, appearing with plain grey ware, BRW and black-slipped ware - a deluxe table ware for the well-to-do, not a household pottery.
  - The Ghaggar sites matter for the Vedic question: this is the Sarasvati tract where late Harappan settlement is densest, and PGW follows it in the same valley - overlapping it outright at Bhagwanpura.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 (Map 5.7); ASI, *IAR*.
