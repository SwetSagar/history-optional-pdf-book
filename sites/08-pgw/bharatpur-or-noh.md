- **Location**: Noh, near Bharatpur in eastern Rajasthan, on the Ruparel drainage at the western edge of the Ganga-Yamuna doab.
- **Period/Level**: Period I OCP; Period II BRW, carrying the first pieces of iron; Period III PGW, c. 1000-600 BCE; Period IV NBPW, with Sunga-Kushana levels above. Excavated by the Rajasthan Department of Archaeology (R. C. Agrawala) in the 1960s.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Carries the full doab sequence - OCP, BRW, PGW, NBPW - into Rajasthan, and is one of the sites where iron appears before PGW.
  - Shapeless pieces of iron occur in the BRW level, below PGW; the PGW level yielded a spearhead, an arrowhead with a socketed tang and an axe with a broad cutting edge. With Jakhera and Kaushambi, Noh is an exception to the doab norm in which the earliest iron accompanies PGW.
  - Burnt bricks and rice were recovered here, and terracotta gamesmen, taken as evidence of gaming as a pastime.
  - Noh shares its cultural sequence with the neighbouring upper Ganga valley. Together with Jodhpura - where the PGW levels produced a crucible-shaped furnace for the direct reduction of ore, with a forging platform beside it - it makes eastern Rajasthan a second centre of early iron working.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5; ASI, *IAR* 1963-67.
