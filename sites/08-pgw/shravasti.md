The capital of the Kosala kingdom in the sixth century BC, in Uttar Pradesh, identified by Sir Alexander Cunningham in 1863.

- It was an important trade site, surrounded by a rampart, and is venerated by both Buddhists and Jainas.
- Most dwellings were wattle-and-daub and mud huts; later phases show sturdy brick houses and fortified structures.
- An elephant structure of iron and eye beads have been recovered.
- King Prasenajit was a disciple of the Buddha, who spent twenty-five retreats here — the longest period he spent anywhere — and the Jetavana monastery was located here.
- Xuanzang found the city in ruins.
- Remains of a stupa and monastic establishment survive.
- It is known in the older sources as Savatthi.
