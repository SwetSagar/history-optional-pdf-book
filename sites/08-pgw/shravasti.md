- **Location**: Shravasti (the twin mounds of Saheth-Maheth), on the south bank of the Rapti - the ancient Achiravati - in Shravasti and Balrampur districts, Uttar Pradesh; the capital of the Kosala mahajanapada.
- **Period/Level**: Identified by Sir Alexander Cunningham in 1863. Occupation begins in the PGW phase, c. 1000-600 BCE - the site marks the eastern edge of the PGW map - and continues through NBPW into the Kushana and Gupta periods. Excavated by K. K. Sinha (Banaras Hindu University, 1959) and by the ASI.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A Mahajanapada capital whose PGW village underlies one of the greatest Buddhist centres in India.
  - The PGW levels give wattle-and-daub and mud huts, the standard architecture of the culture; the later phases bring sturdy brick houses and the great horseshoe-shaped mud rampart around Maheth, revetted and pierced by gateways.
  - Iron objects, among them an elephant figure, and eye beads were recovered - the material of a settlement moving from village to town.
  - Saheth is the Jetavana, the grove Anathapindika bought from Prince Jeta and gave to the Buddha, who by tradition spent twenty-five rainy seasons here, more than at any other place; the Twin Miracle is set at Shravasti. It is also a Jain tirtha, the birthplace of the third Tirthankara Sambhavanatha.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5-6; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Cunningham, *ASI Reports*.
