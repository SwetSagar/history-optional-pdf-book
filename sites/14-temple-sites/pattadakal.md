- **Location**: Pattadakal, Bagalkot district, Karnataka, on the left bank of the Malaprabha where the river turns north - a bend held to be auspicious - with Aihole and Badami close by.
- **Period/Level**: The Early Chalukya coronation site, *Pattada-kisuvolal*, the place of the crown. Ten major temples of the 7th-8th centuries CE: the Sangameshvara under Vijayaditya (r. 696-733), the Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna under Vikramaditya II (r. 733-746). UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The one site where the Nagara and Dravida orders stand side by side, built by the same dynasty at the same time - the textbook case for the two styles as contemporaries rather than a sequence.
  - Four temples are Nagara with curvilinear shikharas - Papanatha, Galaganatha, Kashi Vishvanatha, Jambulinga - and four are Dravida with stepped vimanas: Sangameshvara, Virupaksha, Mallikarjuna, Chandrashekhara. The Papanatha mixes both. This deliberate juxtaposition is what the examiner is after.
  - The Virupaksha, c. 740 CE, was built by Queen Lokamahadevi to commemorate Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas at Kanchipuram, and was modelled on the Kailasanatha there - a temple raised as a war memorial, copying the vanquished enemy's own building. Its inscription names the architect, Gunda, with the title *tribhuvanacharya*.
  - The Virupaksha in turn became the model for the Rashtrakuta Kailasa at Ellora, so a single line of descent runs Kanchi to Pattadakal to Ellora.
  - Two queens, Lokamahadevi and Trailokyamahadevi, were the patrons of the two largest temples - unusually well-documented female patronage.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
