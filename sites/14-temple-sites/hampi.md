- **Location**: Hampi, Vijayanagara district, Karnataka, on the south bank of the Tungabhadra among granite boulder hills - a defensible site identified with Kishkindha of the *Ramayana*.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Vijayanagara empire from its founding in 1336 by Harihara and Bukka to its sack in 1565 after the battle of Talikota. The great building phase is the Tuluva period under Krishnadevaraya, r. 1509-1529. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest medieval Hindu capital to survive as a ruin, and the type-site of the Vijayanagara architectural order.
  - The style's markers are the towering brick-and-plaster *gopuram* over the outer gate, the pillared *kalyana mandapa*, and monolithic piers carved as rearing *yali* beasts. The Vitthala temple has the stone chariot and the colonnade of musical pillars; the Virupaksha, older than the city and still in worship, was enlarged by Krishnadevaraya.
  - The Hazara Rama, the royal chapel, carries Ramayana friezes on its walls and outer processional scenes of elephants, horses and dancers - the Mahanavami festival depicted on the building beside the platform where it was held.
  - The secular quarter is what makes Hampi exceptional: the Mahanavami Dibba, the elephant stables, the Lotus Mahal, the queen's bath and an elaborate aqueduct system. Islamic arches and domes in the courtly buildings show the Deccan sultanates' idiom adopted for palace architecture while temples stayed indigenous.
  - Foreign accounts - Abdur Razzaq, Nicolo de' Conti, Domingo Paes and Fernao Nuniz - describe the city at its height and are the standard external evidence for its scale and markets.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; Thapar, *Early India*.
