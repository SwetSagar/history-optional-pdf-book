- **Location**: Dilwara, on Mount Abu, Sirohi district, Rajasthan, the highest point of the Aravallis, above the West Banas basin.
- **Period/Level**: Five Jain temples in white Makrana marble built between the 11th and 13th centuries under the Chaulukya (Solanki) kings of Gujarat. The Vimala Vasahi, dedicated to Adinatha, was raised in 1031 CE by Vimala Shah, minister of Bhima I; the Luna Vasahi, dedicated to Neminatha, in 1230 CE by the brothers Vastupala and Tejpala, ministers of the Vaghela kings.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest marble carving in India, and the clearest case of temple building financed by merchants and ministers rather than by kings.
  - The work is in the Maru-Gurjara idiom: closed halls with a central dome carried on eight pillars, ceilings cut into concentric rings of lotus petals, and pendant marble bosses so undercut that the stone is translucent. The Luna Vasahi's rangamandapa ceiling is the acknowledged masterpiece.
  - Both principal temples were built by *mantris* - state officials of the Jain mercantile community. This is the standard illustration of how commercial wealth, not just royal revenue, underwrote early medieval religious architecture.
  - The plan is the standard Jain *chaturvimshati-jinalaya*: a central shrine ringed by a corridor of fifty-two subsidiary cells, each holding a tirthankara image.
  - The exteriors are deliberately plain, so that the entire effect is interior - the opposite of the sculpted outer walls at Khajuraho or Modhera, and a reminder that Jain patrons chose restraint outside and extravagance within.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; ASI.
