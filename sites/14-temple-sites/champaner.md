- **Location**: Champaner, Panchmahal district, Gujarat, at the foot of the Pavagadh hill in the Vishwamitri basin, on the route from Gujarat into Malwa.
- **Period/Level**: A Chauhan Rajput stronghold under the Khichi rulers of Pavagadh until Mahmud Begada took it in 1484 after a twenty-month siege, renamed it Muhammadabad and made it his capital for some twenty-three years. Abandoned after the Mughal conquest of Gujarat in 1573. UNESCO World Heritage Site as Champaner-Pavagadh.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only complete pre-Mughal Islamic city in India that was never built over, and the best surviving statement of the Gujarat Sultanate style.
  - The Jami Masjid of 1513 is the set piece: two minarets flanking the entrance, a prayer hall of some 170 pillars, carved jalis, and *chhajjas*, brackets and a fluted central dome taken directly from Hindu and Jain temple craft. Gujarat's sultans employed the same guilds who had built Modhera and Dilwara, and the style is the clearest instance anywhere of an Islamic form executed in an indigenous idiom.
  - The Kalika Mata temple at the summit of Pavagadh is an older Shakti shrine, and remains in worship. Hindu pilgrimage hill and Islamic capital occupy one site continuously.
  - The city retains its full plan - walls, gates, citadel, mosques, tombs, stepwells, granaries and an elaborate water-harvesting system on the hill slopes - so it is used as evidence for medieval urban form rather than for single monuments.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; ASI.
