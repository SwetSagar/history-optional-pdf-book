- **Location**: Tigawa, Katni district, Madhya Pradesh, near Bahuriband on the old route between Jabalpur and Bundelkhand, in the upper Narmada-Son watershed.
- **Period/Level**: Gupta period, c. 400-425 CE - among the oldest surviving structural Hindu temples in India. Of some thirty-six shrines recorded at the site, the Kankali Devi temple survives largely intact.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The first stage of the Indian temple in stone: the simplest possible form, before the shikhara.
  - It is a flat-roofed square sanctum with a small four-pillared porch, no ambulatory and no tower - Percy Brown's Class I Gupta temple, and the starting point of every architectural sequence in the syllabus. Compare it with Sanchi Temple 17, its near-twin.
  - The pillars carry the heavy vase-and-foliage (purna-ghata) capital with back-to-back lions above, the diagnostic Gupta order; the door jambs are plain, before the elaborately carved doorways of Deogarh.
  - Originally Vaishnava, with Narasimha and Chakrapurusha panels. The Chamunda or Kankali image that gave the temple its present name is a later insertion - a small but useful instance of a shrine changing affiliation while the building stays the same.
  - Read the sequence as a set: Tigawa and Sanchi 17 (flat roof), Bhitargaon (brick shikhara), Nachna Kuthara (storeyed sanctum with ambulatory), Deogarh (shikhara with panchayatana plan).
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Percy Brown, *Indian Architecture*.
