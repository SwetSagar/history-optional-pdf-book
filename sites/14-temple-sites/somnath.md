- **Location**: Prabhas Patan near Veraval, Gir Somnath district, Gujarat, on the Arabian Sea at the Triveni confluence of the Hiran, Kapila and Saraswati - a port as well as a tirtha.
- **Period/Level**: The first of the twelve *jyotirlingas*, with temple building attributed in tradition to the Yadavas of Vallabhi. The historically attested structure was sacked by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1025-26 during Bhima I's reign, rebuilt by Bhima and again by Kumarapala c. 1169, destroyed under Alauddin Khalji in 1299 and finally by Aurangzeb in 1706. The present Kailash Mahameru Prasada temple was completed in 1951.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Less an architectural site than a historiographical one - the single best case study in how a medieval raid was turned into a modern narrative.
  - Romila Thapar's *Somanatha: The Many Voices of a History* shows the sources disagreeing sharply. Turko-Persian chroniclers magnify the iconoclasm for a Ghaznavid audience; Sanskrit inscriptions from the site carry on recording grants and repairs with little sense of rupture; Jain texts and local traditions differ again.
  - A Sanskrit-Arabic bilingual inscription of 1264 CE records a Hormuzi shipowner, Nuruddin Firuz, being granted land at Somnath to build a mosque, with local Hindu dignitaries as parties. Trade and cohabitation continue at the very site of the raid.
  - The "trauma of Somnath" as a continuous Hindu memory took shape in the 1843 House of Commons debate over Ellenborough's gates proclamation, and was carried into Indian politics thereafter. Dating the memory, not just the raid, is what the question rewards.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Somanatha: The Many Voices of a History*; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I.
