- **Location**: Kamakhya, on the Nilachal hill in Guwahati, Assam, overlooking the Brahmaputra where the river narrows - ancient Kamarupa or Pragjyotisha.
- **Period/Level**: An early shrine of the Kamarupa kingdom, referred to from about the 8th-9th centuries and elaborated under the Palas of Kamarupa. The standing temple was rebuilt in 1565 by the Koch king Nara Narayana and his general Chilarai, after destruction attributed to Kalapahar; the plinth and some sculpture belong to the older structure.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal Shakta pitha of eastern India and the chief centre of Tantric worship in the subcontinent.
  - It is counted among the fifty-one *shakti pithas*, and the one where Sati's *yoni* is said to have fallen. There is no anthropomorphic image: worship is offered to a natural cleft in the rock kept moist by a spring - aniconic worship at a major temple, which is itself unusual.
  - The *Kalika Purana*, composed in Assam around the 10th-11th century, and the *Yogini Tantra* are the textual authorities. Both are principal sources for Kaula and Vamachara tantric practice, including the rites of animal sacrifice still performed here.
  - The Ambubachi Mela marks the goddess's annual menstruation, when the temple closes for three days. The presence of a mainstream pilgrimage built on that symbolism is standard evidence in questions on goddess worship and on the absorption of pre-Brahmanical fertility cult.
  - The structure defines the *Nilachal* type: a Nagara-derived shikhara capped by a hemispherical dome, on a cruciform plan - a regional hybrid found only in Assam.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Kalika Purana*.
