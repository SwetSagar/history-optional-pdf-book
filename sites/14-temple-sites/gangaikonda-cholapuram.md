- **Location**: Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Ariyalur district, Tamil Nadu, north of the Kollidam (Coleroon) in the Kaveri delta, about 70 km from Thanjavur.
- **Period/Level**: Founded by Rajendra I c. 1025 CE after his northern campaign, and the Chola capital for some 250 years until the dynasty's fall. The Brihadisvara temple was completed c. 1035; its vimana rises about 55 m. One of the three Great Living Chola Temples inscribed by UNESCO, with Thanjavur and Darasuram.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A capital founded as a war memorial - the Chola empire at its widest, commemorated in stone and water.
  - The name means "the city of the Chola who took the Ganga". Rajendra's army marched to the Ganga, and water carried back from it was poured into the *Chola-gangam*, a huge tank beside the temple described as a liquid pillar of victory - a *jalamaya-jayastambha*. Conquest converted into ritual topography.
  - The vimana is lower than Thanjavur's 66 m but concave in profile rather than straight, giving a softer, more graceful silhouette; it is usually described as the feminine counterpart to Rajaraja's masculine tower.
  - Sculptural high points include the *Chandesa-anugraha-murti*, showing Shiva garlanding Chandesa - read as an allegory of Rajendra receiving his father's blessing - and the monolithic Nandi, the second largest in India.
  - The site is central to the debate on Chola polity: Nilakanta Sastri's centralised bureaucratic empire against Burton Stein's segmentary state, in which such capitals are ritual rather than administrative centres.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, *The Colas*.
