- **Location**: Gyaraspur, Vidisha district, Madhya Pradesh, in the Betwa valley on the old route linking Vidisha, Sanchi and Udayagiri - the Malwa corridor between the Gangetic plain and the Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: Pratihara period, 9th-10th centuries CE, with Paramara-era additions. The Maladevi temple, the Bajramath and the Hindola Torana all belong to this phase, when the Gurjara-Pratiharas ruled Malwa from Kannauj.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A compact set of Pratihara-period monuments that fills the gap between Gupta Malwa and Chandela Bundelkhand.
  - The Maladevi temple is built against a hillside, half rock-cut and half structural, with a Nagara shikhara, a sanctum with ambulatory and richly carved outer walls - an unusual hybrid that shows the rock-cut tradition still alive in structural building.
  - The Bajramath has three sanctums in a row. It was built as a Brahmanical temple - the doorway iconography is Vaishnava and Shaiva - and later taken over by the Digambara Jains, whose tirthankara images now occupy the shrines. It is the clearest local example of a building outlasting the sect that raised it.
  - The Hindola Torana, a free-standing gateway of two pillars and an architrave carved with Vishnu's avataras, is the best-preserved early medieval torana in central India.
  - Gyaraspur's value in an answer is as Pratihara evidence: the dynasty is far better attested in inscriptions and Arab accounts than in surviving buildings, and this is one of the few places where its architecture can actually be seen.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI.
