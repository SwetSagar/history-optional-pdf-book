- **Location**: Pushkar, Ajmer district, Rajasthan, around a lake in a hollow of the Aravallis, on the divide between the Luni drainage and the Banas - the desert edge of eastern Rajasthan.
- **Period/Level**: An ancient tirtha named in the *Mahabharata*, the *Padma Purana* and the *Ramayana*. The present Jagatpita Brahma temple is a 14th-century structure, repaired in the 18th; the lake's stone ghats were largely built by Rajput rulers, with Maratha additions of the 18th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal Brahma temple in India - the one place where a god of the classical triad has almost no independent cult.
  - Brahma is named constantly in cosmology but is worshipped almost nowhere. The *Padma Purana* explains Pushkar as the site of his yajna and accounts for the absence of other Brahma temples by Savitri's curse. The rarity is the point: it shows that a deity's textual prominence and its actual cult can diverge completely, and that Puranic myth is often composed to explain an existing situation rather than the reverse.
  - The temple carries the *hamsa*, Brahma's goose, on its red shikhara, and the image is four-faced (chaturmukha), with Savitri and Gayatri shrines on the surrounding hills.
  - Pushkar lake is one of the five sacred *sarovars* and is treated as a self-manifested tirtha, its sanctity resting on water in an arid landscape - the same logic that operates at Amarkantak and at river confluences.
  - The Kartik Purnima fair, joining pilgrimage to one of the largest livestock markets in India, is standard evidence for how religious assembly and commerce reinforced each other.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Padma Purana*, Srishti-khanda.
