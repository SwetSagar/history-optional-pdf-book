- **Location**: Lonar, Buldhana district, Maharashtra, in the Deccan trap country of Vidarbha - a meteorite crater about 1.8 km across holding a saline, alkaline lake, with temples ringing the rim and the crater floor.
- **Period/Level**: The crater was formed by a hypervelocity impact in basalt, dated to roughly 50,000 years ago. The temples are Chalukya and Yadava work of the 12th-13th centuries CE, in the Hemadpanti manner - dressed basalt laid without mortar.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only large impact crater in basalt anywhere in the world, and a Puranic site whose myth encodes its own geology.
  - The Daityasudana temple, dedicated to Vishnu as the slayer of the demon Lonasura, is built on an irregular star-shaped plan in black basalt with dense figural carving. The *Skanda* and *Padma Puranas* tell of the demon crushed into the earth here, a story that reads as an explanation of the crater itself.
  - Kamalja Devi on the lake shore and about fifteen other shrines, several now half-submerged, complete the group. Their Hemadpanti technique - mortarless interlocking basalt - is the diagnostic Yadava building method of the Deccan.
  - The *Ain-i-Akbari* records revenue from salt extracted at Lonar, so the lake's alkaline chemistry was already an economic resource under the Mughals.
  - Its interest in an answer is the combination: a geological feature, a Puranic narrative and a dated body of Deccan temple architecture at one site.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Abu'l Fazl, *Ain-i-Akbari*; Geological Survey of India.
