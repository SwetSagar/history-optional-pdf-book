- **Location**: Srirangam, Tiruchirappalli district, Tamil Nadu, on an island formed where the Kaveri divides from the Kollidam - a river island, which is the source of the site's sanctity.
- **Period/Level**: Attested from the Sangam period and built up continuously by the Cholas, Pandyas, Hoysalas, Vijayanagara rulers and the Madurai Nayakas between the 10th and 17th centuries. The outer Rajagopuram was left unfinished for centuries and completed only in 1987, at 72 m.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest functioning Hindu temple complex in the world, and the doctrinal centre of Srivaishnavism.
  - The plan is seven concentric *prakaras* - rectangular enclosures with twenty-one gopurams, the outer ones containing streets, houses and markets. The temple is literally a walled town, and is the standard example of the south Indian temple as an urban nucleus, landholder and employer rather than merely a shrine.
  - It is the first and foremost of the 108 *Divya Desams*, the seat of Ranganatha reclining on Adishesha, and the sites of the Alwars' hymns collected as the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*.
  - Ramanuja headed the temple in the 11th-12th centuries and reorganised its administration; his Vishishtadvaita was formulated here, and his shrine in the complex is said to hold his preserved body. For questions on bhakti and on the Vaishnava acharyas this is the anchor site.
  - Malik Kafur's raid of 1311 forced the image into hiding for some sixty years, an episode recorded in temple tradition and used to date the Vijayanagara-period rebuilding that followed.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; ASI, *South Indian Inscriptions*.
