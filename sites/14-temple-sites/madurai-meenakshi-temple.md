- **Location**: Madurai, Tamil Nadu, on the south bank of the Vaigai - the ancient Pandya capital, laid out in concentric streets around the temple at its centre.
- **Period/Level**: A shrine of the Pandya period, sacked by Malik Kafur in 1310. The standing complex is overwhelmingly Nayaka work of the 16th-17th centuries, begun under Vishvanatha Nayaka and completed under Tirumalai Nayaka, r. 1623-1659. Fourteen gopurams, the tallest about 52 m.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The fullest realisation of the Nayaka temple-city, and the rare major temple where the goddess outranks her consort.
  - Meenakshi, the fish-eyed goddess identified with Parvati, is the presiding deity, with Sundareshvara (Shiva) secondary - her shrine is approached first and the annual *Chithirai* festival celebrates her marriage. A goddess as principal deity of a temple of this scale is unusual and is the point to make in questions on Shakta worship in the south.
  - The Nayaka style is visible at its height: enormous gopurams encrusted with painted stucco figures, the *Ayirakkal* thousand-pillar hall with its carved yali piers, the Potramarai golden lotus tank, and Tirumalai Nayaka's Pudu Mandapam outside the walls.
  - The city plan follows the temple: concentric rectangular streets named for festival months, so that processional routes structure the whole settlement - the clearest surviving example of the south Indian temple town.
  - Madurai is the seat of the Sangam academy in Tamil tradition, which gives the site a literary claim reaching back to the early centuries CE, long before the standing buildings.
  - *Sources*: J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
