- **Location**: Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu, on the Vegavathi, a tributary of the Palar, about 70 km inland from the coast - the natural centre of Tondaimandalam.
- **Period/Level**: A city of the Sangam age, later the Pallava capital from the 6th to the 9th centuries, then held by Cholas, Vijayanagara and the Nayakas. The Kailasanatha was built by Rajasimha (Narasimhavarman II), c. 700-728 CE; the Vaikuntha Perumal by Nandivarman II Pallavamalla, c. 770.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the seven *moksha-puris*, and the place where the southern structural stone temple begins.
  - The Kailasanatha is the earliest structural stone temple in Kanchi, built in sandstone rather than granite, with a pyramidal vimana, a circuit of small cells around the courtyard, and repeated *Somaskanda* panels - Shiva, Parvati and the infant Skanda - the Pallava dynastic image. Vikramaditya II copied it for the Virupaksha at Pattadakal.
  - The Vaikuntha Perumal has three sanctums stacked vertically, one above the other, and a cloister whose sculpted panels narrate the disputed Pallava succession - historical narrative carved on a temple wall, which is rare anywhere in India.
  - Kanchi was a *ghatikasthana*, a recognised seat of learning, hosting Jain and Buddhist institutions between the 1st and 5th centuries before becoming a Shaiva and Vaishnava centre. Bharavi, author of the *Kiratarjuniya*, the Buddhist Dharmapala who became head of Nalanda, and Ramanuja are all associated with it.
  - Divided into Shiva Kanchi and Vishnu Kanchi, with the Ekambaranatha and Varadaraja temples enlarged under Chola and Vijayanagara rule, it shows continuous accretion over a thousand years. It was also a silk and trade centre from the Satavahana period.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
