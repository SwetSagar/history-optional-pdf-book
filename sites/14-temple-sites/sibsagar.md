- **Location**: Sivasagar, upper Assam, on the south bank of the Brahmaputra near the Dikhow river - the Ahom capital region, known as Rangpur in its own day.
- **Period/Level**: Ahom dynasty at its height, 18th century. The Shivadol, Vishnudol and Devidol were built in 1734 by Queen Ambika, wife of Swargadeo Siba Singha; the Rang Ghar dates to 1746 under Pramatta Singha; the Talatal and Kareng Ghar palaces span the same century. Brick with lime plaster.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The architecture of the one Indian state that held off the Mughals for a century, and a distinct Ahom building idiom.
  - The Shivadol rises about 33 m and is among the tallest Shiva temples in India, its *dol* form - a tall curvilinear tower over a square sanctum, topped by a gold-plated *kalasha* - adapting the Nagara shikhara to brick and to Assamese proportions.
  - The three temples stand on the bank of the Sivasagar tank, a large excavated reservoir raised above the surrounding land. Ahom statecraft ran on such public works, built through the *paik* system of compulsory labour service - the institutional fact behind the monuments.
  - The Rang Ghar, a two-storeyed oval pavilion for viewing buffalo fights and games, is described as the oldest surviving amphitheatre in Asia; its roof is shaped like an inverted Ahom longboat.
  - The Ahoms, a Tai people who entered Assam in 1228, adopted Hinduism progressively; a Tai dynasty building Shaiva temples in a Sanskritic form is the clearest case of that assimilation, recorded in their own *Buranji* chronicles.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; *Ahom Buranji*.
