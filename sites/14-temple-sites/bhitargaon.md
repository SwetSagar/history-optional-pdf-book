- **Location**: Bhitargaon, Kanpur Dehat district, Uttar Pradesh, in the Ganga-Yamuna doab south of Kanpur.
- **Period/Level**: Gupta period, c. early 5th century CE, and the best-preserved brick temple of that age. A square sanctum on a high plinth carries a tall stepped pyramidal shikhara; the whole is brick with terracotta panels, restored by the ASI in the 1890s after the upper shikhara was struck by lightning.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest surviving north Indian temple with a shikhara, and the type-site for Gupta brick-and-terracotta building.
  - It shows the shikhara already present in the 5th century, which is why it sits between the flat-roofed Gupta shrines - Sanchi 17, Tigawa - and the fully developed Nagara temple of Deogarh. The vertical sequence Tigawa, Bhitargaon, Deogarh is the standard answer to how the Nagara temple evolved.
  - The sanctum roof uses a corbelled vault, and the doorway a corbelled arch, often cited as early Indian arch construction. The claim needs care: these are corbelled, not true voussoir arches, so they are evidence of experiment rather than of the true arch, which arrives with the Sultanate.
  - Large terracotta panels set into the outer wall carry Ganga and Yamuna, Shiva, Vishnu and Durga, with mythological scenes - the finest surviving Gupta terracotta in situ, and evidence that a brick region built its sculpture in the same material.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
