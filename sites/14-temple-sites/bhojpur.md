- **Location**: Bhojpur, Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh, on a sandstone spur above the Betwa, about 28 km south-east of Bhopal.
- **Period/Level**: Paramara period, raised under Bhoja, r. c. 1010-1055 CE, the scholar-king of Dhara. The Bhojeshwar temple was never finished: the sanctum stands, but the shikhara was never built and the construction ramp is still in place.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: An unfinished temple that documents how a medieval Indian temple was actually designed and built - evidence no completed building can give.
  - The linga is a single polished sandstone shaft about 5.5 m high on a platform of three superimposed limestone blocks, among the largest monolithic lingas in India, under a sanctum roofed by a corbelled dome carried on four massive pillars.
  - Architectural drawings are engraved on the flat rock surfaces around the site - ground plans, mouldings, pillar and shikhara profiles at working scale. These are the only surviving medieval Indian temple drawings, and they let the *vastu-shastra* texts be checked against actual practice.
  - The earthen and stone ramp used to haul blocks to the roof survives intact, showing the method of construction directly.
  - Bhoja also built the great Bhojpur dam nearby, an earth-and-stone bund impounding a lake of some 650 sq km, breached in the 15th century by the Malwa sultan Hoshang Shah. Temple and dam together make the Paramara case for royal engineering on a very large scale.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI; Kirit Mankodi, *The Queen's Stepwell at Patan* (for the Paramara context).
