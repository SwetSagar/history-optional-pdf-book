- **Location**: Masrur, Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh, on a sandstone ridge above the Beas valley, facing the Dhauladhar range across the plain.
- **Period/Level**: Monolithic rock-cut complex of about the 8th century CE, contemporary with the Ellora Kailasa, carved under local rulers of the Kangra-Jalandhara region. Severely damaged in the Kangra earthquake of 1905, which brought down several of the shikharas.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only monolithic rock-cut temple complex in north India - the Nagara counterpart of the Deccan's Dravida excavations.
  - Some fifteen shrines were cut from a single outcrop, carved from the top downwards, each carrying a curvilinear Nagara shikhara with *amalaka* and clustered *urushringas*. Where Ellora and Mamallapuram cut Dravida vimanas from rock, Masrur proves the same technique was applied to the northern form - which is why it is called the Himalayan Ellora.
  - The main sanctum holds images of Rama, Lakshmana and Sita, though the temple was very likely Shaiva originally; the surviving doorway and wall sculpture is Brahmanical throughout.
  - A rock-cut tank in front reflects the shikharas, and the whole was aligned on the Dhauladhar peaks - the mountain-temple symbolism made literal by siting rather than by carving.
  - Its condition is itself evidence: the 1905 earthquake damage is a reminder that seismicity, not only iconoclasm, accounts for much of what is lost in the Himalayan belt.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI; Michael Meister, *Encyclopaedia of Indian Temple Architecture*.
