- **Location**: Deogarh, Lalitpur district, Uttar Pradesh, on a spur of the Vindhyas above the Betwa, close to the Madhya Pradesh border.
- **Period/Level**: The Dashavatara temple, Gupta period, c. 500-525 CE - the most fully developed Gupta temple to survive. A later Jain group of the 8th-12th centuries stands on the same hill, so the site spans the Gupta to Chandela centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The classic Gupta temple: the first to combine a shikhara, a sculpted programme and the panchayatana plan.
  - It is the earliest known *panchayatana* - a main shrine on a raised platform with four subsidiary shrines at the corners - the plan that governs north Indian temple building thereafter. The curvilinear shikhara survives only in part but was clearly present.
  - Three great relief panels on the outer walls are the finest Gupta narrative sculpture: *Sheshashayi Vishnu*, reclining on Ananta with Brahma on the lotus and the personified weapons below; *Nara-Narayana* at Badarikashrama; and *Gajendra-moksha*, Vishnu rescuing the elephant. Their composition, in balanced tiers with restrained emotion, defines the classical Gupta idiom.
  - The carved doorway with Ganga and Yamuna on the jambs, mithuna couples, dwarfs and foliate scrolls sets the template for the Nagara doorframe.
  - Despite the name the panels are not a *dashavatara* set - a nineteenth-century misnomer worth correcting, since the temple is thoroughly Vaishnava but not an avatara cycle.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
