- **Location**: Nachna Kuthara, Panna district, Madhya Pradesh, on the Vindhyan plateau north of the Ken, in the tract later held by the Chandelas.
- **Period/Level**: The Parvati temple, Gupta period, c. late 5th century CE, built under the Uchchhakalpa or Vakataka-linked feudatories of the region. The Chaumukhnath temple on the hill above is later, c. 7th-9th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Gupta temple at the moment it acquires height and an ambulatory - the step between Tigawa and Deogarh.
  - The sanctum is enclosed by a covered circumambulatory passage (*pradakshina patha*) lit by pierced stone lattice windows (*jali*), and carries a second storey above - the earliest clear instance of a storeyed Gupta shrine, and the germ of the vertical massing that becomes the Nagara shikhara.
  - The exterior plinth is carved as rough rock-work with animals and caves, so that the building represents a mountain. For a temple of Parvati, daughter of Himalaya, the device makes the architecture itself iconographic - the earliest Indian example of this conceit.
  - Sculpture on the walls includes mithuna couples, ascetics and Ramayana scenes, with a famous door-guardian and river-goddess jambs in the Gupta manner.
  - The site is often paired with Bhumara, its neighbour, whose Shiva temple of the same period gives the other half of the picture for temple building in the Vindhyan kingdoms between Gupta and Chandela rule.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
