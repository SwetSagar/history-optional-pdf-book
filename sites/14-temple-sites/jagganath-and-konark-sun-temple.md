- **Location**: Puri and Konark, on the Odisha coast of the Bay of Bengal - Puri on the shore itself, Konark some 35 km north-east near the old mouth of the Chandrabhaga, now silted and set back from the sea.
- **Period/Level**: Both are Eastern Ganga foundations. The Jagannath temple at Puri was begun by Anantavarman Chodaganga c. 1135 CE and completed under Anangabhima III; the Konark Sun temple was built by Narasimhadeva I, c. 1250, to mark his victories over the Bengal sultanate. Kalinga style throughout.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The culmination of Odishan architecture, and the state cult on which Ganga and Gajapati kingship rested.
  - Konark is conceived as Surya's chariot: twenty-four carved stone wheels along the plinth, drawn by seven horses, the wheels doubling as sundials. The 70 m deul collapsed, probably in the 19th century, leaving the pidha-roofed jagamohana standing. European sailors called it the Black Pagoda, using it as a navigation mark against Puri's White Pagoda.
  - Puri's Jagannath - with Balabhadra and Subhadra - is a wooden triad, periodically renewed in the *Navakalevara* rite. The tribal Sabara origins claimed for the deity make Jagannath the standard example of a local cult raised into a state religion; from Anangabhima III the Gangas ruled formally as deputies of Jagannath.
  - The *Madala Panji*, the temple chronicle, and the Ratha Yatra are the two things to cite: one a continuous indigenous historical record, the other the procession from which the word "juggernaut" entered English.
  - Puri is one of the Char Dham; Konark completes the trio of great Indian sun temples with Modhera and Martand.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; *Madala Panji*; ASI.
