- **Location**: Badami, ancient Vatapi, Bagalkot district, Karnataka, in a red sandstone gorge above the Agastya tank, near the Malaprabha and within 25 km of Aihole and Pattadakal.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Early Chalukyas from c. 540 CE, founded by Pulakesin I, and held until the Rashtrakutas displaced them in 753. Four rock-cut caves were excavated in the later 6th century; Cave 3 carries an inscription of Mangalesha dated Shaka 500 = 578 CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The dated anchor of Deccan rock-cut architecture, and the Chalukya capital that Narasimhavarman I burnt.
  - Cave 3, the largest and finest, is Vaishnava: a colossal Trivikrama, Varaha, Narasimha and a seated Vishnu on Ananta, with traces of painting on the ceiling. Mangalesha's inscription makes it the earliest firmly dated Hindu cave temple in India - the fixed point against which Ellora and Elephanta are placed.
  - Cave 1 is Shaiva, with an eighteen-armed Nataraja whose arms resolve into recognisable dance poses; Cave 2 is Vaishnava; Cave 4 is Jaina, so a single cliff carries three faiths.
  - The Pallava Narasimhavarman I captured and sacked Vatapi in 642 CE, taking the title Vatapikonda - the counter-stroke to Pulakesin II's earlier campaign, and the central episode of the long Chalukya-Pallava contest.
  - The Bhutanatha temples on the tank and the Malegitti Shivalaya show the same workshop moving from rock-cut to structural building.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
