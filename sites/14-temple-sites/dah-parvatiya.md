- **Location**: Da-Parbatia, on the western edge of Tezpur, Sonitpur district, Assam, on the north bank of the Brahmaputra - ancient Kamarupa.
- **Period/Level**: A carved stone doorframe of the 6th century CE, Gupta idiom, belonging to the Varman dynasty of Kamarupa whose best-known king is Bhaskaravarman, the ally of Harshavardhana. A brick temple of the Ahom period was later built over the older plinth, which is how the doorframe survived.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The oldest surviving architectural sculpture in Assam, and the proof that the Gupta classical idiom reached the Brahmaputra valley.
  - The jambs carry Ganga and Yamuna standing on their vehicles with garlands and attendants, treated with the elongated grace and restrained modelling of mature Gupta work at Deogarh and Sarnath - not a provincial copy but the same language.
  - It anchors Kamarupa in mainstream early Indian art history, which matters because the north-east is usually treated as peripheral. Read it with Bhaskaravarman's Nidhanpur copper plates and Xuanzang's account of his court.
  - The superimposition is itself instructive: a 6th-century Varman doorway, an Ahom brick shrine of the 17th-18th century above it, one site carrying a thousand-year gap in Assam's building history.
  - Tezpur's other early remains - the Bamuni hills group and the Agnigarh sculptures - belong to the 9th-10th centuries and show the later Kamarupa style diverging from the Gupta model.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; ASI; *Nidhanpur* copper plates of Bhaskaravarman.
