- **Location**: Katarmal, Almora district, Uttarakhand, on a ridge at about 2,100 m above the Kosi valley in Kumaon, facing east across the hills towards the Himalayan snow line.
- **Period/Level**: Katyuri dynasty, c. 9th century CE, built by the Katyuri ruler Katarmalla, who gives the site its name. The Katyuris ruled Kumaon and Garhwal from Baijnath and Dwarahat between roughly the 7th and 11th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The second most important sun temple in India after Konark, and the principal monument of the Katyuri kingdom.
  - A main shrine of stone in a simplified Nagara idiom stands within a group of about forty-five subsidiary shrines, the whole aligned so that the first light of sunrise falls directly on the image - the same solar orientation used at Modhera and Konark, executed with far smaller resources.
  - The presiding deity is Surya as Vraddha Aditya, the aged sun. Surya worship in the central Himalaya, alongside Martand in Kashmir, shows the cult was strong along the whole mountain belt and not only in Gujarat and Odisha.
  - The temple's elaborately carved wooden doors and panels were among the finest surviving early medieval woodwork in the hills; they were stolen in the 1980s and the recovered pieces are now in the National Museum, Delhi. The loss is regularly cited in discussions of antiquities protection.
  - The Katyuri temples - Katarmal, Baijnath, Jageshwar - together form the hill counterpart to plains Nagara building, adapted to stone-and-timber construction and steep sites.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI; *Uttarakhand State Gazetteer*, Almora.
