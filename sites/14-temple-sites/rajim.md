- **Location**: Rajim, Gariaband district, Chhattisgarh, at the confluence of the Mahanadi, the Pairi and the Sondur - called the Prayag of Chhattisgarh for that triple junction.
- **Period/Level**: The Rajiv Lochan temple, dedicated to Vishnu, founded by Vilasatunga of the Nala dynasty in about the 7th-8th century CE, with later additions under the Kalachuris of Ratanpur and repairs into the Maratha period. The Kuleshwar Mahadeva stands on a plinth in the riverbed itself.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The main surviving monument of the Nala dynasty, and a Vaishnava centre in the Mahanadi valley between the Deccan and Odisha.
  - The Nalas are known mainly from coins and a handful of inscriptions, so a dated temple foundation gives them physical presence. They ruled the Bastar-Koraput tract in the 5th-7th centuries, fought the Vakatakas, and are a standard example of the smaller dynasties that fill the gap between Gupta and early medieval power.
  - The temple is *panchayatana*, with a stone-pillared mandapa whose columns carry fine early sculpture of Narasimha, Varaha, Vamana and river goddesses. The garbhagriha image is a tall four-armed Vishnu in black stone.
  - An inscription of the Kalachuri period on the site records grants, giving the temple a documented endowment history across dynasties.
  - The river confluence is the reason for the location: like Prayaga, Rajim's sanctity is hydrographic, and the Kuleshwar temple standing in the water makes the point structurally. The Rajim Kumbh is held here.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; ASI; *Epigraphia Indica* XXVI (Rajim plates).
