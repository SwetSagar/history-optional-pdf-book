- **Location**: Amarkantak, Anuppur district, Madhya Pradesh, on the Maikal plateau where the Vindhya and Satpura ranges meet - the source of both the Narmada, flowing west, and the Son, flowing east.
- **Period/Level**: A group of some sixteen stone temples of the Kalachuri period, the principal ones raised under Karnadeva, r. 1041-1073 CE, when Kalachuri power in Dahala-mandala was at its height. Nagara shikharas in local sandstone; later Maratha-period additions surround the older core.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A river-source tirtha whose sanctity rests on hydrography rather than on any dynasty - the point where the Narmada is born.
  - The Narmada kund and the temple cluster around it make this the head of the Narmada Parikrama, the circumambulation of the whole river from source to sea and back, one of the longest pilgrim circuits in India and evidence for how river geography organised sacred space.
  - The Karnadeva group - the Machhendranath and Pataleshwar temples among them - gives a dated set of Kalachuri Nagara temples, useful for placing the dynasty's architecture beside the better-known Chandela and Paramara work of the same century.
  - The Puranas treat the Narmada as older and purer than the Ganga, a claim that reflects the Deccan's own tirtha tradition asserting itself against the northern one.
  - Amarkantak sits at the watershed between the Ganga and the peninsular drainage, which is why it recurs in questions on the physical framing of early Indian regions.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI; *Matsya Purana*, Narmada-mahatmya.
