- **Location**: Shamlaji, Aravalli district, Gujarat, on the bank of the Meshvo river where the Aravalli foothills meet the north Gujarat plain, on the old route between Gujarat and Rajasthan.
- **Period/Level**: Two distinct phases. A post-Gupta sculptural school of the 5th-6th centuries CE worked here in a soft grey schist; the standing Vishnu (Gadadhara) temple is a Chaulukya-period rebuilding of the 11th century in white sandstone.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of a recognised regional sculptural school, and a Vaishnava tirtha on a long-used trade route.
  - The "Shamlaji school" of the 6th century - Vishnu, Matrika and Skanda images in grey schist, with full modelling and heavy jewellery - is one of the named post-Gupta regional idioms, standing between Gupta classicism and the harder early medieval western Indian style. The images are now largely in the Baroda Museum.
  - Devnimori, a few kilometres away, held a Buddhist stupa whose inscribed relic casket is dated to the year 127 of the Kathika kings, c. 375 CE, with terracotta Buddha figures of high quality. The site was submerged by the Meshvo reservoir. Shamlaji and Devnimori together show Buddhist and Brahmanical patronage side by side in one small valley.
  - The present temple, carved in white sandstone with friezes of epic scenes, elephants and dancers on its outer walls, is standard Maru-Gurjara work and is still a major pilgrimage centre with a large Kartik fair drawing Bhil and Garasia communities.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; ASI; Baroda Museum catalogue.
