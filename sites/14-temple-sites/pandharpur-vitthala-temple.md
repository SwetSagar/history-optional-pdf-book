- **Location**: Pandharpur, Solapur district, Maharashtra, on the right bank of the Bhima, which is called the Chandrabhaga where it curves like a crescent past the town - in the Deccan plateau's dry interior.
- **Period/Level**: The core shrine belongs to the Yadava period; a Kannada and Sanskrit inscription on the temple dates to 1189 CE, and Hoysala patronage under Vishnuvardhana is recorded in tradition. The mandapas and enclosures are later Maratha additions. The Varkari movement takes shape here from the 13th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The centre of the Varkari sampradaya - the most important bhakti site in Maharashtra, and a devotional tradition organised around a pilgrimage rather than a doctrine.
  - The deity, Vitthala or Vithoba, stands on a brick with hands on hips, a form found nowhere else. He is identified with Krishna and Vishnu, but the iconography and the pastoral associations point to an older local cult of a herdsman god absorbed into Vaishnavism.
  - The saint-poets of the tradition span five centuries and, crucially, all social strata: Jnaneshwar the brahmana, Namdev a tailor, Chokhamela a Mahar, Savata a gardener, Janabai a maidservant, Tukaram a shopkeeper. Their *abhangas* in Marathi made vernacular devotion a vehicle for implicit criticism of caste exclusion - the standard evidence for the social reach of the bhakti movement.
  - The *wari*, the walking pilgrimage in which *palkhis* carrying the saints' sandals converge on Pandharpur for Ashadhi Ekadashi, still draws hundreds of thousands, and is what binds the sect together in the absence of a monastic order.
  - Chokhamela's samadhi stands at the foot of the temple steps, outside the shrine - the exclusion is itself part of the historical record.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; R. C. Dhere, *Rise of a Folk God*.
