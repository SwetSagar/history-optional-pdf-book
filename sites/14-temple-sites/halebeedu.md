- **Location**: Halebidu, ancient Dorasamudra, Hassan district, Karnataka, beside the Dorasamudra tank on the Malnad plateau above the upper Hemavati - the name means "old capital".
- **Period/Level**: Hoysala capital from the 11th century to 1327. The Hoysaleshvara temple was begun c. 1121 CE under Vishnuvardhana, the king who also built Belur, and was never finished; work continued for over eighty years. Sacked by Malik Kafur in 1311 and again by Muhammad bin Tughlaq's forces in 1327, after which the capital shifted.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The peak of Hoysala architecture - a distinct Deccan order that is neither Nagara nor Dravida.
  - The plan is stellate: a star-shaped sanctum and outer wall on a raised *jagati* platform that follows the same profile, so the visitor circumambulates a zigzag of projecting angles catching the light. The Hoysaleshvara is *dvikuta*, twin shrines for Hoysaleshvara and Shantaleshvara, the latter named for Queen Shantala.
  - The material determines the style. Chloritic schist - soapstone - is soft when quarried and hardens on exposure, allowing lathe-turned pillars and jewellery carved with a fineness impossible in granite or sandstone.
  - The outer wall carries continuous horizontal friezes in fixed order: elephants, lions, horsemen, foliage, then epic narrative - hundreds of metres of Ramayana, Mahabharata and Bhagavata scenes.
  - Many sculptures are signed, so individual artists such as Kedaroja and Malitamma are known by name - a rarity in Indian art history. Jain basadis nearby show Hoysala patronage extending beyond Shaivism.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
