- **Location**: Modhera, Mehsana district, Gujarat, on the left bank of the Pushpavati, a tributary of the Rupen, in north Gujarat.
- **Period/Level**: Sun temple of the Chaulukya (Solanki) dynasty, built under Bhima I; an inscription on a wall of the sanctum gives Vikrama Samvat 1083, i.e. 1026-27 CE. Maru-Gurjara style in yellow sandstone. It was raised in the years immediately after Mahmud of Ghazni's raid on Somnath in 1025-26, and was later damaged under Alauddin Khalji.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-preserved sun temple in western India, and a building designed around the movement of the sun itself.
  - It has three parts on one east-west axis: the *Surya Kunda*, a rectangular stepped tank lined with 108 miniature shrines; the *sabhamandapa*, an open pillared hall with fifty-two carved pillars, entered through toranas; and the *gudhamandapa* with the sanctum beyond.
  - The alignment is exact: at the equinoxes the rising sun passes straight down the axis to light the sanctum, where the image of Surya once stood. The plinth carries twelve adityas, one for each month.
  - The kunda is as important as the temple. Stepped water architecture - here, and in the Rani ki Vav at Patan built a generation later - is the distinctive Gujarati contribution, joining ritual bathing, water storage and sculpture in one structure.
  - Surya worship, prominent in early medieval western and eastern India (Modhera, Konark, Martand, Katarmal), fades afterwards; these temples are the main evidence for a cult that later disappears.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI.
