- **Location**: Mamallapuram, Chengalpattu district, Tamil Nadu, on the Coromandel coast about 55 km south of Chennai - an open surf shore with no natural harbour, serving the Pallava capital at Kanchipuram some 70 km inland.
- **Period/Level**: Two Pallava phases. The rock-cut *mandapas*, the Pancha Rathas and the great open-air relief belong to Narasimhavarman I Mamalla, r. 630-668 CE; the structural Shore Temple to Rajasimha (Narasimhavarman II), c. 700-728. The port was active into the 9th century. Known to European sailors as the Seven Pagodas. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site where south Indian architecture moves from rock-cut to structural building, and the harbour from which the Pallavas projected trade and culture into Southeast Asia.
  - The Pancha Rathas are five monolithic shrines cut from single granite boulders, named for the Pandavas and Draupadi. Each tries a different roof form - the Draupadi ratha a thatched hut, the Bhima a barrel vault, the Dharmaraja a stepped pyramid - so they are a set of architectural experiments, and the Dharmaraja's form becomes the standard Dravida *vimana*.
  - The Shore Temple is among the earliest structural stone temples in south India: three shrines, two to Shiva and one holding a reclining Vishnu, built of dressed granite and exposed to sea air for thirteen centuries.
  - The *Descent of the Ganga*, also read as Arjuna's Penance, is the largest bas-relief in the world - some 30 m of rock with gods, sages, animals and a famous row of elephants, split by a natural cleft down which water was made to flow. The Varaha and Krishna mandapas carry the finest Pallava narrative panels.
  - As a port it is the principal evidence for what is called the Indianisation of Southeast Asia - better framed as sustained exchange by merchants and monks than as colonisation. Pallava missions sailed from here to Sri Lanka, Srivijaya and Champa, and the forms worked out at Mamallapuram reappear in Southeast Asian temples. Roman and Chinese coins show the coast was on the long-distance circuit before the Pallavas.
  - Its decline after the 9th century, as Nagapattinam and Kaveripattinam took the Chola traffic, tracks the transfer of power from Pallava to Chola.
