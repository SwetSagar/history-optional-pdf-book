- **Location**: Manali, Kullu district, Himachal Pradesh, at the head of the Kullu valley on the right bank of the Beas, below the Rohtang pass into Lahaul.
- **Period/Level**: The Hidimba Devi (Dhungri) temple, built in 1553 CE by Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu around an older rock shrine. Timber-and-stone construction in the Himalayan pagoda form; the Manu temple at Old Manali belongs to the same local tradition.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-known example of the Himalayan timber pagoda temple, and a clear case of a local goddess absorbed into the Sanskritic epic.
  - The structure is four-tiered, three square roofs of timber and slate rising to a conical brass finial, over a sanctum enclosing a natural rock with the footprint of the deity. The technique is *kath-kuni* - alternating courses of dressed stone and cedar beams without mortar - a seismic-resistant method developed for the western Himalaya.
  - The deity is Hidimba, the rakshasi of the *Mahabharata* who marries Bhima. A pre-existing local mother-goddess cult is here given a place in the epic, and worshipped not as a demon but as the tutelary goddess of the Kullu royal house. This is the standard illustration of how regional cults were folded into the pan-Indian tradition without being displaced.
  - Elaborately carved wooden doorframes with goddesses, animals and foliate scrolls give the finest Himalayan woodcarving of the period.
  - Hidimba's role in the Kullu Dussehra, where local deities are carried to Kullu in procession, shows the temple's continuing political function.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI; *Himachal Pradesh District Gazetteer*, Kullu.
