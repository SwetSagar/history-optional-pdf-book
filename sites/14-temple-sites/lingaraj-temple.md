- **Location**: Bhubaneswar, Odisha, beside the Bindusagar tank in the old temple city of Ekamra Kshetra, in the lower Mahanadi delta a few kilometres from the Daya.
- **Period/Level**: Begun under the Somavamshi king Jajati Keshari and completed c. 1090-1104 CE, with the natamandira and bhogamandapa added under the Eastern Gangas. Kalinga style in sandstone; the deul rises about 55 m.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The mature Kalinga temple, with all four components present for the first time - the standard against which Odishan architecture is described.
  - The four-part axial sequence is the syllabus point: *deul* (sanctum with curvilinear rekha tower), *jagamohana* (assembly porch with stepped pidha roof), *natamandira* (dance hall) and *bhogamandapa* (hall of offerings). Earlier Bhubaneswar temples have only the first two; Lingaraj completes the scheme, and Puri and Konark follow it.
  - The deity is Tribhuvaneshvara or Krittivasa, worshipped as Harihara - Shiva and Vishnu in one - and offerings include tulsi. The syncretism is read as Shaiva Bhubaneswar accommodating the rising Jagannath cult of Puri under Ganga patronage.
  - Bhubaneswar's sequence can be walked in date order: Parashurameshvara (c. 650), Mukteshvara (c. 950, the "gem of Odishan architecture" with its famous torana), Rajarani (11th century, noted for its clustered tower and figural sculpture), then Lingaraj. Few places let a regional style be traced so continuously.
  - The *jagati* platform, the horizontal mouldings of the *bada*, and the *amalaka* with *kalasha* finial are the diagnostic Kalinga features to name.
