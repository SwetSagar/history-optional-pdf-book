- **Location**: Tirumala, above the town of Tirupati, Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh, on the seventh hill of the Seshachalam range near the Swarnamukhi river - the old Tondaimandalam frontier.
- **Period/Level**: The Venkateshvara temple is attested by Pallava inscriptions from the 9th century and endowed successively by the Cholas, Pandyas, Hoysalas and Yadavarayas. Its great period is Vijayanagara, above all under Krishnadevaraya, r. 1509-1529, who visited repeatedly and gilded the *ananda nilaya* vimana.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The richest temple in India, and the standard case study for the temple as an economic institution.
  - Over a thousand inscriptions on its walls record gifts of villages, gold, livestock and cash. They show the temple lending money, financing irrigation, employing hundreds and holding land across several districts - the documentary basis for Burton Stein's work on the temple economy of south India.
  - Krishnadevaraya's portrait statues in copper, with his queens Tirumala Devi and Chinna Devi, stand in the complex. Royal images placed before the deity are a Vijayanagara statement about the relationship of king to god.
  - The deity, Venkateshvara or Balaji, is a form of Vishnu, and the shrine is a *Divya Desam* praised by the Alwars; Ramanuja is credited in tradition with settling its ritual order. A long dispute over whether the image was Shaiva or Vaishnava was resolved in Vaishnava favour under Ramanuja's influence.
  - The practice of tonsure and the *hundi* offerings make it the largest pilgrimage economy in the country, a continuity with the endowment pattern the inscriptions record.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; *Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanam Inscriptions*.
