- **Location**: Bishnupur, Bankura district, West Bengal, on the Dwarakeswar river in the lateritic Rarh country west of the Damodar - the old Mallabhum.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Malla kings, whose temple building peaks in the 17th century: the Rasmancha under Bir Hambir, c. 1600; the Shyam Rai in 1643 and the Jor Bangla in 1655 under Raghunath Singha; the Madan Mohan in 1694 under Durjana Singha. Laterite and carved terracotta brick, the local stone being unavailable.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The fullest expression of the Bengal temple - a regional order built in brick, deriving its form from the thatched village hut.
  - The roof types are the classification itself: *chala* forms (do-chala, char-chala, at-chala) reproduce in brick the curved bamboo-and-thatch roof of a Bengali hut, while *ratna* forms (eka-ratna, pancha-ratna) add towers on a flat roof. The Jor Bangla is two char-chala huts joined; the Shyam Rai is pancha-ratna.
  - The walls carry moulded terracotta panels - Ramayana and Krishna-lila scenes, court processions, hunts, ships and Portuguese figures with muskets. Because the subjects include everyday and contemporary life, they are used as social evidence, not only religious.
  - Bir Hambir's conversion to Vaishnavism under Srinivasa Acharya ties the building programme directly to the Chaitanya movement's spread into western Bengal.
  - Bishnupur is also the seat of a distinct gharana of Hindustani music, patronised by the same court - a reminder that temple patronage came with a wider cultural programme.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI.
