- **Location**: Ellora, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) district, Maharashtra, cut into a basalt scarp of the Charanandri hills beside the Elaganga, on the route from the Deccan plateau to the western ports.
- **Period/Level**: Thirty-four excavations spanning c. 600-1000 CE: Caves 1-12 Buddhist (6th-8th centuries), 13-29 Brahmanical (7th-9th), 30-34 Jain (9th-10th). The Kailasa, Cave 16, was cut under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I, r. 756-773 CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest monolithic excavation in the world, and the clearest single demonstration of three religions patronised at one site.
  - The Kailasa was quarried from the living rock downwards, freeing a complete Dravida temple - gateway, Nandi shrine, mandapa, vimana and cloister - from the hillside. Some 200,000 tonnes of basalt were removed with no possibility of correcting a mistake. It represents Mount Kailasa, and the famous panel of Ravana shaking the mountain while Shiva stills it with his toe is carved into its own plinth.
  - Its model was the Virupaksha at Pattadakal, which the Rashtrakutas took over from the Chalukyas - the architectural evidence of a political succession in the Deccan.
  - The Buddhist group includes Cave 10, the Vishvakarma *chaitya* with a rock-cut stupa and seated Buddha, and Cave 12, the three-storeyed Tin Thal. The Jain group is led by the Indra Sabha, a smaller Kailasa in conception.
  - That the Buddhist, Hindu and Jain excavations overlap in date at one cliff is the standard evidence against reading early medieval religious history as a sequence of exclusions.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
