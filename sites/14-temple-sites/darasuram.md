- **Location**: Darasuram, near Kumbakonam, Thanjavur district, Tamil Nadu, in the heart of the Kaveri delta - the old Chola core, a few kilometres from the river.
- **Period/Level**: The Airavatesvara temple, built by Rajaraja II, r. c. 1146-1173 CE, in the late Chola period. Note that the builder is Rajaraja II, not the Rajaraja I of Thanjavur. Granite; the vimana is only about 24 m, far smaller than the imperial temples. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The last of the three Great Living Chola Temples, and the point at which Chola architecture turns from scale to refinement.
  - Where Thanjavur and Gangaikonda Cholapuram assert size, Darasuram is small and minutely worked - a shift from imperial statement to jewel-like execution that matches the dynasty's reduced political reach in the twelfth century.
  - The front mandapa is conceived as a chariot drawn by horses, with carved stone wheels at its base - the same device later used on the grand scale at Konark, and evidence that the idea was circulating in the south a century earlier.
  - A flight of steps at the southern entrance produces musical tones when struck, and miniature friezes along the base of the mandapa illustrate the *Periya Puranam*, the lives of the sixty-three Nayanmar saints - Sekkizhar's text, composed in this same period under Kulottunga II, rendered in stone.
  - The name comes from Airavata, Indra's elephant, said to have been cured by bathing here. The temple's dance and music sculpture is used as evidence for the temple's role in patronising performance.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, *The Colas*; ASI.
