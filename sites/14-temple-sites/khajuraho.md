- **Location**: Khajuraho, Chhatarpur district, Madhya Pradesh, in Bundelkhand between the Ken and the Betwa - Jejakabhukti, the Chandela homeland.
- **Period/Level**: Chandela period, c. 900-1050 CE, with about eighty-five temples built in roughly a century and twenty-five surviving, in Western, Eastern and Southern groups. The Lakshmana temple is dated 954 CE by Yashovarman's inscription; the Kandariya Mahadeva, c. 1030, belongs to Vidyadhara's reign.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The high point of the Nagara temple, and the largest concentrated act of temple patronage by any early medieval dynasty.
  - The Kandariya Mahadeva is the fully evolved Nagara design: a high *jagati* platform, an unbroken axis of entrance, mandapa, mahamandapa and sanctum, and a shikhara of some eighty-four clustered *urushringas* rising in graded peaks to suggest Mount Kailasa. Sandstone throughout, with no enclosure wall - the opposite of the walled Dravida temple.
  - Patronage is Shaiva, Vaishnava and Jaina together: the Lakshmana enshrines a Vaikuntha Vishnu, the Parshvanatha and Adinatha serve the Jain community, and the granite Chausath Yogini is the oldest structure on the site - evidence of tantric yogini worship alongside orthodox cult.
  - The erotic sculpture, about a tenth of the total and concentrated on the junction walls, has been read as tantric, as auspicious *mithuna*, and as a fertility motif. Treat the interpretation as open; what is certain is that it is one element in a full programme of gods, apsaras, ascetics and battle scenes.
  - Al-Biruni and Ibn Battuta both notice Khajuraho, which helps date its continued importance after Chandela decline.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
