- **Location**: Badrinath, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand, on the right bank of the Alaknanda at about 3,100 m, between the Nar and Narayana peaks below Nilkantha - upstream of the Alaknanda-Dhauliganga confluence at Vishnuprayag.
- **Period/Level**: A Vishnu shrine named in the *Vishnu* and *Skanda Puranas* and sung by the Alwars as a Divya Desam. Tradition credits Adi Shankara, 8th century, with recovering the image and founding the Jyotirmath nearby; the present structure is a Garhwal-kingdom rebuilding, repeatedly repaired after avalanche and earthquake.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the Char Dham, and the clearest surviving case of a pilgrimage network binding the subcontinent together.
  - Shankara's traditional foundation of four mathas at the cardinal points - Jyotirmath here in the north, Sringeri south, Dwarka west, Puri east - is the organisational claim of Advaita Vedanta, and Badrinath anchors its northern limb.
  - The head priest, the Rawal, is by custom a Nambudiri brahmana from Kerala. A Malayali priesthood serving a Himalayan shrine is the single most quotable illustration of the pan-Indian reach of the Shankara tradition.
  - The image is a black shaligram Vishnu in padmasana. A Buddhist origin for the site has been argued from the name and the seated posture, and is worth noting as a contested reading rather than a settled one.
  - The temple opens only between Akshaya Tritiya and Vijayadashami; for the winter the deity moves to Joshimath, a transhumance of worship matching the region's own seasonal movement.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; *Skanda Purana*, Badarikashrama-mahatmya.
