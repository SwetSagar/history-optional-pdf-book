- **Location**: Basavakalyan, ancient Kalyani, Bidar district, northern Karnataka, on the Deccan plateau between the Manjra and the Bhima - inland, roughly 80 km from Bidar. It is not to be confused with Kalyan, the Konkan port near Mumbai.
- **Period/Level**: Capital of the Western Chalukyas, the Chalukyas of Kalyani, from about 1050 CE under Someshvara I until the dynasty's fall around 1190; then briefly of the Kalachuris of Kalyani under Bijjala. Its greatest ruler was Vikramaditya VI, r. 1076-1126, who founded the Chalukya-Vikrama era.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The court that produced the standard text of Hindu inheritance law, and the town where the Lingayat movement began.
  - Vijnaneshvara wrote the *Mitakshara* here, a commentary on the *Yajnavalkya Smriti*. It became the authority on inheritance and joint family property across almost all of India apart from Bengal, where the *Dayabhaga* prevailed, and it was applied by British and Indian courts into the twentieth century. A legal text from this court still shaped property disputes eight hundred years later.
  - Basavanna, minister to the Kalachuri Bijjala, founded the Veerashaiva or Lingayat movement in the 12th century. Its *Anubhava Mantapa* was an open assembly where sharanas of any caste or gender debated - Akka Mahadevi and Allama Prabhu among them - and its *vachanas*, short free-verse utterances in Kannada, rejected caste, temple ritual and priestly mediation.
  - The movement's crisis came with a marriage between a brahmana bride and a cobbler's son around 1167, the violent reaction to which scattered the sharanas. It is the sharpest available case of a bhakti movement colliding with caste in practice rather than in principle.
  - Bilhana's *Vikramankadevacharita*, a Sanskrit court epic, records Vikramaditya VI's reign.
  - *Sources*: Thapar, *Early India*; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10.
