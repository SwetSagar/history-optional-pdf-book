- **Location**: Rajapura, generally identified with Rajouri in the Pir Panjal foothills of Jammu and Kashmir, on the Rajouri river - capital of the Kamboja mahajanapada, on the route from the Punjab plains into Kashmir.
- **Period/Level**: A mahajanapada of the 6th-4th centuries BCE, known from texts; no NBPW sequence has been excavated at the site. Kautilya's Arthashastra classes the Kambojas as a varta-shastropajivin sangha, living by both agriculture and arms, and Ashoka's Rock Edicts V and XIII name them with the Yonas and Gandharas on the empire's frontier.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The north-western corner of the mahajanapada map, and a corporation of the frontier rather than a kingdom of the plains.
  - The Mahabharata records Karna's defeat of the Kambojas at Rajapura, the main textual fix for locating the capital here.
  - Kalhana's Rajatarangini calls it Rajapuri and shows its raja as a tributary of Kashmir in the 11th-12th centuries - the name and the polity survive unbroken into the medieval period.
  - Kamboja was famous for its horses; the Kamboja horse trade is the standard example of the north-western supply on which armies in the Ganga plain depended.
  - The sources disagree on whether Kamboja was a monarchy or a sangha, and the capital is unexcavated - a literary entry, and best presented as such.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; Kalhana, *Rajatarangini*; Raychaudhuri, *Political History of Ancient India*.
