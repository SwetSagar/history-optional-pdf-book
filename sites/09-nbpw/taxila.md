- **Location**: Taxila (Takshashila), Rawalpindi district, Punjab, Pakistan, in the Haro valley between the Indus and the Jhelum, where the Uttarapatha from the Ganga plain met the roads to Bactria and Central Asia.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by John Marshall, 1913-34, and by Mortimer Wheeler, 1944-45. Three successive cities: Bhir Mound, c. 6th-2nd centuries BCE (Achaemenid and Mauryan, with local NBPW and silver punch-marked coins); Sirkap, c. 2nd century BCE-1st century CE (Indo-Greek, rebuilt by Shakas and Parthians); Sirsukh, 1st-2nd centuries CE (Kushana).
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The textbook contrast between an organic Indian town and a Hellenistic grid, standing 500 m apart.
  - Bhir Mound has winding irregular lanes and rubble houses; Sirkap is laid out on a grid, a main street with blocks at right angles inside a wall of some 5.5 km - the clearest surviving case of Greek town planning in South Asia.
  - Capital of the Gandhara satrapy under the Achaemenids and a Mauryan provincial capital. Ashoka was viceroy here, and the city revolted twice, once under Bindusara and again in Ashoka's reign. Marshall found an Aramaic inscription of Ashoka at the site in 1915.
  - The great centre of higher learning of the Buddhist texts, above all for medicine: Panini, the physician Jivaka and Kautilya are all associated with it, and the Jatakas send students here for the eighteen silpas. Teaching was by individual teachers taking pupils - there was no corporate university, which is what distinguishes it from Nalanda.
  - The Dharmarajika stupa and the Jaulian and Mohra Moradu monasteries make it a first-rank Gandhara art site; UNESCO World Heritage since 1980.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6-8; John Marshall, *Taxila* (1951); Thapar, *Early India*.
