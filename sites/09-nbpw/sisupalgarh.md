- **Location**: Sisupalgarh, on the southern edge of Bhubaneswar, Khordha district, Odisha, between the Daya and the Gangua about 20 km from the coast - very probably the Kalinga capital Tosali, and adjacent to Ashoka's Dhauli rock.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by B. B. Lal in 1948 (*Ancient India* 5) and re-excavated by R. K. Mohanty and Monica Smith, 2005-07. Period I, c. 400-200 BCE, before the defences; Period II, c. 200 BCE-100 CE, when the rampart was raised; Period III to c. 350 CE, after which the city was abandoned.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-preserved planned fortified city of early historic India - the one whose full plan can still be walked.
  - A near-perfect square about 1.1 km on a side, enclosing some 130 ha within a rammed-earth rampart revetted in laterite, pierced by eight gateways, two on each face, with guardrooms - a scale of public works that implies an organised state, not a chiefdom.
  - Streets run parallel and cross at right angles with the houses aligned to them, and a pillared structure of sixteen monolithic laterite pillars stands at the centre.
  - NBPW, rouletted ware, punch-marked and later coins, glass and semi-precious stone beads, and the distinctive terracotta ear ornaments - a city drawing on both the Ganga route and Indian Ocean trade.
  - The 2005-07 work showed the settlement was large and densely occupied from the start, revising the older picture of gradual growth behind the walls.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6 and 8; B. B. Lal, *Ancient India* 5 (1949); Mohanty and Smith (2008).
