- **Location**: Varanasi (Kashi), on the left bank of the Ganga between the Varuna and the Assi, Uttar Pradesh; the excavated early city is the Rajghat plateau at the Varuna confluence.
- **Period/Level**: Rajghat excavated by Banaras Hindu University under A. K. Narain and T. N. Roy from 1957. The earliest level begins c. 800 BCE with black-slipped ware; NBPW from c. 600 BCE, the phase to which the mud rampart belongs; occupation runs through the Kushana and Gupta periods without a break.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, and the capital of a mahajanapada that lost the contest it was best placed to win.
  - The NBPW levels give a mud rampart, a burnt clay floor, terracotta ring wells and brick revetments on the river front - a city, not a village, by the 6th century BCE.
  - Kashi was famous in the Jatakas for its fine textiles, the kasika vastra, and for its wealth; it is one of the six mahanagaras of the Digha Nikaya. It fought Kosala, Anga and Magadha, was absorbed by Kosala, and passed with Kosala to Magadha - the classic case of a rich city state losing to a territorial power.
  - A teaching centre for Brahmanas, Buddhists and Jainas alike, with the Vedas and Upanishads taught here and Sarnath 10 km away; the twenty-third Tirthankara Parshvanatha was born at Varanasi.
  - Later the Kashi Vishvanatha jyotirlinga and the Banaras gharana of Hindustani music.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; A. K. Narain and T. N. Roy, *Excavations at Rajghat*; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
