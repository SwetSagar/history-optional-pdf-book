- **Location**: Prahladpur, Varanasi district, Uttar Pradesh, in the Ganga plain a short distance south of Varanasi.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by A. K. Narain and T. N. Roy for Banaras Hindu University in 1963 and published as *Excavations at Prahladpur* (1968). Period I is a pre-NBPW black-slipped ware phase; Period II is NBPW, c. 600-200 BCE; the later levels run into the early centuries CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A small dig of large consequence - one of the excavations that fixed the ceramic sequence of the middle Ganga.
  - It showed that black-slipped ware precedes NBPW rather than simply accompanying it, and with Rajghat and Chirand it gives the stratigraphic frame against which every other NBPW site in the middle Ganga is read.
  - NBPW itself is a deluxe ware: thin, wheel-made, with a lustrous black to steel-grey surface produced by a fine slip and a controlled firing. It was valuable enough that broken vessels were repaired with copper rivets - a detail recovered from sites of this group.
  - It occurs with iron, punch-marked coins, burnt brick and terracotta ring wells, the standard package of the second urbanisation, c. 600-200 BCE.
  - Its importance is stratigraphic, not monumental: cite it for the sequence, not for a city.
  - *Sources*: A. K. Narain and T. N. Roy, *Excavations at Prahladpur* (BHU, 1968); Upinder Singh, ch. 6.
