- **Location**: Berachampa, North 24 Parganas, West Bengal, on the Bidyadhari - a former Ganga distributary - about 35 km north-east of Kolkata, in the lower delta.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Calcutta University under K. G. Goswami, 1956-68. A pre-NBPW level; NBPW c. 400-200 BCE; Mauryan; a rich Shunga-Kushana phase of c. 200 BCE-200 CE on the Khana Mihirer Dhipi mound; then Gupta and Pala levels.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest terracotta corpus of early historic India, and the delta's inland answer to the port of Tamralipti.
  - Mould-made terracotta plaques of women in elaborate headdresses and jewellery, with male figures, dwarfs, winged humans and carts. The fabric is red to reddish-brown and the moulds range from single to multiple - a technique suited to mass production for a market, not to one-off votive work.
  - Rouletted ware and Roman-type objects, punch-marked and Kushana coins, seals and sealings, and ivory, wood and bronze figurines - an emporium drawing on both the Ganga route and the Bay of Bengal.
  - Frequently identified with the capital of the Gangaridai of the Greek writers, or with the port Gange of the *Periplus*; neither identification is proven, and no inscription names the site.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI, *IAR*.
