- **Location**: Potali or Potana, usually identified with Bodhan on the Godavari in Nizamabad district, Telangana - capital of Assaka (Asmaka), the only one of the sixteen mahajanapadas lying south of the Vindhyas.
- **Period/Level**: A mahajanapada capital of the 6th-4th centuries BCE, known from texts rather than from a stratified NBPW excavation. Assaka appears in the Anguttara Nikaya list of sixteen, and the Parayanavagga of the Sutta Nipata sends the brahmana Bavari's pupils north from the Godavari to the Buddha.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Marks the southern limit of the mahajanapada map and the Deccan end of the Dakshinapatha.
  - A monarchy rather than a gana, it fought Avanti over the Godavari valley and was eventually absorbed by it - the one mahajanapada whose politics run south rather than east.
  - Its position fixes the Dakshinapatha, the southern high road running from Rajagriha through Ujjayini to Pratishthana; Assaka is the link in that chain between the Vindhyas and the Deccan plateau.
  - Some texts give Paithan (Pratishthana) rather than Potali as the Assaka capital, and the identification with Bodhan is not certain; the entry rests on literature, not on a dig, which is the honest thing to say about it in an answer.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; Thapar, *Early India*; H. C. Raychaudhuri, *Political History of Ancient India*.
