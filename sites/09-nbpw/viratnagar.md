- **Location**: Bairat (Viratnagar), Jaipur district, Rajasthan, in a valley of the Aravallis on the route from the Ganga-Yamuna doab to Gujarat - capital of the Matsya mahajanapada.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by D. R. Sahni (1936-37) and N. R. Banerjee (1962-63). NBPW and punch-marked coins in the early levels; the Buddhist monastery and circular temple on Bijak-ki-Pahari are Mauryan, 3rd century BCE; Indo-Greek coins of Menander come from the levels above.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest free-standing structural Buddhist shrine in India, and the only Ashokan edict that names Buddhist scripture.
  - The circular chaitya on Bijak-ki-Pahari, built of brick and timber with twenty-six octagonal wooden pillars set around a small stupa inside a circular ambulatory - the structural counterpart of the rock-cut chaityas of the Deccan, and proof that the circular plan was not an accident of rock-cutting.
  - Two Ashokan Minor Rock Edicts. The Bairat or Bhabru edict, now in the Indian Museum, Kolkata, is addressed to the Sangha, declares Ashoka's faith in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha, and recommends seven Buddhist texts by name - the only edict to cite scripture, and the strongest evidence for his personal Buddhism. A second Minor Rock Edict survives on a boulder at the site.
  - Matsya, a monarchy, is the Virata of the Mahabharata where the Pandavas passed their year of concealment; it was absorbed by Magadha and then by the Mauryas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6-7; D. R. Sahni, *Archaeological Remains and Excavations at Bairat* (1937); Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*.
