- **Location**: Pushkalavati - the mounds of Bala Hisar and Shaikhan Dheri at Charsadda, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan - at the confluence of the Swat and Kabul rivers, capital of Gandhara.
- **Period/Level**: Bala Hisar excavated by Mortimer Wheeler in 1958, Shaikhan Dheri by A. H. Dani in 1963-64. Occupation from c. 1400 BCE; the Achaemenid-to-Mauryan city of the 6th-3rd centuries BCE carries local imitations of NBPW; Shaikhan Dheri is the Indo-Greek to Kushana city, 2nd century BCE-2nd century CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The north-western terminus of the NBPW map, and a city planned on a grid two centuries before Sirkap.
  - Mud fortifications with a defensive ditch; the city laid out on a rectangular plan with parallel streets and blocks of houses, individual rooms having a fireplace in the middle.
  - The NBPW here is a local copy rather than an import - the ware travels as a fashion along the Uttarapatha, not as a traded commodity, which is the point to make about its western distribution.
  - Alexander's general Hephaestion took the city after a thirty-day siege in 327 BCE and left a Macedonian garrison; Arrian describes it as a great city of the north-west.
  - Panini, the grammarian, belongs to Shalatura nearby in the 4th century BCE, and Pushkalavati traded with Taxila along the Uttarapatha before becoming a Gandhara art centre under the Kushanas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6 and 8; Wheeler, *Charsada* (1962); A. H. Dani, *Ancient Pakistan* II (1965-66).
