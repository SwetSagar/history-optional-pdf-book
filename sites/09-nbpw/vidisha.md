- **Location**: Vidisha (ancient Besnagar), at the confluence of the Betwa (Vetravati) and the Bes, Madhya Pradesh, 10 km from Sanchi, on the route from the Ganga plain to Ujjayini and the Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by H. H. Lake (1910), D. R. Bhandarkar (1913-15) and M. D. Khare (1963-65). NBPW levels of c. 500-200 BCE below a fortified early historic city; its great phase is Shunga and early Satavahana, 2nd-1st centuries BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Carries the earliest epigraphic evidence for Bhagavata worship, and the earliest named craft guild in Indian art.
  - The Heliodorus pillar (Garuda-dhvaja), c. 113 BCE: set up in Prakrit in the Brahmi script by Heliodorus, a Greek of Taxila and ambassador of the Indo-Greek king Antialkidas to the Shunga king Kashiputra Bhagabhadra. Heliodorus calls himself a Bhagavata and a devotee of Vasudeva - a foreigner adopting an Indian cult, and the first inscriptional evidence for Vaishnavism.
  - Ashoka was viceroy at Vidisha for Bindusara and married Devi, a merchant's daughter of the city and the mother of Mahendra and Sanghamitra - the link joining Vidisha to Sanchi and to the Sri Lanka mission.
  - The southern gateway of Sanchi Stupa 1 carries an inscription recording that it was the work of the ivory carvers of Vidisha - rare direct evidence of a guild at work on a monument.
  - The colossal Besnagar yakshi and the nearby Udayagiri caves belong to the same cluster of sites.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7-8; D. R. Bhandarkar, *ASI Annual Report* 1913-14; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*.
