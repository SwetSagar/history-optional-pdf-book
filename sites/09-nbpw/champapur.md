- **Location**: Champa (Champanagar-Champapur), near Bhagalpur, Bihar, where the Champa river joins the Ganga - capital of the Anga mahajanapada.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by B. P. Sinha for Patna University in the 1970s. A pre-NBPW black-and-red ware level at the base; NBPW c. 600-200 BCE, the phase of the rampart and moat; occupation continues into the Gupta period.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The fortified nagara of the Buddhist texts, and the first mahajanapada to be swallowed by Magadha.
  - Defended by a mud rampart with a moat, and listed among the six mahanagaras of the Digha Nikaya - the excavated defences confirm the textual picture of a walled city rather than a village capital.
  - A monarchy under Brahmadatta, annexed by Bimbisara in the mid-6th century BCE. Anga's absorption is the opening move in Magadha's rise and the standard first example of imperial expansion in the Ganga plain.
  - Champa was the Ganga's river port for the eastern trade; the Jatakas send merchants downstream from here towards Suvarnabhumi.
  - A major Jaina tirtha: the twelfth Tirthankara Vasupujya was born and attained nirvana here, and Mahavira spent three rainy seasons in the city.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI, *IAR*.
