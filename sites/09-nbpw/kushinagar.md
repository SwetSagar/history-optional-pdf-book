- **Location**: Kasia, Kushinagar district, Uttar Pradesh, on the Hiranyavati (the Little Gandak), in the tract between the Rapti and the Gandak.
- **Period/Level**: Identified by Cunningham and excavated by A. C. L. Carlleyle in 1876, later by Hirananda Sastri and J. Ph. Vogel. NBPW levels of c. 600-200 BCE underlie the monastic complex; the Nirvana temple and its great image are Gupta, 5th century CE, with Pala-period repairs above.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The place of the Buddha's Mahaparinirvana, and the capital of one of the two Malla republics.
  - Capital of the Malla gana-sangha, a republican oligarchy among the sixteen mahajanapadas - the Mallas of Kushinara and of Pava - later absorbed by Magadha.
  - The Buddha died here c. 483 BCE between two sal trees; one of the four great pilgrimage places with Lumbini, Bodh Gaya and Sarnath, and the site of Ashoka's Nirvana stupa, enlarged under the Guptas.
  - Carlleyle recovered the 6.1 m reclining Buddha in red sandstone in 1876; its pedestal inscription names the donor Haribala and the craftsman Dinna, dating it to the reign of Kumaragupta I.
  - The Ramabhar stupa (Mukutabandhana chaitya) marks the cremation spot, from which the relics were divided into the eight shares that produced the first stupas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6-7; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI reports (Carlleyle, Vogel).
