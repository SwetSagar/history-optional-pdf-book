- **Location**: Basarh, Vaishali district, Bihar, in the tract between the Gandak and the Ganga north of Patna - capital of the Licchavis and head of the Vajji confederacy.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by T. Bloch (1903-04), D. B. Spooner (1913-14), Krishna Deva and Vijayakanta Mishra (1950), and A. S. Altekar (1957-62). NBPW of c. 600-200 BCE at Raja Vishal ka Garh, with Shunga, Kushana and Gupta levels above.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-documented gana-sangha in Indian history, and the site of the earliest excavated Buddhist stupa.
  - Government was by an assembly of Licchavi rajas, not a king. The Buddha's seven aparihaniya dhammas - the conditions on which a republic does not decline - are addressed to the Vajjis, and Ajatashatru needed years of preparation and his minister Vassakara's intrigue to break them, which is the standard illustration of monarchy overcoming the republics.
  - Altekar's 1958 excavation of the relic stupa recovered a soapstone casket with ashes, held to be the Licchavis' share of the Buddha's relics; if so, this is the oldest Buddhist stupa yet excavated, in mud brick, later enlarged four times.
  - The Ashokan pillar at Kolhua carries a single lion capital and stands beside a tank and stupa - one of the few pillars still in situ on its original spot.
  - The Second Buddhist Council met here c. 383 BCE over ten disputed Vinaya points, the dispute that opened the Sthaviravada-Mahasamghika split. Mahavira was born at Kundagrama, a suburb of Vaishali, and the courtesan Amrapali gave her mango grove to the Buddha.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6-7; A. S. Altekar, *Report on Vaishali Excavations*; Thapar, *Early India*.
