- **Location**: Suktimati (Sotthivati), capital of the Chedi mahajanapada in the Bundelkhand-Baghelkhand tract between the Yamuna and the Narmada. Raychaudhuri and Pargiter place it near Banda in Uttar Pradesh on the Ken; D. K. Chakrabarti identifies it with Rewa in Madhya Pradesh. The map dot follows the Banda identification.
- **Period/Level**: A mahajanapada capital of the 6th-4th centuries BCE; no excavated NBPW sequence has been tied to the name. The Mahabharata credits its founding to King Uparichara Vasu.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The one mahajanapada capital that archaeology has never located - which is itself the examinable point about the limits of the list of sixteen.
  - Chedi's importance is locational: it lay astride the routes from the Ganga plain to the Deccan, controlling the Vindhyan passes rather than a rich agrarian core.
  - Shishupala, killed by Krishna at Yudhishthira's rajasuya, is the Chedi king of the epic; Kharavela of Kalinga later styles himself of the Chedi line in the Hathigumpha inscription, carrying the name into the 1st century BCE.
  - A monarchy. When asked for the sixteen mahajanapadas and their capitals, give Chedi-Suktimati, but note that the capital is a textual name without a site.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; Raychaudhuri, *Political History of Ancient India*; D. K. Chakrabarti, *The Archaeology of Ancient Indian Cities*.
