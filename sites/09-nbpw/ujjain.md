- **Location**: Ujjayini, on the right bank of the Shipra, Madhya Pradesh - capital of the Avanti mahajanapada, where the Dakshinapatha met the route down to the Gujarat ports.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by N. R. Banerjee for the ASI, 1955-58. Period I begins c. 700 BCE with black-and-red ware behind a massive mud rampart; Period II is NBPW, c. 500-200 BCE; the city continues through the Shunga, Shaka-Kshatrapa and Gupta periods.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest and earliest urban defence work in central India, and a manufacturing city rather than an agrarian capital.
  - The mud embankment, faced and moated, encloses about 0.9 sq km - a scale of fortification unusually early for the region and the strongest single argument that Avanti urbanised alongside the Ganga plain, not after it.
  - Iron arrowheads, spearheads and crowbars, terracotta spindle whorls, punch-marked coins, terracotta ring wells and a large burnt-brick tank for water storage.
  - Avanti under Pradyota was one of the four great powers of the 6th century BCE with Magadha, Kosala and Vatsa, and was absorbed by Magadha under Shishunaga. Ashoka governed here for Bindusara, and it was on this posting that he married Devi of Vidisha.
  - It stood on the southern high road running from Rajagriha to Pratishthana; later it becomes the seat of the Vikramaditya legend, of Kalidasa, of the Mahakala jyotirlinga, and the prime meridian of Indian astronomy.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; Basham, *The Wonder That Was India*; ASI, *IAR* 1956-58.
