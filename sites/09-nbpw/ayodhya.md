- **Location**: Ayodhya (ancient Saketa), Ayodhya - formerly Faizabad - district, Uttar Pradesh, on the right bank of the Sarayu (Ghaghara), the northern capital of Kosala.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by B. B. Lal for the ASI's Archaeology of the Ramayana Sites project (1975-80) and again by the ASI in 2003. Period I is NBPW, with radiocarbon dates reaching back to c. 700 BCE - among the earliest NBPW horizons anywhere - running to c. 200 BCE; Shunga-Kushana and Gupta levels follow.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the sites that pushes the beginning of NBPW back before 600 BCE, and a rare case of the ware arriving before monumental building.
  - NBPW occurs with a grey ware bearing linear designs. Houses in this level are of wattle and daub; there are no baked-brick structures, so the deluxe ware precedes urban architecture here.
  - Broken votive tanks, wheel-made terracotta discs and animal figurines; ring wells and burnt brick appear only in the later levels.
  - Post-Mauryan coins were struck here by the local Deva kings, and the Dhanadeva inscription of the 1st century BCE records that his ancestor Pushyamitra performed two ashvamedhas - one of the few epigraphic notices of the Shungas.
  - Saketa is one of the six great cities (mahanagaras) of the Digha Nikaya, and a Jaina tirtha as the birthplace of five Tirthankaras including Rishabhanatha.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; B. B. Lal in ASI, *IAR* 1976-77; Thapar, *Early India*.
