- **Location**: Amaravati with the adjoining fortified mound of Dharanikota, Guntur district, Andhra Pradesh, on the right bank of the Krishna near the head of its delta.
- **Period/Level**: Dharanikota (ASI, R. Subrahmanyam, 1962-65) opens with an NBPW level of c. 400-300 BCE behind a mud rampart. The Satavahana capital Dhanyakataka and the Mahachaitya run c. 200 BCE-250 CE, with Ikshvaku patronage after; the carved railing and drum slabs are of the 1st-2nd centuries CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A stratified NBPW level lying directly under a Satavahana capital - the clearest Deccan evidence that the second urbanisation reached the Krishna.
  - Dharanikota yielded a brick-lined navigational channel with a wooden wharf on the Krishna, tying the stupa's wealth to river and sea trade rather than to land revenue.
  - The Mahachaitya (Dipaladinne) had a drum of about 50 m faced in white Palnad limestone, with ayaka platforms carrying five pillars on each of the four sides - the diagnostic feature of Andhra stupas, absent at Sanchi and Bharhut.
  - The Amaravati school: high-relief narrative medallions, slender swaying figures and crowded compositions, with both aniconic and iconic Buddha images side by side. Recorded by Colin Mackenzie in 1797 and 1816; slabs are now in the Government Museum, Chennai, and the British Museum.
  - Tibetan tradition places the Buddha's first teaching of the Kalachakra at Dhanyakataka, and Nagarjuna is associated with this stretch of the Krishna.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI, *IAR* 1962-65; Robert Knox, *Amaravati* (1992).
