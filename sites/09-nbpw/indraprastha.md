- **Location**: The Purana Qila mound, Delhi, on the old west bank of the Yamuna; the village of Indrapat stood on it until 1913.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by B. B. Lal in 1954-55 and 1969-73, and again by the ASI in 2013-14 and 2017-18. PGW in the lowest level, c. 1000 BCE; NBPW from c. 300 BCE; then Shunga, Kushana, Gupta, Rajput, Sultanate and Mughal levels - a near-continuous sequence of some 2,500 years.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the few north Indian mounds with an unbroken PGW-to-Mughal sequence, and the archaeological anchor for the Pandava capital.
  - The NBPW level gives mud-brick and burnt-brick houses, drains built of rectangular and wedge-shaped bricks, and terracotta ring wells used as soak pits - the standard signature of the second urbanisation.
  - Terracotta figurines including a horse with an armoured rider, and a clay sealing.
  - The Mahabharata's Indraprastha, raised on the cleared Khandava forest. The PGW basal level is what allows the identification to be argued at all, but nothing found here names the site, so the equation stays traditional rather than proved.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5-6; B. B. Lal in ASI, *IAR*; Basham, *The Wonder That Was India*.
