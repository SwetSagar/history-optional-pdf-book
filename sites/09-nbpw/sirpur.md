- **Location**: Sirpur (ancient Shripura), Mahasamund district, Chhattisgarh, on the left bank of the Mahanadi - capital of Dakshina Kosala, the southern half of the Kosala tradition.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by M. G. Dikshit (1953-57) and by A. K. Sharma for the Chhattisgarh government from 2000. It enters the maps as the mahajanapada-era capital of Dakshina Kosala; the excavated city is Panduvamshi (Somavamshi), 6th-8th centuries CE, at its height under Mahashivagupta Balarjuna (c. 595-655 CE), and in decline after the 12th century.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A capital where Buddhist, Jaina and Brahmanical establishments stood together, and the finest surviving brick temple of the Deccan fringe.
  - Xuanzang visited in 638-639 CE and reports about a hundred monasteries with some ten thousand Mahayana monks. The excavated Anandaprabhu Kuti and Swastika viharas, with bronze and monolithic stone Buddhas, bear the account out.
  - The Lakshmana temple, c. 625-650 CE, built in brick by Queen Vasata in memory of Harshagupta, stands on a jagati with a circumambulatory path and a Nagara shikhara; its carved walls carry the incarnations of Vishnu together with mithuna figures.
  - The Puranas name the Ikshvakus as the first rulers of Dakshina Kosala, overthrown by Mahapadma Nanda - the Nanda conquest reaching south of the Vindhyas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; A. K. Sharma, *Excavations at Sirpur*; ASI, *IAR*.
