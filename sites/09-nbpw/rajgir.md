- **Location**: Rajgir (Rajagriha, earlier Girivraja), Nalanda district, Bihar, in a valley ringed by five hills on the Banganga - the first capital of Magadha.
- **Period/Level**: Explored by Cunningham and Marshall, excavated by A. Ghosh for the ASI in 1950 and later. The old city of Girivraja with its cyclopean stone wall is pre-Mauryan; NBPW runs c. 600-200 BCE. The capital moved to Pataliputra under Udayin, c. 460 BCE, after which the city survives as a religious centre.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital from which Magadha's ascendancy was built, and the only major mahajanapada capital defended by stone.
  - A cyclopean wall of undressed stone about 40 km long runs along the hill crests, with Ajatashatru's inner fortification below - the largest surviving pre-Mauryan defence work in India, and unusual in a plain where mud ramparts are the rule.
  - Capital under Bimbisara and Ajatashatru. Excavation has exposed the Jivakamravana - the physician Jivaka's mango-grove monastery - along with Venuvana, the Griddhakuta and Maniyar Math.
  - The First Buddhist Council met in the Saptaparni cave immediately after the parinirvana, under Mahakassapa and with Ajatashatru's patronage, Ananda reciting the Sutta and Upali the Vinaya.
  - Mahavira spent fourteen rainy seasons at Rajgir and Nalanda, and the twentieth Tirthankara Munisuvrata was born here; Xuanzang describes the site in the 7th century.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 6; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI, *IAR* 1950.
