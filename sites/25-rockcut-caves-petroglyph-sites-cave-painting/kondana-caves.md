- **Location**: Kondana, near Karjat in Raigad district, Maharashtra, in a wooded ravine of the Sahyadris close to the Rajmachi fort - on the escarpment above the Konkan, near the passes carrying trade from the coast to the Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: A group of sixteen Buddhist excavations of the Hinayana phase, generally dated to the 1st century BCE, contemporary with Bhaja and a little earlier than Karle. They comprise a *chaitya* hall and *viharas*.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the earliest western Deccan cave groups, and an unusually clear case of stone imitating carpentry.
  - The chaitya's facade and interior reproduce timber construction in rock: a horseshoe arch, ribbed vaulting and railing patterns cut to look like assembled wood, with actual wooden ribs originally fixed into the ceiling. The same translation of carpentry into stone begins at Barabar and runs through Bhaja, Bedse and Karle - Kondana is a key stage in that sequence.
  - Sculpture on the vihara facade includes *mithuna* couples and dancing figures of high quality, and the pillars carry the pot-and-foliage forms of the period.
  - Its setting explains its existence. These monasteries stood on the trade routes between the Konkan ports - Kalyan, Sopara, Chaul - and the Deccan markets at Paithan and Ter, and were endowed by merchants, guilds and Satavahana officials, as the inscriptions at Nashik, Karle and Kanheri record.
  - Part of the chaitya collapsed in a landslide in the 1990s, so the site is also cited in discussions of monument conservation.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 8; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI.
