- **Location**: Sitabinji, Keonjhar district, Odisha, on the Sita river in the upper Baitarani basin - a wooded tract of northern Odisha, well away from the coastal temple belt.
- **Period/Level**: A natural rock shelter called Ravanachhaya, shaped like a half-open umbrella, carrying a tempera mural usually dated to the 6th-7th centuries CE. A short inscription in box-headed characters reads *Disabhanja*, tying the painting to the Bhanja dynasty of northern Odisha. A four-faced *mukhalinga* stands at the site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only surviving early mural painting in eastern India, and one of very few anywhere outside Ajanta, Bagh and Sittanavasal.
  - The painting shows a royal procession: a crowned figure riding an elephant under an umbrella, footmen leading, horsemen and women following, some dancing. The subject is secular and courtly, not devotional - which is what makes it unusual, since almost all surviving early Indian painting is religious.
  - It is executed in tempera on the rock face, in red, yellow and white earth pigments, and the drawing is closer to folk idiom than to the Ajanta court style. Together the two give a rare sense of regional variation in early painting.
  - The Bhanja reading gives eastern India a named dynasty for the work, and the site is read as a royal retreat or hunting camp rather than a shrine - the umbrella-shaped shelter being a natural pavilion.
  - The mukhalinga is evidence that Shaivism was established in Odisha well before the great Bhubaneswar temples, filling a gap between the Kharavela period and the Somavamshis.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; ASI; *Orissa Historical Research Journal*.
