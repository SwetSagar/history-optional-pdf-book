- **Location**: Gharapuri island in Mumbai harbour, Raigad district, Maharashtra, about 10 km by sea from the Mumbai shore - a basalt island commanding the Konkan coast's finest anchorage.
- **Period/Level**: Mid-6th century CE, the great Shaiva cave usually attributed to the Kalachuris of Mahishmati under Krishnaraja, though Konkan Maurya patronage is also argued. Seven excavations in all: five Brahmanical caves on the western hill and two Buddhist on the eastern. UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The summit of Shaiva rock-cut sculpture in India - a cave conceived as a single theological argument in stone.
  - The Great Cave, about 39 m across, is planned on two axes at once, one running to the linga shrine and the other to the Sadashiva image, so the abstract and the iconic forms of Shiva are worshipped in one space.
  - The Trimurti or Sadashiva bust, nearly 6 m high, shows three of Shiva's five aspects: Aghora or Bhairava in wrath to one side, Vamadeva serene and feminine to the other, and Tatpurusha in meditative calm at the centre. It is usually read not as the Brahma-Vishnu-Shiva triad but as the unity of Shiva's own natures.
  - The surrounding panels form a deliberate sequence of opposites - Ardhanarishvara and Gangadhara, Nataraja and Yogishvara, the marriage of Shiva and Parvati against the slaying of Andhaka - setting creation beside destruction, union beside asceticism.
  - The Portuguese named the island from a stone elephant at the landing, now in the Bhau Daji Lad Museum, and are reported to have used the sculptures for musket practice, which accounts for much of the damage.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Thapar, *Early India*.
