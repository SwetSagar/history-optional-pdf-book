- **Location**: Udayagiri, Vidisha district, Madhya Pradesh, a sandstone ridge above the Bes near its confluence with the Betwa, about 4 km from Vidisha and within sight of Sanchi. Not to be confused with the Jain Udayagiri in Odisha.
- **Period/Level**: Gupta period. Twenty rock-cut shrines cut in the late 4th and early 5th centuries CE. Cave 6 carries an inscription of Chandragupta II dated Gupta era 82, i.e. 401-02 CE, recording the visit of the king with his minister Virasena; Cave 20 has a Jain record of Kumaragupta I, GE 106 = 425-26 CE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest datable Hindu cave temples in India, and the birthplace of the classical Gupta iconographic programme.
  - The colossal Varaha panel in Cave 5 shows Vishnu as the boar lifting Bhudevi from the cosmic ocean, watched by ranks of gods and sages with the personified oceans below. It is read as political allegory as much as myth - the earth rescued and restored, matching Chandragupta II's own claim to have reunified the land after the Shaka campaign.
  - The site is deliberately syncretic: Vaishnava, Shaiva and Shakta images together - Anantashayi Vishnu, an *ekamukha* linga, Durga Mahishasuramardini, Ganesha and the Saptamatrikas - with a Jain shrine at Cave 20.
  - Architecturally it is a hybrid: rock-cut sanctums given structural pillared porticos, with carved doorways and *purnaghata* capitals. The doorframe with river goddesses first takes its classical form here, and is then repeated at Deogarh and across the Nagara tradition.
  - That a dated royal inscription sits beside the sculpture makes Udayagiri the fixed reference point for dating all other Gupta art.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Thapar, *Early India*.
