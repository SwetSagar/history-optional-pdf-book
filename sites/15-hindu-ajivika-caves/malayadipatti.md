- **Location**: Malayadipatti, Pudukkottai district, Tamil Nadu, in the dry rocky uplands between the Kaveri delta and the Vaigai - the same low granite country that carries Sittanavasal, Narthamalai and Tirumayam.
- **Period/Level**: Two rock-cut shrines of about the 8th century CE, excavated under the Muttaraiyar chieftains, feudatories who held this tract between Pallava and Pandya power. The Olakkanesvara cave is Shaiva, the Ranganatha cave Vaishnava. Inscriptions of the Muttaraiyars and later of the Cholas and Pandyas record endowments; the surviving paintings are later, of the Vijayanagara-Nayaka centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A rare paired Shaiva and Vaishnava rock-cut group, and one of the few Tamil sites where mural painting survives over sculpture.
  - The Ranganatha cave holds a reclining Vishnu on Adishesha cut from the rock face, with Bhudevi and attendant figures - a southern counterpart to the Anantashayana at Undavalli and Mamallapuram.
  - Parts of the sculpture were covered in painted stucco, and traces of lime-ground painting survive on the ceilings and walls, showing birds, human figures and floral work. Because so little Tamil painting survives between Sittanavasal and the Nayaka period, even fragments here carry weight.
  - The Muttaraiyar patronage is the historical point. Cave excavation in the Tamil country is usually credited to Mahendravarman I and the Pallavas, but Malayadipatti shows subordinate chieftains commissioning the same work - evidence that the practice spread well below the level of the great dynasties.
  - Having both a Shiva and a Vishnu shrine cut at one outcrop, endowed by the same line, argues against reading Shaiva-Vaishnava rivalry into every Tamil site.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; ASI, *South Indian Inscriptions*; *Pudukkottai State Inscriptions*.
