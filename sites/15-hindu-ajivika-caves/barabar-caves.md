- **Location**: The Barabar and Nagarjuni hills, Jehanabad district, Bihar, granite outcrops rising from the plain about 25 km north of Gaya, near the Phalgu - in the Magadhan heartland.
- **Period/Level**: Mauryan. Four caves at Barabar were excavated under Ashoka, the Sudama cave dated to his twelfth regnal year, c. 261 BCE; three more on the Nagarjuni hill were dedicated by his grandson Dasharatha, c. 230 BCE. Dedicatory inscriptions in Brahmi name the donor as Piyadasi.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India, and the only monuments anywhere built by a state for the Ajivikas.
  - Each cave is cut into hard granite and finished to a mirror polish - the Mauryan polish also seen on the Ashokan pillars. That so fine a surface was achieved in granite, and never repeated after the Mauryas, is a standing puzzle in the history of Indian stone-working.
  - The Lomas Rishi facade imitates in stone a timber-and-thatch hut: a curved architrave with lattice infill and a frieze of elephants approaching a stupa. This is the earliest *chaitya* arch, and the ancestor of every later rock-cut facade from Bhaja and Karle to the Buddhist caves at Ajanta.
  - They were granted to the Ajivikas, the determinist sect of Makkhali Gosala who held that all events are fixed by *niyati*. Royal endowment of a sect that was neither Buddhist nor Jain nor Brahmanical is the best material evidence for Mauryan religious policy.
  - In several inscriptions the word Ajivika has been deliberately chiselled out - later sectarian erasure, and a rare physical trace of religious rivalry.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *History and Doctrines of the Ajivikas*; Upinder Singh, ch. 7; Thapar, *Early India*.
