- **Location**: Sarutaru, Kamrup district, Assam, on a low hillock in the Brahmaputra valley about 25 km from Guwahati - in the plain rather than the hills, unlike most north-eastern Neolithic sites.
- **Period/Level**: A Neolithic occupation broadly of the 2nd millennium BCE, excavated by T. C. Sharma. Its material connects it to the Daojali Hading group in the hills to the south.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Neolithic of the Brahmaputra plain, showing that the north-eastern tool tradition extended out of the hills onto the valley floor.
  - Shouldered celts and round-butted axes occur here as at Daojali Hading, together with cord-marked pottery, so the same technological province covers hill and plain.
  - The site produced wheel-made pottery in fine kaolin clay, which is unusual for a Neolithic context in the region and suggests either a later phase or contact with more developed ceramic traditions.
  - The Brahmaputra valley's archaeology is thin because the river's shifting course and the deep alluvium bury or destroy sites, so the few excavated localities carry disproportionate weight. Sarutaru and Daojali Hading together are essentially the whole excavated basis for the north-eastern Neolithic.
  - The region's later history - the Varman kings of Kamarupa at Pragjyotishpura, the Da-Parbatia doorframe at Tezpur - begins many centuries after these settlements, so the gap between prehistory and history in Assam is largely unfilled.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; Thapar, *Early India*; T. C. Sharma, *Prehistoric Archaeology of North-East India*.
