- **Location**: Chirand, Saran district, Bihar, on the north bank of the Ganga close to its confluence with the Ghaghara - in the fertile middle Ganga plain opposite Patna.
- **Period/Level**: One of the longest continuous sequences in the Ganga valley, running from the Neolithic of about 2500-1500 BCE through Chalcolithic, Iron Age and NBPW levels to the Kushana and Pala periods.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the middle Ganga Neolithic, and the possessor of a bone industry rivalled only by Burzahom.
  - Bone and antler tools were recovered in great quantity - points, arrowheads, needles, borers, scrapers and ornaments, many finely polished. That two sites at opposite ends of northern India, Chirand and Burzahom, both specialise in bone while most Indian Neolithic sites do not, is a standing question in the literature.
  - The crop package is unusually full: rice, wheat, barley, lentil and moong together, showing summer and winter cropping in the same settlement. That combination is the strongest evidence for a developed agricultural regime in the Ganga plain by the later 3rd millennium.
  - Terracotta figurines of humped bulls, birds and snakes, along with beads of semi-precious stone and steatite, indicate craft production; the pottery includes red, grey, black and black-and-red wares, some with painted linear and geometric designs in ochre.
  - The continuity of occupation into the historic period makes Chirand valuable for tracing how Neolithic villages of the region became the settlements of the mahajanapada age.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
