- **Location**: Gumla, Dera Ismail Khan district, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, in the Gomal valley where the river leaves the Sulaiman range for the Indus plain - the pass route from Afghanistan into the middle Indus.
- **Period/Level**: Six periods excavated by A. H. Dani. Period I is aceramic, Period II Neolithic, Periods III-IV Early Harappan with Kot Dijian pottery, Period V shows Mature Harappan contact and Period VI belongs to the Gandhara Grave Culture of the early 1st millennium BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A long sequence on one of the main routes between Central Asia and the Indus, covering the whole span from first settlement to the Iron Age.
  - Period II has pit dwellings and hand-made pottery; Period III-IV bring mud-brick architecture, the wheel-made Kot Dijian ware with its distinctive horizontal bands and fish-scale motifs, terracotta figurines and copper.
  - A burnt layer separates the Early Harappan from the following phase, and was once read as a destruction; as at Rana Ghundai, that interpretation is no longer accepted without corroborating evidence.
  - Gumla's value is in showing the Gomal valley as an active corridor. Material from Afghanistan and Central Asia reached the Indus this way, and the Gandhara Grave Culture levels at the top mark the later movement of people associated in some accounts with Indo-Aryan speakers - a connection to be stated cautiously.
  - It is read with Rehman Dheri, a few kilometres away, which gives the same valley's urban phase.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; A. H. Dani, *Ancient Pakistan*.
