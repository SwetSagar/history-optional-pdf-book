- **Location**: Burudih, Singhbhum district, Jharkhand, on the Chhotanagpur plateau in the Subarnarekha basin - lateritic upland country rich in copper and iron ore.
- **Period/Level**: A Neolithic settlement of the eastern Indian tradition, with material that also includes wheel-made pottery and iron slag from the same levels - a compression that has complicated its dating.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A site that illustrates both the eastern Indian Neolithic and the interpretive problem of shallow, mixed deposits.
  - Neolithic celts and microliths occur together with wheel-made pottery and iron slag in what excavators recorded as a single horizon. Either the site was occupied over a long period with little accumulation, or later material has worked down into earlier levels. Thin stratigraphy of this kind is common on the plateau and makes eastern Indian prehistory harder to date than the Ganga plain's.
  - The honest reading is that Burudih shows a Neolithic community in a region where stone tool use persisted alongside metal, rather than a neat sequence of ages. The three-age scheme fits the plateau poorly.
  - Singhbhum's copper ores were exploited from the Chalcolithic period - the copper hoards of the Ganga-Yamuna doab are argued to draw on this belt - so the district connects to the wider question of where early Indian metal came from.
  - The surrounding region remains home to Munda-speaking communities, which is why plateau prehistory features in discussions of Austroasiatic settlement.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
