- **Location**: Gufkral, Pulwama district, Kashmir, on a karewa terrace in the Jhelum valley about 41 km south of Srinagar. The name means "cave of the potter".
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by A. K. Sharma. Period IA is aceramic Neolithic, IB and IC ceramic Neolithic, Period II Megalithic and Period III Early Historic - a sequence running from before 3000 BCE into the historic period.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The Kashmir Neolithic with an aceramic phase - farming before pottery, as at Mehrgarh, which makes it the more informative of the two Kashmir sites.
  - Period IA has pit dwellings, storage pits and hearths but no pottery at all, with domesticated sheep, goat and cattle and cultivated wheat, barley and lentil. An aceramic farming phase shows that pottery is not a prerequisite for agriculture - a point the three-age scheme tends to obscure.
  - The pit dwellings repeat the Burzahom pattern, and the use of pits declines in the later Neolithic as above-ground mud-brick structures take over, so the transition can be watched in one stratigraphy.
  - Bone and antler tools, steatite beads and terracotta objects were recovered, with handmade pottery in IB giving way to some wheel-made ware later.
  - The crop package - wheat, barley and lentil - is West Asian, while the pit dwellings are Central Asian and Chinese in affinity. Kashmir's Neolithic drew on both, which is what makes the valley's prehistory distinct from the rest of India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
