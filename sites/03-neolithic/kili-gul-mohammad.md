- **Location**: Kili Gul Mohammad, in the Quetta valley of Balochistan, Pakistan, below the Bolan pass. A duplicate record exists in the missed-PYQ chapter under the spelling *Kile Gile Mohammed*, and the two should be merged.
- **Period/Level**: The type-site for the Quetta valley sequence, excavated by Walter Fairservis in the 1950s. Period I is aceramic Neolithic from about the 5th millennium BCE; Periods II-IV carry the successive painted wares - Kili Gul Mohammad, Kechi Beg and Damb Sadaat - running to roughly 2500 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The ceramic sequence by which the Baluchistan Chalcolithic is dated - the chronological framework for everything preceding the Harappans in the north-west.
  - Period I has mud-brick houses, blade tools, bone points and domesticated sheep, goat and cattle, but no pottery. Aceramic farming horizons are rare in South Asia and occur only here, at Mehrgarh and at Gufkral in Kashmir.
  - The painted wares of the later periods - black-on-red geometric designs, then the finer Kechi Beg and Damb Sadaat types - give the relative chronology used to correlate sites across Balochistan, Sindh and the Indo-Iranian borderlands.
  - Read with Mehrgarh, a short distance away and far older, the Quetta valley shows the long local development from early village farming to the regional cultures that fed into the Harappan civilisation. It is the standard evidence against deriving the Harappans from a West Asian migration.
  - The valley's position at the head of the Bolan explains the Iranian and Central Asian contacts visible in its material.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Fairservis, *Excavations in the Quetta Valley*.
