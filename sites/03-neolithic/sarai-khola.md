- **Location**: Sarai Khola, in the Taxila valley of Punjab, Pakistan, on the Potwar plateau close to the later city sites of Bhir Mound and Sirkap.
- **Period/Level**: Four periods excavated by the Pakistan Department of Archaeology. Period I is Neolithic, from about the 4th millennium BCE; Period II is Early Harappan with Kot Dijian pottery; Period III belongs to the Gandhara Grave Culture; Period IV is early historic.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The only site that carries the Taxila region from its Neolithic beginnings through to the historical city - a four-thousand-year sequence in one mound.
  - Period I has hand-made plain red and brown burnished pottery, ground and polished stone celts, blades, microliths and a large number of bone points, with pit dwellings. Its affinities are with the Kashmir Neolithic at Burzahom rather than with Balochistan, which places the Potwar plateau in a northern rather than a western cultural sphere.
  - Period II shows the transition from pit dwellings to mud-brick houses and the arrival of wheel-made Kot Dijian pottery - a technological and architectural shift visible in a single stratigraphy.
  - Terracotta wheels and toy carts from the site are among the earlier evidence for wheeled transport in the subcontinent.
  - The Period III graves, with their crouched and cremated burials, belong to the Gandhara Grave Culture of the early 1st millennium BCE, and the sequence is therefore also used in discussions of Indo-Aryan movement into the north-west.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; A. H. Dani, *Ancient Pakistan*.
