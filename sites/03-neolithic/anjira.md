- **Location**: Anjira, in the Surab valley of Kalat district, Balochistan, Pakistan - an upland valley in the Jhalawan region between Quetta and the Makran.
- **Period/Level**: A Chalcolithic settlement of roughly the 4th and early 3rd millennia BCE, excavated by Beatrice de Cardi in 1957. Four periods were distinguished, giving the Kalat-Surab region its ceramic sequence.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The reference sequence for central Balochistan, and a demonstration that prehistoric Balochistan was a mosaic of regional cultures rather than one.
  - Anjira ware and the associated Togau ware, with their distinctive friezes of stylised animals reduced almost to abstraction, define a ceramic province separate from the Amri-Nal complex of the south and the Quetta valley wares of the north.
  - De Cardi's work is important methodologically: small, carefully stratified soundings at several sites, rather than one large excavation, are what built the Balochistan framework, and much of what is known of the region still rests on that pattern.
  - The settlements are small stone-and-mud-brick villages in upland valleys, farming with the help of *gabarband* check dams that trapped silt and runoff - an adaptation to a landscape too dry for unaided agriculture.
  - Balochistan matters in the syllabus as the zone through which the Iranian plateau and the Indus were connected, and as the setting in which the Early Harappan regional cultures developed.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; de Cardi, *Excavations at Bampur*.
