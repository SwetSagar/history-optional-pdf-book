- **Location**: Golbai Sasan, Khordha district, Odisha, on the bank of the Mandakini river in the coastal plain south of Bhubaneswar - between the Eastern Ghats and the Chilika lagoon.
- **Period/Level**: Three periods excavated by the Odisha state archaeology department: Period I Neolithic from about 2300 BCE, Period II Chalcolithic from about 1700 BCE, and Period III an Iron Age phase from about 900 BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The reference sequence for coastal Odisha, and one of the few Indian sites where Neolithic, Chalcolithic and Iron Age levels follow each other without a break.
  - Period I has ground stone celts and bone tools with handmade red and grey pottery; Period II introduces wheel-made ware and copper objects - a chisel, a bangle, a fish-hook and a ring; Period III brings iron.
  - The bone tool industry is substantial, and includes points and awls comparable to those from Chirand, tying coastal Odisha to the eastern Indian pattern.
  - Some of the polished stone tools have been interpreted as adzes and gouges suited to woodworking and boat building, and this has been used to argue that the maritime tradition later visible in Odisha's Southeast Asian trade - the *Bali Jatra* memory of voyages to Java and Bali - has prehistoric roots. The inference is plausible but not demonstrated, and should be given as such.
  - The site's position near Sisupalgarh and Dhauli means the region's record continues unbroken from village farming to Kharavela's Kalinga.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; Thapar, *Early India*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
