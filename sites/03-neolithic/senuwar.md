- **Location**: Senuwar, Rohtas district, Bihar, in the Son valley at the foot of the Kaimur hills - on the southern edge of the middle Ganga plain, near Sasaram.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by B. P. Singh of Banaras Hindu University. Period IA is Neolithic, from about 2200 BCE; IB adds more crops and better pottery; Period II is Chalcolithic, c. 1300-700 BCE; Period III Iron Age with NBPW.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The best-dated and most thoroughly sampled Neolithic sequence in the middle Ganga plain - the reference against which the disputed Belan valley dates are judged.
  - The archaeobotany is the site's chief contribution. Period IA yielded rice, barley and field pea; IB adds wheat, lentil, and later millets, sesame and horsegram. Rice cultivated alongside the West Asian winter cereals in one village shows the two agricultural traditions meeting in the Ganga plain rather than remaining separate.
  - Because the samples were systematically floated and the levels radiocarbon dated, Senuwar's chronology is secure where the earlier claims from Koldihwa and Mahagara are not. It is the site to cite when the origins of Indian rice cultivation are at issue.
  - Circular and rectangular houses, ground stone celts, bone tools, microliths, and red, black-and-red and burnished grey wares make up the material record.
  - Its position at the junction of the Vindhyan uplands and the plain explains the mix: Vindhyan stone, Gangetic rice and West Asian cereals all present in one settlement.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; B. P. Singh, *Early Farming Communities of the Kaimur*.
