- **Location**: Sohr Damb, near the village of Nal in Khuzdar district, Balochistan, Pakistan - in a valley of the Jhalawan uplands between the Indus plain and the Makran coast.
- **Period/Level**: A Chalcolithic settlement of roughly the 4th and 3rd millennia BCE, first excavated by Harold Hargreaves in 1925 and re-investigated more recently by a German team. It gives its name to the Nal ware that defines, with Amri, the Amri-Nal cultural complex.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The source of the finest painted pottery in South Asian prehistory.
  - Nal ware is polychrome - a rarity in the subcontinent. A cream or buff slip carries designs outlined in black and then filled with red, blue, yellow and green, in geometric panels and with naturalistic ibex, fish, birds and bulls. Nothing else in Indian prehistory is painted in this many colours, and the technique required firing in stages.
  - Burials are fractional: bones were defleshed or exposed and the collected remains placed in pots, sometimes with rich grave goods. One child burial included a necklace and a crystal pendant. Fractional burial distinguishes this tradition from the extended inhumations of the Harappans.
  - Beads of agate, carnelian, crystal and lapis lazuli show the site drawing on Badakhshan and Gujarat sources.
  - Embankments and channels at the site have been read as water management, appropriate to the *gabarband* tradition of Balochistan, where check dams trapped silt and runoff for cultivation in a dry landscape.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Hargreaves, *Excavations in Baluchistan*.
