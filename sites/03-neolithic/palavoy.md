- **Location**: Palavoy, in the Anantapur region of Andhra Pradesh, on the dry Rayalaseema plateau in the Penna basin - the eastern part of the southern Deccan ashmound belt.
- **Period/Level**: A South Indian Neolithic site with four ashmounds, and a later Megalithic phase. It belongs to the same cultural horizon as Utnur, Piklihal, Kupgal and Budihal, broadly the 3rd and 2nd millennia BCE.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: An ashmound cluster, and the evidence that these were regional gathering places rather than single-village features.
  - Four ashmounds at one locality is unusual. Most sites have one, and the concentration here has been read as marking a regional centre where herds from a wider area were brought together - for seasonal grazing, for exchange, or for the collective ceremonies that the dung burning seems to represent.
  - That reading bears on the long-running question of whether the ashmounds are functional or ritual. The evidence from Utnur and Budihal shows they are physically the residue of cattle pens; the scale and repetition at sites like Palavoy suggest the burning was also ceremonial. Both can be true, and the sensible answer holds them together rather than choosing.
  - The Megalithic remains above show the same landscape reused by Iron Age communities, as at Maski and Brahmagiri.
  - The ashmound belt runs across Karnataka, Telangana and Rayalaseema, and its distribution maps the extent of the southern Neolithic cattle economy - the single most distinctive feature of prehistoric peninsular India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
