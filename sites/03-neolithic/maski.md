- **Location**: Maski, Raichur district, Karnataka, on the Maski river, a tributary of the Tungabhadra, in the Raichur doab - the granite and ashmound country of the southern Deccan.
- **Period/Level**: A long sequence from the Palaeolithic through the South Indian Neolithic and Chalcolithic to the Megalithic Iron Age and early historic periods. Ashoka's Minor Rock Edict I is inscribed on a rock at the site, discovered in 1915 by C. Beadon.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The inscription that identified Ashoka - the single most consequential epigraphic discovery for Mauryan history.
  - All the other edicts call their author *Devanampiya Piyadasi*, beloved of the gods, of pleasing appearance - a title, not a name. The Maski edict is the first found that uses the personal name, reading *Devanampiyasa Asokasa*, and so proved that the Piyadasi of the inscriptions was the Ashoka of the Buddhist chronicles. Without it, the edicts and the literary tradition could not have been securely joined.
  - Later finds at Gujarra, Nettur and Udegolam confirmed the identification, but Maski was first and is the one to cite.
  - The archaeology is substantial in its own right: polished stone tools, microlithic blades, a copper rod, dull red and pinkish-buff wares, black-and-red pottery with incised designs, and beads of carnelian, agate, amethyst, chalcedony, shell, coral and glass.
  - The megalithic phase has pit burials and stone alignments not associated with burials, and rock paintings in the vicinity show horse riders carrying metal axes - imagery belonging to the Iron Age rather than the Neolithic.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 7; Thapar, *Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas*; ASI.
