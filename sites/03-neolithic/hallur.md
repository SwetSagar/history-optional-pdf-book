- **Location**: Hallur, Haveri district, Karnataka, on the bank of the Tungabhadra in the northern Karnataka plain - at the western edge of the southern Deccan Neolithic province.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by M. S. Nagaraja Rao. Period I is Neolithic-Chalcolithic, from about 2000 BCE; Period II is an overlap phase in which iron appears, with dates around 1100 BCE; the sequence continues into the Megalithic.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the earliest dates for iron in peninsular India, and the site that established millet farming in the southern Neolithic.
  - Iron objects - arrowheads, spearheads and blades - occur in levels dated to about 1100 BCE, earlier than the conventional 1000 BCE threshold for the Indian Iron Age. Hallur is a principal citation in the argument that iron working began in the peninsula earlier than the Gangetic sequence implies, and that it was not introduced from the north.
  - The plant remains are equally important: finger millet (*ragi*) and horsegram, with browntop millet. Ragi is of African origin, and its presence here in the 2nd millennium BCE is evidence of contact across the Indian Ocean long before the Roman trade - one of the more striking claims in Indian archaeobotany, and one to state carefully as an inference from crop origins.
  - Round wattle-and-daub huts with floors of stone chips and river sand, black-quartzite blades, small copper axes and fish-hooks make up the material record.
  - Cattle bones dominate the fauna, consistent with the pastoral emphasis seen across the ashmound belt.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Thapar, *Early India*.
