- **Location**: Sohgaura, Gorakhpur district, Uttar Pradesh, on the bank of the Rapti in the eastern Ganga plain - in the territory of the ancient Malla and Kosala janapadas.
- **Period/Level**: A mound with early occupation, but known almost entirely for the Sohgaura copper plate: a small bronze plaque bearing four lines in Prakrit in Brahmi, generally assigned to the Mauryan period and argued by some to be pre-Ashokan.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the two earliest Brahmi records in India, and the oldest documentary evidence of state famine relief anywhere in the subcontinent.
  - The plate is an order issued by the *mahamatras* of Shravasti concerning storehouses at Manavasiti and other places. It directs that the stores of grain be used in an emergency and not withheld - an administrative instruction about relief in time of drought, not a royal eulogy.
  - Its significance is that it shows the Mauryan state in ordinary operation. Almost everything else known of Mauryan administration comes from Ashoka's edicts or from the *Arthashastra*; here is a working order about granaries, comparable in kind to the Mahasthan inscription from Bengal.
  - The plate carries symbols above the text, including what appear to be storehouses on posts, a tree in railing and a taurine mark - pictorial administrative notation, unusual in Indian epigraphy.
  - Whether it predates Ashoka is disputed. If it does, it would show Brahmi in official use before his reign and weaken the argument that Ashoka's chancery introduced writing to India. Present the dating as contested.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 7; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; D. C. Sircar, *Select Inscriptions*.
