- **Location**: Tekkalakote, Ballari district, Karnataka, among granite hills near the Tungabhadra in the Raichur doab - about 40 km from Hampi, in the ashmound and rock art country. A duplicate record exists in the rock-cut caves chapter under the spelling *Tekkalakota*.
- **Period/Level**: A South Indian Neolithic settlement of roughly 2000-1200 BCE with a Chalcolithic overlap, excavated in the 1960s by M. S. Nagaraja Rao and K. C. Malhotra.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A Neolithic hill settlement that produced gold - the earliest worked gold in the peninsula.
  - Gold objects were recovered from the Neolithic levels. The southern Deccan holds India's principal gold sources at Kolar and Hutti, both within reach of this site, and Tekkalakote is the evidence that they were being exploited in the 2nd millennium BCE. Lead-isotope studies matching gold from northern sites to southern ores rest on this kind of association.
  - The settlement occupies granite terraces with circular huts of mud and post construction, ground stone axes, blade tools and burnished grey and buff pottery, with copper and bronze in the later levels.
  - Burials occur under house floors, in pits and urns, with grave goods - the pattern also seen at Brahmagiri and Piklihal, in which the dead remain within the settlement.
  - Rock paintings and bruisings on the same outcrops show humped cattle and human figures. Because the habitation and the art are on the same hills and broadly contemporary, the paintings can be tied to an identified community, which is rare for Indian rock art.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Nagaraja Rao, *Excavations at Tekkalakota*.
