- **Location**: Mehrgarh, on the Kachi plain of Balochistan, Pakistan, where the Bolan river leaves the hills for the Indus lowland - at the foot of the pass that is the second great route from Iran into South Asia.
- **Period/Level**: Occupied from about 7000 BCE to 2600 BCE, excavated by Jean-Francois Jarrige from 1974. Period I is aceramic Neolithic; Periods II-III add pottery and copper; Periods IV-VII run into the Early Harappan, after which the population shifts to Nausharo nearby.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The earliest farming settlement in South Asia, and the site that settled the question of where Indian agriculture came from.
  - Period I has mud-brick houses divided into small rectangular compartments, domesticated barley and wheat, and, in its later phases, domesticated cattle, sheep and goat - with the evidence showing zebu cattle domesticated locally rather than imported. Farming developing in the Indus borderlands undercuts any account of the Harappan civilisation as a West Asian transplant, and this is the single most important point in the chapter.
  - Burials are in flexed position in oval pits with grave goods - shell, turquoise and lapis lazuli beads, and in some cases young goats - showing long-distance material reaching a Neolithic village from Badakhshan and the coast.
  - A cotton thread preserved on a copper bead from the 6th millennium BCE is the earliest cotton anywhere in the world.
  - Human molars with neat cylindrical drill holes, dated to about 7000 BCE, are the earliest evidence of dentistry known.
  - Later periods add wheel-made pottery, terracotta female and humped-bull figurines, and compartmented structures read as granaries - the technological package that the Harappans inherit.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Jarrige, *Mehrgarh: Field Reports*.
