A site in Pakistan spanning the Neolithic, Chalcolithic and Early Harappan periods, and one of the earliest to yield evidence of agriculture and animal domestication, with barley and wheat as the main crops.

- In Periods I and II, the Neolithic, houses were built of handmade mud bricks with rectangular rooms, and granaries have been identified; the transition from hunting to animal domestication is recorded here.
- Burials were in oval pits with the body bent, with grave goods such as lapis lazuli, goats and beads, and secondary burials had the bones covered in red ochre.
- Butchery, cooking and hide processing are attested, and pottery is both handmade and wheel-made.
- Period III, the Chalcolithic, shows a significant increase in craft activity and refined pottery, with necklaces and bracelets of agate, jasper, chalcedony and steatite as grave goods, terracotta humped bulls, and crucibles bearing traces of copper.
- The Early Harappan levels yielded triangular terracotta cakes, long flint blades and perforated jars.
