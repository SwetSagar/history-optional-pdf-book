- **Location**: Rehman Dheri, Dera Ismail Khan district, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, on the west bank of the Indus near the mouth of the Gomal valley.
- **Period/Level**: An Early Harappan town of about 3300-2100 BCE, excavated by Farzand Ali Durrani. Three periods; the settlement covers roughly 22 hectares and is enclosed by a boundary wall.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the earliest planned settlements in South Asia - laid out on a grid several centuries before the Mature Harappan cities.
  - The town is rectangular, oriented broadly north-south, divided by a central street with subsidiary lanes at right angles and houses in regular blocks, the whole within a wall. Because this precedes Mohenjo-daro and Harappa by centuries, it shows that town planning was an Early Harappan development that the cities inherited rather than invented - the key point the site is cited for.
  - Kot Dijian pottery with graffiti and potter's marks was found in quantity, along with beads of lapis lazuli and turquoise brought from Afghanistan and Iran.
  - A bone or ivory seal with incised signs, including what appear to be a scorpion and other motifs, is among the earliest seals from the region and is used in discussions of the origins of the Indus script.
  - The site was never built over by a Mature Harappan town, so its Early Harappan plan survives uncomplicated - the reason it is more legible than Kot Diji or Kalibangan for this period.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; Thapar, *Early India*; Durrani, *Excavations at Rehman Dheri*.
