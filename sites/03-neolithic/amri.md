- **Location**: Amri, Dadu district, Sindh, Pakistan, on the west bank of the Indus below the Kirthar range - on the route between the Indus plain and the Baluchistan uplands.
- **Period/Level**: Four periods, excavated by N. G. Majumdar in the 1920s and by Jean-Marie Casal in the 1960s. Period I is the pre-Harappan Amrian culture, c. 3600-3000 BCE; Period II transitional; Period III Mature Harappan; Period IV the post-urban Jhukar phase.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the Amri-Nal culture, and one of the few places where the whole sequence from pre-Harappan to post-Harappan is stratified in one mound.
  - Period I has mud-brick houses with small cellular compartments, usually interpreted as storage or granary bases, along with chert blades and stone balls. The compartmented plan anticipates Harappan practice.
  - Amrian pottery is fine buff ware, largely wheel-made, painted in black and brown with geometric designs - chevrons, chequers, hatched triangles - and with humped bulls and ibex. It defines, with Nal ware, the Amri-Nal complex of Sindh and southern Balochistan.
  - The value of the sequence is that it shows the Harappan civilisation arriving at Amri as a cultural transformation of an existing settlement rather than as a foundation on empty ground. That is the standard argument against invasion or migration accounts of Harappan origins.
  - Period IV's Jhukar material, with distinctive pottery and seals, documents what happened after the cities declined - continuity of population with loss of urban form.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Casal, *Fouilles d'Amri*.
