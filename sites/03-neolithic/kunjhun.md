- **Location**: Kunjhun, in the Son valley of Sidhi district, Madhya Pradesh, on a tributary of the Son below the Kaimur escarpment - the Vindhyan uplands south of the Ganga plain.
- **Period/Level**: A prehistoric locality in the Son valley sequence, with Mesolithic microlithic and later Neolithic material. The valley was investigated by the joint Allahabad University and University of California project under G. R. Sharma and J. Desmond Clark.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Part of the Son valley sequence - one of the few places in India where Palaeolithic, Mesolithic and Neolithic occupation can be traced along a single river with dated geological deposits.
  - The Son valley project's importance is methodological. By tying artefact horizons to river terraces, volcanic ash layers and pollen records, it established a framework for the Indian Stone Age that rested on geology rather than on typology alone.
  - The volcanic ash found in the valley deposits has been identified with the Toba eruption of about 74,000 years ago, giving Indian prehistory a rare absolute marker and a way to test whether human occupation continued across that event.
  - Kunjhun's microlithic and Neolithic material places it in the later part of that sequence, in the same Vindhyan cultural province as Baghor and the Belan valley sites at Chopani Mando, Koldihwa and Mahagara.
  - The Vindhyan belt is also where the great painted rock shelters occur, so settlement evidence and rock art can be studied together in one region.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2 and 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Sharma and Clark, *Palaeoenvironments and Prehistory in the Middle Son Valley*.
