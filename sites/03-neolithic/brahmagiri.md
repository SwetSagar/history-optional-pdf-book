- **Location**: Brahmagiri, Chitradurga district, Karnataka, on a granite hill in the dry southern Deccan - in the Maski-Raichur belt where the Mauryan frontier met the peninsula.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Mortimer Wheeler in 1947. Three periods: I, the South Indian Neolithic or Stone Axe Culture, c. 2000 BCE; II, the Megalithic Iron Age, from about 1000 BCE; III, the early historic Andhra or Satavahana phase. Ashokan Minor Rock Edicts I and II are inscribed on a boulder at the site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The excavation that established the chronological framework for the whole of south Indian archaeology.
  - Wheeler's three-period sequence gave the south its first stratigraphic chronology, and the terms he defined - Stone Axe Culture, Megalithic, Andhra - organised the field for decades. Whatever has since been revised, Brahmagiri is where south Indian prehistory acquired a spine.
  - Period I has wattle-and-daub huts on stone-chip floors, ground and polished stone axes, blade microliths and burnished grey pottery, with adults buried extended and children in urns beneath house floors.
  - Period II brings iron weapons and implements, black-and-red ware with graffiti, shell bangles and beads of terracotta, shell and jasper, with stone cist burials - the classic megalithic package.
  - The Ashokan edicts are addressed to the *mahamatras* of Isila from the prince at Suvarnagiri and mark the southernmost extent of Mauryan authority. A site with both a Neolithic village and an Ashokan edict lets prehistory and the Mauryan empire be taught from one place.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 7; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Wheeler, *Ancient India* 4.
