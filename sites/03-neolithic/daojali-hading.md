- **Location**: Daojali Hading, Dima Hasao district - the former North Cachar Hills - of Assam, on a low hill above the Langting valley in the hill country between the Brahmaputra plain and Manipur.
- **Period/Level**: A Neolithic settlement generally placed around 2700-2000 BCE, excavated by T. C. Sharma of Gauhati University in the 1960s. It is the type-site of the north-eastern Neolithic.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site that placed the north-east in a Southeast Asian rather than an Indian cultural province.
  - The tool kit is dominated by shouldered celts and rounded-butt axes - forms characteristic of southern China, Burma and Southeast Asia and absent from the Indian Neolithic traditions of Kashmir, the Ganga plain and the peninsula. That typology is the basis for treating the north-east as the western end of a Southeast Asian world.
  - Cord-marked and stamped pottery, made by beating the clay with a carved paddle, reinforces the link: the technique is standard across Southeast Asia and rare elsewhere in India.
  - Grinding stones, mortars and pestles indicate plant food processing, and jadeite among the stone objects points to contact with sources in Burma or China - long-distance exchange reaching the Assam hills.
  - No direct evidence of cereal agriculture was recovered, and it has been argued that the region's first cultivation was of roots, tubers and tree crops under *jhum* shifting cultivation - which would leave little archaeological trace. That possibility is worth stating, since it bears on how many independent agricultural origins South Asia had.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; Thapar, *Early India*; T. C. Sharma, *Prehistoric Archaeology of North-East India*.
