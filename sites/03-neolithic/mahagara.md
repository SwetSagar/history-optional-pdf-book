- **Location**: Mahagara, Prayagraj district, Uttar Pradesh, in the Belan valley where the Vindhyan plateau meets the Ganga plain - a few kilometres from Koldihwa and Chopani Mando.
- **Period/Level**: A Neolithic village excavated by G. R. Sharma of Allahabad University. Radiocarbon determinations cluster around 2000-1600 BCE, though the site is bound up with a much-disputed claim for far earlier dates in the valley.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The clearest direct evidence of cattle penning in the Indian Neolithic, and the centre of the longest-running controversy in Indian prehistory.
  - An enclosure ringed by post-holes contained a compacted surface covered with cattle hoof impressions - a pen whose occupants left their prints in the mud. Direct physical evidence of animal management of this kind is exceptionally rare.
  - The site has round huts with post-holes, ground stone celts, microliths, bone tools and handmade cord-impressed pottery, with rice husk used as temper in the clay.
  - Sharma's excavations at neighbouring Koldihwa produced rice-tempered sherds dated to the 7th millennium BCE, which would have made the Belan valley the earliest rice-farming region in the world. The claim has not survived scrutiny: the association between the dated charcoal and the rice levels is insecure, and most specialists now reject the early chronology. Lahuradewa has since taken over as the better-founded case.
  - Present Mahagara as good evidence for Neolithic pastoralism and as a cautionary tale about dating - both are examinable.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; G. R. Sharma, *Beginnings of Agriculture*.
