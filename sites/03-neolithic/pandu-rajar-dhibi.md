- **Location**: Pandu Rajar Dhibi, Barddhaman district, West Bengal, on the bank of the Ajay river in the Rarh plain west of the Bhagirathi - the name means "the mound of King Pandu".
- **Period/Level**: A Chalcolithic settlement excavated by P. C. Dasgupta from 1962, with four periods running from roughly 1500 BCE into the early historic era. Period I is Chalcolithic; Period II adds iron.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the Bengal Chalcolithic, and the first excavation to establish that Bengal had a pre-iron settled culture at all.
  - Before this excavation, Bengal's archaeological record effectively began with the historic period. Pandu Rajar Dhibi pushed it back more than a thousand years and created the Chalcolithic framework into which Mahishadal, Mangalkot and Bharatpur were later fitted.
  - Period I yielded microliths, ground stone tools, bone tools, and black-and-red ware with painted designs; Period II added beads of semi-precious stone, terracotta figurines, copper and then iron with slag and ovens.
  - Burials were found in the habitation area, extended and with grave goods, and the excavator argued from the bead and copper material that the settlement traded over long distances - an interpretation that has been questioned as over-reading a modest assemblage.
  - The site's identification with the Mahabharata is folk etymology and carries no historical weight; it is worth naming as an example of how epic names attach themselves to mounds across northern India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
