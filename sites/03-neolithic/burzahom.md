A Neolithic and megalithic site in Jammu and Kashmir with four periods from 2920 BCE.

- Its inhabitants lived in mud-plastered pit dwellings, round or oval and wider towards the base, with square or rectangular pits towards the centre; charcoal, ash, potsherds and stone hearths were recovered.
- A well-developed bone tool industry produced harpoons, needles and awls.
- Burials were primary with the body flexed, as well as secondary, with inhumation also attested; humans were buried alongside wild and domesticated animals including deer, ibex, wolf, nilgai, dog and sheep.
- The megalithic remains comprise massive menhirs and a large stone circle, with coarse red ware pottery.
