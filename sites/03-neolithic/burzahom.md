- **Location**: Burzahom, about 16 km from Srinagar, Kashmir, on a *karewa* - a raised lacustrine terrace - overlooking the Jhelum valley and the Dal lake.
- **Period/Level**: Four periods, from about 2900 BCE to the early historic era. Period I and II are Neolithic, Period III Megalithic, Period IV Early Historic. Excavated by the ASI from the 1960s.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The most distinctive Neolithic in India - pit dwellings, a great bone industry, and burial practices found nowhere else in the subcontinent.
  - Period I people lived in pits dug into the karewa, narrow at the mouth and widening towards the base, up to 4 m deep, with steps cut into the side, mud-plastered walls and hearths both inside and outside. Pit dwellings are unknown elsewhere in India but common in the Neolithic of northern China and Central Asia, and they are the main evidence for Kashmir's trans-Himalayan connections.
  - The bone and antler industry - harpoons, needles, awls, points and daggers - is the richest of any Indian Neolithic site, matched only at Chirand.
  - Dogs were buried with their masters, and wild animals including ibex and wolf were given separate burials. Animal burial of this kind occurs nowhere else in Indian prehistory.
  - A stone slab engraved with a hunting scene shows two bright objects in the sky, which some read as a record of a supernova. It is a speculative reading and should be given as such.
  - A Harappan-type pot containing 950 agate and carnelian beads shows contact with the Indus world, and Period III menhirs mark a megalithic phase.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
