- **Location**: Lahuradewa, Sant Kabir Nagar district, eastern Uttar Pradesh, on the bank of an ox-bow lake in the middle Ganga plain north of the river - the record is filed under the spelling *Lahrudeva*.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Rakesh Tewari for the Uttar Pradesh State Archaeology Department from the late 1990s. Period IA yielded pottery and rice from the 7th millennium BCE, with later periods running through the Chalcolithic and into the NBPW phase.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The strongest current claim for very early rice in India, and the site that reopened the question of an independent Gangetic agricultural origin.
  - Radiocarbon dates of roughly 6400-6000 BCE were obtained for levels containing rice, supported by a sediment core from the adjoining lake showing rice pollen and phytoliths from an even earlier date. Crucially, the evidence comes from lake cores as well as from the mound, which gives it a stratigraphic independence the earlier Belan valley claims lacked.
  - Whether the rice is fully domesticated or intensively exploited wild rice is still argued, and the honest position is that Lahuradewa demonstrates very early rice *use* in the Ganga plain while the domestication question remains open.
  - If the dates hold, the middle Ganga plain becomes an independent centre of early agriculture contemporary with Mehrgarh, which would make Indian farming a story of at least two separate origins rather than one spreading east from Balochistan.
  - The site also produced early handmade cord-impressed pottery, placing ceramic use in the region well before the Harappan period.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Tewari et al., *Pragdhara*.
