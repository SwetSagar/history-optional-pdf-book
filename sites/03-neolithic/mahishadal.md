- **Location**: Mahishadal, Purba Medinipur district, West Bengal, near the Rupnarayan river in the lower deltaic plain west of the Hooghly.
- **Period/Level**: A Chalcolithic settlement dated to roughly 1600-1400 BCE in its earlier phase, with a later level carrying iron. Excavated as part of the programme that followed Pandu Rajar Dhibi in establishing the Bengal Chalcolithic.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the small group of sites that define the Bengal Chalcolithic, and a good illustration of how early iron reached eastern India.
  - The assemblage includes microliths, bone tools, black-and-red ware, beads of steatite and semi-precious stone, terracotta bangles and a copper celt - a modest Chalcolithic kit consistent with a farming village rather than a centre.
  - Rice was the staple, as the plant remains and the deltaic setting both indicate, which distinguishes the Bengal Chalcolithic from the wheat-and-barley cultures of the north-west and aligns it with the middle Ganga plain.
  - Iron appears in the upper levels at a date that, with Malhar and Raja Nala ka Tila in eastern Uttar Pradesh, contributes to the argument that iron was worked in eastern India earlier than the conventional 1000 BCE - a live debate worth citing rather than settling.
  - Terracotta figurines and objects bearing incised symbolic marks have been recovered, and are used cautiously in discussions of pre-literate sign use.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 5; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
