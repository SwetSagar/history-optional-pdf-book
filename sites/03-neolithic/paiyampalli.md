- **Location**: Paiyampalli, Tirupattur district, northern Tamil Nadu, in the dry hill country near the Karnataka and Andhra borders - the southernmost extension of the Deccan Neolithic province.
- **Period/Level**: A South Indian Neolithic settlement with a Megalithic phase above it, broadly of the 2nd millennium BCE, excavated by the ASI. It is among the few excavated Neolithic sites in Tamil Nadu.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The southern limit of the Neolithic cattle-keeping culture, and a key site for the domestication of Indian pulses.
  - Horsegram and green gram (*mung*) were recovered from the Neolithic levels - among the earliest evidence for either crop in India. Both are indigenous South Asian domesticates, and Paiyampalli is a principal citation for the argument that peninsular India was an independent centre of crop domestication alongside the West Asian cereals of the north-west and the rice of the Ganga plain.
  - Cattle bones dominate the faunal assemblage, showing the same pastoral emphasis as the ashmound sites further north, though no ashmound occurs here.
  - Ground and polished stone axes, blade tools and burnished grey ware place it firmly in the southern Neolithic tradition rather than in any separate Tamil sequence.
  - The Megalithic levels above, with iron and black-and-red ware, connect the site to the Iron Age culture that spreads across Tamil Nadu and is the immediate background to the Sangam age.
  - Tamil Nadu's prehistory is otherwise dominated by the megaliths and by the Palaeolithic at Attirampakkam, so a Neolithic village here fills a real gap.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
