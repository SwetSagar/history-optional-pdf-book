- **Location**: Piklihal, Raichur district, Karnataka, on granite hills near the Krishna in the Raichur doab - in the same ashmound belt as Maski, Kupgal and Budihal.
- **Period/Level**: A South Indian Neolithic settlement dated from about 2700 BCE, excavated by F. R. Allchin, with a Megalithic Iron Age phase above. Allchin's work here and at Utnur established the southern Neolithic as a distinct culture.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the two sites on which the interpretation of the southern Neolithic as a cattle-keeping culture was built.
  - Allchin distinguished settlement areas from ashmound areas and argued that the ashmounds were the burnt residue of cattle pens - the thesis confirmed by his excavation at Utnur and refined later at Budihal.
  - The settlement has circular huts, microlithic blades, ground stone axes, and a range of pottery - handmade grey burnished ware, black, buff and red-brown wares - along with terracotta figurines of humans, cattle and birds.
  - Rock paintings and bruisings on the surrounding granite show humped cattle, human figures and, in later layers, horse riders carrying metal axes. The cattle imagery belongs with the Neolithic occupation; the riders belong to the Iron Age, and separating the two is essential when the art is used as evidence.
  - Iron appears early in the sequence here, contributing to the argument that the southern Deccan worked iron by the early 1st millennium BCE or before.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; F. R. Allchin, *Piklihal Excavations*.
