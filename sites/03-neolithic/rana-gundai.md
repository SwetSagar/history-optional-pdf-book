- **Location**: Rana Ghundai, Loralai district, north-eastern Balochistan, Pakistan - a mound in a valley of the Toba Kakar ranges on the route between the Zhob valley and the Indus.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by Brigadier E. J. Ross in 1946. Five phases, RG I to RG V, spanning roughly the 4th to the 2nd millennia BCE - the first stratified prehistoric sequence established anywhere in Balochistan.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The excavation that gave Balochistan its first chronology, and the origin of a long-running argument about destruction levels.
  - RG I produced bones of oxen, ass and sheep with no structures, read as a camp of semi-nomadic herders. RG II introduces pottery painted with friezes of humped bulls; RG III black-on-red designs with ibex and antelope. The bull and ibex friezes are the diagnostic motifs of the region.
  - Ross identified burnt layers between phases and interpreted them as destructions by invaders, an argument taken up by Mortimer Wheeler in support of his reading of the end of the Harappan civilisation. That interpretation has since been abandoned: burnt layers at village sites usually record accidental fire or rebuilding, and there is no supporting evidence of conflict.
  - The episode is worth citing as an example of how mid-twentieth-century archaeology reached for invasion to explain change in the material record, a habit that also produced the Aryan invasion reading of Mohenjo-daro.
  - The Zhob valley figurines from nearby sites belong to the same cultural province.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Ross, *A Chalcolithic Site in Northern Baluchistan*.
