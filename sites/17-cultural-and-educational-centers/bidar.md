- **Location**: Bidar, northern Karnataka, on a laterite plateau above the Manjra basin at the edge of the Deccan trap - chosen for its healthier climate and fertile land over the drier Gulbarga.
- **Period/Level**: Bahmani capital from c. 1425-32, when Ahmad Shah I Wali moved the seat from Gulbarga; then capital of the Barid Shahis after the Bahmani kingdom broke up, until Bijapur absorbed it in 1619 and the Mughals took it in 1656. The Mahmud Gawan madrasa was built in 1472.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The finest surviving Persianate architecture of the Deccan sultanates, and a medieval college whose ruin is itself a historical document.
  - The Mahmud Gawan madrasa was a three-storeyed college with lecture halls, a mosque, student quarters and a library said to hold three thousand manuscripts, faced in coloured tile mosaic in the Timurid manner of Samarkand and Khurasan. It is the clearest architectural evidence of direct Iranian and Central Asian contact with the Deccan.
  - Gawan, a Persian merchant who rose to be *wakil* of the kingdom, reformed the provincial *tarafs* and the revenue system. His execution in 1481 on forged evidence was the decisive episode in the feud between the *Afaqi* or foreign nobles and the *Dakhni* old guard - the factional split that broke the Bahmani state into the five Deccan sultanates.
  - Bidar fort, the Rangin Mahal with its mother-of-pearl inlay, the Solah Khamba mosque and the Bahmani and Barid tombs complete the ensemble.
  - The town gives its name to *bidri* ware, zinc alloy inlaid with silver - a craft that fuses Persian technique with Indian production.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* I; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I; ASI.
