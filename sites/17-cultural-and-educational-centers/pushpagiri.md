- **Location**: The Langudi and Assia hills of Jajpur district, Odisha, between the Birupa and the Brahmani in the northern Mahanadi delta - the three sites of Lalitgiri, Ratnagiri and Udayagiri, together called Odisha's Diamond Triangle.
- **Period/Level**: A Buddhist monastic complex active from about the 1st-3rd centuries CE to the 11th-12th, under the Bhaumakaras and Somavamshis. Usually identified with the *Pu-sie-p'o-k'i-li* that Xuanzang describes in the 7th century as a great monastery in Odra.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The principal Buddhist university of eastern India outside Bihar, and the centre where Vajrayana takes shape.
  - Lalitgiri is the oldest of the three, with an apsidal *chaitya* hall and stupas; a casket found in its main stupa held corporeal relics, claimed by the excavators as relics of the Buddha himself - among very few such finds in India.
  - Ratnagiri yielded large monasteries with elaborately carved doorways, hundreds of votive stupas and a mass of Vajrayana images - Tara, Heruka, Marichi and mandala figures. It is a principal source for the iconography of Tantric Buddhism, and for arguing that Vajrayana developed in Odisha as much as in Bengal and Bihar.
  - Udayagiri, the largest, has produced an inscribed seal reading *Madhavapura Mahavihara*, giving the establishment its own name in its own words.
  - Xuanzang's account, the monastic seals and the scale of the remains together show Odisha as a Buddhist centre long after Buddhism had weakened elsewhere in India, which is why it matters in questions on Buddhism's decline.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 9 and 10; Thapar, *Early India*; ASI, *Excavations at Ratnagiri*.
