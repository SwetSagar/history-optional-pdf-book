- **Location**: Udupi, coastal Karnataka, on the narrow Tulu Nadu plain between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, near the Swarna river.
- **Period/Level**: The Krishna Matha was founded by Madhvacharya, c. 1238-1317 CE, who established the Dvaita school of Vedanta here and set up the *Ashta Matha*, eight monasteries under his direct disciples. The Haridasa movement flourished from the 15th-16th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The seat of Dvaita Vedanta - the systematic dualist answer to Shankara's Advaita - and the source of the Kannada devotional tradition.
  - Madhva argued for an absolute and permanent distinction between the individual soul and Brahman, and for a fivefold difference (*pancha-bheda*) running through reality. Placed against Shankara's Advaita at Sringeri and Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita at Srirangam, the three mathas of the western Deccan and Tamil country give the full range of Vedantic positions in one region.
  - The eight mathas govern the temple by *paryaya*, each taking a two-year turn in rotation - a formalised sharing of authority that has worked without a single head for seven centuries, and a useful counter-example to the idea that Indian religious institutions were always hierarchical.
  - The image of Krishna is worshipped through the *Navagraha Kindi*, a nine-holed window. Tradition holds the wall opened for Kanakadasa, a saint of low caste refused entry at the door. The *Kanakana Kindi* is the standard illustration of bhakti's confrontation with caste exclusion, and of the compromise that followed.
  - Purandaradasa, called the father of Carnatic music, and Kanakadasa belong to this Haridasa tradition of Kannada *kirtanas*.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; B. N. K. Sharma, *History of the Dvaita School of Vedanta*.
