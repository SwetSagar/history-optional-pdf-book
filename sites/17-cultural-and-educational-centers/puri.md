- **Location**: Puri, Odisha, on the Bay of Bengal coast south of the Mahanadi delta - a shore town built around its temple, with the processional Bada Danda running from the shrine to the Gundicha temple.
- **Period/Level**: The Govardhana matha is assigned to Adi Shankara in the 8th century. The Jagannath temple was built by the Eastern Gangas from c. 1135; Puri's importance as a devotional centre peaks in the 16th century, when Chaitanya Mahaprabhu settled here from 1510 until his death in 1533.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the Char Dham, Shankara's eastern seat, and the place where the Jagannath cult became the ideology of a state. This entry covers the matha and the town; for the temple architecture see the Jagannath and Konark entry in the temple chapter.
  - The *purvamnaya* matha holds the Rig Veda and the *mahavakya* "Prajnanam Brahma". That the Advaita tradition planted a seat beside a cult as distinctly regional as Jagannath's is itself evidence of how Shankara's network absorbed local religion rather than displacing it.
  - From Anangabhima III the Eastern Gangas ruled formally as deputies of Jagannath, and the Gajapatis after them - kingship exercised on a god's behalf, the clearest Indian instance of a state deity.
  - Chaitanya's long residence made Puri the meeting point of Bengali Gaudiya Vaishnavism and Odia devotion, and it drew the *Panchasakha* poets who wrote in Odia rather than Sanskrit.
  - The *Madala Panji*, the temple's own chronicle, is a rare continuous indigenous historical record, used to reconstruct Odisha's dynastic sequence.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; *Madala Panji*.
