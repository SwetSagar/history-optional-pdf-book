- **Location**: Sringeri, Chikkamagaluru district, Karnataka, on the left bank of the Tunga in the Malnad forests of the Western Ghats, upstream of the Tunga-Bhadra confluence.
- **Period/Level**: The Sharada Peetham, traditionally the first of the four mathas founded by Adi Shankara in the 8th century, with Sureshvaracharya as its first head. Its documented history runs strongly from the 14th century, when the Vijayanagara kings became its patrons; the Vidyashankara temple dates to 1338.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The southern seat of Advaita Vedanta, and the best-documented case of a monastic institution shaping the founding of a kingdom.
  - It is the *dakshinamnaya* matha, assigned the Yajur Veda and the *mahavakya* "Aham Brahmasmi". Its unbroken succession of *jagadgurus* makes it the institutional continuity of Shankara's tradition rather than merely a shrine.
  - Vidyaranya, the 14th-century pontiff, is credited in tradition with inspiring Harihara and Bukka to found Vijayanagara, and the dynasty took the matha's blessing for its authority. The temple-state relationship - a monastery legitimising a kingdom, a kingdom endowing a monastery - is the point examiners want.
  - The Vidyashankara temple is architecturally hybrid, Hoysala in its stellate base and figural carving, Vijayanagara in its pillars. Twelve zodiac pillars are arranged so that the sun strikes each in its own month.
  - Sringeri's copper-plate grants and correspondence, including letters from Tipu Sultan after a Maratha raid on the matha in 1791, are used as evidence for how rulers of every religion dealt with established religious institutions.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; J. L. Mehta, *Advanced Study in the History of Medieval India* I.
