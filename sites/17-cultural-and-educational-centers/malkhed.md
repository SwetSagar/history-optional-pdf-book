- **Location**: Malkhed, ancient Manyakheta, Kalaburagi (Gulbarga) district, Karnataka, on the Kagina river in the northern Deccan - the dry interior plateau between the Bhima and the Godavari.
- **Period/Level**: Rashtrakuta capital from c. 814 CE, when Amoghavarsha I moved the seat here, until the dynasty fell in 973. Sacked by the Paramara Siyaka II in 972, an event that marks the Rashtrakuta collapse; the Western Chalukyas of Kalyani then held the region.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The capital of the dynasty an Arab geographer ranked among the four great kingdoms of the world, and a court that made Kannada a literary language.
  - Al-Masudi, writing in the 10th century, named the Rashtrakuta *Balhara* one of the four greatest kings on earth beside the Abbasid caliph and the rulers of China and Byzantium - the standard external testimony to Rashtrakuta power, and evidence of the dynasty's Arab trade contacts through the Konkan ports.
  - Amoghavarsha I is credited with the *Kavirajamarga*, the earliest surviving literary work in Kannada, and with the Sanskrit *Prashnottara Ratnamalika*. A capital that produced the first Kannada poetics is the key point for questions on regional languages entering literature.
  - The court was a Jain centre: Jinasena wrote the *Adipurana* here, and Mahaviracharya composed the *Ganita Sara Sangraha*, the most important Indian mathematical text between Brahmagupta and Bhaskara II. Shaiva and Vaishnava learning was patronised alongside.
  - Little survives above ground, so Manyakheta is known largely from copper plates and literature - a reminder that an imperial capital can leave almost no architecture.
  - *Sources*: Basham, *The Wonder that was India*; Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*.
