- **Location**: Sharda, in the Neelum (Kishanganga) valley about 140 km from Srinagar, in the part of the erstwhile princely state of Jammu and Kashmir now administered by Pakistan. The ruined temple and college stand where the Madhumati joins the Neelum.
- **Period/Level**: A seat of learning and a Shakti shrine attested from about the 6th century CE and flourishing under the Karkota, Utpala and Lohara dynasties to the 12th. The standing temple, in dressed limestone with the trefoil arches and pediments of the Kashmiri order, is usually assigned to the 8th-9th centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The centre that made Kashmir the byword for Sanskrit scholarship - *Sharada Desha*, the land of the goddess of learning.
  - The Sharada script, used for Sanskrit in Kashmir and the Punjab hills from about the 8th century and ancestral to Gurmukhi and Takri, takes its name from this shrine. A script named after a university is unusual and is the most quotable fact about the site.
  - Its library and its authority in adjudicating scholarship drew visitors from across the subcontinent. Tradition records both Shankara and Ramanuja consulting texts here, the latter said to have come for a commentary on the *Brahma Sutra*; Al-Biruni names the shrine, and Kalhana's *Rajatarangini* refers to it.
  - It is counted among the eighteen *Maha Shakti Pithas*, where Sati's right hand is said to have fallen, so a major pilgrimage and a major college occupied one site - the normal medieval arrangement, seen also at Nalanda and Sringeri.
  - Its decline follows Kashmir's political disruption from the 14th century; the building was largely ruined by earthquake and neglect.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 10; Thapar, *Early India*; Kalhana, *Rajatarangini*.
