- **Location**: Dwarka, Devbhumi Dwarka district, Gujarat, at the mouth of the Gomti creek on the western tip of the Saurashtra peninsula - the first Indian landfall for shipping crossing the Arabian Sea from the Gulf.
- **Period/Level**: Occupation from the late Harappan and post-Harappan centuries, attested at Bet Dwarka offshore. The Sharada matha, Shankara's western seat, is assigned to the 8th century; the Dwarkadhish or Jagat Mandir temple in its present five-storeyed form is largely of the 15th-16th centuries on older foundations.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the Char Dham and the Sapta Puri, a working port, and the most contested piece of marine archaeology in India.
  - The *paschimamnaya* matha is assigned the Sama Veda and the *mahavakya* "Tattvamasi", completing the four-matha scheme with Sringeri, Puri and Badrinath.
  - Offshore excavations led by S. R. Rao from the 1980s recovered stone anchors, wall alignments and pottery in the submerged area, and were presented as the Dwarka of the *Mahabharata*. The dating and the identification are both disputed, and the site is the standard example for discussing how archaeological evidence gets attached to epic narrative. Present the finds and the controversy together.
  - The triangular and ring stone anchors are genuine evidence of a substantial anchorage, and Bet Dwarka has yielded late Harappan material, so an early maritime settlement is not in doubt even where the epic identification is.
  - As a port Dwarka handled the Gujarat coasting trade and the Gulf route, and the temple economy and the harbour reinforced each other - a pattern repeated at Somnath and Prabhas.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 5 and 10; Thapar, *Early India*; S. R. Rao, *The Lost City of Dvaraka*.
