- **Location**: Nabadwip, Nadia district, West Bengal, on the west bank of the Bhagirathi where the Ganga turns south - a river town whose channel has shifted repeatedly, carrying parts of the old settlement away.
- **Period/Level**: A seat of Sanskrit learning from the 11th century, capital of the Sena kings under Lakshmanasena until Bakhtiyar Khalji's raid of c. 1204. Its intellectual peak is the 15th-16th centuries, when the Navya-Nyaya school flourished and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, b. 1486, d. 1533, was born and took sannyasa here.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: Two distinct claims on the same town - the most rigorous school of Sanskrit logic in late medieval India, and the source of the Bengal bhakti movement.
  - Navya-Nyaya, the "new logic", was developed here by Raghunatha Siromani and Vasudeva Sarvabhauma, building on Gangesha's *Tattvachintamani*. Its technical language for stating relations was adopted across Sanskrit disciplines, and Nabadwip displaced Mithila as the school that awarded degrees in logic.
  - Chaitanya founded Gaudiya Vaishnavism on the doctrine of *Achintya Bheda Abheda* - inconceivable difference and non-difference between god and the world - and made *sankirtana*, congregational singing of the divine name, its central practice. He left for Puri in 1510 and spent his last twenty-four years there.
  - The movement's social reach is the examinable point: it admitted low castes and Muslims, Haridasa Thakur being the best-known case, and it produced a body of Bengali devotional literature, above all Krishnadasa Kaviraja's *Chaitanya Charitamrita*.
  - That a centre of austere scholastic logic also produced ecstatic devotionalism is the standard illustration that the two streams were not opposed in practice.
  - *Sources*: Satish Chandra, *Medieval India* II; Krishnadasa Kaviraja, *Chaitanya Charitamrita*; S. K. De, *Early History of the Vaishnava Faith*.
