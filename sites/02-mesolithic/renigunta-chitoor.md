- **Location**: Renigunta, Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh, on the Swarnamukhi drainage below the Seshachalam hills - the gap in the Eastern Ghats through which the routes from the Tamil plain reach the Deccan plateau.
- **Period/Level**: An Upper Palaeolithic industry with a Mesolithic phase above it, investigated by M. L. K. Murty. The Upper Palaeolithic of peninsular India is generally placed between about 40,000 and 10,000 years ago, with the microlithic phase following.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A reference site for the Upper Palaeolithic blade-and-burin industry in south India, and for the transition into the Mesolithic.
  - The assemblage is dominated by burins, blades, scrapers and points struck from prepared cores on quartzite and chert - the blade-and-burin technology that defines the Upper Palaeolithic and replaces the earlier flake industries.
  - Murty's work in this region, including the Kurnool caves nearby, established the south Indian Upper Palaeolithic as a distinct stage, which had been poorly recognised before. Bone tools and ostrich eggshell from related sites in the belt strengthened the case.
  - The overlying microliths show the technological shift to composite tools with hafted geometric elements. Because both stages occur in one locality, Renigunta is used to argue for local continuity rather than population replacement.
  - The site sits in the same hill tract as Tirupati, so the entry pairs usefully with the later history of the region.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; M. L. K. Murty, *Pre- and Protohistoric Andhra Pradesh*.
