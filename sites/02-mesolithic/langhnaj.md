- **Location**: Langhnaj, Mehsana district, north Gujarat, on a sand dune between seasonal marshes in the semi-arid plain north of Ahmedabad.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated from the 1940s by H. D. Sankalia and the Deccan College - among the earliest scientific excavations of an Indian prehistoric site. Three phases run from a Mesolithic microlithic occupation of roughly the 3rd millennium BCE into levels contemporary with the Harappan and later periods.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the Gujarat Mesolithic, and the standard evidence that foragers and urban societies were in contact rather than sealed off from each other.
  - Fourteen human burials were recovered, laid in crouched position, along with a large geometric microlithic industry in chert, agate and chalcedony, and ring stones and hammer stones.
  - The faunal assemblage is unusually rich - rhinoceros, wolf, nilgai, blackbuck, chital, mongoose and fish - and shows a wetter environment than the region has now, which is the principal palaeoclimatic evidence for Gujarat in this period.
  - Copper objects, a Harappan-type ring and wheel-made pottery appear in the upper levels. Hunter-gatherers acquiring metal and urban-made goods is what makes Langhnaj important: the Harappan world is best understood as surrounded by foraging communities it traded with.
  - Sankalia's work here established the methods and the stratigraphic approach later applied across western India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; Thapar, *Early India*; H. D. Sankalia, *Prehistory and Protohistory of India and Pakistan*.
