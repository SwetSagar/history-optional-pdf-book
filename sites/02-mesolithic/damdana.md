- **Location**: Damdama, Pratapgarh district, Uttar Pradesh, beside a former oxbow lake in the Ganga plain - the third of the great Mesolithic sites of this tract, with Sarai Nahar Rai and Mahadaha. The record is filed under the spelling *Damdana*.
- **Period/Level**: Excavated by J. N. Pal and V. D. Misra. Radiocarbon dates cluster around the 8th-7th millennia BCE, making it among the earlier Mesolithic settlements of the plain, though as at Sarai Nahar Rai the chronology is debated.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest Mesolithic cemetery in India, and the site with the best evidence for plant food processing.
  - Forty-one human burials were recovered - more than at any other Indian Mesolithic site. Bodies were laid extended in oblong pits oriented broadly east-west or west-east, singly and in groups, some with elaborate grave goods including an ivory pendant, bone ornaments and antler.
  - Querns, mullers, anvils and hammer stones were found in quantity, together with charred wild grains and burnt floor plaster. Grinding equipment on this scale shows systematic processing of plant foods by people who were not farming, which complicates any simple line between foraging and agriculture.
  - Claims of domesticated rice at the site have been made and are not securely established; wild rice in the Ganga plain is well attested, and the honest position is that the grains show intensive use of wild cereals.
  - Animal bones indicate hunting and fishing across a wide range of species, matching the broad-spectrum pattern of its neighbours.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; J. N. Pal, *Mesolithic Cultures of the Ganga Valley*.
