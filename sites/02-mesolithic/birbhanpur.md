- **Location**: Birbhanpur, Barddhaman district, West Bengal, on the left bank of the Damodar river a little west of Durgapur - on old alluvial terraces at the edge of the Chhotanagpur plateau.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic microlithic site excavated by B. B. Lal in 1957-58, generally placed in the earlier Holocene, roughly the 5th millennium BCE, though it is undated by radiocarbon.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the Mesolithic in Bengal, and an unusual case of a non-geometric microlithic industry.
  - The tools are blades, points, scrapers and burins on quartz, chert and chalcedony, and are largely *non-geometric* - lacking the trapezes and lunates that dominate at Bagor and in the Ganga plain. Regional variation in microlithic form is the site's main analytical contribution.
  - It served as both a habitation and a factory site, with cores, debitage and finished tools together, so the whole production sequence is present.
  - No pottery and no burials were found, which distinguishes it from the Ganga plain sites and suggests either a shorter occupation or a different way of life.
  - The site was found on a terrace that formed under drier conditions than the present, and it is used with other eastern Indian sites to reconstruct the Holocene climate of the lower Ganga basin.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; B. B. Lal, *Birbhanpur: A Microlithic Site*.
