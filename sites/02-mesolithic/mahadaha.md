- **Location**: Mahadaha, Pratapgarh district, Uttar Pradesh, on the edge of a silted oxbow lake in the Ganga-Gomti doab, a short distance from Sarai Nahar Rai and Damdama.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic lake-side settlement of roughly the 6th-5th millennia BCE, excavated by G. R. Sharma, J. N. Pal and V. D. Misra. Three functionally distinct areas were identified across the site.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The clearest evidence anywhere in India that a Mesolithic camp was deliberately organised into zones for different activities.
  - Excavation separated a habitation area, a butchering area with concentrated animal bone and burnt patches, and a cemetery. Most prehistoric sites are known only as undifferentiated scatters, so a site where living, processing and burial spaces can be told apart is exceptional evidence for social organisation.
  - Twenty-eight burials were recovered, including double burials of a man and a woman placed together. Grave goods include microliths, bone arrowheads, antler and bone ornaments, shell beads and ochre - the ornaments suggesting distinctions of status or role.
  - The skeletons show tall, robust people with excellent dentition and little caries, consistent with a coarse, low-carbohydrate diet, but with osteoarthritis in the joints, pointing to heavy physical labour. Bioarchaeology of this quality is rare in Indian prehistory.
  - The microliths are geometric, on chert and chalcedony carried in from the Vindhyas, with bone and antler tools worked on site.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; J. N. Pal, *Mesolithic Cultures of the Ganga Valley*.
