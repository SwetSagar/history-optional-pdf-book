- **Location**: Sanganakallu, Ballari district, Karnataka, on granite hills near the Tungabhadra in the Raichur doab - the ashmound country of the southern Deccan, close to Kupgal and within sight of the Hampi hills.
- **Period/Level**: A long sequence from a Mesolithic microlithic phase through the South Indian Neolithic, c. 3000-1200 BCE, into a Chalcolithic overlap. Excavated by B. Subbarao in the 1940s and re-investigated from the 1990s by Korisettar, Fuller and Boivin.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the South Indian Neolithic, and the location of stone axe production on an industrial scale.
  - The hills carry dolerite quarries and workshop floors covered in flaking debris where ground and polished stone axes were made in very large numbers - far beyond local need, implying production for exchange across the peninsula. Few Indian sites document craft specialisation this early so clearly.
  - Circular huts with post-holes on terraced hill slopes, burnished grey, buff and brown pottery, black-and-red ware, terracotta bull and bird figurines, and cattle-dominated faunal remains complete the settlement picture.
  - Ashmounds nearby - great heaps of vitrified cattle dung - and rock art showing humped cattle on the same hills make the case that the southern Neolithic was organised around cattle, with periodic ritual burning of pens.
  - Archaeobotany from the recent work identified indigenous millets and pulses - browntop millet, horsegram, mungbean - showing an independent South Indian centre of crop domestication rather than borrowing from the north.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; Fuller, Boivin and Korisettar, *Antiquity*.
