- **Location**: The Bundi region of south-eastern Rajasthan, in the Vindhyan sandstone hills of the Chambal basin - rock shelters scattered along the Chambal and its tributaries near Bundi town.
- **Period/Level**: Rock paintings assigned broadly to the Mesolithic and later prehistoric periods, with some estimates of 10,000 to 15,000 years for the earliest layers and historic-period work over them. More than fifty painted shelters have been recorded in the district, many documented by the local researcher Om Prakash Kukki.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The largest concentration of prehistoric rock art in Rajasthan - the western counterpart to the Vindhyan shelters at Bhimbetka.
  - The paintings use red ochre and dark red pigment, with white in later layers, and show hunting scenes, human figures, animals, dancing rows and geometric designs. Superimposition allows a relative sequence even though absolute dates are lacking.
  - Horse-riders and weapons in the upper layers place some work in the historic period, so the shelters record continuous use across many thousands of years rather than a single prehistoric moment.
  - The Chambal basin joins the Vindhyan belt at Bhimbetka and the Kaimur shelters to form the largest rock art province in India. Bundi extends that province west and is the evidence that it was not confined to central India.
  - Microlithic scatters occur in and around the shelters, tying the paintings to the Mesolithic occupation of the region.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; ASI.
