- **Location**: Sundargarh district, north-western Odisha, in the upper Brahmani and Ib valleys on the margin of the Chhotanagpur plateau - hill and forest country bordering Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh. This record is duplicated as *Sudergarh* in the missed-PYQ chapter, and the two should be merged.
- **Period/Level**: A district-wide prehistoric sequence rather than a single site: Palaeolithic and Mesolithic surface industries on the river terraces, painted rock shelters at Ushakothi, Vikramkhol and elsewhere, and Neolithic and later occupation.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the principal prehistoric regions of eastern India, and the source of a famously contested inscription.
  - Microlithic industries on the Brahmani terraces place the district within the eastern Indian Mesolithic, alongside Kuchai in Mayurbhanj, and its rock shelters carry paintings in red ochre and white showing animals, human figures and geometric designs.
  - The Vikramkhol shelter bears a long line of incised signs which K. P. Jayaswal in 1933 read as a script transitional between the Indus signs and Brahmi. The reading has not been accepted: most scholars regard the marks as natural or as undeciphered scratchings, and no sequence linking Indus signs to Brahmi has been demonstrated. Present it as a rejected hypothesis, because it still circulates in note-sets as established fact.
  - The surrounding hills are rich in iron ore, worked by local communities long before modern mining, and the district's Munda-speaking populations make it relevant to arguments about the antiquity of Austroasiatic settlement in India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2 and 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
