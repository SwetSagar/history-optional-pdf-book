- **Location**: The Tirunelveli region of southern Tamil Nadu, on the Thamirabarani river between the Western Ghats and the Gulf of Mannar - the belt of red sand dunes known locally as *teris* stretching towards the coast at Thoothukudi.
- **Period/Level**: Microlithic industries on and within the teri dunes, broadly Mesolithic and running into the later Holocene. Studied by F. E. Zeuner and B. Allchin in the 1950s and 60s, whose work defined the *teri industry*.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-region of the teri microlithic industry - the distinctive Mesolithic of the far south.
  - The teris are fossil dunes of red, iron-stained sand formed when the coastline and climate differed from today. Microliths occur both on their surfaces and stratified within them, so the dunes date the industry and the industry dates the dunes - a rare reciprocal control in Indian prehistory.
  - The tools are worked largely on quartz, which fractures poorly, and the industry is accordingly less refined than the chert-based assemblages of Rajasthan and the Ganga plain. Raw material constraining technology is the clearest lesson of the teri sites.
  - Their distribution along the palaeo-coastline is used to reconstruct sea-level and shoreline change in the far south during the Holocene.
  - The same tract later carries Adichanallur, the great urn-burial site, and the Pandya port of Korkai, so the region has an unusually continuous archaeological record from the Mesolithic to the early historic period.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; B. Allchin, *The Stone-Tipped Arrow*.
