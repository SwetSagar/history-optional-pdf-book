- **Location**: Ratanpura, north Gujarat, a low dune site in the semi-arid plain of the Rupen and Saraswati drainage, in the same sandy tract as Loteshwar and Nagwada.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic and Chalcolithic dune settlement of the Anarta tradition, broadly the 4th-3rd millennia BCE, investigated by MS University, Baroda, as part of the north Gujarat survey programme.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the cluster of north Gujarat dune sites that define the Anarta tradition - the indigenous culture of the region before and during the Harappan period.
  - Note that in the working notes for this book, Ratanpura carries the description written for Loteshwar. The two are separate sites in the same survey region and should not be conflated; Loteshwar is the excavated type-site, Ratanpura one of its neighbours.
  - The north Gujarat dune sites share a pattern: seasonal camps on *tekris* above the flood plain, microlithic tools continuing alongside Chalcolithic pottery, and bones of domesticated sheep, goat and cattle with wild fauna. They represent mobile herding communities rather than settled villages.
  - Their significance is regional: Gujarat's Chalcolithic was not simply Harappan. The Anarta tradition, the Padri ware of Saurashtra and the Harappan settlements coexisted, and the dune sites are the evidence for the non-Harappan component.
  - Systematic survey rather than single-site excavation is what established this picture, which is worth noting as a methodological point.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*; MS University Baroda, north Gujarat survey reports.
