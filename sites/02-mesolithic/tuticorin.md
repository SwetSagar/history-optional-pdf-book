- **Location**: Thoothukudi, on the Gulf of Mannar coast of southern Tamil Nadu - the seaward end of the teri dune belt that runs inland towards Tirunelveli.
- **Period/Level**: Mesolithic microlithic scatters on the coastal teri dunes, part of the same industry defined for the Tirunelveli region and broadly Holocene in date.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: A coastal Mesolithic locality, and the point at which the teri dune sites meet the sea.
  - The assemblage is a flake industry worked predominantly on quartz, the only abundant raw material in the tract. Its coarseness relative to the chert industries of northern and western India reflects that constraint rather than any difference in skill.
  - Coastal sites of this period are scarce in India because Holocene sea-level rise drowned most of the contemporary shoreline. The teri dunes survive because they lie slightly inland of the present coast, which makes them unusually valuable for studying coastal foragers.
  - The sites are used with the Tirunelveli teris to map shoreline change: their positions mark where the coast stood when they were occupied.
  - The same stretch of coast later carries the Pandya pearl fishery at Korkai and the port of Kayal, so Thoothukudi's archaeological record runs from Mesolithic foragers to Roman-period pearl exports along one shoreline.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; B. Allchin, *The Stone-Tipped Arrow*.
