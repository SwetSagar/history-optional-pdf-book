- **Location**: Selbalgre, West Garo Hills district, Meghalaya, on a hill slope in the Garo uplands of the north-east - the record is filed under the spelling *Sebalgiri*.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic and Neolithic occupation in the Garo hills, broadly Holocene, excavated as part of the north-eastern prehistory programme. The region's Neolithic is generally placed in the 3rd-2nd millennia BCE, with the earlier microlithic phase preceding it.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of very few excavated prehistoric sites in the north-east, and evidence for animal keeping in the hills.
  - Microliths and bones of domesticated cattle were recovered, extending to the north-east the pattern seen at Bagor and Tilwara - foraging communities keeping animals without settled agriculture.
  - The Garo hills sites, with Daojali Hading in the North Cachar hills, define the north-eastern Neolithic. Its diagnostic tools are shouldered celts and cord-impressed pottery, forms that connect the region to Southeast Asia and southern China rather than to the Indian Neolithic traditions.
  - That external orientation is the analytical point. The north-east is best understood as the western end of a Southeast Asian cultural province, not as a distant periphery of the Gangetic world, and these sites are the evidence for it.
  - The region's *jhum* cultivation, and the possibility that root and tuber crops rather than cereals were the first domesticates here, make it important in arguments about multiple independent agricultural origins in South Asia.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; T. C. Sharma, *Prehistoric Archaeology of North-East India*.
