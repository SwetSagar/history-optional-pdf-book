- **Location**: Tilwara, Barmer district, western Rajasthan, on the bank of the Luni - the only substantial river of the Thar, draining south-west into the Rann of Kutch.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic settlement with two cultural phases, excavated by V. N. Misra. Period I is aceramic with microliths; Period II carries pottery and later material, the sequence running into the historic period.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: With Bagor, the evidence base for animal domestication among Mesolithic communities of western India.
  - The faunal remains include domesticated humped cattle, *Bos indicus*, alongside wild species - goat, canine, pig, spotted deer and hog. Humped cattle at a Mesolithic site in the Thar margin is a significant datum for the Indian domestication of zebu cattle, which is independent of West Asian cattle domestication.
  - Period I has stone-paved living floors and hearths, with an extensive geometric microlithic industry on locally available chert and chalcedony.
  - The Luni valley sequence - Tilwara with Bagor to the east - is the framework for prehistoric occupation of the Thar, and matters for reconstructing when and how the desert became arid. The faunal and pollen evidence indicates a wetter early Holocene.
  - The site is also cited in discussions of how foraging and pastoral ways of life persisted in western Rajasthan long after farming had been established elsewhere in India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; V. N. Misra, *Prehistoric Human Colonization of India*.
