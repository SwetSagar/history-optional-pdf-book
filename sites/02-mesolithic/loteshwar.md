- **Location**: Loteshwar, Patan district, north Gujarat, on a sand dune - locally a *tekri* - rising above the plain near the Rupen river, in the semi-arid country between the Little Rann and the Aravalli foothills.
- **Period/Level**: Two periods, excavated by P. Ajithprasad of MS University, Baroda. Period I is Mesolithic, roughly the 6th-5th millennia BCE; Period II belongs to the Anarta tradition, c. 3700-2000 BCE, overlapping the Harappan centuries.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The type-site of the Anarta tradition, and the clearest sequence anywhere in Gujarat from foraging to herding.
  - Period I has a microlithic industry and hearths; Period II adds gritty red ware, fine burnished ware and Anarta pottery, with pits, post-holes and two burials. The two periods on one dune let the shift be watched in a single stratigraphy.
  - Bones of domesticated sheep, goat and cattle from the lower levels show animal keeping starting early here, as at Bagor and Tilwara in Rajasthan.
  - The Anarta tradition is the indigenous Chalcolithic of north Gujarat, named from the region's ancient name. Its pottery occurs alongside Harappan wares at sites such as Nagwada and in the later levels here, so Anarta communities were neighbours and trading partners of the Harappans rather than their subjects - the key point for questions on Harappan Gujarat.
  - Period II pottery shows affinities with Amri-Nal wares of Sindh and with Surkotada in Kutch, tracing the contact routes.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 3 and 4; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; P. Ajithprasad, in *Indus Civilization Sites in India*.
