- **Location**: Thenmala, Kollam district, Kerala, on the Kallada river in the Western Ghats - a forested valley on the road over the Ariankavu pass between Kerala and the Tamil plain.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic occupation with wood charcoal dated to about 5000 BCE, among the earliest absolute dates for prehistoric occupation in Kerala. The Ghats interior has yielded far fewer prehistoric sites than the peninsular uplands, so the sequence here is thinly documented.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: One of the few dated Mesolithic sites in Kerala, and evidence that the Western Ghats forests were occupied rather than merely crossed.
  - Wood charcoal and worked wood recovered from the deposits gave the radiocarbon determination. Organic preservation of this kind is rare in Indian prehistory, where almost all dating rests on associated material rather than on the artefacts themselves.
  - Kerala's prehistory is dominated by the megalithic period - the laterite chambers, umbrella stones and urn burials of the Iron Age - and by the Edakkal petroglyphs in Wayanad. Thenmala pushes occupation of the state back several millennia before those.
  - The Ghats were long treated as a barrier between the Malabar coast and the Tamil country. Sites in the passes suggest instead that the forest belt was inhabited and that the passes were routes, which matters later for the trade through Muziris and the Palghat gap.
  - The evidence remains limited, and the honest position is that Kerala's Mesolithic is known from a handful of localities rather than a regional sequence.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*; Kerala State Department of Archaeology.
