- **Location**: Kuchai, Mayurbhanj district, northern Odisha, near Baripada on the Chhotanagpur plateau fringe - lateritic upland between the Subarnarekha and the Baitarani.
- **Period/Level**: A Mesolithic settlement with a Neolithic phase above it, the later levels placed around 1750 BCE. It was the first Mesolithic site reported in Odisha and remains the reference sequence for the region.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The site that established Mesolithic-to-Neolithic continuity in eastern India - a transition visible in one stratigraphy rather than inferred.
  - The lower levels carry microliths on quartz and chert; the upper add ground and polished stone celts, shouldered axes and pottery, without a break in occupation. Communities adopting new technology in place, rather than being replaced, is the point the site is cited for.
  - The pottery is handmade reddish-brown with a slip and incised decoration, of a kind found across the eastern Indian Neolithic.
  - The shouldered celts link Odisha's Neolithic to the north-eastern group in Assam and Meghalaya and beyond to Southeast Asia, rather than to the Gangetic or southern Neolithic traditions. That typological link is central to arguments about Austroasiatic-speaking populations in eastern India.
  - Mayurbhanj's earlier Palaeolithic sites, investigated from the 1870s by V. Ball and later by N. K. Bose and D. Sen, give the district one of the longest investigated prehistoric records in India.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2 and 3; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; *Indian Archaeology - A Review*.
