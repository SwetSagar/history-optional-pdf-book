- **Location**: Bagor, Bhilwara district, Rajasthan, on a sand dune beside the Kothari river, a tributary of the Banas - in the eastern Rajasthan plain below the Aravallis.
- **Period/Level**: Three phases excavated by V. N. Misra. Period I, c. 5000-2800 BCE, is Mesolithic; Period II, c. 2800-600 BCE, adds copper; Period III, to c. 200 CE, adds iron. The sequence is continuous, which is unusual and is the site's main value.
- **Key Finds & Significance**: The richest microlithic assemblage in India, and the best evidence that Mesolithic communities kept domesticated animals long before they farmed.
  - Some fifty thousand microliths were recovered, in quartz, chert and chalcedony, predominantly geometric - trapezes, lunates and triangles. Finished tools are relatively few against the debitage, which suggests tools were made here and carried elsewhere.
  - Bones of domesticated sheep, goat and cattle appear in Period I alongside wild fauna. Herding without cultivation is the key point: it breaks the tidy progression from hunting to farming that the three-age scheme implies, and Bagor is the standard citation for it.
  - Stone-paved floors, hearths and five extended burials with grave goods show a settled or repeatedly reoccupied camp rather than a transient one.
  - Copper arrowheads in Period II indicate contact with the Chalcolithic settlements at Ahar and Gilund a short distance away - foragers and farmers exchanging goods rather than one replacing the other.
  - *Sources*: Upinder Singh, ch. 2; R. S. Sharma, *India's Ancient Past*; V. N. Misra, *Excavations at Bagor*.
